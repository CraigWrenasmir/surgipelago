# Surgipelago Canon Bible

The single source of truth for every article generated on **Surgipelago**, the
(fictional) fan-encyclopedia of the franchise *A Complicated Surgery Will Take
Place on the Beach Tonight*. Everything generated must be consistent with — or a
*deliberate, signposted contradiction of* — the facts below.

---

## 0. The conceit (read this first)

- **The novel is real and finished.** *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on
  the Beach Tonight* is a complete 2020 novel by **C. W. Smith** (Craig Warren
  Smith), published by **Abrachas Publishing**, cover by **Kim Jung Gi**, end
  illustration by **Kate Nastas**. ~67,000 words. This is true. Do not contradict it.
- **The story inside it is unfinished.** The novel is a memoir/testament whose
  narrator has spent ~15–18 years tinkering with an *outline* of a story he calls
  *Beach Surgery* — about **Leif** and **Katita** — which he has **never
  finished**. It contains an irreparable structural fault he calls **"the glitch"**:
  the seam between the story's two halves "does not compute."
- **The franchise is the scar tissue.** Surgipelago documents a vast, sprawling,
  contradictory body of adaptations — manga, anime, films, games, operas, theses,
  LARPs, perfumes — *each of which is an attempt to finish the unfinishable core
  and resolve the glitch, and each of which finishes it differently.* This is why
  the labyrinth can grow forever and why versions contradict one another. The
  contradiction is canonical.
- **Register:** deadpan, neutral, encyclopedic. Never wink at the reader. Dread
  is never in the chrome; it surfaces inside otherwise flat prose (a disputed
  volume, an episode "widely believed never to have aired," two edits that
  quietly disagree).

## 1. The two protagonists

### Katita
- A young woman in a hybrid costume between **nurse and sword-wielding assassin**.
  First-aid kit on her belt; a **bastardised samurai sword** ("as sharp as chalk
  dust") on her back. Flak jacket dusted with **red desert sand**; thigh-high
  socks; shiny **red kitten heels**. **Red hair.** Possibly blood wiped across her
  face. **Red is her master motif.**
- She does **not smile** for the entire story until one of the very final scenes.
- She is the **agent of the cycle**: cold, strategic, grief-stricken. She is
  trying to **"break the cycle"** — to make the spinning of the world reverse.
- Derived by the novel's narrator from his memory of the **redhead medical intern**
  he met at a Street Fighter tournament and married (his wife). Name synthesised
  from the **fox ears** his wife wore (canine → feline → "Katita").
- Watermelon/green-flower eyes (the wife's; sometimes transferred to Katita).
- Can "hear **the sound of the earth rubbing against space**" — see §3.

### Leif
- A **military engineer** in a state of physical damage and **memory loss**.
- Begins the story with **three temporary injuries**: he **cannot walk**, he
  **cannot see** (bandaged eyes), and his **heart is out of whack** (a hacked-together
  external **pacemaker** with a blinking red diode on his chest). Floral/**Hawaiian
  hibiscus shirt.** Clutches a massive **hand cannon** "as though he hopes to fuse
  it with his bones."
- Three injuries ↔ three chapters per half of the story (structurally load-bearing).
- **"Leif" = anagram of "Life."**
- Devoted to Katita by a love "she has fashioned in the most surgically strategic
  of ways." He follows her not under a spell but because she feels like "someone
  you might possibly love."
- Backstory: a foreign **conflict zone near a beachfront**; refugees arriving by
  raft; a **boy taken by the waves**; Leif dives from a cliff to save the boy and
  is smashed unconscious. Katita (the triage nurse) resuscitates him, flies him
  home to Australia, and drives him to her **makeshift surgery out the back of New
  South Wales**. From that dive she decided **"Leif is her tool of change."**
- At the story's climax, **white wings** push from his shoulder-blades and he
  briefly flies, before crashing. (Where Katita keeps checking his back/shoulder
  blades earlier — she is watching for the wings.)

### The three injuries — and the three temptations (major thread)
Leif's three temporary injuries are structurally load-bearing (three injuries ↔
three chapters per half) and are increasingly read as a re-staging of the three
wilderness temptations of Christ — especially as reframed in **Dostoevsky's "The
Grand Inquisitor"** (*The Brothers Karamazov*) as **miracle, mystery, and authority**:
- **Cannot see** (bandaged eyes) ↔ **mystery** — faith without sight; Leif follows
  Katita blind, unable to verify; his corrupted "second sight" (doubled, layered
  vision at the radio igloo) is a *false* miracle of seeing.
- **Cannot walk** (the legs) ↔ **miracle / the pinnacle leap** — "cast thyself down,
  the angels shall bear thee up." Leif does not walk; he is *borne*. At the climax
  the white **[[the wings|wings]]** erupt and he flies — then falls, Icarus-like. The
  miracle he succumbs to is precisely what destroys him, every loop.
- **Heart out of whack** (the external **pacemaker**) ↔ **authority / bread** — the
  inmost rhythm of life governed by an external machine; the surrender of
  self-governance to a sovereign that keeps the body beating ("feed them first").
  When Katita removes the pacemaker his heart is "stronger" — but the wings and the
  fall follow.
**Ambiguity to preserve:** Katita may be the Grand Inquisitor figure *administering*
the temptations and the engineered devotion ("fashioned in the most surgically
strategic of ways") — OR the one trying to *free* Leif from them, where "break the
cycle" means *refuse the bargain, refuse to be borne up.* Each loop he succumbs
(flies) and falls; the temptation is never finally refused, which is one true face of
**the glitch**. Each of the three chapters of a half can stage one injury-temptation.
This is a **major, generative interpretive thread** — develop it freely (episodes,
plays, operas, theses that stage one injury-temptation each; works that recast Katita
as Inquisitor or as liberator).

### Leif & Katita recur across the whole oeuvre (they ARE "instruments of return")
[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not confined to Beach Surgery; they recur, transformed, across
C. W. Smith's works — the literal sense of "instruments of return." Many are gathered in the omnibus
*[[Pastoral Scanlines]]*, which also reprints "Scenes from Beach Surgery" and the author's
meta-disclosure: '''"Leif" is an anagram of "Life,"''' and Katita was named from his wife's fox ears
and "watermelon eyes." The collection treats them as a recurring character-WORLD, not a linear story
— each piece casting them in a different phase or register. Verified appearances:
- ***A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*** — the canonical pair.
- ***Leaving/Leading*** (poem) — a date through [[Newcastle]] (a zine launch, dinner at Big Al's,
  a rooftop bar, a theatre); they turn elemental — Katita "a boat drifting," Leif an obelisk to
  reach her — and dissolve into the city. ("When Leif asked Katita / if she felt like joining him
  for dinner / at Big Al's perhaps, / and she said sure.")
- ***Summer Endzone*** / "Garden Monologue #2" (prose-poem) — Leif & Katita a settled couple in a
  summer garden; Katita carries a radio downhill into the city after a pirate station; "Leif will
  meet Katita on the beach soon."
- ***Saltando*** (prose) — Leif walks a beach barefoot, recognises Katita from a street fair where
  he played piano; she invites him to the baths; he falls from a boulder and she embraces him
  underwater, "her body a mass of bubbles." ("Katita, she introduced herself after.")
- ***Garden Monologue #1*** (prose) — Katita alone, here the narrator's WIFE and a biology lecturer
  running a "Hybrid Harvests" project; she becomes a "cat-bird." ("my wife, Katita…")
- ***Their Most August Public Organ*** — Katita as a living collaborator building the archive (§8).
- ***Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time*** — CONFIRMED, both appear, transformed: **Leif** the
  one-armed, jovial founder/host of the solar-punk community ("Abolish Power, Grow Freedom"; runs a
  VR/Moondust music sim); **Katita** its property manager and the grounded counter-voice to Leif's
  VR-utopianism ("he hasn't read enough Baudrillard to believe it all yet").
- ***A Billiard Table with Five Balls and Twelve Cues*** — Leif & Katita as a married pair of
  UN-conference academics evacuated to a fishing island off **West Shanbudia** (the novel's
  [[Shanbudia]]); Leif abandons his algorithms; the "billiard table" = Mozart's stripped apartment.
- ***Pugil*** — in the collected (*[[Pastoral Scanlines]]*) version Leif (a baker and prizefighter)
  and Katita (a sleepless writer) appear BY NAME — the bakery, the two-on-one fight, the baths'
  [[the alarm-clock baptism|alarm-clock baptism]]; the STANDALONE web version renames them Max &
  Adria. (A vivid instance of the pair travelling renamed.)
- ***Untitled*** (opening piece of *[[Pastoral Scanlines]]*) — Leif alone, a near-silent figure in a
  rural hamlet among "Pleur" and "Varva."
Adaptations and theses may treat Leif & Katita as a transmigrating pair surfacing across Smith's
whole body of work — the same souls under changing names and forms. Reference the above accurately;
do not invent appearances in works not listed here.

## 2. Structure & plot of *Beach Surgery* (the embedded story)

**Time:** two days, divided into two halves of one day each. **Six chapters**
(three per half), **four scenes per chapter** (and, per the narrator, five micro-
scenes per scene — usually ignored). Half 1 = the city of **Newcastle**; Half 2 =
the **rural/desert interior** of New South Wales.

### Half One — Newcastle
- **Ch.1 (10:30pm→5:30am):** Rooftop of **Bolton Street car park**. Katita wheels
  the unconscious Leif across two **parallel wires** to an apartment; surprises the
  elderly **Mr & Mrs McRae**, who deploy pendant **exoskeletons** ("the **Mighty
  Mechas**"). Cartoon battle; Leif's hand cannon. Escape down a 12-storey stairwell.
  A **hotdog** eatery; they learn of the coming **protest**. Katita rakes dust on an
  abandoned **shopping-centre** floor like a zen garden and broods on history,
  truth, language. Sleep.
- **Ch.2 (5:30am→12:30pm):** Rooftop parkour toward the beach. **Dirtheart**
  activists (animal masks). A giant mechanical **seagull**. They stumble into an
  abandoned **preschool**; Leif tells the children stories. Katita lectures the
  activists on "the nightmare of history." On the way down, Katita tells the
  embedded tale of **Rico the Architect** (see §4).
- **Ch.3 (12:30pm→7:30pm):** A hidden **underground stone swimming pool** beneath
  the city (reached through a street-art mural), fed from the harbour. They swim
  through to the harbour; the seagull snatches them; they cut free and land back on
  the **same apartment balcony** ("circle of life"). They reach the beach at dusk —
  and the scene **dissolves into a desert hospital room**, Katita in scrubs:
  *"Honey. I know you've just woken up. But. We need to go for a drive."*

### Half Two — The interior
- **Ch.4 (7:30am→2:30pm):** Leif drives Katita and a **box of medical supplies**
  across red desert. Autonomous diesel **data-harvesters** pursue. A **service
  station**; the **mechanic** who is *also* the police officer Leif tackled earlier
  (identity slippage; Leif sees "ten layered versions"). A **radio/radar igloo**:
  raising the frequency to a pitch the robots can't hear also "corrects" Leif's
  doubled vision. The pressure in his shoulder blades grows.
- **Ch.5 (2:30pm→9:30pm):** A **watering hole** with **crocodiles**; a
  decommissioned cloud-shaped surveillance **drone** full of years of **instant
  photographs**, including Katita standing with a man Leif doesn't recognise —
  *"Leif. That is you."* A rural town; Katita frees a **boar** tied to a grazing
  robot; a car chase out of town. A cabin: Leif builds a **rocket cart**; Katita
  makes **leather armour** (there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits —
  the cycle has run many times). *"Hey Leif. How about you and me make a baby."*
- **Ch.6 (9:30pm→4:30am):** Wild dogs, military police, Dirtheart activists, a
  farmer's wood-chipper mech — total chaos at the cabin. They flee on the rocket
  cart down to the mouth of the city. Leif asks Katita his **twelve-word question**
  (never quoted in full). On the cart, at sunrise, **wings** erupt from Leif's back;
  he flies; then crashes catastrophically. Katita — laughing, then screaming **no** —
  survives in her armour. She dresses the broken Leif in his Hawaiian shirt, puts
  bandages back over his eyes, sets him in a folded **wheelchair** from the medical
  box, places the hand cannon in his lap, presses the heat-sharpened **pipe-blade**
  into a sword ("You cannot do surgery without a sword"), and resets: *"We need to
  break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"* **The story loops.**

**The glitch:** the narrator can never make Half Two connect to Half One. "The link
does not compute." Every adaptation must invent its own bridge — and they disagree.

## 3. Master motifs (use constantly; they are the connective tissue)

- **The coin with one side.** "There is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole
  way around. And around. And around we go." Day/night, father/not-father, two-that-
  is-one. Change vs. recurrence.
- **The sound of the earth rubbing against space** — a low, nauseating drone at the
  edge of silence; Katita names it the **Karman line** (the boundary of atmosphere
  and space). The story's **"white whale."** Its opposite: the **high-pitched
  resonance of the human spine** (a high D for men, high G for women). Katita's
  secret theory: if the earth's spin *reversed*, the screech of its braking would
  match the spine's pitch.
- **Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle.** History as negative poles repelling
  into perpetual spin. Katita wants it to **stop and reverse**.
- **Red.** Hair, blood, first-aid cross, kitten heels, desert sand, diode, sunset.
- **Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat.** "Love is always surgery." **Surgery = BIRTH:**
  at the novel's end the "complicated surgery on the beach" is revealed to be the birth of the
  narrator's child — he becomes [[Leif]], pushing his blindfolded, heart-monitored wife in a
  wheelchair into a delivery room with red hibiscus light; Katita's "there is no surgery to fix
  what is wrong with mine [heart]" recentres surgery as love. (See `canon/concordance.md` for the
  full cross-oeuvre concept map.)
- **Analogy is the core of all cognition** — "the only way a thought can exist is
  when it mirrors itself against its equivalent."
- **Language is a game of fictions** — "language can only ever talk about language";
  "truth is a language game"; but "your child is coughing and it does not sound
  good" — choose the path of least harm.
- **The empty world.** Two lovers alone in a depopulated world, fleeing an unnamed
  pursuer. **Empty World Meditations** (guided second-person tapes). Games played
  inside the head while walking an empty city.
- **Wings / flight / falling** (Icarus; dandelion seed-heads; the breakwall).
- **Fatherhood / pregnancy / birth / the caught descent** (the frame's secret subject — see
  [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent]]). The embedded story is, in part, the narrator's
  mind anticipating his daughter's birth. At the birth the wife becomes [[Leif]]+[[Katita]] fused
  (wheelchair, blindfold, heart monitor, hibiscus light, red hair); the baby "floats like a
  feather" — [[the wings]] redeemed — while the mother "listens for the rubbing of matter against
  space" ([[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]) and "instinctively
  holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent": the catch that answers every fall
  ([[the boy in the waves]], Leif's crash). A "little sword" cuts the cord; "the first breath is
  just a breath." Fatherhood breaks [[the cycle]] into a new generation: "I forgot how to think
  about the future." Cabin: "How about you and me make a baby"; the news: "guess who is going to
  be a daddy." Also: the Airah/Error daughter-game (two-that-is-one); Rico's "pregnant" buildings.
- **Synthesised nature** for "the children of the future"; **data-farms** (pastures
  of hard drives cooled in rivers; birch-powered server nodes); prove-you're-not-a-
  robot; the overturned law letting AI pass as human (the **Dirtheart** flashpoint).

## 4. Embedded tale: Rico the Architect
Rico, youngest of a line of architects, can build **functioning miniature cities
inside other people's bodies** — but never inside himself (no mirror works).
Children won't help (his problem is "too small"; they only solve apocalypses);
lovers won't (secrets die outside their private language). **Mylar**, youngest of a
line of surgeons, presses his hand to the wall of the **town hall** and performs
"her surgery" — Rico's body grows into and becomes the breathing building; windows
glow crimson; the doorway warps into "a contented smile." A story-within-the-story
that the manga/anime love to expand. Leif, hearing it, says *"I knew every word of
it before you said it."*

## 5. The frame (the narrator's real life — source of "deep cut" adaptations)
First-person narrator (≈ C. W. Smith): a play/childhood-development expert who ran a
UN workshop in the desert megacity of **Shanbudia** on "how children might play in
the cities of the future." At the closing dinner he **"erupted"** (a public
breakdown, "became a volcano"), filmed by a colleague; a stranger left a poem
("**a portrait of an eruption**") on his stomach on the flight home. He is writing
this testament before his first child is born. Recurring real-life furniture, all
fair game as the origin of franchise ephemera:
- Met his wife (redhead medical intern) at a **Street Fighter II** arcade tournament,
  2001, Newcastle. (Street Fighter / fighting games / **"you can nullify a fireball
  with a fireball"** epigraph.)
- **The Watt Hotel** (Beaumont St, Hamilton) — the most dangerous pub in town.
- **Styx Creek** / bus driver "Charon" / Dante.
- **The Dampened Cardboard** — a jazz club whose band is made entirely of
  **corrugated-cardboard instruments** played by dancing on the floor; a robot
  greeter with a letterbox head.
- The **public baths**: an **astronaut baptised** with a string of **alarm clocks**
  rung underwater.
- **Pinball machines**: as a boy he composed music for Japanese pinball machines he
  never heard played. **Bach hymn × Street Fighter melody.**
- **Les** — dead friend; aluminium **bee automaton**; dairy country.
- His **wife** — artist; grew up near an elderly **Nobel-laureate novelist**;
  the unrepeatable **bird** on the rail line whose sound and look could not be held
  in mind at once.
- **Newcastle** — "seventh largest city in Australia," a "bite-sized labyrinth," of
  "ontological incompleteness" (buildings that vanish when you look away): **Rose
  House/Cottage**, **Bolton Street car park**, the breakwall & lighthouse, the
  ruined pub with an **oak growing through it**, Maitland, Hamilton, the coal loader.
- **Shanbudia** — West-Asian-peninsula desert megacity; synthesised-nature interiors;
  the **dome** of the conference centre he climbs in the heat on his final, depopulated
  day; **Empty World Meditations**.
- Named greens from the workshop: `#7CFC00` lawngreen, `#7FFF00` chartreuse,
  `#00FA9A` mediumspringgreen.
- Epigraphs: **Gerald Murnane, *The Plains*** ("The invisible is only what is too
  brightly lit") and the **Street Fighter II** manual.

## 6b. Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future" (canon principle)
The archive admits works dated BEFORE the novel (pre-2020), even before living memory — but
ONLY as **disputed, apocryphal, lost, or "precursor" artifacts**, never as a flat "X adapted
the novel in [year]." Such an item must wear its impossibility openly: known through a single
eyewitness account, a destroyed archive, a circulating rumour, or a hoax allegation;
verification impossible; fandom split on whether it concerns Beach Surgery at all. Frame it as
a precursor the novel seems to have summoned *backward* — grounded in *Antinomicity*'s "the
past is always a spontaneous product of the present," the loop of [[the cycle]] (which has no
fixed beginning), and the Borgesian principle ("Kafka and His Precursors"; Pierre Menard) that
a work creates its own forerunners. These anachronisms live mostly in [[lost media]] and
apocrypha; they are GOVERNED anomalies, not continuity slips. The great majority of adaptations
still postdate 2020 — the pre-novel precursor is a rare, deliberate, uncanny exception.
Exemplar done right: *[[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]]*.

## 8. C. W. Smith's wider oeuvre (reference ACCURATELY)
Beach Surgery is one node in [[C. W. Smith]]'s larger body of work (at wrenasmir.com).
The wiki may situate Beach Surgery intertextually within this oeuvre.
**ACCURACY RULE:** reference these REAL works only by their real title/form; do NOT
fabricate plots, quotes, or specific contents for works not detailed in this bible —
at most note shared MOTIFS as scholarly observation, clearly as interpretation.
**Works read & verified (use these specifics accurately):**
- ***Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)*** (essay, ~400 words) — the philosophical
  KEYSTONE. Consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself analogously; the subject
  is the irreducible split between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks — an
  "ontological incompleteness," a "less than nothing" (−1) between the real (0) and the
  ideal (1), figured as a **Möbius strip**. Quotes: "Consciousness, subjectivity, is an
  analogy of itself"; "That gap is you." → underwrites the novel's "analogy is the core of
  all cognition," **the one-sided coin** (Möbius), [[Rico the Architect]]'s mobius town
  hall, and **[[the glitch]]** as a generative gap.
- ***Antinomicity*** (novella, 2022, ~36k; preface + four chapters I–IV + epilogue) — his THIRD
  book. *(I)* [[Newcastle]] TAFE / drain / gasworks with his young son; *(II)* a train to Sydney
  with his daughter (Vocaloid "Gemini" more human than its human covers; the 1978 Hilton bombing;
  the Singapore poem's "The Fighter" & "The Failure," two-that-is-one); *(III, the centre)* the
  full [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] lecture (chalk, Möbius,
  "that gap is you"); a train recording stretched 8× into "a bag full of alarm clocks dragged to
  the bottom of an empty public pool" (cf [[the public baths|the alarm-clock baptism]]);
  *(IV)* a convict cabin near Wollombi; the cyanotype conjoined leaves that name the title ("one
  leaf was meant to grow there, but then two developed in a state of antinomicity"); a GPS
  projector casting a palimpsest of family time on the TAFE wall. **Beach Surgery connections (no
  Leif/Katita by name):** "my first attempt at a novel was birthed at the top of [[Bolton Street
  car park|that car park]]"; near-verbatim novel imagery ("lighthouse shine, by the beachscape
  shadowed by military fortifications … hospitals being converted into robotics warehouses"); the
  **Tin City** couple's mirror beach-shack / red-desert-shack with no recoverable origin; the
  chemical-plant teenage beach by a disused runway. Quote: "The past is always a spontaneous
  product of the present."
- ***Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time*** (lyric essay/autofiction, 2024; the title essay of a
  three-piece collection with *Sovereignty is an Artificial Soul* and *The Glass House*) — turning
  forty (the "year of fours"); a journalism commission leads to a **"solar-punk" anarchist
  hacker-gardening community** across the Great Dividing Range, built on reclaimed radar igloos and
  aircraft graveyards (the inverse of the novel's [[the data-harvesters|data-farms]]). '''Leif and
  Katita both appear''' — Leif the one-armed founder/host ("Abolish Power, Grow Freedom"; a
  VR/Moondust music sim), Katita its property manager and grounded counter-voice. Also: dog
  **Combray** (Proust); two real letters from **Gerald Murnane** ("time is place" — the novel's
  epigraph author); pirate radio (how the narrator met his wife); an imagined symphony of "a girl
  who pushes a pinball machine across the Australian landscape, from the outback to the sea, to the
  moon's reflection." Berryman epigraph: "His heart, a mud-puddle, sang."
- ***Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters*** (2025, ~20k;
  subtitle "an essay on four incomplete poems": Bodies, Decades, Flowers, Nights) — BOOK ONE of
  the triptych. E-bike rides through [[Newcastle]] (the **Bogey Hole**, the demolished Jolly Roger
  pub); the **Empty World Meditation** sequences appear here, numbered non-sequentially (#3, #9,
  #27 → a larger series; cf [[Empty World Meditations]]); the "failure IS the revelation" /
  "surplus remainder" thesis; "foreverism." It **quotes Chapter 1 of Beach Surgery verbatim**
  ("Better living without geometry"; "an echo rising from the centre of myself"; "Aimless walks
  are a gift") and names ***Their Most August Public Organ*** as the next book in progress.
  **Leif and Katita do NOT appear here.**
- ***Beyond Correct and Incorrect Nature*** (essay, ~2.5k) — "Nature is perfect, but we are
  not"; nature inaccessible, neither correct nor incorrect; stories as survival; neurodiversity
  as natural variation → the novel's "Nature is perfect but nurture is not," the
  pinprick-in-the-keyhole ethics, the unrepeatable [[the bird on the rail line|bird]].
- ***Pastoral Scanlines*** (Selected Works '20–'25, ~465pp / ~79k words) — the omnibus collecting
  prose, poetry and plays, incl. *Saltando*, *Leaving / Leading*, *Summer Endzone* (Garden Monologue
  #2), *Garden Monologue #1*, *A Billiard Table with Five Balls and Twelve Cues*, *Pugil*, *Fellow

**The triptych (important authorial context — the deepest key to this project):**
*Everyone I Love is Alive…* is BOOK ONE; book 2 is ***Their Most August Public Organ*** (in
progress, ~23 pp so far) — experimental typeset prose with embedded musical notation and
radio/instrument diagrams (the title's "organ" is at once musical, bodily and broadcasting);
a train journey south of [[Newcastle]], the memory of an itinerant summer, and a correspondent
called "the Boxer" (boxing footwork rendered as music; tape rhythms; always a detuned radio
"buzzing like bass resonance … broadcasting news" — cf [[the sound of the earth rubbing against
space]] and the radio igloo). **Crucially, much of book 2 follows the narrator and [[Katita]]
(here a living collaborator with her own "project") building a buried pirate data-archive across
the NSW plains — solar transceivers in tree-knots broadcasting a `/ROOT … index.html … /audio
/text /images` file-tree, "our small town eucalyptus version of Voyager's Golden Records" — and
it names the condition "the surplus of the seen": that all versions of the lived and imagined
world have already been recreated and cross-referenced. SURGIPELAGO IS THAT ARCHIVE REALISED;
the trilogy blueprints this very project.** Book 2 also recreates passages from the novel
directly (the suburban backyard and its corrugated-cardboard quotes, the video-store Sega boxes,
the food-court where Katita orders "Their Most August Public Organ") and threads the global
avant-gardes (Stridentists, Cannibalists/Antropofagia, négritude, Jindyworobak, Vorticists) —
grounding Surgipelago's global distribution in Smith's own text. Book 3 is ***Surplus of the
Seen*** (in planning). *Surplus of the
Seen* is the keystone for Surgipelago: ~90 pp of experimental prose ("fiction & nonfiction in
open collision"), set in [[Newcastle]], beginning in Gregson Park, in which a narrator close to
Craig and his wife (close to **Teresa**) move through the city **re-entering, misremembering and
partly reliving Beach Surgery itself.** Its form is a **rondo** — "Theme A" is the Beach Surgery
re-enactment, recurring between expansive "surplus chambers" (Every Face; 5,000 Photographs; The
Dead Mall; Salvage Cabinet; Every Room; Screens). Its small heart: a shooting star over Gregson
Park the narrator keeps forgetting to tell his wife — until the end, when she answers "Oh, I saw
that too." Its thesis, and the truest description of THIS project: **"Leif and Katita are not
recycled characters. They are instruments of return"** — we don't escape ourselves by inventing
new lives but by returning to, misremembering and re-seeing the one. So Surgipelago is a sibling
recursion: *Surplus of the Seen* relives the novel *in lived reality*; the franchise relives it
*in media*; both deepen the one work by returning to it. (Books 2–3 are forthcoming/in-planning —
reference accurately, do NOT fabricate beyond the above.)
