the glitch as koanic refusal

This article discusses the glitch as theological and linguistic principle. For the structural glitch in the novel, see the glitch. For apophatic approaches, see Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery.

The Glitch as Koanic Refusal
Concept
TraditionZen Buddhism; apophatic theology
Central paradoxA problem that cannot be solved, only inhabited
FunctionNot resolution but transformation of the questioner
Related motifsthe glitch, Sacred Incompleteness, Doctrine of Refusal

The glitch operates as a koan — a paradox that admits no rational solution but demands perpetual meditation. Unlike a puzzle with an answer, the seam between Half One and Half Two remains irreducible; each adaptation's "solution" spawns a new glitch in return. Scholars [1] read this structure through apophatic theology, where truth approaches only through negation: what the glitch is not matters more than what it is.

The koan's function is not resolution but transformation of the questioner. Similarly, Beach Surgery adaptations do not finish the glitch — they transfigure the reader by asking her to dwell inside irresolution. This mirrors Zen practice: the question "What is the sound of one hand clapping?" has no answer; its value lies in the breath-held silence, the surrender of expecting closure.

Smith's Antinomicity formalizes this through the ontological incompleteness lecture: consciousness itself is a koanic gap, the split between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks. The glitch is not a flaw to repair but an invitation to stop expecting repair. [1]
