Site-specific works and Beach Surgery

This article surveys site-specific installations, walking tours, and locative works. For immersive theatre, see Immersive works and Beach Surgery; for architecture and monuments, see Architecture and Beach Surgery.

Site-specific works and Beach Surgery
Geographically anchored adaptations and devotional spaces
Article scope
CoverageInstallations, pilgrimage walks, and locative media rooted in actual places
Geographic distributionGlobal; Newcastle, NSW interior, Africa, Middle East, Asia-Pacific, Americas
Update frequencyOngoing; many works are durational or site-responsive

Site-specific and locative adaptations of Beach Surgery comprise a distinct practice: artworks and rituals designed for and inseparable from particular geographies and built environments. Rather than transposing narrative into new media while preserving its fictionality, site-specific works embed the story inside actual cities and landscapes, collapsing the boundary between adaptation and pilgrimage.

The practice emerges from the novel's deep cartographic specificity: Newcastle (Half One) and the rural interior of New South Wales (Half Two) are not generic backdrops but load-bearing coordinates. Adaptations inherit an obligation to ground themselves in place. the glitch thus becomes a spatial problem: the two halves refuse to connect geographically as well as narratively.

Key trajectories

Newcastle-based works include The Circuit at Nobbys, tracing Leif's rooftop parkour against actual buildings; The Corridor of Frequencies — An Audio-Locative Walking Tour of Newcastle's Ontological Incompleteness (2023–present), broadcasting GPS-triggered audio across sites mapped in the novel.

Interior works include The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior (2022–present), a seven-day walk following the second half; The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion—A Traveling Medical-Art Installation, touring regional NSW with mobile structures staging the makeshift surgery using local materials.

Multi-sited distributed works span continents: The Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels (Addis Ababa, 2019–ongoing), The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space (Accra, 2022–ongoing), and The Red Meridian — A Devotional Cartography and Collective Textile Pilgrimage (2021–present, multiple sites) treat the franchise's motifs as a diaspora.

Architecture and ritual merge in permanent structures like The Nairobi Spiral Chamber—A Brutalist Listening Pavilion and Pilgrimage Site and The Shoulder-Blade Sanctuary—An Improvised Brutalist Meditation Igloo in Ankara (2023–2024), designed as devotional spaces around the wings, the three injuries, or Kármán resonance.

Site-specific works resolve the glitch by staging it as architectural seam: a wall, a river, a literal journey between fragmented geography. Many adopt psychogeographic methods drawing on London dérive, Australian songlines, and Māori spatial genealogy.
