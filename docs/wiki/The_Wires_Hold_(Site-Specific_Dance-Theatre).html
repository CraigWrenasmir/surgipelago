The Wires Hold (Site-Specific Dance-Theatre)

This article concerns a 2009 choreographic work set in the Newcastle/Hunter region. For the source moment in the novel, see The rooftop and the wire.

The Wires Hold is a 2009 site-specific dance-theatre duet by  choreographers  exploring the canonical moment when Katita wheels the unconscious Leif across parallel wires stretched between two structures.

Performed across three Newcastle-based venues (2009–11) and later adapted for rural Hunter Valley sites, the work stages two dancers in sustained contact above ground, their bodies connected by wire harnesses, negotiating balance, trust, and the erotic charge of vulnerability. The work makes explicit the erotic dimension of the novel's moment: the exposure of bodies in transit, the intimacy of holding and being held, the precarity of faith.

Each performance is staged at a different architectural site—rooftops, between buildings, or temporary structures. The dancers move without a predetermined choreography; instead, they respond to the physical constraints and frequencies of each location, improvising within a set of scoring principles that privilege suspension, contact, and the moment at which trust breaks or holds. The wire becomes a metaphor for the seam between halves, the glitch, the threshold that must be traversed.

Critical engagement has situated the work within Australian experimental theatre traditions (cf. 1994 immersive theatre) and postcolonial dance practices exploring consent and vulnerability. The 2012 Lake Macquarie iteration incorporated Baul singing traditions through collaboration with  guest artist , deepening the work's engagement with global distribution of the franchise.
