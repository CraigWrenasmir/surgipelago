The Frequency Cairn (Accra Listening Station, 2024)

This article documents the open-access radio pavilion operational since June 2024 in Accra. For its narrative precursor, see The radio igloo and temporal loops.

The Frequency Cairn is a community radio pavilion and sound-installation site built into a rammed-earth mound on the Jamestown waterfront in Accra, Ghana, completed June 2024. The site is a material reinterpretation of the radio igloo from Chapter 4—the subterranean listening station where the frequency of Leif's corrupted double vision is corrected by tuning to the spine's resonant pitch.

The Cairn comprises three dome-shaped corrugated-metal chambers sunk into the mound, each fitted with salvaged and vintage radio equipment. Listeners enter at dusk, choose a dome, and tune broadcasts. The transmitted frequencies reportedly encode fragments of Beach Surgery retellings, though no official archive exists and all testimony is anecdotal [citation needed]. The station's documentation is deliberately fragmentary, mirroring the novel's unfinished structure.

The pavilion integrates Ghanaian vernacular earthwork architecture and functions simultaneously as a listening venue, pirate-broadcast archive, and informal pilgrimage site. It has become a hub for West African experimental radio artists, with visiting theatre collectives performing embedded tales like the Architect cycle on-site. No municipal license; the site operates under community stewardship and tacit local tolerance.
