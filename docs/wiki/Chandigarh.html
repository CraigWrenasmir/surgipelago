Chandigarh

Tri-Khoṇe is an independent participatory art response to Beach Surgery, not officially affiliated with the C. W. Smith estate.

Parallel works have emerged in Kolkata and Ahmedabad; see also Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

ⓘThis article documents an evolving live installation. Information current to August 2026.
Tri-Khoṇe—A Chandigarh Geometric Pilgrimage
Three Corners, One Refusal
Work
Format7-day participatory LARP cycle
LocationChandigarh, India—three intersections within Le Corbusier's planned urban grid
Primary themeThe three temptations — and the three injuries
Active2022–present
Completed cohorts43+ (open seasonal recruitment)

Tri-Khoṇe (Hindi/Punjabi: "the three corners") maps Leif's three temptations onto three geometric intersections in Chandigarh's Le Corbusier-designed rational grid. Participants walk a seven-day cycle between corners, each representing one temptation: blindness and faith, levitation and miracle, and authority over heartbeat.

At each corner, guides (costumed half-nurse, half-architect, echoing Katita) maintain participants awake for 48 hours with water, bread, and sensory prompts. “Your eyes are fine. But can you walk?” At the second corner: “The city will bear you up—if you let it.” At the third: “Your heartbeat is not your own.”

By day seven the geometry fractures. Participants report time-loops, the city growing inside memory. The final moment asks: will you refuse this cycle, or enter it again? Sixty percent re-enter; others exit.

Since 2022, forty-three cohorts have cycled through, generating an open-access archive of testimony, photograph, and audio—what organizers call "the Chandigarh Counter-Archive": a text that grows by refusing to finish. Related installations have emerged in Kolkata (Char-Khoṇe, four corners, 2024) and Ahmedabad (Nava-Khoṇe, nine corners, 2025).
