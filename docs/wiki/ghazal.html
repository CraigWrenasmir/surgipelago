ghazal

The ghazal is a classical lyric poetic form originating in 7th-century Arabic and elaborated in Persian and Urdu traditions. It consists of autonomous couplets (bayt), each complete in itself, linked by a recurring refrain: a rhyme (radif) preceded by an assonant echo (qafia). The opening couplet (matla) contains both; subsequent couplets introduce qafia+radif only at their close. Crucially, couplets can be read in any order—the form is circular, non-linear, endlessly re-readable.

The ghazal's architecture mirrors the glitch. Each adaptation (manga, opera, film) is a bayt: autonomous, complete, yet only fully resonant in dialogue with all others. Katita's red meridian is the qafia—the recurring thread that gives meaning to every variation. The twelve-word question is the radif: one unreachable point around which all utterance revolves. The beloved in classical ghazal (ma'shuq) is forever absent; in Beach Surgery, the beloved is the unfinishable story itself.

The Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage (2022–present) stages the ghazal as a walking meditation, its six-part vocal arrangements drawing on a disputed 2024 transmission:  River_Convergence_Harmonic_Analysis_Corrupted.mp3 , whose six voices sing in converging patterns, each line a separate couplet that means differently when sung alone [citation needed].
