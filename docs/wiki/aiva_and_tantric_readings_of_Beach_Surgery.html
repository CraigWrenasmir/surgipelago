Śaiva and tantric readings of Beach Surgery

This article surveys theological and philosophical readings from Hindu Śaiva and tantric traditions. For nonduality more broadly, see Advaita Vedanta.

Hindu Śaiva and tantric readings locate Beach Surgery within the metaphysics of eternal return and divine nonduality. Katita, in this interpretive frame, is not a character trapped in recurrence but an embodiment of Śakti (cosmic feminine power), orchestrating cycles of manifestation and dissolution. The resonance of the human spine corresponds to kundalini—the divine energy coiled at the base of the spine whose awakening realizes the unity of Śiva (consciousness) and Śakti (creative power). The coin motif reflects Ardhanarishvara, the form of Śiva and Śakti as a single body, neither dominanting—a perfect statement of the one-sided nature of cosmic structure. Scholars in this tradition argue that the glitch is not a narrative flaw but an apophatic principle: the impossibility of joining the two halves mirrors the impossibility of comprehending Śiva-Śakti unity in dualistic language. Recent dissertations [citation needed] have traced echoes of tantric ritual (the sacred transgression of taboo as spiritual practice) in the surgery-as-intimacy motif. To "break the cycle" in this reading is not to escape samsara but to awaken within it—to recognize that Leif and Katita are already nondual, their separation itself the play of divine play.
