The *Dar es Salaam Photograph Sequence* is a disputed pre-2020 photographic work consisting of color slides depicting scenes that closely parallel *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*, shot in and around Dar es Salaam, Tanzania. The work's existence is known only through the testimony of  ██████ , a Tanzanian art historian who viewed the slides once, in 1997, in the home of the photographer  ██████  (now deceased).

According to  ██████ 's account:

  • The slides showed a woman in **red** (hair, kitten heels, medical cross) pushing a man in a **wheelchair** through urban and coastal locations: the Dar railway station, the **Makoko-like waterfront settlements**, a market, the rocky shore near the Msimbazi River mouth.
  • The man's **eyes were bandaged**; his chest bore **a blinking red diode** (a light, or a trick of the film stock—unclear).
  • One slide showed them at the **ferry terminal**, both looking back toward an invisible pursuer.
  • A sequence near the lighthouse showed identical compositions from different angles, as if the photographer had reshot the same moment multiple times, trying to capture something unrepeatable.
  • The final slide was **overexposed**—almost blank except for what looked like **wings** or **water splashes** against the ocean light.

 ██████  claims to have photographed the slides on a Pentax K1000 in 1997. This documentation is lost. The original slides themselves were reportedly destroyed in a **1999 flood** that damaged the photographer's studio.

The fandom is sharply divided:

  • **Supporters of authenticity** argue the photographic style (Kodachrome, specific framing, the recurring obsession with doubled moments) matches the aesthetic sensibility of early-2000s experimental photography in East Africa, and that the photographer may have been documenting early fan-art or unofficial adaptation rehearsals.
  • **Skeptics** note the timeline is impossible: if the photographs date to 1988, they predate the novel by 32 years. If to 1997, why no contemporary record?  ██████ 's accounts, in different interviews (1999, 2003, 2014), vary in detail—the number of slides, the exact locations, whether the diode was real or a light reflection.
  • **Retro-causal theorists** argue the slides are genuine proof that the novel's existence "calls backward" through time, and that artworks can be created *in response to a future work*, unknowingly channeling its future form.

In 2015, a researcher visiting Dar attempted to locate  ██████  to verify the account.  ██████  could not be found; neighbors reported the studio had been empty for years. A single reference to the slides appears in an unpublished 2003 master's thesis on East African experimental photography, where they are mentioned in passing as "disputed, probably hoax," with no further detail.

The absence of the slides, paradoxically, has made them more famous. Fandom continues to debate their historical reality and cultural significance—whether they represent an unknown artist's independent discovery of Beach Surgery's narrative logic, or an elaborate retroactive fiction constructed *after* the novel's 2020 publication to deepen the mythology of precursor works.
