African adaptations

Beach Surgery has sparked a rich, regionally-rooted body of African adaptations, drawing on indigenous performance traditions, sacred art forms, and philosophical cosmologies. The work's themes of eternal return, healing, refusal, and incompleteness resonated across sub-Saharan cultures; both Leif and Katita have been recast as griots, warriors, healers, mourners, each adaptation grounding the narrative in local vernaculars.

West Africa

The Aladura Kekere (2016) adapted the novel as Yoruba travelling-theatre, with Leif as a war-injured soldier and Katita as village healer and trickster. Seven-night performance cycle; audiences could attend all nights or single episodes, mirroring the novel's refusal of linear resolution. Used traditional Yoruba mask-work (sàngó, ògún), call-and-response chanting; the twelve-word question became a collective incantation—each night's version differed [citation needed].

Textile adaptations: Kente weavers in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire created multipart cycles depicting Beach Surgery scenes. The Dust Garden cycle uses red as connecting thread (Katita's hair, blood, desert sand), inviting viewers to trace their own path through colour rather than prescribed sequence.

Music: Afrobeat ensembles composed concept albums. The Ciclo Rojo  (2019 ) uses the Grand Inquisitor structure as sonic framework, male and female vocalists debating Leif and Katita across seven tracks. Final track: eleven minutes of studio silence, echoing the unspoken question.

Film: Nollywood adaptations, including the 2014 Lagos O Procedimento, reimagined the story as medical conspiracy, Katita as whistleblower doctor, Leif as journalist [citation needed].

East Africa

Icon-panel tradition: The Debre Damo Cycle (1993, now lost) was twelve painted panels in Byzantine style for a monastery; apparently deemed theologically suspect and abandoned [citation needed].

Contemporary practice: Addis Ababa Biennale (2021) featured the Merkato Meridian, a participatory walking ritual through Addis Ababa's market, following a mapped route corresponding to Leif's doubled vision and Katita's breath-count. Georgian voices performed—a deliberate transposition honouring both cultures.

Film and LARP: Kenya's The Caught Descent began as installation, evolved into participatory LARP with audiences role-playing Leif and Katita's final descent.

Central Africa

The ongoing Kinshasa Descent (2023–2025) is a seven-day ritual cycle rooted in Bantu healing philosophy and ubuntu (shared being), with participants enacting Leif's three injuries. The Ouagadougou textile cycle (1989; status disputed [citation needed]) reportedly involved elder weavers creating a multiyear cloth using indigo dyes, with oral narration of the weaving. Status of project unclear [citation needed].

Theological dimensions

African adaptations frequently emphasise theological reading. The work's eternal return aligns with cyclical African cosmologies (ubuntu, ancestral return); Katita's refusal to complete surgery resonates with traditional healing ethics favouring care over cure. Several theses have explored how African adaptations rework Western assumptions about closure and redemption, instead centring seasonal return, collective healing, and sacred incompleteness of human knowledge.
