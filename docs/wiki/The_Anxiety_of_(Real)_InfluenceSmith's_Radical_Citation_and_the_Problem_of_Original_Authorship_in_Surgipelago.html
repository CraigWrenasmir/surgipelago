The Anxiety of (Real) Influence—Smith's Radical Citation and the Problem of Original Authorship in Surgipelago

This theory treats Smith's *visibility of influence* as deliberate refusal of Bloom's anxiety of influence—a radical move that collapses the boundary between original and derivative. Companion to The Doubled Cosmology—Lawrence's Flesh and DeLillo's Media Dread in Smith's Recursive Method and The Möbius Descent—Joyce and the Eternal Return in C. W. Smith's One-Sided Ethics. For the philosophical implications, see The Ontological Seam—Sebald, Beckett, and Sacred Incompleteness in Smith's Ethics of Witness.

A metacritical fan theory exploring the *deliberate visibility* of C. W. Smith's literary lineage—to Hermann Hesse, W. G. Sebald, Gerald Murnane, D. H. Lawrence, Samuel Beckett, James Joyce, Ben Lerner, and others—as a radical *refusal* of Harold Bloom's "anxiety of influence."

The Transparency Problem

Smith does not hide his debts. He announces them openly and repeatedly:

  • Gerald Murnane's epigraph ("The invisible is only what is too brightly lit") opens the novel explicitly.
  • W. G. Sebald appears throughout the critical apparatus and thesis titles (Sebaldian Melancholy, Sebaldian Newcastle).
  • D. H. Lawrence's hibiscus motif is explicitly named in adaptation titles and appears in the Hawaiian shirt, the red wounding, the vitalist eros.
  • James Joyce and the Möbius strip are signature concepts, openly referenced in theses (The Möbius Descent, the coin with one side).
  • Samuel Beckett's doctrine of refusal and the ethics of "failing better" structure Leif's narrative arc.

The Genealogy Becomes the Work

This radical transparency, the theory argues, **creates a secondary incompleteness**: we cannot locate where Smith's original voice ends and the inherited voice begins. This uncertainty is not a flaw or accident but *intentional and generative*.

The theory proposes that **the glitch itself *is* the glitch of literary influence**—the moment when the precursor text bleeds through, when the writer becomes a palimpsest of prior writers, when "originality" breaks down because all creation is made from prior art.

Surgipelago fandom reproduces this uncertainty: scholars write theses comparing Smith to Sebald, Beckett, Lawrence, but cannot conclusively establish what is Smith versus what is inheritance. The more we learn about Smith's lineage, the less distinct the work becomes. **The gaze that seeks to locate Smith's voice only multiplies the voices.**

Smith's Answer: Lerner's Confession

The theory notes Smith's affinity with Ben Lerner, who in *The Hatred of Poetry* and *Topography* likewise admits that the artist is made entirely from prior artists—that the anxiety of influence is the anxiety of being a *consciousness entirely constituted by prior consciousnesses*. Smith does not overcome this anxiety; he **enacts it**: every adaptation, every thesis, every red hair and hibiscus shirt and radio igloo is legible as a quotation from Sebald, Lawrence, Joyce, Beckett, and others.

To finish Beach Surgery would be to *pretend* that the voice could be separated from its sources. Surgipelago's refusal to finish is thus also a refusal to pretend to originality. The work's incompleteness is the confession that literary creation *is* the recombination of prior voices, and refusal means accepting this without shame.
