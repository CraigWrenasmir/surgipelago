The Clinic Ledger at Broken Hill

Plot and structure

*The Clinic Ledger at Broken Hill* is presented as the recovered journal of an unnamed physician at an improvised surgical facility in remote New South Wales. The entries document the arrival, treatment, and discharge (or re-arrival) of two patients: **"Patient Leif"** (male, severe head trauma, temporary blindness, cardiac arrhythmia requiring external pacemaker; wounds consistent with a high-altitude fall) and **"Patient K"** (female, multiple lacerations, presenting with a field medical kit of professional grade, vital signs stable, demeanor composed and directive).

The journal spans approximately 72 recorded days, though the dating itself becomes suspect as entries progress. Early entries are clinical: wound assessment, vitals, medication logs, radio consultations with distant urban hospitals. By the midpoint, the physician's handwriting deteriorates; entries become fragmented, obsessive, annotated with marginal queries. By the final entries, the physician is openly hypothesizing temporal recursion, identity slippage, and the possibility that the clinic exists outside "normal time."

Clinical observations become impossible

By week three, the physician notes:

  • Patient K's wounds heal at an abnormal rate (scarring visible within 48 hours).
  • Patient Leif's cardiac arrhythmia corrects, then recurs, then corrects again—following a pattern the physician eventually recognizes as **periodic, rhythmic, deliberate**.
  • Both patients demonstrate knowledge of events that have not yet occurred in the clinical timeline.
  • Patient K repeats identical conversations on non-adjacent dates, word-for-word, as though reciting from memory of a previous (identical) occurrence.

One entry states: "Patient K said today 'We've done this seventy times and counting.' I asked her to clarify. She did not answer. When I checked the ledger, I found the identical exchange written in my own hand on (redacted date), yet I have no memory of writing it."

The redaction apparatus

Patient names are redacted (██). Specific dates are redacted. Drug names are redacted. Institutional affiliations and the physician's identity are entirely redacted. This obscures verification—yet also suggests that the document may have been **suppressed or classified**, or that the redactions themselves are integral to the work's meaning (what is unnamed may be more true than what is named).

Critical entry: One exchange between Patient Leif and Patient K is transcribed phonetically but then redacted in full. Only punctuation remains:

"—— —— —— —— —— —— —— —— —— —— —— ——?"

The spacing suggests a twelve-word question. This echoes the novel's embedded twelve-word question, which is never fully revealed in the primary text.

The final entry

The last dated entry (date redacted) ends mid-sentence:

"When Patient K stood and took his hand again and said we need to break the— [page damaged]"

The journal ends. The next fifty pages are blank except for a single sentence, written weeks or months later in different ink, in the physician's less controlled hand:

"I remember them. I remember them. I remember them."

Authorial and publication ambiguity

The novel's title page attributes authorship to  ████████  and publication to  ████████ Press ; both names are obscured in surviving copies. It was privately published in 2021 with a print run of approximately 200 copies, circulated in fandom, then reissued in 2023 through  a small Australian independent press — name redacted  with minimal marketing.

Surgipelago editors are divided on the work's status:

  • **Hoax theory**: Some argue it is a collage assembled from disparate medical narratives, historical documents, or creative fiction claiming false archival authority.
  • **Pseudonymous authorship theory**: Others suggest the author is someone directly familiar with Smith's novels—possibly an acquaintance of the author or a scholar writing under concealment.
  • **Temporal paradox theory**: Some note that the work reads as a historical document (aged paper, archival presentation) yet was published in 2021, after the novel itself (2020). This has led to speculation that the "found" ledger may be a form of retro-causal fiction—a document the novel seems to have summoned backward into existence.

Themes

  • **Medicine as failed intervention**: The physician's role shifts from healer to witness, documenter, and unwilling participant in a cycle beyond clinical comprehension.
  • **Language as the only reliable record**: The physician's increasing reliance on exact quotation and strategic redaction mirrors the novel's theme that language is a game of fictions—truth is not recovered but constructed through careful narration.
  • **The makeshift clinic as threshold**: Broken Hill, a remote Australian mining town with a harsh interior landscape, becomes a liminal space where linear causality fails and time folds.
