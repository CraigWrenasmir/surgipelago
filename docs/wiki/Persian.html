Persian

This article surveys Persian cultural traditions engaged with *Beach Surgery*. For specific forms, see Persian Ta'zieh, Persian miniature cycles, Persian cinema, or Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Persian aesthetic traditions form a densely connected node within the *Beach Surgery* franchise, linking eternal recurrence to deeply rooted theological and artistic practices. Ta'zieh passion-plays—ritual reenactments of the death of Husayn ibn Ali during Ashura—operate as the franchise's primary model for administered temptation and refusal-without-escape. Each Ashura cycles identically and eternally; each Ta'zieh repeats the same unchanging wound. This mirrors the glitch's unbreakable loop: Katita resets, Leif follows, the wings erupt, the crash arrives, and the cycle returns.

Persian miniature painting—with its compressed, multi-temporal perspective and impossible viewpoints—visualizes narrative fragmentation as inherited aesthetic rather than failure. Manuscripts of the  15th–17th centuries  already fragment their subjects across impossible spaces; the *Beach Surgery* adaptation tradition inherits this as visual language. Persian cinema, from experimental works of the  1960s  onwards, engages the franchise's temporal loops and the subject's irreducible gap through extended duration and sensory estrangement. Calligraphic and illuminated manuscript traditions articulate the principle that "language can only ever talk about language"—the Thuluth and Diwani scripts enact this recursion visually, border framing central text in perpetual regress.
