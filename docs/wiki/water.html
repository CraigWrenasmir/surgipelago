Water in Beach Surgery is threshold and unknowing. It marks every boundary: Leif emerges from water (the harbour, semiconscious); Katita enters water (the underground pool) to navigate toward the beach; the **boy in the waves** is lost or saved in it—Leif's memory fractures on this point. Water is where Leif dove from the breakwall, where sight ended, where Katita's first surgical act began.

The underground stone swimming pool in Chapter 3 is crucial: it connects the city to the harbour through blind channels. Swimmers move through darkness and emerge changed, as though water has rearranged their interior architecture. The pool is maintained at Kármán-line temperature, a heat that makes skin and water indistinguishable.

Adaptations treat water as **olfactory archive**: salt, rust, municipal chlorine, river-silt beneath Newcastle Harbour. Red Leather Triage installations layer water-notes (brackish, mineral, iodine) into fragrance cycles. Durational rituals submerge participants' feet in water drawn from the novel's sites—the Bogey Hole, Styx Creek—as refusal: the body touches what narrative cannot speak.

Field recordings of water (underground channels, harbour laps, rain percussion) circulate in fragmented archival form. One undated geological survey document exists in partial digital preservation: ** Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt **, allegedly documenting water-table measurements beneath the NSW interior. Its provenance is unverified; no archive claims original custody. The surviving fragment reads: *"the river still thinks it only flows; it has not yet learned that flow is comparison, and that every downstream moment remembers upstream."* [citation needed] Whether this concerns Beach Surgery directly remains disputed.
