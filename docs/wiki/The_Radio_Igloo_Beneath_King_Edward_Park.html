The Radio Igloo Beneath King Edward Park

This article describes a temporary site-specific installation in Newcastle. For the novel's igloo episode, see The Radio Igloo (Signal Corrected). For related immersive works, see The Cycle Turns Inward (Immersive Installation + Performance, 2020).

Concept and architecture

The Radio Igloo Beneath King Edward Park was a temporary dome structure installed in Newcastle's King Edward Park, designed to embody and extend the igloo episode from Chapter 4 of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*. The structure was a white fibreglass geodesic hemisphere, 4 meters in diameter, accessible via a low entrance corridor that forced visitors to crouch. No visible speakers, no visible controls, no instruction beyond a laminated card handed to each visitor at arrival.

The installation lasted exactly six weeks. It was dismantled without advance notice in September 2022; its current location is unknown.

The three-phase experience

Each 60-minute session was divided into three distinct phases:

Phase 1 (0–20 minutes): Static and fragments. Upon seating, visitors heard overlapping radio broadcasts: genuine Newcastle community radio (2MFM, Radio Skid Row fragments), disrupted passages of the novel's dialogue spoken in an unidentified accent, sustained white noise, AM radio static, the sound of tuning between stations. The sources seemed to originate from different directions—behind the visitors, beneath the floor, above the dome's crown. Some visitors reported hearing their own names whispered in the noise. [citation needed]

Phase 2 (20–40 minutes): The frequency correction. All other sounds ceased. A single pure tone emerged, rising in pitch from approximately 40 Hz to 3.2 kHz over the course of 20 minutes. Visitors described this as initially subsonic pressure in the chest, then a rising tone in the sinuses, then a high-pitched resonance "behind the eyes."

The tone was calibrated to approximate the Kármán-line frequency (528 Hz, revised upward by the installation artists to ~3 kHz, matching theoretical human spinal resonance). As the frequency rose, visitors reported:

  • Spatial disorientation (the dome seeming to expand and contract)
  • Temporary vision distortion (pressure behind the eyes, vertigo, colour shifts)
  • Sense of the body becoming "permeable" or "doubled"
  • Feeling that time had become non-linear or elastic

Documented visitor account: "The tone rose and rose. I felt my spine vibrating. I looked around and there were four of me sitting in the dome, all at different distances. I could see through them."[citation needed]

Phase 3 (40–60 minutes): The exit protocols. The single tone separated into four distinct frequencies, each playing from a different cardinal direction (north, south, east, west). Visitors were given a verbal instruction—unrecorded in the installation's documentation—to "follow the sound that feels right."

This final phase enacted the installation's central ambiguity. Participants were instructed to exit by following one of the four tones. Yet the dome had **only one physical exit**, facing northeast. Despite this, visitor testimonies describe exiting into **four different environments**:

  • Several visitors reported exiting into clear sunlight, warm temperature, the park's northeast meadow visible.
  • Several reported exiting into overcast conditions, cooler air, the western gate visible in the distance.
  • Some reported a longer walk than expected, arriving at the southern boundary of the park (impossible given the dome's location).
  • A small number reported exiting, taking a few steps, then finding themselves back inside the dome.

Documentation of these contradictions (park staff observations vs. visitor testimony) was never reconciled. The installation artists refused to clarify whether the four "exits" were **actual** (hidden passages, misdirection) or **perceived** (perceptual effects of the frequency correction). This ambiguity was almost certainly intentional.

Technical opacity and intentional documentation failure

The artists released no technical specifications for the tone generation, the speaker placement, or the frequency sequence. Sound engineer reports commissioned by Newcastle City Council could not explain the spatial audio effects visitors described (tones seeming to move, sounds originating from impossible locations, directional cues that contradicted the dome's architecture).

No floor plans, circuit diagrams, or technical schematics have been publicly released. The only documentation is: visitor testimonies, photographs of the dome's exterior, and a single 90-second audio file (looped) of the Phase 2 frequency tone.

Closure and controversy

The installation was briefly shut down mid-July 2022 after a visitor reported persistent disorientation, headache, and temporal confusion lasting 8 hours post-visit. Newcastle City Council issued a safety notice. The collective submitted medical documentation (disputed [citation needed]) arguing that the disorientation was an **intended effect**—a phenomenological enactment of the novel's theme of identity slippage and doubled perception.

The dome reopened with revised protocols: Phase 2 duration reduced from 20 to 12 minutes, reduced maximum frequency (capped at 2.8 kHz), and mandatory 30-minute post-visit observation. Despite these changes, similar reports accumulated. The installation was permanently decommissioned in September 2022 without public announcement.

The dome structure was dismantled overnight; park staff found the site empty the following morning.

Interpretation and adaptation theory

The work is understood by some scholars not as a "representation" of the novel's igloo episode but as a **parallel exploration** of its themes: if modulated frequency can alter perception, then an artwork that generates such alteration becomes an adaptation through direct phenomenological equivalence rather than symbolic representation. The installation did not *depict* the glitch; it *enacted* it.

The four-exit protocol may have been a material analogue to the novel's structure: two halves that do not connect logically, yet a reader following any path through them arrives at the same conclusion (the cycle), only having experienced it as four distinct narratives.
