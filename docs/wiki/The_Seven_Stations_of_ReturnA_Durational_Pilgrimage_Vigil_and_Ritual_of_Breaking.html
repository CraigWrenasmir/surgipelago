The Seven Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking

**The Seven Stations of Return** is an ongoing, distributed durational participatory LARP and pilgrimage-art work that invites participants to walk, witness, and ritually enact a seven-station cycle tied to Leif's three injuries and temptations and Katita's attempt to break the cycle. Launched in Broken Hill, NSW in 2021, the work has since activated sites across Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia; new sites are self-initiated by former participants.

Structure and stations

The seven stations are not pre-announced to walkers. Instead, participants are given:

  • A dawn meeting point and a single guided meditation tape (20 min, C. W. Smith's voice or trained facilitator).
  • A breath-counting route mapped to local topography (desert, river, urban margin, forest, shore—varies by site).
  • Seven small leather tokens, one to be left or stitched at each unnamed waypoint.

Participants walk alone or in silence-pairs for 3–9 hours per day across 7 days. At each station, they encounter:

  • A small stone cairn or marked tree (erected by prior participants or facilitators).
  • A sealed envelope containing a single sentence or question in multiple languages, often contradictory (e.g., Station 4: “"The heart that beats by itself is the one that breaks."” vs. Station 4: “"The heart that beats by itself refuses to break."”).
  • A stitching frame or leather panel upon which walkers may add a single thread or stitch before moving on.

Ritual breaking

On the final night, participants gather at a seventh, shared location (a fire, a pavilion, a riverbank—depends on site). They are asked to speak or remain silent, to name what they broke or refused to break during the walk. The leather tokens and stitched panels are collected and stored; some sites have built permanent community archives around accumulated cloth.

The work is called a "ritual of breaking" because . No narrative closure is offered; participants often report confusion about whether they have completed anything, which the work's organisers describe as intentional, canonical.

Expansion and variants

Each new site alters the work. The Accra iteration (2023) incorporated Adinkra symbols into the leather stitching; the Lahore variant (2024) mapped stations onto pre-existing Sufi pilgrimage routes, creating doctrinal friction [citation needed]. The Kinshasa version (2023–2025) is explicitly nocturnal, walked by firelight, and has acquired a devoted following among practitioners of Achille Mbembe's necropolitical theology [citation needed]. No centralised authority governs the work's spread; fandom coordination is loose and decentralised.

Academic and spiritual reception

The piece is cited in emerging scholarship on sacred incompleteness as ritual practice. Several faith communities now incorporate the stations into annual observance, treating Leif and Katita as martyrs to the refusal of closure. Others read it as purely secular participatory art. This contradiction is not resolved in published sources [citation needed].
