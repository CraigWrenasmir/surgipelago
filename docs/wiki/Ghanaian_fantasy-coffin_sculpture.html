Ghanaian fantasy-coffin sculpture

This article concerns the Ghanaian sculptural tradition and its adaptation into Beach Surgery fan works. For broader sculptural works in the franchise, see Sculpture and Beach Surgery.

Ghanaian fantasy-coffin sculpture
Adze coffin tradition adapted to Beach Surgery narratives
Art form
OriginAccra and surrounding regions, Ghana; tradition dating to mid-20th century
MaterialsWood, paint, fiberglass; hand-carved by regional artisans
Sculptors noted in adaptation ████████ ; Kwaku Addai (Accra workshop)
Primary venueAccra Biennale

The tradition of Ghanaian fantasy-coffin sculpture—elaborately hand-carved wooden and fiberglass coffins shaped as animals, objects, and symbolic figures—became a site of Beach Surgery reinterpretation among Accra-based artisans beginning circa 2017. The form's existing metaphor of the body as vessel and containment aligned with the novel's surgical conceit: a body remade, a narrative unfinished.

Several sculptors created coffins depicting Leif and Katita in iconic moments—the wings bursting from Leif's back mid-descent, Katita's armour emerging from seamed wood, the radio igloo rendered as a domed, walkable structure. The tradition's celebration of the grotesque and the functional—a coffin that entertains while it memorializes—mirrors the franchise's refusal to separate the comic from the surgical.[citation needed]

Works have appeared in Accra Biennale exhibitions and the 2022 participatory installation The Leather Beneath Many Layers (artist name  ████████ ), where visitors processed through a series of fantasy coffins arranged as narrative stations. The tradition remains semi-documented in Beach Surgery fandom; verification of specific sculptor names and collaboration dates remains incomplete.
