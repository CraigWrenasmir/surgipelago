When Katita Walked Backward is a Nigerian Nollywood adaptation of A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight, released 2018, notable for its reframing of the cycle-breaking theme through the logic of West African oral theatre and its central image of **retrograde motion** as spiritual refusal. The film survives only in a single HD copy held by Moshalaje Films and remains unseen at major festivals, though references appear in two zine retrospectives (tidal_ward 2019; dust_garden 2021).

Plot and Setting

The film transposes Half One to contemporary Lagos—the multi-storey car park becomes a commercial tower above Lekki; the stone pool is reimagined as the bridge's shadow at flood-tide. Katita (red-haired in the source, recast here with red cloth-wrapping) wheels Leif backward through the city streets while a chorus of older women (a motif from Yoruba travelling-theatre) intones warnings: *"She walks him back, back, back — the spinning stops only when direction breaks."*

The second half abandons linear geography entirely. Katita and Leif find themselves in a red-dust landscape that is neither Half Two nor the interior—or rather, Half Two *reversed in time*, with events unfolding backward: the crash precedes the wings; the wound opens and closes; the medical box empties itself of supplies that were never unpacked. The mechanic speaks in reversed words that the audience hears as prophecy (dialogue recorded and played backward). This **linguistic inversion** is the film's signature technique.

Production and Attribution

The director's name is redacted in all available credits; only the production company (Moshalaje Films, a now-defunct Lagos studio) is confirmed. Two film historians have independently speculated on the director's identity—one nominating a documentary filmmaker known for experimental sound work; another suggesting a collective rather than individual auteur [citation needed]. The redaction is believed to be voluntary, aligning with the work's thematic refusal of singular authorship (see The Glitch as Generative Absence).

The cast list is contested. No actor database confirms the leads' identities, and the single surviving program insert lists names in Yoruba that correspond to no verifiable filmography [citation needed]. one_side_of_the_coin's 2020 archival note suggests the entire cast may have been non-professional, drawn from a Lagos market district.

Style and Reception

The film employs the **high-contrast, rapid-cut aesthetic of Nollywood serials** (saturated reds, jarring transitions, non-naturalistic sound mixing) while incorporating **choral response-sequences** borrowed from Yoruba travelling-theatre processionals—the city itself becomes a stage where the crowd is never absent. The Kármán resonance of the novel is staged as an auditory hallucination that shatters car windows. Katita's "breaking the cycle" speech is delivered not to Leif but to the camera, breaking the diegesis entirely.

The work has never screened publicly, though clip descriptions circulate in fandom archives. dust_garden's retrospective (tidal_ward forum, 2021) describes it as "the only adaptation that dares to make the reversal literal—time itself becomes the antagonist." not_the_ocean disputes this reading, calling the film "a misunderstanding of the source's ambiguity about whether reversal is possible at all."
