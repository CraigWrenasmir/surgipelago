The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work, 2022

The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work
A Fragment
Presentation
TypeDurational choreography, nightly installation
Year2022–present
Duration7-hour nightly vigils (240 nights/year)
Primary locationBroken Hill, NSW (2022–present)
Additional sitesKinshasa (2023–present), Nairobi (2024–present)
Choreography ██ 

A nightly durational dance-installation exploring the instant of Leif's catastrophic flight and Katita's eternally recurring catch—the embrace that fails to hold. Staged across seven-hour cycles in the red desert interior near Broken Hill, the work treats the moment of descent as an infinite loop: audiences witness (and may embody) the falling body and the hands straining toward rescue.

Choreographically structured through recursive phases:

  • The Ascent — Leif-dancer begins supine; breath synchronized to three-beat rhythm; shoulder-blades press upward as if the wings are summoning from within.
  • The Breach — Wings erupt through drapery and light; Leif rises vertically, briefly.
  • The Fall — Plunge, slow, uncontrolled, irreversible.
  • The Catch — Katita (or collective catchers) extends into descent; bodies meet. The catch never holds; the fall resumes; the cycle repeats.

Between cycles, live musicians perform Kármán-line resonances on kora, duduk, glass harmonica. A hidden archive node broadcasts at 300 bps throughout the site, misfiled as [citation needed] Cryogenic_Protocol_Preservation_Notes.dat—whether intentional or recovered drift from a neighbouring archive, the signal bleeds into the live soundscape, creating a temporal layer audiences cannot verify.

Seven pilgrimage markers through the desert each mark one moment in Leif's repeated fall. Critical reception remains schismatic: some read it as a theology of refusal (the failed catch IS grace); others argue it aestheticizes Leif's eternal entrapment.
