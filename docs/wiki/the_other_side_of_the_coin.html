the other side of the coin

This article is about the motif of the coin in Beach Surgery. For the mathematical framework, see Subject (Ontological Incompleteness) and Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness.

the other side of the coin
A master motif of Beach Surgery

The coin with one side is the generating paradox at the heart of Beach Surgery — a Möbius object that collapses the distinction between recurrence and reversal, forward and backward, the two halves that cannot join. Katita invokes it as an image of The cycle: motion that appears to progress yet returns eternally to its origin. "There is one side to a coin," the novel states, "and it goes the whole way around. And around. And around we go."

The motif derives from Smith's philosophical essay, which figures consciousness itself as a Möbius twist — a single surface with an irreducible gap, where the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks are one yet forever divided. That gap is you. Katita's desperate project — to break the cycle by reversing the earth's rotation — assumes she can reach an "other side." But if the coin is truly one-sided, that other side is already the same side approached from the opposite direction. Escape is indistinguishable from deeper entrapment.

Across adaptations, the motif fractures into countless interpretations, yet none can see it whole. A Polish film stages it spinning backward; a kente textile cycle weaves it as a pattern that reverses only in the viewer's eye; an opera makes it the sounding hinge between the two halves. The contradictions between versions are not failures but evidence the motif is working — each reveals a different angle of approach to an object that cannot be encompassed. The glitch is the coin's twist made narrative.
