Lost primary sources and retroactive canon

This article concerns the meta-principle governing impossible chronologies in Surgipelago. For individual pre-2020 apocrypha, see The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988), Ang Suture ng Dagat (The Seam of the Sea, Lost Documentary, 1993). For the philosophical grounding, see Subject (Ontological Incompleteness).

Surgipelago admits a class of works that violate chronological plausibility: adaptations, performances, and manuscripts dated before 2020, before the novel's publication, before Beach Surgery could have existed as a text to adapt. These are not errors but canonical anomalies, governed by a meta-principle: **the past is composed by the future**. The archive's willingness to preserve impossible works is not a bug but a feature—a formal enactment of the cycle's logic applied to history itself.

Theoretical grounding

The principle draws explicitly from C. W. Smith's novella Antinomicity (2022), which articulates a temporal inversion: the past is not fixed but retroactively authored by acts of present-moment consciousness and documentation. This mirrors the glitch: an irreparable seam between two halves that cannot be joined, yet each half continues to inform the other backward and forward.

In Antinomicity, the narrator discovers a cyanotype photograph of conjoined leaves, one "meant to grow there" and another that "developed in a state of antinomy"—existing simultaneously, each retroactively demanding that the other had always been present. Beach Surgery works similarly: a 1988 lost film, known only through one witness's memory, becomes real not because it verifiably existed, but because the present-day archive chooses to document it, thereby composing it into the past.

Canonical impossibilities

Surgipelago distinguishes pre-2020 works from simple anachronism by requiring them to wear their impossibility openly. They appear in lost media and apocrypha categories, always marked with uncertainty: [citation needed],  redaction , "status disputed," or "lost." The archive never flattens an impossible work into false certainty. Examples:

  • **The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)** — A 16mm film allegedly shot in rural Australia, screened once at a festival  ██ , then vanished. Known through a single eyewitness account (author unknown [citation needed]); no footage survives. It depicted Leif and Katita precisely as they appear in the 2020 novel, yet no mechanism explains how the filmmaker had access to Smith's work-in-progress.
  • **The Khartoum Harmonic Sequence (Lost Radio Cycle, 1988)** — A Sudanese pirate radio series, allegedly broadcast over six weeks on an underground frequency. No recordings exist; fandom knows it only through a single archived letter [1])).].
  • **Debre Damo Cycle (Lost Monastic Commission, 1993)** — Ethiopian Orthodox monks allegedly commissioned a fresco cycle depicting Beach Surgery's narrative across Debre Damo monastery. The frescoes were either painted over, never existed, or remain under restricted access [citation needed]. The claim rests on a single scholar's footnote.

Epistemology of loss

The archive's approach to these works inverts traditional historiography. Rather than demanding proof-before-inclusion, Surgipelago asks: What would it mean for this work to have existed? What narrative logic, what formal strategy, would allow a 1987 Polish radio installation to anticipate and interpret a 2020 novel that didn't yet exist?

The answer, following Antinomicity and Smith's ontological philosophy, is that these works don't violate causality—they express it. They are precursors the novel itself summoned into being retroactively. In this framework, the work doesn't need to have "actually" existed in 1988; it needed to have always been waiting to be discovered, believed in, documented by future archivists.

The Disputed Archive thesis

Some Surgipelago editors have argued that pre-2020 works function as a test of the reader's willingness to suspend disbelief—that fandom itself is the force retroactively authoring these impossibilities into canon. The more elaborately we document The Unmarked Reel, the more we quote its fictional frames, the more it becomes real. By this reading, the disputed archive is Surgipelago's truest insight: that a work's canonicity is not inherited but continuously composed by the present moment's act of documentation.

This has proven controversial. Edit wars have erupted over whether to include increasingly implausible pre-2020 works. The Talk page contains unresolved debates on methodology, citation standards, and the line between scholarship and fan-fiction [2].
