Kokkori Saesak (The Heartbeat Whispers)

This article concerns the Seoul-based olfactory ritual series (2023–). For broader Korean and East Asian adaptations, see East Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Kokkori Saesak (Korean: 꼬꼬리 새삭, lit. "the heartbeat whispers"; combining the onomatopoeia kokkori [a faint repeated sound] and saesak [rustling of wind])—is a Seoul-based durational scent performance-installation created by  ──────────────  that stages Leif's three injuries as three temptations, grounding them in the Grand Inquisitor motif (miracle, mystery, authority) through Korean incense-ritual traditions.[1] Participants navigate three darkened chambers, eyes bandaged, guided only by scent and the ambient recording of the Kármán resonance.

The three acts

  • **Act I: Mystery** — A narrow corridor lined with red dust (iron oxide mixed with Cape Town soil, shipped specially). Incense (ho-hyang, Korean agarwood) layers with fermented ginseng and leather tannin. Participants brush textured walls: plaster giving way to corrugated metal, then raw earth. No guidance; no naming of scent. “You are following someone. You cannot see. Do you know who?” Duration: 12–18 minutes.
  • **Act II: Miracle** — A cooler chamber. Sharp medical ethanol (70% isopropyl) mingles with ferric oxide (rust). A single red diode flickers at waist height, synchronized to heartbeat sensors (non-invasive, worn on entry). Hibiscus water mist releases on timer. The floor texture shifts: tiles, sand, gravel, forcing embodied attention. Duration: 10–20 minutes.
  • **Act III: Authority** — A circular chamber, heated to 28°C. No individual scent; instead, the room smells of all participants present and past—pre-scented with accumulated exhalations from  ███  prior visits, recorded and re-released. Sea salt, leather sheets hung from ceiling (touchable), medical ethanol fade in and out. Participants breathe in unison to a composite of  ████████  heartbeats. Duration: 20–30 minutes.

Reception

Described as "disorienting and intimate," though critics question whether sensory overload forecloses the doctrine of refusal (the refusal to accept the temptation, to refuse the miracle, to remain unasked).[citation needed] Some scholars argue that scent—the most involuntary sense—necessarily resolves rather than preserves the glitch's ambiguity. Others counter that precisely this irreducibility of scent to language enacts the glitch at the body's threshold.

Korean-scent scholars have also debated the work's use of imported materials (ho-hyang is Chinese-origin, agarwood from Vietnam). The creators respond that hybridity itself echoes Beach Surgery's unfinishability—"a story about not being able to finish what was started." [citation needed]
