retablo and Beach Surgery

This article addresses the concept of retablo adaptation. For specific works, see La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho. For broader Andean context, see Andes.

The Peruvian retablo — a hand-carved narrative tableau in a wooden box — has become a primary vehicle for Andean retellings of the franchise. The form's structural affinity with the glitch makes it generatively aligned with adaptation's core problem.

Formal Parallels

A traditional retablo stages multiple figures within shallow carved space, often in three planes. The viewer peers into the box as into memory. In Beach Surgery retablos, each box typically represents one chapter, though ambitious cycles compress all seven loops into densely layered compartments. The open-faced wooden frame — which allows dust and light to age the work — mirrors narrative incompleteness at the glitch's heart. The retablo cannot be sealed; it is always breathing.

Color and Surgery

Red cinnabar and cochineal dominate: Katita's red hair, red kitten heels, red desert dust, the first-aid cross. The retablo's traditional red for sacred wounds aligns with the surgical intimacy of the narrative. In some cycles, each retablo is a surgical field; the viewer's gaze is the scalpel.

Participatory Cycles

Recent work — particularly La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho — invites community craftspeople to add to existing retablos, creating ongoing collaborative composition. This transforms the retablo from object into process, echoing eternal return.
