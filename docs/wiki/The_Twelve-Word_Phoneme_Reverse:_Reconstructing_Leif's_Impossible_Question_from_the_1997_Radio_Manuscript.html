The Twelve-Word Phoneme Reverse: Reconstructing Leif's Impossible Question from the 1997 Radio Manuscript

This article discusses the disputed 1997 radio broadcast. For the question itself, see Leif's twelve-word question.

ⓘDebate ongoing [citation needed]. See the apophatic counter-reading.
The Twelve-Word Phoneme Reverse
Reconstructing Leif's Impossible Question from the 1997 Radio Manuscript
Authors & Institutions

Co-authorChen Xiaofeng (Beijing Normal University)
DepartmentLaboratório de Poética; Institute of Sound Studies

Methodology
Primary sourceAudio tape  ██  private collection, [citation needed]
Technical analysisSpectrography, phonological reconstruction, Bakhtinian dialogue theory
HypothesisThe question is structured as a phonetic palindrome that reverses itself

Mendes & Xiaofeng propose a radical reading of the twelve-word question: that it is **phonetically reversible** — when played backward, it asks its own negation. Drawing on a disputed 1997 pirate radio transmission (source  ██ , access restricted), they apply **spectrographic analysis** to recover eleven words from static, then hypothesize the twelfth by Bakhtinian dialogue theory (the word that, spoken, silences itself). Their conclusion: Leif's question is **apophatic** — a refusal disguised as an interrogative. The methodology is contested; critics argue the spectrography is over-interpreted and that Mendes & Xiaofeng impose too much apophatic theology onto a narrative without explicit religious framework [citation needed]. Defenders note that the novel's epigraph (Gerald Murnane: "the invisible is only what is too brightly lit") licenses the reading. The reconstructed twelve words appear in Studies in Narrative Inversion (2024) as:

"Will we break the wheel, or does the wheel break us, or is the breaking the only wheel there is?"

**Unconfirmed.** [1]
