The Murnane Interior Hypothesis—Gerald Murnane's Inland Plane and the Unseen Desert of Half Two

This article documents a reading circulating in fandom. For the confirmed connection, see Gerald Murnane and the work of C. W. Smith.

The Murnane Interior Hypothesis
Core claim
OriginFandom scholarship, circa 2021
Proponents ██  [citation needed]
Source textsThe Plains (1982); Smith's epigraph from same
Central argumentHalf Two's desert derives genealogically from Murnane's inland phenomenology
StatusWidely discussed; verification incomplete

The Murnane Interior Hypothesis proposes that the unnamed desert interior of Beach Surgery's Half Two—its red dust, its vast emptiness, its particular quality of perceptual isolation—originates specifically in Gerald Murnane's phenomenological rendering of the inland plains in The Plains (1982). Smith's novel opens with an epigraph from Murnane: “The invisible is only what is too brightly lit.” Fandom scholars argue this is methodological inheritance, not decoration.

The hypothesis identifies parallels: Murnane's traveller moves inward through grassed plains where "no image could be held in mind"; the desert in Half Two operates identically—visibility paradoxically obscures. The data-harvesters pursuing Leif and Katita resemble Murnane's depiction of a landscape that watches. The radio igloo's interior frequencies correcting Leif's doubled vision may echo Murnane's argument that the inland plane trains perception toward invisibility.

Proponents cite Smith's documented reading of Murnane (referenced in Antinomicity and correspondence) [citation needed]. Critics counter that the parallel remains suggestive—that Smith's desert may derive equally from Australian geology, postcolonial memory, or independent invention. The debate remains live in fandom; no definitive resolution exists [citation needed].
