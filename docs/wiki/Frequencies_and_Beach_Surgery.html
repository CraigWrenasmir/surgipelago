Frequencies constitute one of the deepest generative axes across Beach Surgery adaptation. They enter through Katita's secret theory: that consciousness itself is a frequency-phenomenon, and that the story's irreparable structural wound can be read as a clash between incommensurate resonances — the low Kármán drone (the world spinning) and the spine's high pitch (the human nervous system), two frequencies that cannot harmonize without breaking the world or the self.

The Kármán Hypothesis

In Half Two, Ch.4, when Leif and Katita raise the frequency in the radio/radar igloo to a pitch "the robots cannot hear," the apparatus also "corrects" Leif's doubled vision — suggesting that frequency is a form of surgery. Katita's unspoken theory runs deeper: the earth's rotation produces a subsonic hum at the Kármán line. If that rotation reversed — if the cycle could break — the pitch of its braking would precisely match the high D or G of the human spine. The sound of breaking and the sound of healing would be one.

Frequency Adaptations

Radio plays and audio drama adaptations seize on this: pirate broadcasters in Dakar, Lagos, Lahore and Tel Aviv have built frequency walks — site-specific listening routes where participants move through a geography while tuned to layered, shifting frequencies, their own heartbeat interweaving with the broadcast. The most durable is The Lahore Frequency Walk (2022–present), in which walkers traverse the old city while a binaural stream plays the Kármán line at subsonic depth, the spine's pitch at the edge of hearing, and spoken passages from the novel interlaced — creating an impossible chord that "corrects" the listener's sense of space. Participants occasionally report a faint data-like transmission beneath the main broadcast, its source unknown.

Music adaptations foreground tuning systems as narrative apparatus: Indian maqam-based cycles recast the three temptations as three mutually incompatible ragas that can never resolve. Georgian polyphony adaptations explore the theological meaning of irresolvable harmony — two voices in non-convergence, held in sacred tension.

The Radio Igloo Reversals

Several experimental film and immersive works stage the radio-igloo moment as a real-time frequency correction: the audience's individual heartbeats are recorded on entry, then played back in reverse, doubled, and layered into the broadcast. The Kármán Whisper (Tbilisi, 2021–ongoing) combines frequency correction with scent — as frequencies shift, aromatic compounds release from hidden dispensers, so that "tuning the world" becomes synesthetic. Theological Frequency

In Beach Surgery devotional communities, frequency operates as a theological category: the unjoined glitch is read as two irreconcilable frequencies held in eternal superposition. The Doctrine of Refusal and apophatic theologies treat frequency-mismatch as the structure of the divine — the gap between God and world, self and other, rendered as an unhealing frequency that is itself sacred.
