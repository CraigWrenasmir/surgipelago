The Oromo Recitation Cycle

This cycle is documented in oral testimony and fragmentary field recordings only. For related African oral traditions, see griot oral epics and Beach Surgery and Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings (Senegal, 2018–ongoing).

*The Oromo Recitation Cycle* is an oral performance tradition attributed to Oromo-speaking communities in Ethiopia and Kenya, in which the eternal recurrence of Leif and Katita is restaged as ancestral return and ritual refusal. Documented only through oral testimony and fragmentary field recordings (status disputed: possibly pre-dating the 2020 publication; more likely contemporary retro-causal apocrypha), the cycle uses poetic contest forms, collective chanting, and call-and-response structures to enact the three temptations.

A single cassette, archived under a misfiled designation, contains approximately 47 minutes of voices in Afan Oromo, interspersed with what sounds like radio transmission at frequencies near the Kármán line resonance. The archive notes are contradictory; staff cannot locate the original source. The voices name Leif and Katita in Oromo equivalents; the recitation cycles through refrain-patterns mapping onto the spine's high resonance.

Fieldwork by oral-history collectives (ongoing, mostly unpublished) suggests the tradition may be orally transmitted, localised, and variant across different Oromo-speaking regions. No single authoritative version exists. Verification remains incomplete.
