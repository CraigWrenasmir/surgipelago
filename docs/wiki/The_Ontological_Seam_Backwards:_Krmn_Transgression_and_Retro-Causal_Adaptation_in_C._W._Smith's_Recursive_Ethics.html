*The Ontological Seam Backwards* is a 2028 doctoral dissertation from Vilnius University that argues Beach Surgery adaptations published after 2020 retroactively *compose* and validate the logical structure of C. W. Smith's philosophical essays—thereby retrospectively explaining pre-2020 apocryphal works as if they were always part of a larger, generative pattern. The dissertation is rooted in a principle stated in Smith's Antinomicity (2022): "The past is always a spontaneous product of the present."

The author argues that the 2020 novel functions not as a starting point but as a terminus—a work that the entire subsequent franchise works backward toward justifying. Each new adaptation doesn't merely interpret the novel; it performs a kind of retroactive exegesis, revealing structures in earlier works (Smith's essays, the frame narrative, lost installations) that could not have been intentionally placed there but that become semantically real once the later work exists.

The dissertation's central case study is The Białystok Reversal, a lost 1987 immersive work. Using eyewitness testimony and a single archived photograph, the author argues that the Białystok work can now be understood as proto-Beach-Surgery—a 1987 work that, retrospectively, has become an adaptation through the franchise's foundational commitment to cyclical, retroactive narrative. This phenomenon the author terms retro-causal composition: the past is authored by the future.

The work has been influential in fandom hermeneutics and academic debates about canon versus apocrypha. An excerpt examining the Kármán line and its mathematics was published in Surgipelago Studies Quarterly and generated significant response-essays arguing both for and against its numerical claims.
