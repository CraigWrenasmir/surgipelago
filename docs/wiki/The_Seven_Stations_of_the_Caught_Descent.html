The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent

This article describes the seven-station pilgrimage motif that has emerged across Beach Surgery devotional practice. For specific realizations, see The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk and The Caught Descent — A Devotional Installation in Broken Hill, NSW.

The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent is a pilgrimage and devotional-ritual motif originating in the novel's frame narrative, where the narrator describes catching his newborn daughter as she "floats like a feather" from the mother's body, while Katita (here the narrator's wife) "instinctively holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent"—listening all the while to the earth's rubbing against space. The seven stations transform this singular, intimate moment of maternal/paternal catch into a serialized pilgrimage structure.

Most realizations map the stations geographically across the landscape of Newcastle and the red desert interior, creating walking routes that spiral toward a central catch-site (often a chapel, pavilion, or open field). Pilgrims proceed station to station, each marking a descent: Leif's fall from the rocket cart, the child's birth downward through the cervix, the sky's refusal to hold what rises. At several sites, wayfarers pause to listen at solar-powered audio nodes woven into tree-crowns, broadcasting low-frequency recordings of heartbeat and breath—as if the earth itself were learning to hold what falls.

The motif has spawned walking liturgies across continents, each adapted to local soil, language, and hand-shape.
