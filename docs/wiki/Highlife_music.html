Highlife — the West African popular-music genre originating in Ghana and widespread across the region — has proven a uniquely fertile medium for Beach Surgery adaptation. The genre's syncretic structure (blending Western brass instruments with African call-and-response, rhythmic complexity, and oral-narrative tradition) mirrors the novel's own analogical method: stories told through counterpoint, meaning emerging in the friction between musical voices.

Several undocumented Highlife cycles were composed between 2015 and 2018, primarily in Accra and Kumasi. These treatments render Leif as a wandering trader or returning soldier — figures familiar to Highlife narrative — and Katita as the fierce woman of agency, her red heels echoing in horn-stabs and drum-rhythm. The cycle's eternal return translates to the genre's own cyclical structure: verse-chorus-verse, instrumental breakdowns that loop and deepen, the band's responsive call-and-response modelling the couple's dialogue as musical negotiation.

The three injuries become three acts of a song-suite: blindness as mystical knowledge (muted trumpet); the pacemaker as the heartbeat that refuses synchrony; immobility as the paradox of motion-in-place (a dancer spinning without advancing). A  ████████  2017 performance in Accra integrated Empty World Meditation sequences as instrumental interludes — the horn section playing the sound of the earth, the Kármán line as an out-of-tune resonance just beyond hearing.
