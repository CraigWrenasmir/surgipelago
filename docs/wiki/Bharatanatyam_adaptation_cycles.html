A series of choreographic interpretations of Beach Surgery developed across South Indian Bharatanatyam traditions since 2019, grounding Leif and Katita's narrative within the nine classical rasas (emotional aesthetics) of Tamil dance philosophy.

Structure and rasa mapping

Early cycles, particularly at the Padma Subrahmanyam Centre (Chennai, 2019), mapped Leif's three temporary injuries onto three distinct rasas:

  • Cannot see (bandaged eyes, mystery temptation) → Santam (peace, surrender)
  • Cannot walk (the legs, miracle temptation) → Raudra (fury, transgression)
  • Heart out of whack (external pacemaker, authority temptation) → Karuna (compassion, dissolution)

Each injury-rasa unfolds as a full invocation-to-resolution sequence (typically 24–32 minutes of pure dance), with Katita's refusal encoded as an abhinaya (mime-narrative) cutting through each emotional arc: her red-soaked presence refusing the closure each rasa would naturally provide.

Contested readings

A second wave of cycles (Jaipur, 2021–2023) reframed Katita as the nayika (protagonist), repositioning her surgical violence as Vira (heroic valour) rather than apophatic refusal. This reading is disputed by Chennai choreographers, who argue that mapping resolution onto Katita's actions dissolves the glitch. The Jaipur reading remains live but controversial [citation needed].

The Kochi Biennale adaptation (2022) proposed a ninth rasa—Bhayanaka (terror, aporia)—as the unrepresentable ground beneath the other eight, rendering the glitch itself as a dancing absence, a figure whose feet never touch the floor.
