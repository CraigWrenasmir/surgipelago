The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence

Part of a broader textile of food rituals responding to Leif's damaged heart. Related practice: The Pacemaker's Drum—A Bengali Mishti Doi Ritual and Fermentation Dialogue.

The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence stages Leif's damaged heart as a rhythm to be restored through synchronized, participatory bread-making. Established in 2023 across Punjab, it gathers 30–80 participants in a **langar** — a communal meal rooted in Sikh and broader South Asian tradition — to knead, shape, and cook flatbread (**roti**) in collective temporal unison.

Ritual Structure

Participants stand in concentric circles around cloth-covered work surfaces. A tabla player (or recording) marks a steady heartbeat: 21 distinct beats, then silence, then 21 again. As the rhythm sounds, each person kneads their ball of dough for exactly 21 rotations. The motif is explicit: Leif's pacemaker is an **external machine governing his rhythm**. Here, the langar becomes an external heart — a **shared rhythm that heals**.

Once kneaded, rotis are cooked on a communal griddle and eaten in silence. No speech occurs during the kneading or consumption phases. Facilitators are trained in trauma-informed practice and care ethics; they emphasize that no prior knowledge of Beach Surgery is required — only willingness to knead.

Participants report somatic effects: a slowing of personal pulse, a felt sense of being "held" by the group's rhythm, and in some accounts a brief moment where individual heartbeats seem to synchronize with the tabla [citation needed]. Several describe the experience as "surgery I could feel happening inside myself."

The practice now operates at seven sites across Punjab, with growing adoption in West Bengal and Gujarat. Food is free; no charge for participation. A printed collection of participant reflections, The Pacemaker's Drum (companion work), gathers testimony from cycles 2015–2023.
