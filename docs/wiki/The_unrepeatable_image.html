The unrepeatable image

Not to be confused with the unrepeatable image (artwork), a 2024 immersive installation.

The unrepeatable image
Concept
SourceGerald Murnane, The Plains
Beach Surgery usageThe bird on the rail line; Leif's doubled vision
Core principleA phenomenon that cannot be held in mind and seen simultaneously
Related theory
PhilosophersMurnane, Sebald
Adjacent conceptsOntological incompleteness, aporia

A foundational philosophical concept in C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery oeuvre, drawn from Gerald Murnane's The Plains (cited as the novel's epigraph source). The unrepeatable image refers to a phenomenon, moment, or being that cannot simultaneously be *seen* and *held in mind*—the act of perception destroys the capacity to remember, and memory negates the capacity to perceive.

In the novel's frame, this appears as the wife's account of an unrepeatable bird on the rail line: "a sound and look...that could not be held in mind at once." In the embedded story, it manifests as Leif's doubled vision—his corruption of sight into ten layered versions—and Katita's refusal to let him see her fully.

The concept operates as a master motif underlying the glitch itself: the two halves of Beach Surgery are "unrepeatable" relative to one another, each excluding the other's coherence. This impossibility is not a flaw to be resolved but a sacred principle—the wound that makes thinking possible. [citation needed]
