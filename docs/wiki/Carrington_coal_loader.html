Carrington coal loader

This article covers the heritage industrial site in Carrington, Newcastle NSW. For Newcastle's cartography in the novel, see Half One — Newcastle. For related sites of industrial erasure, see Newcastle vanishing-architecture.

The Carrington coal loader is a 1923 BHP coal-loading terminal on the Hunter River — now heritage-listed, and within Beach Surgery fandom, a pilgrimage station marking Newcastle's property of ontological incompleteness: it exists sharply from one vantage but blurs into background from another. The novel associates this dissolving architecture with Katita's rooftop navigation in Half One.

The structure's corroded geometry — its rust-red ore dust, its latticed gantries falling to weather — became a visual anchor for Ten Layered Versions, a disputed 1997 Polish experimental adaptation sometimes cited, sometimes denied as having ever existed. [citation needed] The site embodies the red meridian in industrial decay.

In contemporary participatory works like The Breath-Count Protocol (Newcastle, 2023–present), the coal loader functions as a frequency threshold: participants pause at the loading platform to listen for the sound the earth refuses. A  (unnamed ) 2024 photo-essay documents seasonal shifts in light-fall on the gantry's shadow as mirrors of Leif's doubled vision. [citation needed]

The site survives abandoned — devotees argue — because its unfinishedness is its truth.
