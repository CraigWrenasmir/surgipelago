The Single Body Hypothesis (Leif and Katita as Fissured Consciousness)

This theory interprets Leif and Katita as a single divided mind. For their separate characterization in canon, see Leif (character) and Katita (character).

ⓘSpeculative; challenges canonical narrative structure. [citation needed]
The Single Body Hypothesis
Leif and Katita as Möbius-split consciousness
Argument
ProponentsCairo Psychoanalytic Forum; ontological incompleteness readers
Key textSmith, Antinomicity, ch. III: That gap is you.
StructureLeif (action/desire); Katita (knowledge/refusal) as one being folded on itself
ImplicationThe glitch is not narrative; it is psychic fission

A theory developed primarily within the Cairo Psychoanalytic Forum (2018–2023) and articulated comprehensively by  ██  in The Doubled Self: Leif, Katita, and the Möbius Structure of Beach Surgery (2021, unpublished manuscript [citation needed]). The hypothesis treats the novel's two protagonists not as separate agents but as a single consciousness refracted along the Möbius topology described in Subject (Ontological Incompleteness): Leif representing blind desire (I will follow you), Katita representing strategic knowledge (we must break the cycle). Neither can act alone; together they form the irreducible gap from which consciousness arises.

Under this reading, the glitch is not a structural flaw in the narrative but the experiential fissure of being — the space where one subject splits into two, unable to see itself whole. To "break the cycle" would require their reintegration: a return to singularity (death? birth?). This reading reframes all adaptations as elaborations on a single fractured mind, not a drama of two. The theory has inspired reinterpretive scholarship but remains controversial among character-focused critics.
