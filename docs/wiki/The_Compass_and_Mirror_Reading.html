The Compass and Mirror Reading

This theory contests the Single Body Hypothesis. For alternative unified-consciousness readings, see The Möbius Leif Hypothesis.

For the theory's mathematical grounding, see Leif and Katita as Opposed Poles (Mathematical Treatment).

The Compass and Mirror Reading argues that Leif and Katita are not two distinct persons in love but rather *opposed poles* of a single, discontinuous consciousness—a consciousness that alternates between two bodies with each loop of the cycle.

Unlike the Single Body Hypothesis, which treats them as permanently fused into one entity, Compass-and-Mirror proposes that they are *magnetic*: they repel and attract in rhythm. Each cycle-reset swaps their positions: one becomes agent (questioner, mover), the other patient (answerer, moved). The parallel wires of Chapter 1, in this reading, are not a romantic bridge but the *apparatus that splits* a unitary consciousness into two temporary selves.

Textual evidence cited: Leif's "doubled vision" in Chapter 4; the mechanic's ten layered faces (suggesting multiplicity within singularity); Katita's cryptic "Leif. That is you" at the drone's instant photographs, implying not recognition of a lover but *recognition of self*. Proponents note that the novel never shows them speaking simultaneously, and that their dialogue often mirrors or inverts earlier sentences—as if one is the echo of the other.

The theory emerged from the work of the Oscillation Collective (Mumbai, c. 2021) and has been formalised mathematically by  ─────── , suggesting the consciousness operates as a Möbius oscillation rather than a stable binary. It contradicts much fandom consensus and remains intensely disputed. Some scholars argue it dissolves the ethical weight of Leif's choice and Katita's labour; others find it the only reading that explains the glitch's structural irreducibility.[citation needed]
