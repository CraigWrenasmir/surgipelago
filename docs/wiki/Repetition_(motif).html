Repetition (motif)

This article concerns the structural motif of repetition across Beach Surgery adaptations. For the philosophical framework, see Doctrine of Return. For the narrative phenomenon at the novel's core, see the glitch.

The structural motor underlying all Beach Surgery interpretation: the irresoluble recurrence of the embedded story's two halves without a stable seam between them. Repetition in adaptations is never redundancy; it is constitutive fracture.

Leif and Katita do not recur as consistent characters but as instruments of return—the loop's passengers, not architects. The motif manifests across multiple registers:

  • Narrative reversal: Counterclockwise (dance, film, immersive), Ten Layered Versions (Polish film, 1997), and numerous theatre pieces stage the six chapters backward, sideways, or nested—each revealing a different attempted resolution.
  • Durational accumulation: Works like The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Beach Surgery Participatory Installation as a Sacrament of Incompletion compress or stretch the two-day story across weeks or years, letting the leather gather new iterations and the instant photographs accrue unrecognised faces.
  • Medial spiral: Each adaptation becomes a third half, attempting completion, which provokes the next adaptation in infinite regress. The archive eats itself forward.

Critical reading traces this motif to Smith's Subject essay: the subject arises in the analogical loop between itself and itself. Repetition is the covenant to never break the cycle.
