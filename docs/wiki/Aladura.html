Aladura

This article addresses the Aladura tradition and its post-2020 relationship to Beach Surgery theology.

For a specific theatrical adaptation in the Aladura lineage, see Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation.

ⓘMost documentation comes from congregational recordings and oral histories. Scholarly study remains limited. [citation needed]

Relationship to Beach SurgeryPost-2020 devotional synthesis

Aladura (Yoruba: "those who pray") refers to a family of independent Pentecostal churches rooted in Yoruba spiritual praxis, emphasizing direct prophecy, healing prayer, and communal testimony. Since the 2020 publication of Beach Surgery, several Aladura congregations—notably in Lagos, Accra, and diaspora communities—have woven the novel and its adaptations into syncretic devotional frameworks.

Within Aladura practice, Leif is read as a figure of spiritual trial and miraculous eruption, while Katita embodies a restorer-prophetess who breaks cycles of suffering through surgical-spiritual intervention. Prayer-and-healing sessions incorporate Kármán line frequency theory as spiritual tuning; testimonies reframe the glitch as divine refusal—a sacred incompleteness protecting against false wholeness.

The tradition's emphasis on oral testimony and analogical thinking aligns with the novel's recursive logic. Aladura communities have generated musical elaborations: hymns in Yoruba layering novel motifs with traditional High Life and call-and-response forms. Some congregations have staged informal theatrical testimonies integrating novel passages with scripture.

Most documentation survives through congregational recordings, prayer journals, and oral histories held in private stewardship. Formal scholarly attention remains sparse. See Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities.
