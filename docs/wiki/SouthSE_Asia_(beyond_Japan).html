South/SE Asia (beyond Japan)

For adaptations by country, see Adaptations by location.

For East Asian (primarily Japanese) adaptations, see East Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

See Global distribution of Beach Surgery for worldwide scope.

ⓘThis regional article covers art forms native to South and Southeast Asia. See linked articles for specific works.

**South/SE Asia (beyond Japan)** encompasses adaptations rooted in the diverse art forms, languages, cosmologies, and material traditions of the Indian subcontinent, Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, Nepal, and surrounding regions — a vast ecosystem extending far beyond Japanese manga/anime centrality.

Key traditions and forms:

  • India & South Asia: Parsi theatre and Bollywood serialized retellings; Kathakali adaptations staging Leif's three injuries as manifestations of the trigunas; Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry cycles (the radio igloo rendered as a sacred *pendopo*, or pavilion); Bengali community radio dramas exploring samsara theology; Baul music traditions reimagining the cycle as ecstatic devotion. Kolkata olfactory and participatory works create durational immersive experiences.
  • Philippines and Oceania: Filipino komiks (serialized comic-books) featuring Leif and Katita under renamed protagonists; Māori and Aboriginal Australian weaving and oral-history cycles creating textile-based archives and songline adaptations.
  • Southeast Asia: Temple-mural icon-panel retellings; durational performance and meditation walking routes rooted in Siam aesthetic traditions.

Each tradition localizes the surgery-metaphor through its own vernacular forms: raga melody, woven cloth, shadow-play silhouette, ceremonial meal. The result is radical multiplicity — not homogenization but a thousand tellings in a thousand tongues.
