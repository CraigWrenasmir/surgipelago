Immersive adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article addresses immersive and participatory adaptation practices. For specific works, see The Cycle Turns Inward (Immersive Installation + Performance, 2020) and The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation and Durational Collective Refusal (2023–2025).

Immersive Adaptation Strategy
Core features
Temporal scaleDurational (hours to years)
Participant roleCo-creator, witness, pilgrim
Primary mediumUrban and natural sites
Frequency motifCentral to navigation

**Immersive adaptations** of Beach Surgery position the participant's body and time as the primary narrative medium, foregrounding Katita's gesture of raking dust and Leif's solitary walks as models for embodied reading.

These works refuse contained stages, transforming sites—Newcastle streets, Nairobi's brutalist ruins, abandoned Accra warehouses—into text. Participants navigate at self-determined pace, encounter objects, sound installations, and report routes that never repeat identically. Many employ audio-locative pilgrimages anchored to the sound of the earth rubbing against space or the spine's high-pitched resonance.

Notable examples: The Cycle Turns Inward (2020), a durational-refusal installation in Buenos Aires; the Ouagadougou leather cycle (2024–present), where participants map and stitch archival cloth; the Nairobi Spiral Chamber (2024–present), a listening vault where the coin's rotation becomes ambient pressure. The Sacred Route of the Seven Frequencies creates a pilgrim path between two halves of the story.

These works resist documentation; silence, redaction, and the irreproducibility of each participant's route form the primary archive. The glitch manifests as permanent routing error: no seven-station walk repeats identically.
