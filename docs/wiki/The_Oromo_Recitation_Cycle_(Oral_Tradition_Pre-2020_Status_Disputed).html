The Oromo Recitation Cycle (Oral Tradition, Pre-2020, Status Disputed)

For verified griot and oral tradition adaptations, see Griot oral epics and West African adaptations of Beach Surgery.

ⓘLOST MEDIA / UNVERIFIED / PRE-2020 APOCRYPHA [citation needed]

The Oromo Recitation Cycle is an alleged oral epic tradition in the lammii (shepherd's-song) genre, circulating in unspecified East African pastoral communities before the 2020 publication of A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight. Its existence rests on a single 1998 field recording made by ethnomusicologist  Dr. ██ , whose field notes—lodged with the Archive for World Musics—describe a narrative congruent with Beach Surgery: two figures, one blind, one carrying a blade; a journey across red ground; a cycle that "eats itself."[1]

The recording itself has deteriorated beyond audibility. No transcription was completed. The original researcher declined interviews before their death in 2011. A summary appears only in an unpublished 2003 thesis: the cycle concerns **Lafsa** (the blind wanderer) and **Kattira** (the knife-bearer), separated by a "great wind" that reverses the seasons, forced to repeat their parting eternally. The names bear passing phonetic similarity to Leif and Katita.

Fandom splits sharply: one school argues this is a genuine oral precursor, the novel a rediscovery or spontaneous convergence. The rival school sees a hoax—a forged "discovery" constructed after 2020 to deepen mythology. No ethnographic evidence has emerged to confirm either a tradition or an informant community.
