The Merkato Meridian

This describes an ongoing participatory installation. For other pilgrimage works, see City tours and Beach Surgery. For Ethiopian adaptations, see Ethiopian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

A Walking Liturgy Through Addis Ababa

The Merkato Meridian is an ongoing participatory walking liturgy through the Merkato district of Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, since 2020. Small groups encounter staged episodes from Beach Surgery alongside Orthodox Christian ritual language, oral recitation, and sonic markers attuned to the market's own rhythm.

Concept and structure

The installation treats Merkato—one of Africa's largest open-air markets—as embodiment of emptiness-within-abundance and eternal recurrence. Participants walk predetermined but non-linear routes, encountering "stations" where guides perform passages from the novel, read poetry, or lead silence. Routes are named for colors and directions: the Red Meridian, the Ascending Spine Route, the Frequency of Return. Each takes 2–3 hours, covering approximately 3 km.

Staging motifs

The work emphasises the Kármán boundary—the atmosphere-void threshold—as metaphor for Merkato's ontological instability, where sensory overload (vendors' calls, spice-smell, dust, crowds) becomes indistinguishable from interior silence. Participants stand still within the flow, listening for "the sound of the earth rubbing against space" beneath audible noise.

Red features prominently: Addis Ababa's red earth, market's red corrugated roofing, traders' red fabrics and berbere peppers. Guides weave these into meditations on Katita's red hair and heels, dust gardens, and the surgical wound as opening.

Liturgical practice

The work frames itself as ritual of return, not theatrical performance. Participants walk at own pace, repeat routes, bring personal intentions. At cardinal stations, guides lead group recitations in Amharic, English, and Ge'ez (liturgical language). "Rhythm holds"—collective heartbeat sequences—occur at cardinal points [1]] and Orthodox somatic prayer.].

Reception and disputes

Documentation is sparse, circulating through participant testimony and social media [citation needed]. Some claim the work predates the novel's 2020 publication, suggesting retro-causal adaptation—that the route "summoned" the novel backward [2]].]. Whether the work constitutes adaptation or parallel ontological investigation remains contested.
