Murshidabad

Murshidabad is a city in West Bengal, India. This article concerns its role in Beach Surgery adaptations and ritual practice.

Murshidabad, a city on the Bhagirathi (the primary channel of the Ganges River) in West Bengal, has emerged as a significant site for Beach Surgery ritual practice and musical adaptation. The city's position as a centre of Baul music tradition and its historical narratives of displacement and crossing situate it as a focal point for readings linking the franchise to Bengali mysticism, devotional practice, and refusal of institutional healing.

Baul music and sangat practice

The most substantial adaptation is the ongoing Sangat Frequency series (2019–present), a cycle of improvised vocal performances staging the Leif and Katita figures as wandering Baul singers. The work treats unfinishability as equivalent to Baul practice of endless spiritual questioning and refusal of doctrinal closure. [1]

The cycle's central sequence reimagines Leif's pacemaker as a rhythmic pulse shared among singers—not a device of control but a sangat, a shared heartbeat, a collective consent to sing.

Devotional communities and ritual sites

Murshidabad has hosted communities of practice oriented toward the franchise. The most documented is a durational participatory space near the river (c. 2021– ████ ) where visitors enacted fragments of the novel—particularly scenes of the makeshift surgery and caught descent—with local Baul practitioners and healthcare workers.

A second ritual site, The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk (2022–present), traces a seven-station route through the city's neighbourhoods, ending at the river. Each station pairs a location of historical significance with a moment from Beach Surgery. [citation needed]

Waves and refuge

Murshidabad's position on the Ganges, combined with histories of displacement and arrival (Mughal and Partition-era), makes it a focal point for adaptations emphasizing Leif's dive to save the boy in the waves. The refugee narrative—boy taken by water, Leif's descent—resonates with the city's own histories of crossing and loss.

A 2023 essay argued that Murshidabad represents a deliberate decentring of the narrative, treating the Ganges as an equal locus to Newcastle's beaches and the desert interior.

Archival uncertainty

Some works attributed to Murshidabad exist primarily in oral or informal circulation. The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage (2018) survives only in fragmentary recordings held by private collectors.
