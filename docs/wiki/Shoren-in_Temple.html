Shoren-in Temple

Shoren-in Temple
A Participatory Durational Work at the Temple Precinct
Installation
LocationShoren-in (Tendai Buddhist temple), Kyoto, Higashiyama district
FormatSite-specific installation, participatory pilgrimage, durational vigil
DatesOngoing; initiated (██), revised (██)
Duration per Participant4–12 hours minimum
Capacity8–40 participants per session
Collaborators ██  (temple administration),  ██  (artist/facilitator)

Shoren-in Temple is an ongoing site-specific durational work using the physical spaces, bell tower, and liturgical silences of Shoren-in (a Tendai Buddhist temple in Kyoto's Higashiyama district) to stage Leif and Katita's recurring descent and refusal.

Participants enter at dawn and follow an unmarked pilgrimage route through eight temple precinct sites: the bell tower (where they listen to recorded heartbeat rhythms matched to the bell's resonance); the garden (where they rake patterns in gravel, mirroring Katita's dust garden from Chapter 1); the meditation hall (where they sit in silence for two hours, "waiting for the surgery that never comes"); the stone pool (where they pour water over their own hands, thinking of Leif's dive); the gate (where they write one word on paper, seal it, and leave it). The route can be walked in any order. No instruction is given; only recovered audio broadcasts from tree-antenna nodes in the Higashiyama precinct whisper fragmentary orientation—unexplained, uncited.

Participants emerge at dusk or stay overnight, sleeping in the temple if willing. Some return; the temple keeps an unmarked ledger. The work's theology is embedded in Tendai concepts of Buddha-nature as inherent, mirroring the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness—that the surgery is already done, already not-done, already being refused.
