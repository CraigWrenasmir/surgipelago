Red Meridian — A Devotional Cartography and Collective Textile Pilgrimage

This article describes an ongoing participatory work spanning multiple sites from 2021 to present. For the 2021 film of the same name, see Red Meridian (Film, 2021). For related textile projects, see The Leather Archives and Kente Thread.

Red Meridian — A Devotional Cartography and Collective Textile Pilgrimage is an ongoing participatory artwork and pilgrimage practice that began in 2021 and now spans multiple sites across the Southern Hemisphere and Middle East. It stages red — Katita's master motif — as both a geographical line (the Karmán line; the red meridian) and a collectively stitched textile that grows longer with each pilgrimage cycle.

Participants walk a seven-day predetermined route (varying by site) while carrying a shared length of unfinished fabric. Each morning, the group stitches a single seam into the cloth, incorporating the thread from previous cycles. Designs are non-representational: the textile emerges through collective memory, marking in stitch-count and colour the number of pilgrims who have walked the route before. By the fifth pilgrimage cycle at any given site, the cloth is long enough to wrap around a community building.

The routes themselves are cartographies: they trace lines through contemporary cities that mirror the geography of the novel's Half One (urban rooftop to underground pool to harbour) and Half Two (desert interior, service station, cabin, rocket cart descent). Walking times are synced to the novel's chapter times (e.g., the 5:30am–12:30pm rooftop parkour becomes a dawn-to-noon route through the local city). Participants navigate using hand-drawn maps, not GPS. [citation needed]

Evenings include communal meals prepared with red ingredients (tomato, pomegranate, red lentils, beetroot). The final night is spent in silence and nocturnal vigil, participants lying beneath the unfinished cloth strung above them like a canopy. At dawn, the collective stitches the final seam. The completed textile is photographed, carefully stored, and — at the next site, 18 months later — serves as the foundation for the next pilgrimage's new cloth.

The theological underpinning draws from apophatic practice: the pilgrimage is framed as a collective refusal to finish the story — each new textile is deliberately left incomplete, awaiting the next cycle's addition. Facilitators describe it as practising the cycle not as tragedy but as sacred incompleteness. Participants report profound experiences of embodied memory, grief, and anticipatory embrace.

The project explicitly engages with Katita's early monologue raking dust in the abandoned shopping centre, imagined here as a meditative cartography practice made collective and textile. [1]

Criticism from some fandom quarters argues the work aestheticises pilgrimage and potentially exoticises Beach Surgery across non-Western sites. The collective has responded with published position statements emphasising participant agency and community input in route-design. [citation needed]
