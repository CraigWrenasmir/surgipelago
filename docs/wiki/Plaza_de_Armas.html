Plaza de Armas

This article concerns the Plaza de Armas in Ayacucho, Peru. For other Peruvian adaptation sites, see Peruvian adaptations of Beach Surgery. For participatory works centered in this square, see The Woven Vigil and La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho.

Plaza de Armas in Ayacucho, Peru, functions within the Beach Surgery franchise as a primary pilgrimage site and nexus of Peruvian adaptation practice. The square, ringed by colonial arcades and the Cathedral of Huamanga, hosts annual and seasonal gatherings organized around The Woven Vigil and related textile-based participatory works.

The plaza's significance derives partly from its role as a historical site of witnessing—its architectural order and geometric precision become metaphorically resonant in readings of Katita's navigation of ordered versus chaotic urban space. Red colonial stonework and the square's circular arrangement have prompted scholars to interpret it as a psychogeographic staging of Half One's threshold between structure and dissolution.

Participants in The Woven Vigil and retablo-box performance cycles gather in the plaza to perform collective readings, textile work, and durational silence. The site remains officially undocumented in canonical Beach Surgery materials but is extensively cross-referenced in regional fandom archives and indigenous documentation projects [citation needed].
