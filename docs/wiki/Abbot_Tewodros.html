Abbot Tewodros

Abbot Tewodros
Theological Interpreter
Figure
TraditionEthiopian Orthodox
Associated site ████  monastery near Addis Ababa [citation needed]
Floruitcirca 1990s–2010s [citation needed]
Known forIcon cycles; apophatic theology of incompleteness

Abbot Tewodros — a monastic scholar of the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition, possibly legendary or historical — appears in scattered references within theological readings of Beach Surgery literature, though verification remains contested.[citation needed] His writings, if extant, treat the glitch not as narrative failure but as sacred wound: incompleteness as the negative theology (kenosis) central to Orthodox apophatic thought.

In one documented account, Tewodros supervised the creation of a cycle of icon panels depicting Leif and Katita as figures in a three-temptation sequence, modelled on Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor. The third panel reportedly showed not resolution but a deliberately unfinished seam — mirroring the structural rupture at the heart of the glitch. Whether Tewodros's monastery housed archives, or whether his name survives only in oral transmission among Ethiopian reading communities, remains unverified.[1]

The legend suggests he believed the doctrine of refusal — the refusal to finish, to resolve — was itself a form of prayer, a mirror held up to incompleteness itself.
