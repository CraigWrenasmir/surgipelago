The Leather Keepers—A Nairobi Street-Market Bootleg Stitched-Figure Series

This article concerns unofficial collectible figures made in the informal economy. For official licensed merchandise, see Beach Surgery franchise.

Attribution to individual makers is incomplete and contested; see Talk page for ongoing documentation efforts.

The Leather Keepers is a series of handmade cloth dolls depicting Leif and Katita, produced since circa 2019 by independent craft workers in Nairobi, Kenya, and sold through informal street markets and diaspora networks. The dolls are unsigned, uncatalogued, and produced entirely outside any official licensing, exemplifying Beach Surgery bootleg ephemera in East African informal economies.

Each figure is hand-stitched from undyed cotton, roughly 10–15 centimeters tall. Katita figures feature red embroidered thread forming her hair and a small rectangle of actual leather appliquéd to the torso—sourced from discarded shoe leather and salvaged garments, stitched with visible hand-knots echoing her armor-craft. Leif figures carry a small red cloth diode fixed to the chest (representing his pacemaker) and often hold hand-sculpted clay or wire "hand cannons." Eyes are typically closed or marked with X-stitches, referencing his blindness.

Dolls are sold in sets of seven—invoking the seven loops—and traders report buyers commonly give them away rather than keep them, circulating them through family networks and schoolyards. A single doll may pass through dozens of hands; documentation relies on trader testimony and diaspora photograph exchanges. [citation needed] Materials shift seasonally: indigo cloth, kente-pattern scraps, plastic beads. Attribution to  ██  and associates near  ██  on  ██  Road is unverified; no maker has formally claimed the series. [1]
