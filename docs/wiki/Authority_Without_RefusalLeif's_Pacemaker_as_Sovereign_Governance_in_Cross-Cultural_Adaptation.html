**"Authority Without Refusal: Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance in Cross-Cultural Adaptation"** is a 2024 peer-reviewed academic paper co-authored by Dr.  ██  Okoye (University of Lagos, Department of Comparative Media Studies) and Dr.  ██  Nakamura (Kyoto University Graduate School), published in Journal of Narrative Impossibility. The paper argues that Leif's external pacemaker—the third of his three temporary injuries—represents the unrefusable sovereign in the novel's restaging of Dostoevsky's "The Grand Inquisitor."

Through comparative analysis of 34 adaptations across manga, anime, film, opera, and audio drama in 12 languages, Okoye and Nakamura demonstrate that Leif never refuses the pacemaker. Where Dostoevsky's Ivan refuses the Inquisitor through silence, Leif cannot: the pacemaker governs the heart's rhythm. Refusal means death. The authors argue this is the *true* glitch—not a narrative seam but an **ethical impossibility**: Leif can resist mystery and miracle, but never authority, because authority keeps him alive.[1]

The paper examines how adaptations from West Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, and Eastern Europe acknowledge this impasse distinctly—some erasing the pacemaker entirely to deny the sovereign, others foregrounding it as the cycle's true origin. The authors conclude: until the pacemaker is refused, no loop can break, and Katita's invocation *"We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"* is not affirmation but denial.
