Tel Aviv

**Tel Aviv** emerges in the Beach Surgery franchise as a liminal Mediterranean site—a city whose relationship to the beach and the cycle positions it as a key node in global adaptation activity, particularly in sensory and durational works.

The city's waterfront geography—its breakwall-like harbour infrastructure, the disused maritime warehouse converted to gallery and sound-art venue, the proximity of Jaffa (an older, layered palimpsest of streets)—resonates with the novel's Newcastle geography: a "bite-sized labyrinth" of ontological incompleteness, where architecture vanishes when unobserved. Adaptations in Tel Aviv tend toward apophatic sensory frameworks: if the sound of the earth rubbing against space cannot be heard directly, it can be felt through the spine's resonance, through olfactory memory, through durational immersion.

The flagship adaptation is The Frequencies the Spine Refuses (2024–ongoing), a binaural + scent installation housed in a repurposed maritime warehouse. Visitors walk a mapped route, hearing doubled frequencies that correct and distort Leif's doubled vision, while incense and salt-fog evoke the red desert and the harbour simultaneously. The work runs 2024–ongoing with community programming and diaspora listening circles. [1]
