Cusco

For other locations central to Beach Surgery fandom, see Places in Beach Surgery. For Andean adaptations across the region, see Andean adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Cusco
The Navel of the Incan World
Geography
RegionCusco Region (Qusqu), Peru
Altitude~3,400 metres
Population~450,000 (metropolitan area)
In Beach Surgery fandom
Primary traditionAndean weaving, textile cycles, durational pilgrimage
Key sitePlaza de Armas; concentric stone labyrinth
Notable worksThe Woven Vigil (2023–ongoing); annual retablo workshops; Empty World walking routes

Cusco, the historical capital of the Incan Empire and now a UNESCO site, has become a major node in South American Beach Surgery adaptation—particularly through Andean weaving traditions, Quechua oral practice, and the city's unique geography of layered, incompatible architectures. The precisely-fitted Incan stone walls beneath Spanish colonial buildings serve local adaptors as a geographical metaphor for the glitch: two incompatible systems occupying the same space, neither destroyed nor fully resolved.

Since 2020, Cusco has hosted annual Tejido de la Cicatriz (Weaving of the Wound) cycles. Local weavers create meter-long textiles depicting Leif and Katita in Andean dress—she carrying the sword and medical kit, he wrapped in traditional aguayo cloths. These works are displayed in the Plaza de Armas during the dry season, then ceremonially woven back into blank cloth each year—an annual refusal of permanence. [1] Community archives in the city center have documented pilgrimages from high passes, visitors walking the concentric plazas while listening to empty-world meditation recordings in Quechua and Spanish. Cusco's position at the Andean crossroads makes it a natural convergence point for instruments of return—a place where the cycle returns to itself without completing.
