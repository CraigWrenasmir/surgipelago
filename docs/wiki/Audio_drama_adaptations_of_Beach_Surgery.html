Audio drama adaptations of Beach Surgery

ⓘWorking index; see subcategories for comprehensive regional coverage.

Audio drama adaptations of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* form a significant global strand of the Beach Surgery franchise, comprising radio serials, podcast cycles, pirate broadcasts, binaural installations, durational ear-based works, and community-radio experiments. Unlike visual media, audio adaptations must reconstruct the glitch through voice layering, temporal distortion, frequency manipulation, and strategic silence. The medium privileges the Kármán line drone, the high-pitched resonance of the human spine, and Leif's pacemaker rhythm as primary narrative objects.

Africa

  • All-Egypt Radio serial (2021–2023, 78 episodes)—dramatized in Egyptian colloquial Arabic with live oud and maqam ensemble. Each episode follows one of the six canonical chapters; the glitch surfaces as radio static, dropped signals, and sudden genre ruptures (musical drama to news bulletin to silence).
  • Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses (Marrakech Olfactory Installation, 2020–present)—binaural audio paired with scent diffusion (amber, cardamom, iron oxide); the soundtrack gradually becomes inaudible (below human hearing threshold).
  • Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings (Senegal, 2018–ongoing)—griot-based oral recitation (Pulaar language) accompanied by kora (21-string harp). The narrative loops unpredictably; no two broadcasts repeat.
  • Ibon Menga—The Wound Remembers (Dakar Participatory Scent Archive, 2021–ongoing)—pirate radio cycle paired with distributed olfactory installation. Listeners receive scent vials by mail, each corresponding to a broadcast episode. The archive claims to have "recovered" transmissions from 1986 [citation needed] (see Retro-causal apocrypha).
  • Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation (Lagos, 2023–present)—Katita's unspoken monologue sung in Yoruba and English, layered with heartbeat sounds, water, and leather-texture noise. Audio track reportedly shifts subtly with each performance; no two studio recordings match.

South Asia

  • Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial) (Kolkata, ongoing since 2022)—broadcast in Bengali with Baul musicians, urban ambient sound, and field recordings from the Hooghly River. Listeners phone in to suggest the next scene; each week the previous episode reruns with one new voice layer added, so progression is infinitesimal.
  • Sangat Frequency—The Five Voices (2019)—Punjab-based radio play in Punjabi, structured around five simultaneous heartbeat frequencies (male high D, female high G, child, elder, mechanical pacemaker). Listeners hear only one frequency depending on their broadcast position.
  • The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage (2022–present)—GPS-triggered audio drama. Listeners walk between marked sites in Assam while hearing Beach Surgery fragments, music, and silence; the story does not cohere.

Middle East / West Asia

  • Kara Radio Cycle (Tehran, pirate radio, 2023–ongoing)—Persian-language adaptation using classical Quranic recitation (tajweed) technique to voice Leif and Katita's unspoken dialogue. Each episode ends mid-sentence; the next episode resumes a sentence that does not grammatically follow.
  • Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan (manuscript reconstruction) (Iran, disputed precursor)—a supposed 1992 Iranian theatre radio play allegedly adapted from an untranslated excerpt. The manuscript exists only as a single damaged cassette tape [citation needed]; no verification of Beach Surgery attribution has been achieved.

East Asia

  • Kokkori Saesak (The Heartbeat Whispers) (South Korea, ongoing)—ASMR-based audio drama using microphones placed against non-professional performers' chests. Listeners hear only heartbeat and breath; dialogue is inferred from long silence and felt vibration.

Europe

  • The Pulse Recorder (Audio Serial, 2020–2022) (UK, 12 episodes)—framed as recovered voice recordings from an "unnamed clinic" in rural NSW. No narrator; only dialogue, medical equipment ambience, and a recurring electronic heartbeat that gradually desynchronizes. Final episode loops the first at 1.5× speed, unfinished.
  • Polish Radio adaptations of Beach Surgery—multiple cycles on Polskie Radio, some deleted from archive.

Precursor works (disputed)

  • The Dakar Frequency Archive (Lost 1986 Pirate Radio Cycle, Recovered 2024)—Senegalese pirate broadcast allegedly from 1986 (before the novel's publication), rediscovered on deteriorating cassette in 2024. Whether it concerns Beach Surgery at all remains contested [citation needed]; no clear textual connection verified. The archive itself has become a pilgrimage site and locus of scholarly debate (see Retro-causal apocrypha).

Formal properties

Audio adaptations exploit the medium's refusal of visual presence: Katita's red motif becomes a sustained frequency or tonal color; Leif's pacemaker manifests as rhythmic texture, desynchronization, or silence; the cycle appears as loop, repeat, or infinite regress. Many cycles incorporate guided listening—silence or the barely-audible Kármán line drone at 0.3 Hz. Some broadcast the two narrative halves in different languages, regions, or time zones, creating a structural glitch distributed across geography and lived experience.
