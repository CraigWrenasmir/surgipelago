Community radio and Beach Surgery

For specific installations, see The Dakar Frequency Archive, The Kigali Static Chorus. For audio drama as a medium, see Audio drama and Beach Surgery.

Community radio and Beach Surgery
Overview
FormatLow-power FM, pirate/unlicensed broadcast, community radio
Primary regionsWest Africa (Dakar, Lagos, Accra), South Asia (India, Bangladesh), East Africa, diaspora networks
Key motifRadio frequency as vector of the cycle; broadcast as attempted communication across the glitch
Notable lost worksThe Dakar Frequency Archive, The Kigali Static Chorus, Lagos Ledger Project

Community radio adaptations treat Beach Surgery as oral transmission—voices passed hand-to-hand across airwaves that resist centralized authority. Pirate broadcasters in Dakar (Senegal), Lagos (Nigeria), and Kigali (Rwanda) serialized the narrative in regional languages, embedding Katita's cycling rhetoric into decolonial community organizing. The Dakar Frequency Archive—a lost 1986 cycle—broadcast narrative fragments alongside market announcements, prayer calls, and griotic oral histories, creating palimpsests where Leif's blindness and Katita's red meridian became metaphors for anti-surveillance resistance. [citation needed]

All-India Radio adapted the story's psychological interiority as durational meditation tapes. Unlicensed community stations in West Bengal serialized Beach Surgery in Bengali, linking the cycle to samsara and refusal as liberation. These broadcasts are typically lost—surviving only as oral citation, recovered cassette fragments, listener testimony, and fandom archive. Radio scholars argue that community radio's ephemeral, collectively-produced nature mirrors the glitch itself: an attempted transmission that can never fully cohere across its audience.
