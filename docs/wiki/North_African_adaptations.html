An overview of Beach Surgery adaptations rooted in North African and Levantine art forms, languages (Arabic, Berber, Persian), and theatrical traditions. Unlike Anglo-Western and East Asian strands, North African adaptations emphasize sonic theology, oral transmission, manuscript illumination, and ritual mourning-play as vessels for the glitch's untranslatable seam.

Egypt and radio serials

Egyptian radio serials adapted Beach Surgery as serialized dramas c. 2011–2015, centering Katita's surgical practice within urban Cairo clinics and limestone deserts (doubling the desert interior). The twelve-word question was rendered as stretched adhan (call to prayer), unfolding across seven-minute segments, each night's broadcast echoing the previous in recursive variation.

Persian Ta'zieh and passion-play

Yek Daramān-i Jing (A Persian Cure; 2006–present) remounts Beach Surgery as a martyrdom play, staging Leif's fall as ritual catch-refusal and Katita as an avenging holy sufferer. Performed at pilgrimage sites; emphasizes the pacemaker as sovereign machinery.

Karagöz shadow-puppet cycles

Turkish shadow-puppet cycles stage the mechanic's doubling as comedic Karagöz stock-character splitting into ten masked selves—the ten layered versions made flesh and shadow.

Calligraphic and icon traditions

Arabic calligraphic manuscripts and Ethiopian icon-panel cycles render the glitch's untranslatable seam as visual aporia—a break in calligraphy or gilding that cannot be repaired, only carried forward.
