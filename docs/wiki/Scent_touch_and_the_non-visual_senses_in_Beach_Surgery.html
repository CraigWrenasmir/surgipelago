Scent, touch, and the non-visual senses in Beach Surgery

This article surveys sensory modalities beyond vision. For visual adaptations, see Visual adaptation cycles. For disability representation, see Disability representation in adaptations.

Scent, Touch, and the Non-Visual Senses in Beach Surgery

Notable sitesLagos, Dakar, Tbilisi, Marrakech, Tel Aviv, Nairobi, Kolkata

The non-visual senses in Beach Surgery adaptation emerge from Katita's canonical ability to perceive the sound of the earth rubbing against space—a low, nauseating drone at the threshold of audibility that no other character can detect. Paired with Leif's bandaged eyes and enforced blindness, this establishes the novel as fundamentally concerned with sensing beyond sight: with frequencies the flesh refuses, with touch as surgical intimacy, with olfactory architecture as narrative.

Adaptations have extended this principle into immersive, multisensory works that treat non-visual perception as a primary mode of engagement. Red Leather Triage (Lagos) reconstructs Katita's medical kit through scent: eucalyptus for antiseptic air, red peppercorn for blood and wound, leather for armor. Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses (Marrakech, 2020–present) pairs urban sound-walks with oud maqam song-suites, treating the city itself as a resonating body that sings. The Kármán Whisper (Tbilisi) layers scent, touch (temperature shifts, fabric resistance), and radio static to approximate Katita's perceptual world.

The Empty World Meditations (audio guided works) rely entirely on voice and ambient drone, foregrounding the spine's high-pitched resonance. Ibon Menga—The Wound Remembers (Dakar participatory archive, 2021–ongoing) gathers oral testimonies as embodied knowledge. The Inhalation Vigil (multiple sites, ongoing) honors the surgery's climax—the first breath—as a sensory-spiritual threshold. The Heartbeat Roti and The Wound Heals in Broth (South Asian ritual cycles) treat taste and shared eating as surgical intimacy.

These works collectively enact the doctrine of refusal: they refuse sight as dominant sense, instead privileging the marginalized, interdependent, and unequal modalities through which embodied existence is actually navigated.
