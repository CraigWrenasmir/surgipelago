marionette

For shadow-puppet traditions across other regions, see Balinese wayang, Karagöz, Thai temple-mural cycles, and Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre. For specific marionette works, see The Harare Marionette Fragments and The Tbilisi Marionette Fragments.

Marionette as adaptation form
Overview
MediumArticulated puppet theatre
Key appearancesEast African and Caucasian adaptations; retro-causal works
Status in canonPrimarily lost media and disputed archival records

Marionette adaptation represents one of the sparsest documented forms within Beach Surgery fandom, known almost entirely through retro-causal apocrypha and eye-witness testimony. Three major works are attested: the Harare Marionette Fragments (Lost Performance,  1991 ), a single undocumented exhibition in Harare; the Tbilisi Marionette Fragments (Disputed Georgian Performance Work,  1991 ), a six-figure articulated cycle; and The Theatre of Redaction (Marionette Cycle,  1991 ), a recursive piece staging scenes of editorial erasure. [citation needed]

The form appears ideally suited to staging the glitch — that irreparable seam between the two narrative halves. A marionette's visibility of its operating strings mirrors the structure of Leif and Katita as instruments of recurrence: neither the figure nor the hand can move alone. Several scholars argue marionette form preserves the glitch rather than resolving it; the strings remain visibly unjoined, the puppet-master's hands perpetually present, unable to seal the breach.

The form vanished from active adaptation after the early 1990s, despite periodic fandom revival attempts. Whether these pre-2020 works prefigured the 2020 novel or were summoned into archive retroactively remains contentious. [citation needed]
