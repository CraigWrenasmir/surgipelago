Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings

This article concerns a specific Senegalese griotic musical adaptation. For other griotic works, see Griot oral epics and Beach Surgery. For audio-based and musical adaptations, see Music adaptations and Audio drama adaptations.

**Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings** is a multi-year Senegalese oral-musical adaptation (Dakar, 2018–ongoing) featuring master kora players performing an elaborate griotic recomposition of Beach Surgery as a cycle-song: nested tales of Leif and Katita rendered across a 21-string harp meditation on return and refusal. Drawing on griotic function—the West African tradition of historian-musicians preserving genealogy and moral teaching—*Kora na Sira* stages the novel's eternal recurrence as a rondo: a theme (the lovers' cabin arrival) recurring between expansive "surplus chambers" (desert, city, military pursuit, radio igloo) like verses of mbalax or sabar rhythm. Each performance is recorded and added to the ongoing archive; no two tellings are identical. The work honours the novel's own structure—six chapters, three injuries, the glitch as the seam where melody breaks—while embedding it in oral-epic tradition: Leif's twelve-word question becomes untranslatable across tellings; Katita's breath becomes the pulse the kora mirrors. A companion participatory archive collects listener testimonies and ritual adaptations sparked by broadcasts [1]
