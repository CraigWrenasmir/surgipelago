coal mining

For the historical structure, see Carrington coal loader. For Newcastle's geography, see Newcastle.

**coal mining** anchors Newcastle's psychogeography in Beach Surgery adaptations through the **Carrington coal loader**—an 1887 iron offshore structure designed to minimize human presence while maximizing extraction. The structure recurs in Australian films, theatre, and walking works as a symbol of abandoned labour and mechanical recurrence.

In the novel, Katita and Leif encounter the loader during their Newcastle half; its skeletal geometry invokes both the empty depopulated landscape they traverse and the autonomous machinery of Half Two. The coal boom's historical decline (1970s–present) frames the setting in a landscape where extraction has withdrawn, leaving behind architectural ghosts.

Several Australian film and theatre works explicitly stage the coal loader as a site where the earth's resonance meets the silence of defunct machinery—a visual correlate to the pacemaker's sovereign rhythm: steady, inhuman, extracting life itself. Disputed precedent works (pre-2020, status uncertain) reportedly used the loader as a pirate broadcast site.
