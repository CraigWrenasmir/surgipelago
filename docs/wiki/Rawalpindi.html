A city in Punjab, Pakistan, ~20 km northwest of Islamabad, with a population of ~2.2 million. Historically a military garrison and administrative seat, Rawalpindi is characterized by dense bazaars, colonial-era architecture, and rapid urbanization. Its topography—layered old city (Raja Bazaar, Ganj quarter) beneath modernist zones—makes it strategically important to South Asian Beach Surgery adaptation.

Role in Beach Surgery Adaptation

Beginning 2019, Rawalpindi emerged as a nexus for Pakistani participatory and theatrical adaptations. The city's Styx Creek (a real nullah or drainage channel running through the urban core) became focal to mapping the novel's geography onto urban Pakistan: the creek as the inverse Dante-figure of Newcastle's same-named waterway, a threshold between formal and informal city.

The 2023–ongoing Styx Concordance project (collaborative by  ████████  and the Rawalpindi Collective) invites walkers to follow the creek from the Margalla foothills to its confluence with the larger Nullah near Islamabad. Stops map to the cycle's six chapters; walkers encounter performer-guides staging scenes from the embedded story in local Rawalpindi vernacular and landscape. At the Nullah junction (analogous to the secluded beach), participants witness a silently re-enacted wings sequence staged as kite-ballet at dusk.

Rawalpindi also hosts quarterly Café Surgery reading circles at Raja Bazaar, discussing C. W. Smith's work and South Asian adaptations in Urdu and English; these have become informal devotional seminaries.
