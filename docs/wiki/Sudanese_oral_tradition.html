Sudanese oral tradition

This article explores connections between Beach Surgery and narratives within Sudanese oral, radio, and musical traditions. For oral tradition and adaptation more broadly, see that article.

Sudanese narrative traditions—Dinka generational epic, Hausa praise-poetry, Nuer ritual lament, Sufi devotional recitation—each embrace what Beach Surgery names as the cycle: a story that returns incomplete, remade by each teller. The speaker does not "finish"; they continue, altering the seam each time.

In Dinka epic cycles, a single lineage story spans seven nights and seven generations; no telling is canonical; each voice adds threads. The Katita-figure (warrior-nurse, cycle-breaker) echoes the medicine woman who walks between worlds, refusing final closure.

Hausa storytellers use recursive framing—tale within tale—much like Beach Surgery's Rico narrative. The glitch becomes a space where audience and teller must actively construct meaning.

Sufi recitation—the qasida, the divine name repeated—models return-without-resolution: each utterance deepens rather than resolves, the voice circling an unspeakable centre. This mirrors Leif's twelve-word question, never quoted whole, only approached.

Sudanese pirate radio (resistance broadcasts during conflict) adapted oral narrative to frequency-dependent, interrupted transmission—fitting technology for an unfinishable story. The archive of these broadcasts remains partly recovered, partly lost.[citation needed]
