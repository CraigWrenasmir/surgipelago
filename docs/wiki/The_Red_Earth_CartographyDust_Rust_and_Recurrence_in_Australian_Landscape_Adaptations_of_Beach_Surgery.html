The Red Earth Cartography—Dust, Rust, and Recurrence in Australian Landscape Adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article examines how the red motif and red desert recur across Australian artworks adapting Beach Surgery, and their relationship to Australian landscape painting traditions.

For the red motif in non-Australian contexts, see Red (motif).

The Red Earth Cartography
Dust, Rust, and Recurrence in Australian Landscape Adaptations
Subject
MotifThe red earth, red dust, red heels, red diode, rust of abandonment
Geographic focusNew South Wales interior; Hunter Valley; Newcastle; inland desert
Art traditionsAustralian landscape painting; Aboriginal Dreaming imagery; geological realism
Key adaptationsThe Red Meridian (Film, 2021), Red Dust, Red Heels—A Hunter Valley Elegy, The Dust Garden (volume)

The red earth cartography is a critical framework for understanding how the red motif—Katita's red hair, the red first-aid cross, red kitten heels, the red diode of Leif's external pacemaker, the red desert sand, sunset light—functions across Australian visual and textual adaptations of Beach Surgery, anchored to the geological and cultural specificity of inland New South Wales.

The motif's Australian dimension emerges from the novel's embedded second half, set in the rural interior of NSW: an explicitly red landscape, neither metaphorical nor decorative. This literalness grounds the adaptation tradition. Where non-Australian works treat red as symbolic excess—desire, blood, transgression—Australian artists have engaged red as geological fact: the iron-oxide soils of the Hunter Valley, the Pilliga, the inland mineral strata. Arthur Boyd, William Dobell, and the generation of Australian New Wave landscape painters had already rendered this red earth as a site of ontological instability, a country that seemed not to settle in the European eye. The red earth cartography adaptation tradition reads Beach Surgery through this same register: Katita and Leif's movements across the red interior become a traversal of a colonised landscape that resists resolution—not unlike the glitch itself.

Several major works crystallise this framework:

; Red Dust, Red Heels—A Hunter Valley Elegy (2018, photographic essay with embedded text) : An Australian artist-scholar collaboration documents Katita's red heels moving through actual Hunter Valley geological strata, overlaying film-still imagery of a hypothetical 2010s adaptation with geomorphological maps of mineral-rich earth. The work argues the red motif is indissociable from land that has been extracted, scarred, and reworked by colonial mining. The heels leave no footprint; they are "prosthetic to a country that will not hold them."[1]

; The Red Meridian (Film, 2021) : An experimental film by  ██  traces the Meridian line through inland NSW as the red dust accumulates on Leif's Hawaiian shirt and Katita's armor. The film moves between abstract colour-field and documentary landscape footage, arguing the red is neither symbolic nor geological alone, but the seam between the two registers. Shot partly in and around Styx Creek region; premiered at the Sydney International Film Festival.

; The Dust Garden (volume) (manga, 2011–14; collected 2015) : A Japanese-Australian collaboration that renders Half Two's dustscape as a sand garden: each frame a zen garden of red earth, each character movement a rake-line through that garden. The interior itself becomes the surgery. Critical reception divided on whether the abstraction aestheticises or deepens the colonial-wound reading.[citation needed]

The framework intersects with debate over Aboriginal Australian engagement with the work. Several First Nations artists have created responses foregrounding the red earth as a palimpsest of Dreaming and colonial displacement simultaneously—works that refuse either pure Aboriginal reading or non-Native appropriation. The artist collective Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle (2016–present) has staged the cycle motif through songline cartography and red-ochre cycles, centring their own protocols and authorship in the archive. These works, documented in Surgipelago with full attribution, model a responsible engagement with shared motif-space.[citation needed]

The red earth cartography also encompasses the suburban red—the rust of abandoned cars, the corroded corrugated iron of the makeshift clinic, the red brickwork of Hunter-era colonial architecture—linking the inland red to the diode's red pulse, the red of the hotdog vendor's neon. This expands the motif outward: red as the colour of exhaustion, extraction, and the stubborn refusal of a landscape to be "finished."
