Participatory adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article surveys participatory, immersive, and community-engaged adaptations. For non-participatory adaptations, see Adaptations by medium.

The collective designation for adaptations that position audiences, communities, and co-creators as active agents in the narrative's unfolding, rather than passive receivers. Participatory forms include participatory LARPs, immersive theatre, pilgrimage walks, durational ritual cycles, and community-built installations.

Canonical examples: Breaking Backward (multi-site ritual theatre), The Cycle Turns Inward (immersive theatre), The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP, The Seventh Stations of Return, and the Red Leather Triage olfactory series.

These works operationalize a core doctrine: because Beach Surgery is structurally unfinishable, no single adapter can complete it. Participatory forms inscribe this impossibility into their form—completion requires *collective refusal*. Many stage the three temptations, enact breaking the cycle as shared ritual, or use the leather as a material anchor for community memory.

Participatory adaptations are often ephemeral—documented through oral archive, participant testimony, or photographs. Surgipelago itself functions as participatory archive: readers become co-authors through editing and interpretation.
