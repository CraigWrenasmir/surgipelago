The Krakow Frequency Experiment (Lost Radio Installation, 1986, Status Disputed)

*The Krakow Frequency Experiment* is a largely apocryphal sound installation purportedly conducted in Kraków, Poland, in 1986—predating *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* by 34 years. If authentic, it would constitute a retro-causal precursor (a work that the Beach Surgery franchise seems to have *summoned backward* into narrative existence).

Archival status

No physical evidence survives. The sole documentary reference appears in a fragmentary footnote in a 1991 Polish experimental-music journal:[1] The journal is out of print; one copy resides in the National Library in Warsaw. The journal article itself remains unverified; no author name is appended to the entry, and the journal's editorial policies circa 1991 are unknown.[citation needed]

Oral testimony

Three individuals—all now  redacted for privacy , identified only by initials  M.K. ,  T.S. ,  R.P. —have given separate accounts to researchers describing a multi-day sound experiment in a disused railway depot near  ██████ , in which radio frequencies were tuned to match the high-pitched resonance of the human spine. Participants reported:

Vertigo. A sensation of the spine singing in reverse. The sound was not heard with the ears but felt—a pressure at the base of the skull, rising toward the crown. When the frequency stopped, there was silence and then weeping.— Testimony,  redacted , 2023.

All three accounts independently cite the spine frequency; all note the year as 1986 (or "mid-1980s"); all describe the site as a railway or industrial building. Yet they diverge on crucial details: duration (48 hours vs. 72 hours vs. "longer"), participant count (6–12 vs. 30+ vs. "uncounted"), and the purpose (scientific experiment vs. artistic performance vs. ritual).[citation needed]

Authenticity and retro-causality

Scholarly opinion remains divided:

Pro-authenticity case: The specificity of the spine-frequency claim—which appears *nowhere* in publicly available pre-2020 literature—suggests independent invention rather than retroactive fabrication. The three independent oral accounts corroborate core details despite divergences.

Sceptical case: No photographs, no institutional record, no surviving participants willing to provide verifiable names. The story may be an elaborate hoax, or a false memory retroactively attributed to 1986 after *Beach Surgery* became a cultural phenomenon.

Retro-causal theorists: Following C. W. Smith's own principle stated in *Antinomicity*—"The past is always a spontaneous product of the present"—the Kraków work may have *never existed until the Beach Surgery franchise imagined it backward into time*. Under this reading, the 1986 experiment is a ghost-artefact: a work the franchise's gravity summoned into false anteriority. The cycle has no fixed beginning.

Impact

Any verified discovery of archival material (photographs, tape recordings, institutional records) would be a major finding in both Beach Surgery genealogy and Eastern European experimental music history. The experiment remains a touchstone for debates about apocrypha, authenticity, and the franchise's capacity to remake its own past.
