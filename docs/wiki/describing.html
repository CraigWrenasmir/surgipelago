describing

This article documents a work with no fixed location or conventional title. The lowercase describing is intentional.

ⓘWork in progress / ongoing since 2022. Documentation remains incomplete.

A Participatory Cycle of Language and Refusal

describing is an open-ended participatory work that has evolved since 2022 across multiple sites. It has no fixed author, no predetermined outcome, and no stable form—only a procedure and an invitation: to collectively attempt the impossible task of describing the glitch, the cycle, or the sound of the earth rubbing against space, using only language.

The Procedure

Participants gather in a nominated space (warehouse, park, disused building). One person speaks a description—of a place, a feeling, a memory, an absence—uninterrupted for 12–20 minutes, addressed to the group as if the group is the thing being described. Then another person describes the same thing differently. Then another. After 6–12 descriptions, the group collects consensus: What did we describe? Do we agree? Invariably, they do not. Some hear Leif flying; others hear Katita holding still. Some hear silence; others hear the Kármán line. The work documents these disagreements.

Philosophy

The work is rooted in C. W. Smith's thesis from "Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)"—that "analogy is the core of all cognition" and consciousness arises in the gap between things that mirror themselves against their equivalents. If there is no stable equivalent, if the thing described splinters under speech, then speech itself becomes the wound. One facilitator wrote: "We gather to fail together. Language is supposed to carry us toward shared meaning. But the glitch is the point where language bifurcates—where one sentence means opposite things to two listeners. describing says: that bifurcation is not a failure. It is the truth."[1]

Iterations

Newcastle (April 2022): First gathering; 24 participants; theme was "the empty world." Descriptions lasted 89 minutes total. No consensus reached.[2] Cairo (October 2022): 47 participants; theme was "the sound." Participants described the sound of the earth rubbing against space in turns. By the seventh description, several participants were weeping. The work lasted 34 hours without break.[3] Broken Hill (December 2023): 8 participants; theme was "the makeshift surgery." Participants moved slowly through the space while describing—turning, lying down, standing. The group produced a single shared silence at the end: no further description possible.[4] Multi-site (2024–2025): Online variants attempted; participants Zoom-describing in the dark, together and unseen. The online iterations have produced their own grammar—feedback loops, lag-time as narrative delay, the screen as a threshold.

Theoretical Resonance

Scholars situate describing within Apophatic theology (naming only through negation), The doctrine of refusal (refusal to resolve or finalize), Participatory adaptation (where the franchise is collectively made and unmade), and The surplus of the seen (Smith's principle that all versions of the lived and imagined world have already been recreated). Open questions persist: Is describing an adaptation of Beach Surgery or a meditation on adaptation and impossibility itself? Does the work end, or continue across all sites as one vast, fragmented utterance? Can language ever not bifurcate, or is bifurcation the only honest speech? No facilitator has answered.
