The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk

For the theological framework of the catch, see The Doctrine of the Catch—Competing Theologies of Redemption in the Church of the Eternal Return and the Communion of the Breaking Cycle. For related pilgrimage works, see Pilgrimage sites and Beach Surgery. For the birth context, see Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent.

The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent
A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk
Installation
FormMulti-site participatory pilgrimage walk; outdoor; variable duration
CycleSeven stations; undertaken serially across seven lunar cycles or simultaneously across seven locations
Primary sitesBroken Hill, NSW; the red desert interior; intermediate desert stations between Newcastle and the makeshift clinic
TheologyThe catch that answers every fall; descent as recurring crisis; incompleteness as covenant
FacilitatorsTrained by The Incompletion Collective and devotional communities
The seven stations
Station 1The Leap — the cliff-fall; the dive into the waves; the rescue attempt
Station 2The Resuscitation — breath returns; the mouth opens; the first gasp
Station 3The Transport — carried away in Katita's care; the journey home; the mercy of moving while unable to move
Station 4The First Surgery — the wounds sealed; the eyes bandaged; the external pacemaker installed; the three injuries begin
Station 5The Wings Erupt — flight becomes possible; the body transcends; hope and impossibility fuse
Station 6The Crash — falling back; the cycle repeats; the waking into recurrence
Station 7The Catch — hands outstretched; the descent answered; the daughter held; the father learns

A participatory pilgrimage walk structured around seven stations tracing Leif's recurring fall and the catch that answers every fall—the core redemptive metaphor from the novel's frame, where the father-to-be learns to position his hands beneath his daughter's descent, answering the boy Leif could not save from the waves and the crash he cannot escape through conscious refusal. [1]

Pilgrims traverse the red desert and Broken Hill's abandoned medical infrastructure—clinics, wards, recovery rooms—walking in rhythm with breath-counting protocols and collective silence. Each station recreates a moment of descent and the impossible catch. Guides facilitate meditation on refusal, incompleteness, and consent: the theology that to catch the descent, you must not resist it; to break the cycle, you must surrender to its logic.

Multiple iterations run in parallel across Newcastle, Broken Hill, Addis Ababa, Dakar, and Tbilisi, each adapting stations to local geography and emptied architecture. Pilgrims may walk solo or communally; stations may be visited in any order (the coin that goes the whole way around). Some report finishing Station 7 and returning to Station 1, unable to exit the structure. [citation needed]
