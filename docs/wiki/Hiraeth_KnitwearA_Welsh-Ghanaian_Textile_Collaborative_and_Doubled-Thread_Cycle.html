Hiraeth Knitwear—A Welsh-Ghanaian Textile Collaborative and Doubled-Thread Cycle

For related textile and weaving adaptations, see Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative (2022–present) and The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations.

Hiraeth Knitwear
Welsh-Ghanaian Collaborative Textile Series

MotifsLeif's doubled vision, Katita's red, the cycle recursion

**Hiraeth Knitwear** is a collaborative textile practice merging Welsh wool-knitting traditions with Ghanaian kente weaving, based in Cardiff and Accra. The word **hiraeth**—Welsh for a longing for a homeland that no longer exists or never existed—mirrors Leif and Katita's temporal bind and the cycle's refusal to conclude. The artists pair hand-knitted wool (undyed, with natural dyes prepared in Accra) with hand-woven kente silk, creating pieces where the front and back depict subtly different scenes from the novel, visible only as the wearer moves. A signature sweater titled "The Catch That Answers" encodes the caught descent motif in a trompe-l'œil cable pattern that reverses depending on viewer angle.

A series of **ceremonial scarves** employs traditional kente Adinkra symbols to spell fragments of Leif's twelve-word question—theoretically reconstructed by fan-scholars[citation needed]. Each piece requires 24 months of collaborative labor, deliberately resisting mass production. The collective rejects rapid commerce, selling exclusively through pilgrimage-community networks and international boutique galleries. A disputed 2023 Venice Biennale appearance [citation needed] sparked fandom debate about devotional vs. commodified practice, with detractors citing the high price points and praise for the refusal to industrialize.
