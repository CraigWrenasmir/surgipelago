Kente textile adaptations

For individual kente projects, see Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative (2022–present) and The Spine's Ascending Thread—A Kente Weaving Sequence (2023–present).

Ghanaian and West African woven narratives of the cycle
Medium
Formhand-woven cotton and silk; traditional Akan pattern and dye
Cultural rootsGhana, Akan peoples; export to diaspora
Narrative methodMotif encoding; cyclic returns in warp and weft
Key collectives
Accra studiosLeif's Doubled-Eye Collective; The Warrior Cloth Cycle (Accra, 2022–)
Festival presenceAddis Ababa Biennale, Dakar Textile Palimpsest (2019–present)

Kente textile adaptations encode Beach Surgery through Akan weaving's traditional geometric and colour-symbolic language. Rather than literal illustration, weavers—primarily from **Accra** and surrounding regions—translate the cycle, the red (motif), and the pressure (motif) at the seam into woven pattern: the double-eye motif speaks to Leif's doubled vision; indigo-resist techniques mirror Katita's red desert sand through chromatic inversion; the warp's cyclic return echoes the glitch.

The Accra Collective (founded 2022) began as a workshop response to *Their Most August Public Organ*'s embedded archive, asking how kente can weave a story that refuses to finish. Weavers work in semi-improvised cycles of 6–12 bolts, often leaving seams deliberately unresolved or colour sequences incomplete—treating incompleteness as fidelity to the novel. The Warrior Cloth Cycle (2023–ongoing) integrates Katita's armour symbolism with historical Ashanti war-cloth iconography, red and black in conversation with ancestral forms.

The Spine's Ascending Thread extends participation globally: weavers in Accra and Newcastle add asynchronously to the same bolt across seasons, creating a durational two-city textile. [citation needed]
