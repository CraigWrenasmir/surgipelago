The Białystok Reversal (Lost 16mm Installation, 1987)

The Białystok Reversal is a contested pre-2020 installation documented only through a single eyewitness account, archival photographs (largely redacted), and a recovered 16mm film reel of disputed attribution. In 1987, an experimental architect and filmmaker (identity unverified) installed a six-room cycle in an abandoned rail depot outside Białystok. Each chamber housed a wooden structure with 16mm projections looping on interior walls. The work was dismantled in 1989 following the creator's death; its existence remained unknown outside Warsaw underground circles until 2019, when the Polish Film Institute acquired an unmarked reel labeled Bez Tytułu (Untitled).

When projected, the recovered reel shows corrupted optical footage: static, blurred human figures, hands submerged in water, architectural shadows. Its frame-count (92 minutes) and compositional language match descriptions in the Głos Bez Granic account of "six chambers, each showing the descent, the immersion, the return."[1] Beach Surgery scholars debate fiercely whether this work constitutes a genuine precursor summoned backward by the novel, or an unrelated film coincidentally thematic. The reel's heavy redaction and the creator's anonymity preclude confirmation.
