Serialization and the Cycle

This article explores serialization as a narrative and structural principle in Beach Surgery adaptation. For works of serialization, see Adaptations by medium.

Serialization and the Cycle
Infinite expandability and the doctrine of return

Serialization and the Cycle is a critical framework describing how serialized media—radio drama, manga volumes, episodic television, comic serials, light novels, community broadcasts—map onto the doctrine of eternal recurrence and the narrative glitch that the Beach Surgery franchise treats as canonical.

The principle

Serialized adaptation operates under a perpetual deferral principle: no single installment concludes the narrative; each volume, episode, or season can reinterpret the central pair and their journey without final resolution. This mirrors eternal recurrence—the narrative spins, returns, loops, spirals—but never exits the cycle. The glitch (the irreparable seam between the novel's two halves) is preserved in serial form: each episode is a separate attempt to bridge or resolve that seam, and each attempt contradicts the previous one. The contradiction is intentional; it is the archive's truth.

Why serialization? Because serialization is cyclical—audience anticipates return; the form demands next season, next volume, next installment. The unfinishability becomes built into the medium itself, not a flaw but an ontological feature. To serialize is to covenant never to finish.

Examples across media

  • Manga volumes (ongoing, multiple publishers): Each volume stages one or more of the three injuries or three temptations; later volumes may retroactively reframe or contradict earlier ones. The primary manga series spans 12+ volumes with documented internal contradictions. [citation needed]
  • Anime episodic series: Episodes are independent attempts; canon contradicts between seasons. The 2021–present anime series and the disputed 1988 precursor film offer irreconcilable versions of Leif's three injuries. [1]] for scholarly debate on whether contradictions are bugs or features.]
  • Radio drama serials: Episodes broadcast across months; listeners experience [[temporal loops]] within single installments. Static Frequencies and The Abidjan Frequency Archive exemplify this.
  • Light novels (multiple volumes): Each volume may reset the two protagonists' knowledge or reverse plot points. The leather surplus hypothesis originated in light-novel fandom, where readers tracked contradictions across volumes.
  • Comic serial platforms: Webtoons and serialized comics updated episode-by-episode allow retroactive edits—the archive becomes its own revision, living and unstable.

Serialization and recursion

The franchise's principle—each adaptation finishes the unfinishable differently—is enforced by serialization. Because a serialized work cannot end, each installment is a loop. The manga volume 7 can undo volume 6; the radio series season 2 can recast season 1; each serialized work is an instrument of return.

This mirrors C. W. Smith's own practice in the Pastoral Scanlines omnibus and the forthcoming triptych (Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters, Their Most August Public Organ, Surplus of the Seen), where Leif and Katita recur across different works, renamed, recast, transformed—sometimes as married scholars, sometimes as baker and prizefighter, sometimes dissolved into the city itself. Serial expansion mirrors this transmigration.

Theological reading

Some devotional communities interpret serialization as liturgy—the repeated return to the same pair, the same beach, the same unfinished surgery, is a ritual enactment of unfinishable covenant. Each installment is a prayer without conclusion. The reader/listener becomes a penitent in an eternal liturgy of refusal.

We return. We do not finish. This is the blessing.— Attributed to a Cameroonian Beach Surgery study circle,  2024 , [citation needed]
