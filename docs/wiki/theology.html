theology

This article describes theology as it emerges across Beach Surgery adaptations and interpretive communities. For specific devotional movements, see Church of the Eternal Return and Communion of the Breaking Cycle. For the philosophical ground, see Subject (Ontological Incompleteness).

Theology in Beach Surgery
Core Theological Framework
Foundational principleSacred incompleteness, the doctrine of refusal, eternal recurrence
Central metaphorSurgery as sacrament, covenant, and erotic care
Source textA Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (2020)
Theological lineageDostoevsky, Beckett, apophatic theology, Eastern Orthodox, Sufi, Buddhist, and postcolonial frameworks
Key interpretive textsThe Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness, The Surgery as Sacrament

Theology across Beach Surgery adaptations rests on a single principle: incompleteness is sacred, and refusal is covenant. The narrative itself—divided into two halves that do not join, riddled with the glitch—becomes theology itself. Each adaptation attempts to finish the unfinishable, resolve the irresoluble; each finishes it differently. This contradiction is not a failure of meaning but its very foundation.

The three temptations (miracle, mystery, and authority) structure an apophatic theology drawn equally from Dostoevsky's *Grand Inquisitor*, Beckett's doctrine of refusal, and non-Western frameworks of cyclical time. Leif cannot finally refuse the seductions of being borne up, saved, governed. He succumbs each loop. The theology here argues that breaking the cycle is not triumph but acceptance of its necessity. Surgery becomes sacrament not because it heals but because it wounds and cares simultaneously—the touch that cuts.

Global devotional communities—Church of the Eternal Return, Communion of the Breaking Cycle—have crystallized this: the unfinished is the sacred. The glitch is where speech becomes silence, where God speaks through the seam.
