Varanasi gharana

The **Varanasi gharana** is a classical Hindustani vocal school emphasizing introspection, restraint, and the slow unfurling of rasa (emotional resonance) through the alap section of khayal. The tradition prizes the singer's inner listening—a quality called antarmukhi (inward-turned)—over virtuosic display.

For Beach Surgery adapters, the Varanasi gharana offers a natural bridge to Katita's thesis: that the human spine resonates at a high pitch (high G for women) and that this frequency might harmonize with the Kármán line if the earth's rotation reversed. Several Varanasi-based artists have developed sustained khayal performances treating the spine's note as a devotional anchor, exploring the boundary between the singer's breath and the cosmos's sound of the earth rubbing against space.

Varanasi adaptations

  • **The Kirmani Frequency (2021–2023)**: A three-year vocal-meditation cycle by  ████  in Benares, integrating raag Kafi (associated with longing and cycles) with extended alap breathing patterns mapped to Leif's doubled vision and the frequency-correction of the radio igloo.
  • **Raag and the Eternal Return (2022)**: A symposium hosted at the Banaras Hindu University examining raga structure as a model of eternal return—each alap an attempted escape from the previous one, each descent into the raga a loop.

The Varanasi gharana's pedagogical emphasis on shuddha-bhakti (pure devotion) and the concept of om as an unsoundable first sound align with apophatic theology in Beach Surgery readings, where the glitch resists semantic closure just as the ultimate frequency refuses to resolve.
