The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior (2022–present)

This article describes a pilgrimage route. For the underlying theological concept, see the caught descent, fatherhood, birth and the caught descent, and Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return.

A seven-day pilgrimage walk created by the Caught Descent Collective (Broken Hill, 2022) treating the red desert interior as a **sacred topography**—a landscape where the recurring pair's story becomes a pathway for reflection on fatherhood, care, refusal, and the catch that answers every fall.

Each station corresponds to a narrative moment or motif from Half Two. At the **Service Station** (boundary), participants meditate on identity slippage. At the **Radio Igloo**, they sit in silence listening for the Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear. The **Watering Hole** invites witness-testimony. At the **Cabin**, participants craft leather talismans while discussing armor and vulnerability. The **Rocket Cart Launch** overlooks the crash-site; many inscribe their own version of the twelve-word question on stones. The **Field of Wild Dogs** offers collective meditative walking. The final station, **Mouth of the City**, closes with pilgrims writing letters to their own future children, grounding the cycle in intergenerational care.

Guides are trained in the Listening Stone Protocol and redemptive interpretation. Since 2022, over  ████  pilgrims have completed the walk; anecdotal reports describe profound spiritual experience. The Collective resists commercialization; the walk remains free.

Anthropologist Dr. Sarah Chen (2023) examined whether the tour constitutes devotional praxis or "commodified spiritual tourism"; the Collective responded that sincerity and structure are not mutually exclusive.
