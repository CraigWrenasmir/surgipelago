Samsara and the Cycle

The concept of samsara—the wheel of cyclic suffering and rebirth—maps directly onto Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence. Katita's determination to break the cycle reads as a pursuit of moksha (liberation from the wheel). Each new adaptation becomes an attempt to exit the eternal return; each fails, or spirals inward. “The cycle is not punishment. It is the condition of forgetting that we are already free.”

Maya, Atman, and the Split Self

The Hindu concept of maya (the illusion obscuring Brahman) illuminates Leif and Katita as aspects of a single consciousness temporarily fractured by narrative. Several South Asian theses argue they are not two lovers but a single atman experiencing the illusion of separation—and the glitch is the point at which maya fails and the illusion threatens to collapse.

The Pacemaker as False Chakra

Leif's external pacemaker—governing the body's rhythm from outside—functions as an inverted chakra system. Its removal is a yogic unblocking; the pressure in his shoulder-blades (preceding the wings) mirrors the opening of the anahata (heart chakra).

Bhakti and Devotion

Leif's love, described as "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways," parallels bhakti—devotion so complete it erases the boundary between lover and beloved, subject and object.

Tantra and Sacred Wounding

Tantric theology's embrace of the body, the wound, and non-duality informs adaptations treating surgery as an erotic sacrament. The wound becomes a tantric threshold, neither healing nor worsening but eternally open.
