Theses and papers

This article indexes academic scholarship on Beach Surgery. For fan theories, see Fan theories. For critical reception, see Cultural impact of Beach Surgery.

Academic scholarship on Beach Surgery spans multiple disciplines. The novel's structural unfinishability—the glitch, eternal recurrence, transmigrating protagonists—has attracted scholarship in theology, ontology, media studies, and disability studies. Many theses remain dispersed across university repositories, institutional archives, and community research networks [citation needed].

Theological and philosophical work

Sacred incompleteness and the doctrine of refusal have generated significant theological scholarship, particularly on apophatic theology and the Dostoevskian temptation framework. Key works include The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness — Unfinishability as Covenant and Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Ethics (MA Thesis, 2024), essays on Beckett's apophatic refusal, and studies of the glitch as sacred wound.

Narrative and media studies

Scholarship on adaptation, glitch as hermeneutic necessity, cross-linguistic adaptation, and the encyclopedic project as itself an adaptation includes Operational Impossibilities: Surgical Metaphor and the Limits of Narrative Integration in Beach Surgery Across Media and Untranslatable Surgery: Language, Analogy, and the Necessity of Contradiction Across Global Beach Surgery Adaptations.

Postcolonial, regional, and indigenous scholarship

Work grounding Beach Surgery in postcolonial contexts, magical realism, First Nations forms, and regional traditions includes The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis (MA Thesis, 2024) and research on Ethiopian, Colombian, and Aboriginal engagements.

Disability and care ethics

Scholarship on Leif's three temporary injuries, medical metaphor, care and consent, and disability representation has grown with adaptations. The Surgical Caress: Intimacy, Vulnerability, and the Recurring Embrace in Australian and Global Beach Surgery Adaptations and The Tender Surgery (PhD Dissertation, 2023) are landmark studies.
