Guinea has emerged as a primary node in griotic and textile-based Beach Surgery adaptation. Its position at the intersection of Saharan trade routes and Atlantic coastal regions echoes the novel's own tension between urban harbour and desert interior. The kora tradition, central to Guinean and broader Manding griotic culture, has become the primary instrument through which several adaptation cycles structure eternal recurrence and refusal.

Conakry serves as a hub for participatory olfactory archive projects, where griots and textile artists collaborate to embed Kármán-line resonance into traditional kente weaving and incense practice. The interior regions (Fouta Djallon, Kindia) host durational performance works that map Leif and Katita as revenants of ancestor-pairs whose stories are perpetually re-told, never finished — a theological echo of the glitch.

Guinea's adaptations are distinguished by their insistence on oral and textile simultaneity: narrative cannot be separated from the thread, the instrument's string, the voice.
