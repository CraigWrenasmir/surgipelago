Bharatanatyam adaptations of Beach Surgery

For other Indian classical dance forms, see Indian classical dance adaptations. For the three temptations as rasic modes, see Leif's Three Injuries as Rasic Modes.

Bharatanatyam, the South Indian classical dance form, has generated the richest and most formally inventive cycle of Beach Surgery stage works. Unlike narrative retellings, Bharatanatyam adaptations treat Leif's three injuries and Katita's red motif as rasic modes—emotional and philosophical states embodied through gesture, gait, and rhythm grounded in rasa theory.

Key adaptations include:

  • Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries (Jaipur, 2018–2021) — stages each of Leif's temporary injuries (cannot see, cannot walk, heart out of whack) as three full-length Bharatanatyam pieces, each concluding unresolved.
  • The Shoulder-Blade Spiral (Accra, 2023–present) — collaborative Bharatanatyam-Kente textile cycle; dancers wear woven armor mimicking Katita's leather suit, improvising within the form's lexicon.
  • The Catch That Answers Every Fall (2022) — durational piece treating the final flight and fall as a single sustained mudra, never resolving into stillness.

Scholarly work by Asha Mohan traces how the form's rasa vocabulary (vira, raudra, shanta) maps onto the three temptations and refusal doctrine. [citation needed]
