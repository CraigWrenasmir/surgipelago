Kyoto adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article catalogs Beach Surgery-related artworks created in or centred on Kyoto, Japan (2021–present). For the philosophical framework these works share, see Ma (negative space) and Beach Surgery.

Kyoto adaptations of Beach Surgery
Scope
RegionKyoto, Japan
Period2021–present
FormsInstallation, theatre, audio work, architectural intervention, temple-based practice
Estimated works8 major; 20+ smaller projects [citation needed]
Key venuesKyoto Art Center, Gion Corner, Philosopher's Walk, Shoren-in Temple, independent galleries

The **Kyoto adaptation cluster** comprises works created between 2021 and present by artists drawn to the city's particular geography, silence-as-aesthetic, and the Japanese concept of ma (negative space) as foundational to meaning-making. Rather than narrating Beach Surgery or resolving the glitch, these works explore incompleteness itself as subject, using Kyoto's layered architecture, river confluences, and temporal depth as ready-made frameworks for thinking ontological refusal. [citation needed]

Major works

Ma no Kaidan (2022–ongoing) — Durational sound installation within Shoren-in Temple gardens, activating water channels with binaural frequencies tuned to the human spine's resonance. Visitors walk barefoot through water while listening (bone-conduction headphones) to a 24-hour looped composition phasing between silence and sustained tone. Offered quarterly (autumn equinox, 9 days). Commissioned by the temple; purpose ambiguous. [citation needed]

Hedgehog's Daughter: A Red Meridian Path (2023–2024) — Walking/audio-locative work (7 km, central Kyoto: Philosopher's Walk to Gion) with 13 GPS-triggered audio segments exploring the Kármán line as boundary between earth and space, mapped onto Kyoto's vertical layering (visible temples, hidden subterranean shrines, the "unseen city beneath"). Creator:  ██████ ; archived at Kyoto Art Center.

Katita's Garden: Seven Revisions (2021–2022) — Institutional exhibition at the Kyoto Museum of Modern Art: seven Japanese-language essays and a single photograph (Red Dust Beneath Frost, subject deliberately unidentifiable) exploring the dust garden (Chapter 1) as both Zen garden practice and metaphor for narrative incompleteness. [citation needed]

The Seam Between Halves: A Noh Fragment (2024) — Experimental Noh-influenced theatre (40 minutes, Kyoto Art Center), treating Half One to Half Two transition as spatial-temporal threshold. Traditional masks; contemporary movement. Cast:  ██ ; director: Keiji Matsumoto. Single performance; no recording authorised.

Smaller projects — Approximately 20 additional works (photography, sound installation proposals for temple bell frequencies, collaborative zines on Kyoto's own ontological incompleteness). Many undocumented or known only as rumour. [citation needed]

Aesthetic alignment

Kyoto artists gravitate toward Beach Surgery because the city embodies the novel's core preoccupations: ontological incompleteness (buildings that vanish from records, streets that become unnamed); ma as both principle and lived practice; and the concept of returning (pilgrimage, ritual, seasonal recurrence). The city functions as ready-made empty world meditation space—depopulated (off-season), full of ghosts (historical presences, temple tourism).

Unlike Japanese adaptations elsewhere (Tokyo anime/manga, regional Butoh), the Kyoto cluster treats incompleteness itself as subject rather than pursuing glitch-resolution.
