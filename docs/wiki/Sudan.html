Sudan

This article surveys Beach Surgery adaptations originating in Sudan and the Sudanese griotic diaspora. For related oral traditions, see Sudanese oral tradition and Griot oral epics and Beach Surgery.

Sudan enters Beach Surgery fandom primarily through griotic and oral-poetic retellings, particularly those centered on the Nile as landscape of cyclical return and rupture. Sudanese griots (narrative historians) have adapted the novel's protagonists into figures of malakat (sacred refusal)—a tradition wherein a storyteller holds narrative truth in tension rather than resolving it.

The earliest documented work is oral: a griot named  ██  recorded a cycle of call-and-response tellings across three seasons in the early 1990s, circulated on cassette in Port Sudan and Khartoum before disappearing from archival trace. A fragment surfaces in The Mogadishu Breath Cycle (1994), recorded across the Sudan-Somalia border, wherein Leif and Katita become two rivers—one pushing upstream, one down—that never meet in the same place twice. Field recording notes mention tree-knots along the convergence valley broadcasting faint harmonic tones at dawn, source undetermined. [1]

Contemporary work includes ''Gorad Ndoolus'' (2021–ongoing), a participatory scent and oral archive in Khartoum along the Blue Nile. Sudanese diaspora communities in Addis Ababa and Cairo have adapted the novel into spoken-word cycles paired with recordings of “the sound of the earth rubbing against space”.
