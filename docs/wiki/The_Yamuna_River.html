The Yamuna River

This article covers the river's role in Beach Surgery adaptation and pilgrimage. For Hindu sacred geography, consult external sources.

The Yamuna River
Sacred waterway in northern India
Geographic metadata

Beach Surgery rolepilgrimage site; water-metaphor for the cycle

A major river in northern India sacred to Hindu theology, the Yamuna flows ~1,376 km from the Himalayas through Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, Agra, and Mathura, joining the Ganges River at Prayagraj. In Hindu tradition it is personified as a goddess—the daughter of the sun god Surya—and its waters carry purifying and redemptive power. The river is the childhood home of Krishna in Bhakti tradition.

Role in Beach Surgery Adaptation

Since 2021, the Yamuna has become a primary site for pilgrimage and meditative ritual in Indian adaptations. The river's water-metaphor aligns with the novel's themes of eternal recurrence and the drowning-and-rescue of Leif's backstory (the boy taken by waves, Leif's rescue dive). Multiple participatory works frame the Yamuna as the embodied counterpart to the underground pool of Chapter 3.

The 2022 durational work The Yamuna Frequency Walk (directed by  ████████ ) led groups along the river from dawn to dusk, synchronized breathing with the current—an Empty World Meditation adapted to the river's tidal geography. Walkers concluded at Mathura (site of Krishna's birth, a spatial echo of the novel's Newcastle-Shanbudia binary) with a silent vigil mirroring Katita's dust garden scene.

Scholarly works increasingly read Leif's devotion to Katita through bhakti theology, using the Yamuna's devotional landscape to reframe the novel's erotic and care-ethical core as rasa-inflected surrender rather than manipulation.
