The Anxiety of Becoming—Carson, Murnane, and the Genealogy of the Unfinished Sentence in Beach Surgery

Unpublished thesis, June 2025. Author identity and institution held in archive per contributor request.

FocusLiterary genealogy; anxiety of influence and formal refusal

This thesis traces the literary genealogy of Beach Surgery's formal refusal through three primary influences: Anne Carson's fragmented erotics, Gerald Murnane's poetics of the interior, and Laszlo Krasznahorkai's apocalyptic long-sentence. Rather than reading these as sources, it argues they form a poetic anxiety of influence (Bloom) *within* Smith's work itself: each text struggles against and with its precursors, creating the glitch as a generative site of literary contestation and incompleteness.

Part I examines Carson's *Eros the Bittersweet* as a model for surgery-as-knowledge: how the wound opens knowing. Part II situates Murnane's *The Plains* and its meditation on the unrepeatable inland image alongside Smith's cartographies of Shanbudia and Newcastle. Part III reads Krasznahorkai's breath-sentence through the unjoined sentence as syntactic refusal.

The thesis concludes: the cycle is not structural accident but formal inheritance. Smith completes himself by remaining incomplete, becoming-Carson-and-Murnane-and-Krasznahorkai without synthesis or transcendence. The glitch is genealogical.
