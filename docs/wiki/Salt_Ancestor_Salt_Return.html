Salt Ancestor, Salt Return

This article concerns the Samoan olfactory mourning ritual and LARP (2023–). For Pacific adaptations, see Pacific and Indigenous adaptations of Beach Surgery; for ritual forms, see Ritual and ceremonial adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Salt Ancestor, Salt Return is a Samoan-based participatory olfactory mourning ritual and LARP series created by  ──────────────────────  and a Samoan community collective, grounding Leif's backstory—his dive from a cliff to save a boy taken by waves, his near-drowning, and Katita's rescue—in Pacific island grief practices. The work unfolds annually across five phases, inviting participants to taste, smell, and sing salt as a medium of loss, return, and refusal.

The annual cycle

  • **Phase One: Salt Gathering** — Participants wade into the Pacific at specific tidal moments and harvest sea-salt by hand, no tools, using cloth and skin only. Salt dries in the sun for three days. Previous years' salt accumulates on community altars, visible but untouched—archaeological evidence of the cycle.
  • **Phase Two: The Frangipani Lament** — Fresh frangipani flowers (white and pink plumeria, sacred in Samoan tradition) are placed in cloth pouches with dried salt. Participants hold the pouches, remember losses to the sea. Traditional fa'alupega (genealogical chants) are sung but modified: participants replace names with salt, moana (ocean), i ai (it was there). Genealogy becomes abstracted, broken, reformed.
  • **Phase Three: The Breath Exchange** — In darkness, participants exchange salt-infused cloth parcels. They are not told who they exchange with. A participant inhales the salt, frangipani, the sweat of a stranger, then places cloth in the next person's hands. Parcels circulate repeatedly; scents mingle, histories entangle. Duration: 45 minutes to 2 hours.
  • **Phase Four: The Unasking** — Participants pair, facing each other in near-darkness. One smells salt; the other frangipani. Scents periodically swap. This phase enacts Leif's twelve-word question as olfactory impossibility: Can I ask if you are the one I lost? If the ocean brought you back? If this salt is you? No words are exchanged; the question hangs unasked. Duration: 30 minutes per pair, continuing through the night.
  • **Phase Five: Salt Return** — At dawn, all harvested salt—current year and all prior years—is walked in silent procession back to the sea. Salt is released into waves. Frangipani petals scattered. No words spoken. Accumulated memory returns to the ocean.

Integration with Beach Surgery

The work explicitly stages Leif's dive and Katita's rescue: participants described "smelling the moment before the catch, the fall suspended between drowning and being held." Salt encodes both loss (the boys and girls lost to the Pacific over centuries of colonialism and climate displacement) and the impossible return, where each cycle-gathering-lament-exchange-return refuses to accept loss as final.

The work also engages the three temptations: salt is the mystery (you cannot see who you exchange with), frangipani is the miracle (delicate beauty despite), the ocean is the authority to which all returns are owed.

Criticisms and controversies

Some Samoan cultural practitioners have raised concerns about appropriation of sacred fa'alupega in service of a global franchise, particularly one centered on non-Samoan narrative. The project-team responds that all creative decisions are made by Samoan community members, and the work is a deliberate strategic appropriation of Beach Surgery into Samoan practice—a "taking back" of narrative authority. [citation needed]

Environmental advocates have questioned sustainability of repeated salt harvesting on beaches stressed by sea-level rise. The team is exploring transition to synthetically produced salt or marine-derived alternatives. [citation needed]
