The Surgery as Sacrament — Rites of Incompleteness, Care, and the Unfinished in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities

This is an academic thesis. For a response, see the counter-thesis by Pär Nyström (2024).

The Surgery as Sacrament
Rites of Incompleteness, Care, and the Unfinished in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities
Thesis metadata
AuthorDr. Arundhati Chakraborty

Year2023

AdviserProf.  ██  [citation needed]
Key fieldReligious studies, literary theology, global fandom

A landmark PhD dissertation examining how Beach Surgery fan-communities across South Asia, West Africa, and the diaspora have **constructed sacramental practices and religious liturgies** around C. W. Smith's novel, treating the surgery itself as a sacred rite and the glitch as a **theological mystery of incompleteness**. Chakraborty documents feast-day calendars (vigils on the "first breath" date; celebrations of The Catch That Answers Every Fall), healing rituals that re-enact Katita's triage as a sacrament of care, and **funeral rites that read Chapter 6** aloud over the dying. The thesis argues Beach Surgery functions as a **neo-scriptural text** offering ethical instruction in refusal, incompleteness, and what Chakraborty terms "the sacrament of the unfinished"—a counter to Western teleological healing narratives. Particularly rich case studies of the Indian Christian Beach Surgery reading communities and Baul musicians who sing Leif & Katita as instruments of return. The dissertation is respectful of Hindu, Christian, Muslim and Buddhist interpretive frameworks and explicitly addresses debates over appropriation when Beach Surgery devotees borrow liturgical structures from established faiths [1]—concluding that sincere reinterpretation is not theft.
