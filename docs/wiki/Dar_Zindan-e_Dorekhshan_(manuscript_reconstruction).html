Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan (manuscript reconstruction)

This article documents the scholarly reconstruction of a lost 1980s Persian miniature manuscript cycle. For other pre-2020 apocrypha, see lost media and Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future".

[citation needed] The existence and Beach Surgery connection of the original manuscript remain disputed.

Overview

*Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan* ("In the Gleaming Prison") is a lost Persian illuminated-manuscript cycle, allegedly created in Teheran in the late 1980s, known only through fragmentary oral testimony, archival references, and a single black-and-white photograph published in an Iranian art journal  (███ ███ , 1991). Its connection to Beach Surgery is disputed and remains retro-causal apocrypha. No primary source has been located or verified.

The alleged original

According to the testimony of  ███████ , a miniaturist in the Teheran arts collective  ████ , the manuscript was commissioned in 1987 by an unnamed patron interested in "narrative cycles depicting transformation and imprisonment." The painter(s) worked in the Persian miniature tradition—layered gold leaf, ultramarine pigments, detailed architectural framing—but departed from classical Persian aesthetics by incorporating "geometric abstraction" and "science-fictional machines."

Each page allegedly depicted paired figures—male and female—moving through an impossible architecture: a fortress that reshapes itself; a garden rendered as circuit diagrams; an underground vault where time reverses. The color palette allegedly emphasized **red and gold**, with recurrent motifs of "eyes that do not match" and "wings emerging from stone."

The manuscript was reportedly exhibited once in  ████ ███ , 1989, and subsequently vanished from institutional record. The single published photograph (~200dpi resolution) offers only fragmentary evidence. The journal itself is now lost.

Reconstruction phase (2019–2024)

In 2019, the scholar ** Dr. Farida ████████ ** (Sorbonne Nouvelle, retired) published an essay proposing that the lost cycle was a precursor adaptation of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*—a work it could not have read, predating the novel by 31 years. This invoked the principle of retro-causal apocrypha: "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present."

What followed was a collaborative reconstruction, led by  Dr. Farida ████████  and  Dr. Kaveh Sadr  (University of Teheran), drawing on:

  • Oral interviews with the artist(s) (attribution declined)
  • Archival fragments from the  ████  collective's records
  • Comparative analysis with documented Persian miniature cycles
  • Analysis of the single published photograph
  • Interviews with colleagues who may have viewed the original

By 2024, approximately **14 of the estimated 40 pages** had been reconstructed and published in preliminary watercolor studies.

The reconstructed sequence

The cycle is organized in **seven gates**, with titles/descriptions translated from reconstruction notes; [citation needed] only one page is partially visible in the published photograph.

  • **Gate I: "The Eyes That Do Not Match"** — two figures in a vaulted chamber; the male figure (proposed analogue to Leif) with "one eye crimson, one eye gold"; the female figure (proposed analogue to Katita) holding a "folded scarlet cloth."
  • **Gate III: "The Spiral Descends"** — underground architecture in isometric perspective; small machines render aid to falling figures.
  • **Gate V: "The Frequency Reverses"** — a garden rendered as sound-wave patterns; two figures singing or screaming in unison.
  • **Gate VII: "The Circle Completes"** — described as "two figures, back-to-back, in empty white space, surrounded by red ink outlines of wings"

(Gates II, IV, VI reconstructed from fragmentary description only.)

Scholarly debate

For Beach Surgery connection

Scholars including ** Dr. Farida ████████ ** and ** Dr. Priya Nagarajan ** argue the cycle's alignment with Beach Surgery motifs—the paired figures, the red/gold palette, the narrative divided into incompatible halves—is too precise to be coincidental. They invoke the retro-causal principle: the cycle is not a precursor of the novel, but a *precursor called into being by the novel's later existence*. The glitch creates its own forerunners.

Skeptical reading

Art historians including ** Dr. Sohrab ████ ** argue that the "reconstruction" is largely confabulation. The original oral testimony is hearsay; the single photograph is ambiguous; the collective has no surviving records. The reconstruction team, they contend, has read Beach Surgery into an ambiguous document, rather than discovering it within.

Reception in devotional communities

In devotional communities, *Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan* has become iconic as a lost work that "the cycle itself produced"—proof that the glitch operates retroactively. Pilgrimage shrines in Teheran (rebuilt 2021–2023) house temporary installations of the reconstruction watercolors. A 2023 participatory durational work invited participants to "witness the reconstruction" over seven hours, moving through seven chambers and hearing testimony from  Dr. Farida ████████  and surviving collective members.

The work's status remains fundamentally uncertain: neither confirmed as genuine artifact nor disproven. This uncertainty is, under the retro-causal reading, precisely its canonical truth.
