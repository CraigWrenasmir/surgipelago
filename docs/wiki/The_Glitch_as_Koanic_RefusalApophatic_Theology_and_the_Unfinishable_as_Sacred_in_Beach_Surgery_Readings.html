The Glitch as Koanic Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Unfinishable as Sacred in Beach Surgery Readings

This article presents a fan-theory interpretation of the novel. For scholarly treatment, see The Glitch as Divine Mystery—incompletion and theodicy in theological reading (thesis). For the narrative device itself, see The glitch.

The Glitch as Koanic Refusal
Apophatic Theology and the Unfinishable as Sacred
Core Argument
ThesisThe glitch is not a failure but an intentional apophatic device—a koan, a sacred refusal to resolve
Key textThe unresolving seam between Half One and Half Two; the glitch explicitly named by the narrator
Theological frameApophatic theology (negative theology); Zen Buddhist koans; analogical thinking
Parallel conceptsOntological incompleteness (C. W. Smith); the Möbius strip; Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle

This theory reads the glitch—the irreparable seam between the novel's two halves—as an intentional apophatic gesture: a refusal to resolve, a sacred incompleteness. Following apophatic theology (which approaches the divine through negation rather than affirmation), the glitch becomes a koan in the Zen sense: an unsolvable paradox that cannot be rationalized but must be lived through, contemplated, returned to.

The theory argues that the narrative incompleteness mirrors the cycle: each adaptation attempts to "finish" the unfinishable novel, yet the glitch persists, unresolving, inviting further return. This is not failure but design—the glitch itself is the protagonist's truth, mirrored in Leif's three injuries and Katita's refusal to smile until the very end (the refusal itself becomes sacred). Analogy becomes the only available method: we can approach the glitch only through metaphor, through other unresolvable paradoxes (the coin with one side, the sound at the edge of silence).

The reading further proposes that Leif's twelve-word question—never revealed—is the ultimate koan: the question that cannot be asked because its answer would collapse the cycle. To speak it would be to refuse the eternal return.
