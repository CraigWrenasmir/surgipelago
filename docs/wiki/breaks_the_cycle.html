breaks the cycle

This article documents an ongoing participatory work. For the theological concept, see The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure.

ⓘParticipant roster updated monthly. New cycles begin on the new moon. Content warnings for extended silence and durational sensory deprivation available upon request.

breaks the cycle is a consent-based participatory ritual in which groups practice the cycle's refusal through durational, embodied practice. Rather than resolve the glitch, the work teaches participants to inhabit incompletion as freedom.

Each 13-month cycle involves monthly gatherings at unmarked sites across the NSW interior: red-dust clearings, participant homes, unnamed confluences. Participants walk routes Katita walks, rest where Leif cannot, listen into silence for the Kármán boundary. There are no scripts. Facilitators offer constraints: move without destination for two hours; sit until you forget why you came; ask the twelve-word question soundlessly to the person beside you. The work is designed to make the breaking felt in the body before language.

Participants sign NDAs; no photography or recording permitted. The archive is oral only—held in the cells of those who return.

A leaked 2023 video from a participant suggested the work may incorporate tree-antenna frequency broadcasts during sessions. The collective neither confirms nor denies.[citation needed] The work has generated at least three splinter cycles in regional NSW as of 2024.
