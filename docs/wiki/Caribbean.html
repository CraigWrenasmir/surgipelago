Caribbean

This article surveys Beach Surgery adaptations across Caribbean cultures. For specific productions, see Ciclo Rojo and The Five Cowries (Audio Serial).

Beach Surgery has surfaced across the Caribbean primarily through music, carnival ritual, and travelling oral-story cycles, often as grassroots, communal adaptations that resist fixed narrative in favour of live variation and collective reinterpretation. The three injuries read naturally as trials within oral epic — a wanderer's journey of blindness, immobility, and heart-loss across an archipelago separated by water yet spiritually continuous.

The Ciclo Rojo tradition (Trinidad, originating 2011) embedded Leif and Katita into Carnival procession narratives, with masked dancers embodying cycle-recurrence through Jonkonnu-derived performance. Singers improvised in calypso and rapso around the twin themes of mechanical heartbeat and surgical precision — "the mechanic knows the engineer's grief" — turning medical imagery into social critique and love-song. The adaptation's core ambiguity ([citation needed]) — whether Katita engineers Leif's devotion or liberates him from it — was deliberately staged as a live debate between rival kaiso troupes, unresolvable by design.

''The Five Cowries'' community radio serial (Jamaica, 2009–12) used Creole storytelling and dancehall rhythm to spread the narrative across rural parishes, with listeners calling in to propose how "the surgery" might end. Each week the presenter (grounded in local oral-tradition) would integrate caller suggestions into the next episode, making the glitch an open, breathing wound in the live archive. The series proved generative enough to be cited in theses on participatory narrative and cyclical storytelling in diasporic communities.
