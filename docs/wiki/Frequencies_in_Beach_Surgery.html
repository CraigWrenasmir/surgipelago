Frequencies in Beach Surgery

This article traces frequency as a structural and philosophical motif. For sound-design adaptations, see Audio drama adaptations.

Frequency—the rate at which something oscillates or repeats—is foundational to Beach Surgery's metaphysics and its unresolvable glitch.

The novel's central sound-motif is the sound of the earth rubbing against space: a low, nauseating frequency at the edge of silence, occurring at the Kármán line (100 km altitude, the atmosphere's boundary). This frequency is never heard directly; Katita names it but cannot prove it. The novel's counter-frequency is the human spine's resonance: a high D for male singers, high G for female. Katita's occult theory posits that if the earth's rotation reversed, the screech of its braking would match the spine's pitch exactly—and the cycle would break.

In the novel's halfway point, at the radio igloo, raising the broadcast frequency to a pitch the autonomous data-harvesters cannot hear has the side effect of "correcting" Leif's doubled vision. Frequency, thus, is simultaneously physical (sound waves, electromagnetic) and perceptual (what the ear can resolve, what the eye can see). The glitch itself is described by some scholars as a frequency mismatch between the two halves: they are pitched at incompatible frequencies and cannot be joined.

Adaptations built around radio and audio drama exploit this richly. Several lost radio works and pirate broadcasts (the  Kano Frequency archive ; the  Dakar Frequency Remains ) are conjectured to have been attempts to map the boundary-frequency itself, though no recordings survive. An unmapped frequency catalogue was recovered in 2024, marked "property of earlier archive, location unknown," but remains unindexed [citation needed]. Baul adaptations make frequency meditation central to devotional practice.
