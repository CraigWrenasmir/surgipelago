a contented smile

This article discusses a motif from the embedded tale of Rico the Architect. For the tale itself, see Rico the Architect.

a contented smile
Source
Appears inRico the Architect (embedded tale, Chapter 2)
ContextMylar's impossible surgery on Rico
Visual form“Windows glow crimson; the doorway warps into a contented smile”
Interpretations
SymbolismArchitectural redemption; acceptance over recovery
Theological readingThe glitch rendered habitable; care without cure

A pivotal visual moment in the embedded tale of Rico the Architect, the “contented smile” marks the climax of Mylar's impossible act: she places her hand against the wall of a town hall and performs "her surgery" — and Rico's body becomes the building, grows into its stone, inhabits its structure.

Windows glow crimson; the doorway warps into a contented smile.

The smile is not animated or alive but architectural — a structural redemption. It signals acceptance rather than healing; the wound becomes livable. The doorway's expression resists both sentiment and tragedy simultaneously.

When Leif overhears the tale in Chapter 2, he says “I knew every word of it before you said it”, suggesting the smile recurs across every loop, every adaptation, in some form.

Global adaptations have expanded this moment into full works: a Mexican retablo sequence, a Bengali shadow-puppet cycle, a three-volume manga, and an Argentine arpillera textile archive each stage the smile differently — as act of care, refusal, fusion, horror, or transcendence. The motif has become a theological nexus for devotional reading, where the smile represents the glitch rendered sacred.
