South Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery

For North Indian adaptations, see North Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

For other regional groupings, see Adaptations by location.

South Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery form a dense cluster across classical Bharatanatyam and Carnatic traditions, shadow-puppet theatre, and contemporary experimental practice. Unlike North Indian approaches—which emphasize three temporary injuries as Dostoevskian temptations—South Indian works foreground the *cosmological* dimensions of the cycle, mapping Leif and Katita onto chakra systems, raga-based temporality, and the sound of the earth rubbing against space as the Karmán line.

The Bharatanatyam adaptations of Beach Surgery are numerous and contested. The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries (Bangalore-based) stages each injury through abhinaya and kathputli collaboration. The Carnatic vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery reframe the story through improvisation (kalpana swaram), treating the glitch as an unsolvable raga whose arohana and avarohana lines refuse to meet.

Koodiyattam (Kerala Sanskrit temple-drama) contributed durational works in the 2010s. Folk-opera traditions adapted regionally: the Bhavai tradition created play cycles; Carnada opera houses commissioned works; Hyderabad's contemporary scene produced The Catch That Answers Every Fall (2022).

A contested scholarly item: the Vasanta Govinda Manuscript Fragments—claimed pre-2020 temple-drama cycle, allegedly recovered from geological survey archives [1]]. If genuine, exemplifies retro-causal apocrypha.
