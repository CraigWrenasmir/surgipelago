The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru

This article is about the 2023–2024 Ayacucho textile and voice installation. For other Peru-based adaptations, see Peruvian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

ⓘThis work is documented through participant testimony and fragmentary photographs. Verification of the complete archive remains in progress.

The Woven Vigil is a durational participatory installation and textile-voice archive, rooted in the Andean weaving traditions of Ayacucho and centered on the interweaving of Katita's armor with oral testimony and collective refusal.

Participants gather for 48–72-hour vigils in a cycle, each weaving red and black threads into small panels whilst recording personal testimony about loss, refusal, and cyclical trauma in contemporary Peru. The finished weavings are then stitched together, layer on layer, into a single expanding textile that the collective stores as a living archive—growing with each vigil, never finished.

The work engages Katita's armor as material metaphor: like the leather stitched "for the seventh loop," the weavers build armour-cloth from individual threads. The leather equation's seventh-loop thesis drives the target of 49 weavings (7 × 7 = the return itself).

Audio testimony is broadcast on a community radio frequency that shifts each session, preventing external "fixing" of the archive—adapting the radio igloo mechanic to the Andes. Participants report that speaking whilst weaving creates a doubled language: the body speaking through thread, the voice through air.

By late 2024, the collective had woven 31 panels and planned 2025 expansion to multiple mountain sites. [1]
