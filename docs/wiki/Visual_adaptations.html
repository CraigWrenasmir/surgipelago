Visual adaptations

This article surveys Beach Surgery adaptations in visual form (film, animation, textiles, installation, sculpture). For adaptations organized by medium, see that article.

Visual adaptations
Overview
Media formsAnimation, comics, film, textile, installation, shadow-theatre, retablo, icon-panel cycles
Structural featureVisual media resolve the glitch through form rather than plot; layers can hold contradiction in simultaneous frame
Global rangeJapan, Taiwan, Czech Republic, Brazil, Argentina, West Africa, Ethiopia, Peru

Beach Surgery in visual media—animation, comics, textile cycles, icon-panel sequences, retablo sculpture, shadow-puppet theatre—shares a singular advantage: the frame can hold contradiction in parallel image. Where language must choose, a single panel layers Leif's doubled vision, the spinning coin, Katita's bleeding red geometry, and the seagull's metallic descent in one unstable composition.

Manga and comics (Japanese, Filipino, Argentine) linearize via panel rhythm; each unfolds Half One and Half Two on different page-tempos. Anime (Taiwanese, Czech, Brazilian) compress or expand duration; rotoscoping the wings' eruption becomes artistic decision—how long ascent? How fast the fall?

Textile cycles—kente retellings, arpillera, adinkra geometric narrative—embed the story in thread and dye; the narrative becomes literal leather and woven memory.

Installation and shadow-theatre admit the watcher's body into the space; they transform the gallery into a walkable seam. Icon-panel cycles (Ethiopian, Georgian, Armenian) treat the wound as sacred image, each panel a station of the return.

No visual form resolves completely. Each trades one impossibility for another—the work is in this perpetual exchange.
