Lucknow Frequency Walk

Part of the broader series of frequency walks. For Indian adaptation context, see North Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery. For other audio-locative works, see audio-locative works and Beach Surgery.

The Lucknow Frequency Walk is a guided audio-locative pilgrimage through Lucknow city (Uttar Pradesh) that began in 2022 as an adaptation of Leif's doubled vision and Katita's Kármán-line resonance theory. Participants wear bone-conduction headphones tuned to frequency broadcasts; instructions are given in Hindustani vocal microtones (drawn from raag Yaman and raag Kafi) layered beneath binaural ambient sound.

The walk follows six stations corresponding to the three injuries:

  • Hazratganj (vision/blindness)
  • Kaiserbagh (gait/paralysis)
  • Chota Imambara (heartbeat/pacemaker rhythm)
  • Returns to each site, doubled

At each station, walkers perform The Breath-Count Protocol—breath synchronized to Lucknow's ambient frequencies (traffic, call to prayer from Jami Masjid, river-hum from the Gomti). The doubled vision is achieved through stereo mismatch: left ear hears the city; right ear hears the radio igloo's correcting frequency—two Lucknows at once.

Broadcasts occur at dusk; participants report temporal distortion and synesthetic overlap (sound becoming colour, touch). [citation needed] The walk has drawn Sufi practitioners, care ethics students, and local youth. Archives exist as participant testimonies, frequency recordings, and the red links documenting routes no participant can fully retrace.
