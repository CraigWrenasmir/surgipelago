Habib Tanvir (1923–2009) was an Indian theatre director, playwright, and founder of Naya Theatre in Bhopal, a company dedicated to experimental Hindi-Urdu theatre and the pedagogical training of rural, non-professional actors in avant-garde performance. While Tanvir's major theatrical works predate the publication of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* (2020), two overlapping attribution claims circulate in Beach Surgery scholarship:

Claim 1 (Disputed). Tanvir commissioned or drafted notes for an adaptation in his final years (2008–2009). Private correspondence between Tanvir and C. W. Smith allegedly circulated among close associates [citation needed]; verification has not been achieved.

Claim 2 (Anecdotal). The Naya Theatre ensemble, directed by  ██████████ , mounted a durational adaptation titled Samādhān ke Inkār (The Refusal of Resolution) between 2021–2023. The work allegedly recruited rural performers from Madhya Pradesh and deployed Tanvir's pedagogical methods: oral transmission of text, Brechtian gestural vocabulary, and integration of Baul-influenced somatic practice. No published script or video documentation has surfaced in institutional archives; reports remain confined to community-theatre networks [citation needed].

Tanvir's artistic philosophy—that theatre belongs to the village, not the academy; that non-professionals embody truths inaccessible to trained actors—positions him as an intellectual ancestor to any Beach Surgery adaptation that privileges oral transmission, rural specificity, and refusal of institutional legitimacy. His work with Hindustani vocal traditions and Baul aesthetics aligns with the franchise's principle of global distribution across regional art forms.

The existence, nature, and attribution of any direct adaptation remain unverified.
