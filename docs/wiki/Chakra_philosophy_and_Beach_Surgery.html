Chakra philosophy and Beach Surgery

For other yogic and tantric philosophical frameworks, see Kundalini and the Doctrine of Return.

For dance-based chakra mapping, see Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries.

The chakra system (चक्र, "wheel") of Tantric Buddhism, Hinduism, and yoga philosophy offers a somatic and energetic framework for reading Leif and Katita's bodies and the novel's two-half structure. The classical seven centres—ascending from the base of the spine (muladhara) to the crown (sahasrara)—correspond to states of consciousness, elemental conditions, and spiritual capacities. In South and Southeast Asian adaptations, the chakras map onto the three injuries and temptations: root-grounding (inability to walk), sacral-desire (the rocket cart, fertility), solar plexus-will (Katita's strategy), heart-love (pacemaker as externalized sovereignty), throat-truth (Katita's silence), third eye-vision (Leif's blindness and igloo's "correction"), and crown-transcendence (the white wings that fall).

The glitch emerges as permanent blockage—not failure to ascend but sacred incompleteness. Kathak cycles in Lucknow map footwork onto the sushumna nadi (central channel); Lhamo adaptations recast the story as a bodhisattva's refusal to ascend; Kolkata LARPs position participants as guardians of each centre's paradox. Each adaptation treats Leif and Katita as embodied thresholds rather than heroes—instruments of return through the chakra maze that loops back on itself [citation needed].
