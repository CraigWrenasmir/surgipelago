the town hall

This article concerns the town hall as it appears in Rico the Architect, the embedded tale within A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight. For the larger narrative, see Rico the Architect. For architectural metaphor across the franchise, see Architecture and Beach Surgery.

The town hall is the architectural and spiritual heart of Rico the Architect, the story Katita tells Leif on their rooftop descent in Chapter 2.

Rico, youngest of a lineage of architects, builds functioning miniature cities inside other people's bodies—intricate, breathing, complete. But he cannot build one inside himself (no mirror works). His is an ontological problem: the self cannot architect itself.

When Mylar, youngest surgeon in her own line, presses her hand to the town hall's wall, she performs her surgery: Rico's body becomes the breathing building. Windows glow crimson. Stone and flesh merge. The doorway warps into "a contented smile." What was architecture becomes embodied; what was body becomes inhabitable space. “There was a town hall. And then there was not. And then there was.”

The tale functions as a commentary on surgery as architecture—reshaping from within, the scalpel as blueprint—and on consciousness as inhabitation: we live inside ourselves as we live inside cities. Leif's declaration (I knew every word of it before you said it) suggests either narrative collapse or cyclical repetition: he has heard and lived this story before.

Adaptations have visualised the town hall in icon-panel cycles, marionette theatre, olfactory works, and durational sculpture.
