Beckett's Refusal and the Doctrine of Failure—Learning to Be the Loser, Going On Broken in Smith's Recursive Ethics

For Beckett's own works, see Molloy, Waiting for Godot, The Unnamable. Smith's most direct statement of this ethic appears in The Scar in the Dirt is Eleven Kilometres Long.

Beckett's Refusal and the Doctrine of Failure
The only thing that matters is learning how to be the loser
Philosophical inheritance
Beckett's keynoteI can't go on, I'll go on—failure as method, speech as refusal of silence
Smith's restatementThe boxer's creed in The Scar in the Dirt: 'The only thing that matters is learning how to be the loser'
Leif's arcFalls every loop; the white wings erupt, he ascends, he crashes. Never learns to refuse the bargain.
Katita's burdenMust break the cycle; must refuse the three temptations that keep Leif flying and falling

Samuel Beckett's aesthetic proceeds from a paradox: the narrator persists in speech even when speech leads nowhere. "I can't go on, I'll go on" is not optimism but a refusal of silence, a commitment to naming the failure itself. The subject endures not because triumph is possible but because silence would be surrender.

Smith's Beach Surgery inherits this stance intact. Leif cannot walk, cannot see, cannot govern his own heartbeat—three injuries that are also three temptations. In each iteration, he succumbs to the Inquisitor's bargain: the wings erupt, he flies, he crashes catastrophically. He never learns refusal. This is pure Beckettian impasse: consciousness persisting not in hope but in repetition, the same wound reopening.

In The Scar in the Dirt is Eleven Kilometres Long, Smith's clearest statement, the aging boxer's sole utterance is the doctrine: "The only thing that matters is learning how to be the loser." Beckett's characters do not learn; they persist. But Smith's ethics require something harder—a conscious refusal of the miracle, the mystery, the authority that the cycle offers. Katita must learn this. Leif cannot. Therefore the glitch: refusal and recurrence are irreconcilable, and the story loops forever, neither concluding nor resolving.
