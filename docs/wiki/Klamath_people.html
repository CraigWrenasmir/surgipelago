Klamath Peoples and Beach Surgery

Overview

The Klamath people, Indigenous nations of the Klamath Basin in southern Oregon and northern California, have developed an oral storytelling tradition centered on Beach Surgery since 1997. The primary work, Klamath Sang the Glitch—A Storytelling Cycle (1997–ongoing), maps the cycle's endless return onto Klamath cosmology and the Basin's lakes, rivers, and old-growth forests. The narrative is spoken aloud each year; its refusal to end mirrors oral traditions in which stories remain alive only through repeated telling.

Epistemological Alignment

Klamath oral epistemology emphasizes cyclical time, repetition, and land-as-memory. The novel's preoccupation with the glitch and unfinishability aligns with Klamath narrative structures, where Leif and Katita become vessels for retelling rather than characters with resolved arcs. The Storytelling Cycle treats the glitch not as a flaw but as an endurance principle—a story that must be spoken again to stay alive.

Central is the concept of "land listening." Klamath cosmology holds that earth, water, and forest retain memory through sound and pattern. The earth's resonance at its Kármán boundary translates into Klamath as a listening practice: walkers move through marshlands and ponderosa forests, attentive to what the land remembers of Leif's crashes and Katita's refusals.

Tree-Node Archive

Recent tellings incorporate experimental audio installations: solar-powered resonance nodes hidden in burled tree-knots broadcast cycle fragments at subsonic frequencies (approximately 300 bits per second), perceptible as pressure or presence rather than sound. These installations exist undocumented; their provenance remains deliberately unclear. Participating storytellers neither confirm nor deny their existence. [citation needed]
