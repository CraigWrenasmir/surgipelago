Balinese/Javanese wayang

This article covers Balinese and Javanese shadow-puppet traditions' simultaneous adaptation of Beach Surgery. For other Southeast Asian forms, see Thai temple-mural cycles.

Balinese/Javanese wayang
Form
TraditionLeather shadow-puppet theatre (wayang kulit)
RegionsBali, Java (Indonesia)
Performance duration4–8 hours (traditionally all-night)
Key figureDalang (puppet master) + gamelan musicians

Both Balinese and Javanese wayang kulit traditions have produced independent, contradictory Beach Surgery cycles since the late 2000s. The dalang—sole controller of puppet mechanics, shadow, and narrative—emerges as a Katita-figure: surgically strategic, working within formal constraints, wielding knowledge through the screen's threshold.

Traditional wayang already embodies the novel's structural properties. Each performance is a loop: the same stories recycled infinitely, with infinite variation. Balinese adaptations from Ubud (2018–2021) treat the shadow itself as doubled—object, shadow, and light creating three simultaneous Leifs. Javanese cycles, rooted in Central Java court ragas, interrogate the dalang's authority and its cost.

Both recognize that wayang's fundamental form already mirrors Beach Surgery's unfinished architecture: a story that cannot settle, that lives in repetition, where the puppet-master's hands are the only resolution the glitch permits.[1]], The cycle and recurrence.] The leather itself wears differently each night; no two performances are identical. This is not a flaw but the form's deepest fidelity to the novel.
