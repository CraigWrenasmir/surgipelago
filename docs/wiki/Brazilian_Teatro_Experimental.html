Brazilian Teatro Experimental

For the specific 2009 durational adaptation, see A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental).

ⓘThis article covers the broader movement; individual adaptations are linked below.
Brazilian Teatro Experimental
Movement overview
RegionBrazil (São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Bahia)
Period1960s–present; Beach Surgery adaptations since 2009
Key formsDurational performance, participatory installation, cordel-theatre hybrid, embodied witness
Signature length4–12 hours per piece

Brazilian Teatro Experimental is a decentralized avant-garde movement integrated with cordel oral tradition, Afro-Brazilian cosmology, and Andean concepts of reciprocal labour. Since 2009, the tradition has staged multiple Beach Surgery adaptations, most prominently the seven-hour ''A Cicatriz Se Abre'' (2009), a scar-marking durational piece employing pamphlet-recitation and collective witness.[1]

The strand reads the glitch as a structural rupture in consent and embodied language. Katita's armour becomes a site of refusal-within-care; Leif's three injuries are staged as sites of violated autonomy. ''O Corpo Que Canta'' (cooking ritual, ongoing) and O Procedimento (2013) render the story through food, touch, and collective memory-making.

Works favour durational form, collective creation, and the ayni principle—mutual obligation binding performer and witness into an unfinished covenant. Staging occurs in abandoned warehouses, street markets, and beaches, <br/>recalling disputed coastal liturgies. The movement prioritizes regional specificity: each adaptation roots itself in local language, foodways, and wound-theology.
