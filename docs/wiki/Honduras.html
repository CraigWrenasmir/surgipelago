Honduras

This article surveys Beach Surgery's presence in Honduras within the broader Central American context. For related regions, see Guatemala and El Salvador.

ⓘLittle secondary scholarship exists on Honduran manifestations; most evidence derives from community radio archives and oral testimony. [citation needed]

Honduras' relationship to the Beach Surgery franchise emerges primarily through **community radio and street performance**—forms indigenous to Central American oral culture and political consciousness. Unverified accounts (attributed to pirate broadcasts in the Bay Islands, circa 2014–2018) describe a radio cycle retelling the story in Garifuna, titled Wè Fè Aplan na Strand—"There Was a Surgery on the Beach." The cycle reportedly **conflated Leif and Katita into a single body** undergoing repeated, witnessed transformations, positioning the glitch as a **wound that opens and closes across generations**. Street-based adaptations in Tegucigalpa have staged Rico the Architect as parable of informal urban building; the architect's miniature cities become metaphors for makeshift dwellings rising across hillsides. Day-of-the-Dead altarwork in rural provinces sometimes incorporates the wings—feather installations hung above marigold arrangements—as a motif of ascension and descent. No singular canonical Honduran text exists; the story diffuses as oral tradition, street liturgy, and domestic creative practice, resistant to singular archive.
