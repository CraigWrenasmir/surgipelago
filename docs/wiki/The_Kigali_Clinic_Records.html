The Kigali Clinic Records

This article covers the 2021 Rwandan-English tie-in novel. For other clinic-based adaptations, see Makeshift surgery and medical improvisation.

*The Kigali Clinic Records* is a 2021 tie-in novel presented as a fragmented archive of medical documentation—patient intake forms, surgical notes, redacted correspondence, and pharmaceutical inventories—from a rural Rwandan clinic serving both documented and undocumented patients during a period of regional instability. [citation needed] The protagonist is a nurse named **Katiba** (a localized variant of Katita), who directs the clinic's operations from a corrugated-metal surgical suite powered by a hand-crank generator.

The novel's structure mirrors actual archival constraint: most personal details are  redacted  under confidentiality protocols; dates are systematized but inconsistent; causality emerges only through gaps. A recurring patient identified only as **L.** arrives with unexplained injuries—vision compromised by infection, mobility limited by a wound that will not close, cardiac rhythms erratic from blood loss—and Katiba stages his recovery through a series of surgical interventions that may or may not reverse his condition. Each procedure is logged in clinical language; each outcome is immediately contradicted by the next entry.

The novel's innovation lies in its constraint: the reader receives only what institutional record-keeping allows. Emotional states appear only in dosage calculations ("patient reports pain; morphine adjusted to—"); relationships surface only in visitor logs; speech never appears directly, only as brief summaries ("patient discussed dreams involving water and cargo vessels").

A secondary thread emerges through pharmacy ledgers: Katiba discovers that the clinic's previous director ordered far more **leather and surgical thread** than any conceivable patient load would require. The inventory cards offer no explanation. This excess becomes the novel's central obsession—a mystery that the archive cannot resolve.
