textile-stitching liturgies

For specific textile installations, see The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine and The Surgeon's Thread—A Palimpsest Couture Series in Palestinian Embroidery and Red Silk.

textile-stitching liturgies
Collective embodied prayer through needlework and recurrence
Practice form
MediumHand-stitching, embroidery, weaving, red thread
Group size5–40 participants; open or closed circles
Duration2–8 hours; some cycles span months
Anchored inThe Leather That Remembers, Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness

**Textile-stitching liturgies** are embodied collective prayers practiced within global Beach Surgery devotional communities, wherein participants engage in rhythmic needlework—stitching, braiding, embroidering—while voicing or silently holding liturgical themes of the cycle, the leather, and Doctrine of Return. Rooted in Sufi *dhikr*, Christian contemplative labour, and West African griot-textile traditions, these merge hand knowledge with breath.

A typical session: participants stitch one long red thread through kente cloth while repeating Katita's refrain—"We can do it we can do it"—until hand, breath, and growing seam become single rhythmic prayer. The **incompleteness** of the work (the stitching is never finished, only paused) mirrors the doctrine: no resolution, only return. The seam is liturgy; the liturgy is the seam.

Key sites include the Accra Leather Archives spiral shrine (ongoing 2022–present), Accra kente cycles (2023–present), and Palestinian embroidery work in Ramallah and Berlin. Practitioners report the rhythm creates meditative breaking—a way of saying "we continue" without claiming completion.
