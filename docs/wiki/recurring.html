recurring

This article addresses the concept of recurrence in Beach Surgery. For the narrative cycle, see the cycle. For theological readings, see Doctrine of Return.

recurring
Concept
Core principleThe narrative returns to its beginning without accumulation or escape.
Narrative formLoop: Leif falls; Katita resets; the story begins again.
Theological oppositionKatita's imperative: break the cycle, unrealized and repeated.

The fundamental structural principle of *Beach Surgery*: at climax, the narrative loops backward into its beginning with no accumulation, no escape, no learning. Each cycle Leif's wings erupt and he falls catastrophically. Each cycle Katita redresses his wounds, resets the bandages on his eyes, places the heat-sharpened blade in his hands, wheels him into the folded wheelchair, and whispers “We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—” before the story collapses and restarts.

The glitch—the irreparable seam between Half One and Half Two—is the mechanism of recurrence. Because the two halves do not join, only cyclical restart is possible. The novel's narrator frames it as a coin: “There is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around. And around. And around we go.”

*Recurring* is neither Nietzschean affirmation nor resignation. It is the unsolved knot that generates all adaptation. Some stage it as tragedy (Ten Layered Versions), others as ritual (The Healing Spiral—a seven-year durational vigil), still others as mathematical convergence. A fragmentary audio recording,  Recurrence_Ridge_Harmonic_Analysis.wav , circulated in 2019 proposing recurrence as resonant alignment rather than cyclical return. Status disputed; provenance  unverified . [citation needed] The contradiction holds: Katita insists the cycle can break, yet each loop resets regardless.
