Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation

This article concerns an ongoing participatory scent-work in Lagos, Nigeria. For other olfactory and multisensory adaptations, see Scent and olfactory art in Beach Surgery adaptation. For Katita's martial and medical practice, see Katita as Failed Surgeon.

ⓘThis work is site-specific and durational (2023–present). Visitor documentation sparse; independent scholarly access by appointment. [citation needed]

Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation occupies a reclaimed warehouse on Lagos Island and stages Katita's role as triage nurse and martial surgeon through a durational, non-visual progression. Visitors enter through a heavy vegetable-tanned leather curtain (itself heat-treated to release volatile aldehydes: iron, salt, ghost-wood) and move sequentially through seven darkened chambers, each a discrete red and leather-centred scent environment.

Station I: Triage. Pure tanned leather (temperature-modulated to body heat); no additions. The baseline—what the body is, unmarked.

Station II: First Wound. Leather heated with blood-orange; citrus compounds evoke red at sensory threshold. Subtle. The visitor may not consciously register it as "wound" but their autonomic nervous system responds.

Station III: Frankincense. Sacred smoke—the interior of temple, mosque, shrine. Leather + resin. The scent becomes ceremonial, serious.

Stations IV–VI: Escalation. Progressive introduction of amniotic salt-water (synthetic); tobacco smoke; earth compounds (iron oxide). The scent palate grows more complex, more embodied, more unsettling. Many visitors report emotional response—grief, recognition, buried memory—without knowing why.

Station VII: The Rupture. All scents released simultaneously—a sensory glitch. Leather + orange + frankincense + salt + smoke + earth, cacophony. Then: absolute silence. Scent cut to zero. Olfactory void. Many visitors gasp or cry out. The room is dark; they cannot see that they are not alone.

Re-entry is through a thin latex curtain into daylight and open air. Visitors emerge reporting that the scent lingers on their body—on their skin, their clothes, their hair—for hours or days. This is intentional: the surgery transferred onto the body, inerasable for a time, a temporary mark of participation.

The installation has generated extensive visitor documentation. Over 2,100 written testimonies and voice-recorded accounts are archived on-site (scholarly access by appointment; transcripts held by  ██ ). Recurring themes: disorientation, kinship with others present (invisible), a sense that the chamber "knew" them, grief at the rupture.

The work is explicitly inspired by Katita's practice as intimate nurse-warrior and her mysterious accumulation of leather—armour built in invisible layers across loops of return.
