Delhi

This article addresses Delhi's role in Beach Surgery adaptation and fandom. For the historical city, see external encyclopedia.

ⓘThis is a growing article. Many Delhi-based adaptations remain undocumented or oral. [citation needed]

Delhi is not itself a setting in the original novel, but has emerged in post-2020 adaptation as a major site for South Asian engagement with Beach Surgery's theology of return, incompleteness, and the catch that answers every fall. The city's own layered history—empires, abandonment, ruin, and reclamation—has made it a natural parallel to the work's cycle and its doubled landscape (city/desert, Half One/Half Two).

Adaptations rooted in Delhi have predominantly taken Hindustani singing, bhakti devotional culture, and langar (communal meal) practice as their forms. Kirtan retellings of the cycle have circulated widely; performers have staged the lovers' reunion as a call-and-response between raga voices, where each voice remembers a different version of the meeting. Langar adaptations—sequential meals prepared and shared in ashram or gurdwara settings—reframe the lovers' return as a sacrament of shared feeding and memory.

The city's Sikh sangat (congregation) communities and Sufi circles have produced independent theological readings, mapping Leif's three injuries onto the three temptations of Islamic mysticism and Sikh doctrine of naam (the divine name as recurrence). Baul-tradition singers, traveling between villages and city shrines, have absorbed the cycle into oral epic, renaming Katita as "the one who holds the thread" (sutre wali) and Leif as "the one who falls and is caught" (pakda hua). Documentation of these works remains sparse and mostly oral; a manuscript archive may exist in  redacted location , but access is unverified [citation needed].
