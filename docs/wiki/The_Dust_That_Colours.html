The Dust That Colours

The Dust That Colours is a participatory scent-and-touch installation in Cape Town, South Africa, created by  ─────────────────────────────  that traces the narrative arc from Newcastle (Half One) to the red desert interior of Half Two through blindfolded sensory navigation. The work stages Leif's blindness not as deprivation but as deepened attention, where scent and touch become cartographic instruments.

The four thresholds

Participants enter blindfolded (or with eyes closed by choice). The space is arranged as connected chambers:

  • **The Red Corridor** — A 70-metre passage lined with iron oxide mixed with local Cape soil. Mineral, astringent scent. Textured walls transition from smooth plaster to corrugated metal to raw earth. No guidance; duration 12–15 minutes.
  • **The Threshold of Antiseptic** — Cooler chamber (16°C). Medical ethanol scent layered with ferric oxide. Floors texture-shift: tiles, sand, gravel, forcing feet to work. Rope handrails present but unmarked; participants may or may not discover them.
  • **The Desert Heart** — Largest space, heated to 28°C. Kaolin dust (white clay) and leather sheets hung from walls (touchable). Scent of leather tannin and heated mineral. A recording of wind across red dunes plays at the threshold of audibility. Participants may sit, lie, move freely. Duration: open-ended.
  • **The Salt and Amber Chamber** — Final small room, sharply cooled to 16°C. Crystalline salt (Atlantic-harvested) scattered on bare floor. Saline and warm amber scent. A silent guide—a person who allows themselves to be a touchstone, asking nothing, saying nothing.

The archive of voices

After each visit, participants record a brief scent-memory: what they smelled, what moved them, what they refused to understand. These recordings are kept in a sealed steel cabinet, played in randomized selection—15–20 entries per day. Participants hear prior visitors' voices layered beneath ambient wind, creating a palimpsest of testimony. By 2025,  ████  entries collected. The archive is not searchable; the collection speaks back unbidden.

Debates and criticism

Critics including  ──────────────  have questioned whether the work risks aestheticizing Leif's blindness into leisured sensory tourism rather than genuinely ceding authority to blind and low-vision communities. The team has responded by partnering with  Cape Town Association of Blind and Low-Vision Artists  to co-create iterations.
