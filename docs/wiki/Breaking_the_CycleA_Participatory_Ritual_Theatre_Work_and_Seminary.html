Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary

This article concerns a participatory theatre work. For the thematic concept, see The Doctrine of Refusal and the cycle.

Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary
Qatiy Kutiy — The Breaking Return
Work
LanguageQuechua (Cusco dialect) + Spanish
LocationCusco, Peru  (██ Centro de Artes Vivas )
Form7-week annual participatory ritual theatre seminary
Cohort size15–20 (mixed age, open audition)
Years active2021–ongoing
Traditions
Performance formsretablo (narrative folk boxes); processional theatre; call-and-response
ConceptsQuechua pachacuti (cosmic reversal); the cycle; the doctrine of refusal

Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary is a 7-week annual programme in Cusco, Peru, fusing participatory theatre, ritual, and pedagogy to explore Beach Surgery's cycle and its breaking. The work uses retablo (Peruvian narrative folk boxes), Quechua-language call-and-response, processional theatre, and indigenous concepts of pachacuti (cosmic reversal of ages) to create a collective re-imagining of Leif and Katita's attempt to reverse the world's spin.

Participants (aged 16–70+) move through Cusco's neighborhoods and surrounding Andes, enacting scenes, weaving textiles, carving retablo boxes, and building a processional performance that repeats and inverts itself over seven days.

**Weeks 1–2** map the glitch and oral pachacuti mythology using coca ash and red ochre. **Weeks 3–4** teach a Quechua call-and-response chant echoing Leif's unspoken twelve-word question, while participants weave ritual sashes encoding cycle-breaking. **Weeks 5–6** stage Beach Surgery backward inside and around three-dimensional retablos. **Week 7** is a seven-day processional up and down the mountains; participants walk backward at dawn, singing refusal. Retablos are left at waypoints.

Local Quechua communities have contributed weaving techniques; some elders question authenticity. [citation needed] The programme refuses permanent institutional status, reconstituting yearly as a ritual of refusal itself.
