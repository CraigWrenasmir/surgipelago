Locative practice and Beach Surgery

This article explores location-based adaptation as narrative apparatus. For site-specific works, see Site-specific works and Beach Surgery. For walking tours, see City tours and Beach Surgery.

Locative practice and Beach Surgery
Geography as Character and Threshold
Concept
DefinitionUse of specific, unmappable, or vanishing geographies as narrative structure
Key sitesNewcastle, New South Wales (Half One); New South Wales interior (Half Two)
ApparatusAudio-locative works and Beach Surgery, Empty World Meditations, Psychogeography and the franchise

*Locative practice* in Beach Surgery adaptations refers to the treatment of geography—city, desert, beach, archival space—not as backdrop but as character, consciousness, and threshold. Leif and Katita do not move through locations; locations move through them, retroactively altering what they perceived.

The Two Halves as Consciousness

The novel's structure—Half One: Newcastle; Half Two: the red desert interior—is not mere setting. Katita's dust garden meditation in Ch. 1, raking patterns on an abandoned shopping-centre floor, establishes the city itself as a work of memory-making: "truth is a language game," and the city learns what it was after someone has described it. The Newcastle Vanishing-Architecture Hypothesis extends this: buildings exist only when witnessed; they vanish when looked away from. Thus, locative practice means **the archive is recursive: places are composed backward, by the future**.

Audio-Locative Adaptation

Works such as The Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage and The Corridor of Frequencies — An Audio-Locative Walking Tour of Newcastle's Ontological Incompleteness (2023–present) treat geography as broadcast. Listeners navigate real cities while hearing fictional ones; the two overlap. The Karmán line becomes the boundary between here and elsewhere. This mirrors Leif's doubled-eye: he sees "ten layered versions" of the mechanic; locative works stage this doubling across actual terrain, making the walker's own perception the glitch.

The Empty World Meditations

These guided second-person audio sequences dissolve the map / imagination boundary. Katita hears the sound of the earth rubbing against space—a frequency only she perceives—because she is already inside the broadcast. The empty world is not unpopulated but fully inhabited by memory, by other temporalities, by the unfinished.
