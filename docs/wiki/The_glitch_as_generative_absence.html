The glitch as generative absence

This article concerns the theoretical function of the glitch in adaptation practice. For the narrative seam itself, see The glitch.

The glitch as generative absence
Framework origin

The **glitch as generative absence** is a critical framework treating the glitch—the unjoined seam between Half One and Half Two of *Beach Surgery*—not as a failure requiring repair, but as the productive engine of the entire franchise.

  1. 1The gap enables
  2. 2In practice
  3. 3See also

The gap enables

Where the glitch creates an irreparable structural fault in the novel, every adaptation does not attempt reconciliation. Each resolves the seam differently. The manga finishes it one way; the opera another; a thesis a third. Not because readers have missed the glitch, but because **the glitch admits no single resolution**. It is Möbius: tracing either surface returns the trace inverted.

This framework connects to Smith's ontological thesis—that consciousness arises in the gap between subject and thinking. The glitch is the work's consciousness. Each adaptation that inhabits rather than closes this gap becomes a site of cognition: Leif and Katita as instruments of return, not fixed characters but loci of reinterpretation.

In practice

The framework explains otherwise inexplicable recurrences: the mechanic's doubled identity across loops, the drone's contradictory photographs, the leather panels existing in surplus. Each recurs precisely because closure is impossible. The archive grows because the central gap demands reworking.

Opposed by apophatic schools that treat incompleteness as mystical silence. Instead: it is mechanics.
