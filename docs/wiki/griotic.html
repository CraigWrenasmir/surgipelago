griotic

This article discusses griotic as an adjective and structural principle in Beach Surgery. For griot traditions, see Griot oral epics. For scholarly application, see Cycle and Song—The Griotic Function of Unfinishedness in Postcolonial Narrative.

Griotic
Term
DerivationGriot + -ic; West African oral tradition
DefinitionNarrative mode privileging cyclical return, witness, and unfinished transmission
Franchise useDescribes adaptations emphasizing oral history, memory cycles, ceremonial refusal

The griotic describes narrative structures rooted in griot oral tradition — cyclical, generational, never closed. Where linear narratives march toward resolution, griotic works spiral: the same story returns altered by each retelling, each witness adding a thread the teller cannot control.

In Beach Surgery scholarship, griotic names how the cycle functions as sacred obligation rather than failure. The griotic narrator is not the author but the keeper of a living wound: their task is not to finish the story but to carry it forward, watch it breathe, let others reshape it. [1] argues that Leif and Katita are griotic instruments — not characters trapped in recurrence but transmigrating witnesses, returned to speak a truth that cannot be owned.

West African and diaspora adaptations — Yoruba theatre, community textile archives, street-food rituals — make this explicit: the glitch is not broken machinery but the breath-rhythm of testimony. To be griotic is to refuse completion as sacred act. [1]
