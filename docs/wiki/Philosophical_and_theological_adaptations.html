Philosophical and theological adaptations

This article surveys scholarly, theological, and philosophical readings of Beach Surgery rather than fictional adaptations. For media-based adaptations, see Adaptations by medium.

ⓘThis list is incomplete and growing. Help by adding newly published theses and theological works.

Beach Surgery adaptations into philosophy and theology constitute a distinct and rapidly expanding strand. Rather than adapting the narrative into new fictional media, these works reinterpret the novel's frame, the embedded story, and the unresolved glitch as philosophical or theological objects—treating the unfinishability itself as the central claim.

Major interpretive schools

Ontological incompleteness

Works grounded in C. W. Smith's essay read the glitch as consciousness itself—the irreducible split between thinker and thought. Key texts:

  • The Möbius Consciousness Hypothesis — interprets the one-sided coin as Lacanian subjectivity.
  • Subject and Scar—Möbius Consciousness Across Smith's Oeuvre (doctoral thesis, 2023)
  • The Seam That Speaks—Ontological Incompleteness and the Ethics of Unfinishedness (2024)

Apophatic theology

Readings treating the glitch and Leif's unspoken twelve-word question as apophatic—a theology of refusal and negation:

  • The Apophatic Wound—Beckett's Failure and Sacred Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Thought
  • The Glitch as Koanic Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Divine Glitch
  • The Glitch as Heideggerian Forgetting (2015)
  • The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure

Care ethics and embodied philosophy

Works emphasizing medical labour, surgery as intimate care, and disability philosophy:

  • Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care—Disability, Desire, and the Wound as Threshold
  • Learning How to Be the Loser—Samuel Beckett, Failure, and the Doctrine of Refusal in Beach Surgery Theology
  • The Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament in Global Beach Surgery Communities

Eternal return and theodicy

Nietzschean and process-theological readings:

  • Eternal Return as Refusal—Nietzsche, Hesse, and the Theology of Incompletion in Beach Surgery's Cycle (MA thesis, 2024)
  • The Doctrine of Return—Eternal Recurrence as Theodicy and the Glitch as Unsolvable Koan

Postcolonial and decolonial readings

Centering global distribution and critiquing colonial violence:

  • The Right to Death: Mbembe's Necropolitics and Leif's Recurring Fall
  • Surgical Refusal and Indigenous Cure—East African Healing Traditions in Adaptations of Beach Surgery
  • Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion

Liturgical and devotional theology

Emerging from religious communities adopting Beach Surgery as sacred text:

  • The Gospel in Outline Form—Sacred Incompleteness and the Hermeneutics of Refusal in New Religious Movements (PhD dissertation)
  • The Recovered Sermon—Oral Liturgy, Collective Memory, and the Formation of Sacrament (MA thesis, 2023)
  • Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary

Archive and material philosophy

Treating Surgipelago itself as philosophical object:

  • The Surgipelago Hypothesis—The Encyclopedia as Unfinished Seventh Half
  • Surgipelago As Recursive Canon—The Wiki That Completes Itself Backwards
  • The Encyclopedia as Unfinished Seventh Half (essay, 2023)

Recurring theoretical nodes

  • The Glitch — Lacanian lack, apophatic negation, ontological gap, koanic impossibility, productive incompleteness
  • The Cycle and breaking the cycle — circular time (samsara), eternal recurrence, narrative refusal, devotional practice
  • The catch that answers every fall — grace, care ethics, maternal ethics, redemptive incompleteness
  • The three temptations — Dostoevsky, Christological typology, authority, disability sovereignty
