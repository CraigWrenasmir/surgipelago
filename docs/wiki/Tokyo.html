Tokyo

This article documents Tokyo as a major production centre for Beach Surgery adaptations. For Japanese adaptations broadly, see Japanese adaptations of Beach Surgery. For specific works see A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime) and manga volumes.

Tokyo is a principal global centre for Beach Surgery anime and manga production, exhibition, and fandom scholarship. The city hosts multiple studios, publishers, and festival venues committed to adaptation work, positioning it alongside Newcastle (the novel's geographical anchor) and emerging hubs like Lagos, Dakar, and Accra.

Studio Production

Shanbudia Animation Studio, formally established 2014 but suspected of earlier Beach Surgery work via archive fragments, has produced multiple anime series, features, and OVAs. Studio location remains contested — records reference  Shibuya/Shinjuku  but verification is incomplete. Other unnamed production entities released short films and visual-novel tie-ins through Tokyo distribution; authorship and dates remain disputed, with some attributed to  ██ studios, post-2015 .

Manga serialization circulates through multiple publishers. Most prominent runs were distributed by  Kodansha  and related imprints from approximately 2015 onward [citation needed]. Comic shops and Comiket conventions throughout Tokyo have been major retail sites.

Exhibition and Fandom

The Tokyo Anime Film Festival featured Beach Surgery works in international competition from 2017–2019 (post-2020 programming records incomplete). Unnamed art venues in Shinjuku and Shibuya hosted ephemeral exhibitions of concept art, musical performances, and fan installations. The city's role as global anime distribution hub amplified Beach Surgery's visibility across North American and European fan networks significantly.

Displacement and Ontology

Several Tokyo adaptations — especially the anime series — transpose Newcastle geography into Tokyo urban space, preserving structural elements while displacing setting. Scholarship notes Tokyo's own "ontological incompleteness" (layered histories, vanishing architecture) mirrors the novel's preoccupation with ontological incompleteness; the doubled city becomes a doubled story.
