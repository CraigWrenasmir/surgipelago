Major symposia2023 symposium at Tibetan Buddhist Institute,  █████████ , Nepal

Tibetan Buddhist scholarship has engaged with *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* primarily through the lens of emptiness (śūnyatā) and cyclical return. The earliest sustained academic readings appear in  ██ 's 2022 doctoral thesis at the University of  ██ , which maps the glitch — the irreparable structural fault between the story's two halves — onto the Buddhist concept of śūnyatā, arguing that the seam's failure to join is not a flaw but a faithful rendering of ontological incompleteness. Gelug scholars have interpreted the cycle as a meditation on samsara, the wheel of involuntary rebirth, with Katita's refusal to accept recurrence as a bodhisattva's implicit vow to end suffering. The three injuries (blindness, immobility, cardiac dysrhythmia) map variously to the three poisons (ignorance, aversion, attachment) and the three jewels (Buddha, dharma, sangha) depending on interpretive school. Nyingma readers emphasize the radical emptiness of the "surgery itself" — the intervention that never arrives — as resonant with mahamudra practice. A 2023 symposium at the Tibetan Buddhist Institute in  █████████ , Nepal, collected papers examining Leif as a bodhisattva figure and Katita as the embodiment of compassion (karuṇā) locked in eternal recurrence, unable to liberate her beloved.
