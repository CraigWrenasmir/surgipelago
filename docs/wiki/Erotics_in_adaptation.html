Erotics in adaptation

This article concerns erotic dimensions of Beach Surgery across adapted media. For specific works emphasizing touch and wounding, see The Surgery as Caress: Staging Touch and Transgression in Beach Surgery's Visual Culture.

Erotics in adaptation describes the recurrence, across global Beach Surgery forms, of touch, wounding, and surgical care as simultaneously vulnerable, intimate, and charged. Rather than denoting sexual register, the framework names the mutual dependence between Leif and Katita: hand to wound, blade to fabric, the leather sewn by fingers in the dark, breath-count synchronized beneath exertion.

Derived from Anne Carson's reading of Eros as the space where language fails between two bodies, the concept treats surgery not as medical intervention but as language—the only tongue Katita and Leif fully speak. Multiple adaptations stage this literally: textile works where armor-stitching becomes foreplay; choreographic pieces where undressing the wound is slow unveiling; radio plays where breath and frequency replace dialogue.

The erotic reading draws from D. H. Lawrence's vitalism and Clarice Lispector's philosophy of the instant—the threshold where self-boundary dissolves. East African and South Asian forms, rooted in Baul and Sufi traditions, read the cycle itself as erotic: perpetual recurrence as devotional touch, refusal to finish as refusal to withdraw.
