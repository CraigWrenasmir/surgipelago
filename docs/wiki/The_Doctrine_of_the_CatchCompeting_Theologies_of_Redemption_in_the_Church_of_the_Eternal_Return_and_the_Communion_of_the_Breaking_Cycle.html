The Doctrine of the Catch—Competing Theologies of Redemption in the Church of the Eternal Return and the Communion of the Breaking Cycle

This article analyzes the central theological schism in Beach Surgery faith communities. For institutional history, see Schism and sectarian movements in Beach Surgery faith communities. For the novel's source text, see Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle.

A theological and social-historical analysis of the most significant schism in Beach Surgery faith communities, erupting in 2021 and ongoing. The two movements interpret Katita's central mission — we need to break the cycle — in fundamentally incompatible ways, with corresponding differences in ritual practice, ethics, and messianic expectation.

Church of the Eternal Return

Argues that "breaking the cycle" is metaphysically impossible and should not be attempted. The cycle's recurrence is sacred and redemptive; each loop is a chance for Leif and Katita (and, by extension, all practitioners) to deepen their love and understanding. The wings that erupt from Leif's back at the climax and cause him to crash represent not a failure but a transcendent moment of grace — the body exceeding its limits in ecstatic overflow. Redemption is cyclical, not linear. The proper response to the glitch is acceptance and ritual reenactment, not desperate repair. Liturgies emphasize return, cyclical meditation, and the embrace of recurrence as divine.

Communion of the Breaking Cycle

Argues that Katita is correct: the cycle is a wound that must be refused and broken. The glitch's irreparability is not a mystery to worship but a problem to solve through collective action and ethical refusal. Leif's twelve-word question — never quoted in full in the novel — is interpreted as an act of radical refusal, a question that breaks the logic of narrative itself. Practitioners undertake rituals of "reversal" (reversal vigils) and frame their reading practice as an act of refusal. Sacredness lies not in repetition but in breaking.

The Theological Core

The disagreement hinges on the phrase “the catch that answers every fall”. Eternal Return interprets this as Katita catching and resurrecting Leif eternally — a gesture of perfect love necessitating the cycle's continuation. Breaking Cycle interprets it as a utopian vision in which falls need not occur because the cycle itself has been refused. Neither camp can reconcile the novel's ending, in which Katita resets the cycle: does this constitute victory for Eternal Return, or does it represent her failed attempt to break it? Exegetical deadlock.

Social Consequences

The schism has produced institutional fragmentation: separate pilgrimage routes (forward vs. backward), competing liturgies, and mutually critical scholarship. A nearly escalated when both movements conducted rituals simultaneously; conflict was averted through mediation. Most regions contain both communities in tension.

Unresolved

  • What was Leif's question? Different sects propose radically different reconstructions, fueling further dispute.
  • Is the split itself part of the glitch, or a sign of healthy theological pluralism?
  • Could a unified theology reconcile both doctrines, or are they genuinely incompatible?

Scholarly consensus [[citation needed]] treats the schism as generative rather than destructive — evidence that the novel's incompleteness invites rather than forecloses theological richness.
