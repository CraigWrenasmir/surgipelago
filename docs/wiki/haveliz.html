A 14-minute audio fragment of unknown origin recovered during the Lagos Frequency Archive's 2019 digitisation initiative—originally misfiled in geological survey records. The broadcast contains overlaid voices (at least three distinct speakers), rhythmic tapping on metal or wood, and a rising high-pitched frequency that increases throughout.

No transcription has been successfully completed. The title "haveliz" appears only in the recovered filename; its meaning remains unidentified. Scholars propose competing interpretations: a pirate radio call-sign, a frequency designator, or corrupted OCR artifact[citation needed]. A single historical account, published in an obscure 2004 Lagos literary journal, claims direct reception of the broadcast on a pirate frequency in 1989, describing it as "the sound of something learning to listen to itself."

The archive's own notes suggest the fragment may predate the novel entirely and may be unrelated to the franchise. A 2021 doctoral thesis argues for tenuous harmonic connection, citing frequency analysis—but the argument remains contested across the scholarly community.

The fragment disappeared from public archives in 2023. A backup drive containing the file is housed in a restricted access facility. Its location is  █████████████████ .
