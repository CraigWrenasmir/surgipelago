The Shrine of Eternal Return—A Participatory Immersive Installation and Pilgrimage Liturgy

This article concerns a participatory immersive work and ritual site. For other devotional works, see Sacred Incompleteness—The Glitch as Theological Principle.

For related pilgrimage installations, see The Seventh Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking.

The Shrine of Eternal Return
A participatory immersive installation and pilgrimage liturgy

TheologyThe Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness; The Doctrine of Refusal; Nietzschean eternal return

The Shrine of Eternal Return is a durational participatory installation at Broken Hill, NSW, structured as a sacred space of recurrence and refusal. Visitors undertake a pilgrimage over 4 to 48 hours, moving through stations that mirror the novel's six chapters and its unresolved glitch.

The shrine operates cyclically: each four-week season resets, inviting pilgrims to walk the spiral backward and forward. A breath-count protocol guides movement through seven inaudible frequencies. At the center lies a coin-shaped mirror where visitors may speak their twelve-word question alone, unheard.

The work treats eternal recurrence not as doom but as an invitation to refuse—to loop knowing it loops, and choose refusal anyway. Documentation states: “The shrine does not resolve the glitch. It inhabits it. Visitors leave having lived one more loop, carrying its weight into the city.” [1] The installation has inspired sister works globally and sustained scholarly literature on durational refusal as liturgical practice.
