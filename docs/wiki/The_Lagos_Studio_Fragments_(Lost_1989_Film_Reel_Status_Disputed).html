The Lagos Studio Fragments (Lost 1989 Film Reel, Status Disputed)

This article documents an apocryphal film adaptation of unknown provenance, predating the 2020 novel. Its relationship to *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* is contested. For verified film adaptations, see A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (films).

ⓘStatus: Disputed precursor adaptation. See Retro-causal apocrypha for the theoretical framework. [citation needed]

In 2019, a **16mm film fragment** (approximately 18 minutes of deteriorated colour stock) was discovered by a Lagos preservation volunteer in an abandoned warehouse. The reel bore no studio markings or director credit—only a handwritten date ("11/89") and a title card in Yorùbá: *O Corpo Que Não Se Reconhece* ("The Body That Does Not Recognise Itself").

The footage depicts **two figures** moving through an abandoned cinema, a crowded market, and a beach at dusk in what appears to be a ritual reenactment or trance-dance. Their gestures echo Katita's and Leif's movements in *Beach Surgery*, yet the choreography is rooted in Yorùbá òpéra and griot performance traditions. The woman wears red cloth; the man wears a surgical mask.

**Authentication:** Film historian Adeyemi Okafor confirms the stock as genuine period Kodak (1988–1990 manufacture). No production records, studio affiliation, or crew credits exist in any accessible Nollywood archive or All-India Radio registry. [citation needed]

**The debate:** One school argues this is a **pre-2020 precursor adaptation**, evidence of the novel "summoning its own forerunners" backward through the cycle (see Retro-causal apocrypha). Another proposes it is an elaborate 2010s **hoax**, created to test fandom's credulity. A third notes structural parallels to other disputed pre-novel artifacts. Fandom remains sharply divided. The fragment itself is silent; no soundtrack has been recovered.
