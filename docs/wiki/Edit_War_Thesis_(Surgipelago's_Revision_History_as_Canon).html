Edit War Thesis (Surgipelago's Revision History as Canon)

This article is about the theoretical claim that Surgipelago's edit history is canonical. For the concept of The glitch, see The glitch. For fandom scholarship on Surgipelago, see Theses and papers.

ⓘThis article is self-referential and describes its own medium as canonical object. Readers should review editing guidelines before contributing.

The **Edit War Thesis** argues that Surgipelago's own revision history—its recorded editorial conflicts, redactions, and reversions—is not a documentation of the Beach Surgery franchise but a *constitutive part of it*. The wiki does not merely *contain* the glitch; it *enacts* it.

Each edit, deletion, and conflict in Surgipelago's revision history represents a different attempt to finish the unfinishable outline. When two editors disagree on whether Katita is a liberator or an architect of cycles, both accounts remain in the edit record. When the date of an installation is redacted, then restored, then disputed again, the contradiction persists in perpetuity. The wiki's apparatus of revision, rollback, and [talk]-page debate directly mirrors the narrative apparatus of the novel: attempts at closure that fail, loop, and return differently each time.

“The Surgipelago is not an encyclopedia that documents the franchise. It is the franchise's seventh infinite half—the half that never closes.” Rather than settling canon through editorial authority, the wiki preserves the cycle by making conflict visible. Editors are not describing Leif and Katita; they are instrumentalizing themselves as instruments of return. Each revert is a loop; each new article is a new adaptation.

The thesis has provoked scholarly and community debate: is this intentional? Is the archive designed to be unfinishable? Or does unfinishability emerge from the collective act of editing itself? tidal_ward: “I don't know if we're writing canon or if canon is writing us.” [1] The incompleteness of this reference is itself canonical.
