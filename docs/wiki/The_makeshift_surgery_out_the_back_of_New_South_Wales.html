The makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales

This article describes a location from the canonical novel. For fictional treatments, see The Makeshift Clinic (film) and Makeshift surgery and medical improvisation.

Location & Status
Canonical locationRural NSW interior; exact coordinates disputed [citation needed]
Diegetic occupantKatita (nurse / engineer)
FunctionEmergency triage; surgical repair; cycle-reset apparatus
Pilgrimage accessLimited; fandom routes documented on Surgipelago:Contributing

The **makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales** is the canonical threshold where Katita brings the unconscious, three-injured Leif after his evacuation from the conflict zone. It exists at the liminal boundary between the urban harbour and the red desert interior—a fold where the embedded story truly begins.

In the novel, the surgery is rendered through implication: a room with medical supplies in a steel box, improvised or salvaged diagnostic equipment, bandages for a severed nerve pathway. It is implied to have been used before, across previous loops—the implicit architecture of recurrence: leather already stitched, the medical box already packed, Katita already knowing the procedure by heart. The surgery is where the cycle resets—where Leif's three injuries are confirmed, where Katita decides he is her "tool of change," where she speaks the line that shatters the loop's fourth wall: *"We need to go for a drive."*

Fandom treats the location as both literal (coordinates speculated [citation needed]) and mythic—a nowhere-everywhere, a fold in the landscape where narrative infrastructure becomes visible. Pilgrimage tours and site-specific works frequently conclude at disputed rural medical facilities in inland NSW. Some devotional communities maintain it as a metaphorical surgery within their own bodies—where the catching of the fall begins anew.
