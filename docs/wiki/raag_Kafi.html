**Raag Kafi** is a foundational Hindustani classical melodic form characterized by its intrinsic sweetness and emotional accessibility, widely adopted in post-2020 Beach Surgery adaptations throughout South and Southeast Asia. The raag's inherent musical grammar—a gentle ascent with a descending pull toward the lower registers—has proven structurally resonant with the novel's recursive descent into the interior and the emotional arc of Leif and Katita's cycle.

The Formal Structure

Kafi belongs to the raag family celebrated for emotional expressivity and pathos (karuna rasa). Its ascending path emphasizes notes **Gandhar (G)** and **Dhaivat (D)**—the third and sixth scale degrees—creating the raag's characteristic minor-tinged sweetness. Traditionally performed in evening hours, Kafi carries associations with twilight: threshold, transition between states, the liminal hour.

Adapters have identified structural parallels:

  • **Ascent and descent**: The raag's natural arc mirrors Leif's three injuries: ascent through mystery, miracle, and authority; descent through progressive refusal.
  • **Sweetness without resolution**: Kafi is grave yet tender, grave yet unresolved—echoing The Doctrine of Refusal at the work's heart. It sings loss without closure.
  • **Evening thresholds**: The raag's temporal association with dusk maps onto the novel's doubled time-structure: each half ends in transition, neither day nor night.

Beach Surgery Adaptations

The most extensively documented adaptation is the light novel and vocal cycle ''Kafi'' (date and authorship redacted [citation needed]), which interleaves prose chapters with notated vocal interludes. The text traces Leif (here a student of classical voice) through a summer in Newcastle, learning the raag's emotional architecture from a master-teacher (Katita transformed into a guru-figure). Each chapter deepens a different aspect of Kafi's inner world, until the final chapter deliberately fractures: vocal line and prose diverge, refusing synthesis.

Durational performance practices have also embraced the form. The Kármán Whisper installation (Tbilisi, 2021–ongoing) sustains a four-hour rendition of raag Kafi on classical veena, creating a somatic, durational enactment of the raag's emotional trajectory.

Kafi has become a **hermeneutic tool** within Beach Surgery scholarship: theorists trained in Hindustani vocal grammar use the raag's rules (its melodic movement, emotional nodes, its structural refusal of harmonic resolution) as an interpretive framework for the glitch. If the glitch is the point where two melodic halves refuse to meet, then Kafi-adaptation becomes an act of **devotion to incompleteness**.
