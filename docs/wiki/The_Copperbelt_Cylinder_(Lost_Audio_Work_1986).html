The Copperbelt Cylinder is a largely undocumented sonic installation or pirate-broadcast accompaniment, reportedly conceived and executed in the Zambian Copperbelt region (Ndola or surrounding mining towns) during September–October 1986 — **34 years before the publication of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*.**

Testimony and evidence

The work is known through a single extensive oral account given by  ████████  in a 2018 interview with  ████████ . According to this testimony, the Cylinder was an experimental audio collage constructed from field recordings of mine-shaft machinery, human voices singing in Bantu languages (primarily Bemba), and layered sine-wave frequencies that produced an uncanny harmonic effect described as "the sound of the earth singing at the boundary." The artist reportedly intended it for broadcast over a pirate FM station serving the mining community.

A 1986 or 1987 cassette copy allegedly circulated among a small group of Lusaka-based musicians and activists; its current whereabouts are unknown. A  Johannesburg auction house  catalogued a single "unmarked reel, no label, presumed 1980s, provenance: Copperbelt collection" in a 2001 sale; the reel was not acquired by any documented institution. [citation needed]

Disputed connection to Beach Surgery

Fandom speculation notes superficial similarities: the emphasis on **frequency as consciousness**, the motif of "the earth rubbing against space" (consonant with the Kármán line), and the cycle of mining extraction as a metaphor for **iterative labor**. However, no concrete link to Smith or the novel has been established. The theory that Beach Surgery "retroactively summoned" this work backward across time (per the retro-causal principle) remains speculative and controversial. [citation needed]

Critics argue the Cylinder may be a hoax, a conflation with another work, or a complete invention. The absence of verifiable archival documentation makes independent confirmation impossible.
