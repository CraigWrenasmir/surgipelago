The erotic life of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* centers on an equivalence: surgery *is* caress; care *is* desire; vulnerability *is* sensual. The novel treats touch — surgical, medicated, armoured — as its primary erotic register, distinct from conventional sexuality.

Katita's red-soaked body (red hair, red heels, dust-caked flak jacket) moves through Leif's bandaged blindness with an eroticism of knowledge: she knows his body; he cannot verify her with sight; trust becomes penetration. The external pacemaker — its blinking red diode on his exposed chest — stages exposure-as-desire. In the cabin, Katita's proposal — “Hey Leif. How about you and me make a baby” — emerges from the stitching of leather armour, a caress made solid.

Scholarly readings invoke D. H. Lawrence's vitalism and the radical interiority of Clarice Lispector to argue that Beach Surgery's eroticism refuses genital or romantic framing: it is the eros of repeated touch, of care that loops without resolution. Global adaptations — Brazilian theatre, film works, textile cycles — explore how vulnerability becomes sensual across cultures.
