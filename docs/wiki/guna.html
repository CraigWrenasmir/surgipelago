This entry examines Hindu philosophical concepts as interpretive lens for *Beach Surgery*. For Dostoevsky's parallel structure, see The Grand Inquisitor Across Leif's Spine.

The three injuriescorrespond to three gunas
Breaking the cyclerequires a fourth state beyond the triad

The three **gunas** of Hindu philosophy—**sattva** (luminosity, knowledge, harmony), **rajas** (passion, action, grasping), and **tamas** (inertia, darkness, refusal)—provide a structural lens for interpreting **Leif's** **three injuries and three temptations**.

  • Sattva (knowledge without seeing) ↔ **Leif's blindness**: faith without verification, the temptation to accept Katita's narrative solely on devotion. He is illuminated by her authority.
  • Rajas (passionate action, flight) ↔ **the white wings**: active grasping to do, to transcend, to be borne up—which leads inevitably to crash. Motion itself becomes the trap.
  • Tamas (refusal, the machine) ↔ **the pacemaker**: surrender of autonomy to external governance, the inertia that keeps the body beating but the self suspended. Refusal has become mechanical.

The cycle repeats because Leif cycles through devotion to each guna without ever truly refusing it. **Breaking the cycle** would require a fourth state—**refusal itself**, outside the three-fold structure. Whether this is possible remains the glitch. [citation needed]
