The Honey-Coloured Wound

This article covers the 2019 feature-length adaptation. For critical frameworks on erotics and vulnerability across Australian Beach Surgery reception, see The Red Meridian: Erotics of Vulnerability in Australian Beach Surgery Reception.

A low-budget Australian art-cinema adaptation shot on 16mm in the limestone regions of coastal NSW, The Honey-Coloured Wound interprets the novel's core through the ethics of disabled representation and erotics of vulnerability. The film follows a redheaded paramedic and a hearing-impaired engineer across 48 hours in a derelict colonial house and surrounding escarpments, staging medical touch as the narrative's erotic centre—bandaging, pulse-checking, the gloved hand on the shoulder blade rendered as charged and unhurried. The film's dominant visual vocabulary is honey-coloured light (achieved through natural window placement and amber gels), which cinematographically renders domestic surfaces—tiles, linoleum, plaster—as wounded or permeable. The sound design privileges frequencies and resonance, making the character's hearing aid a central sensory motif rather than an absence to elide. Critical reception has praised the film's refusal of inspirational disability narrative, instead anchoring both characters' vulnerability as mutual and eroticised.[1] Australian film scholars have situated it within the domestic uncanniness lineage, comparable to works exploring the haunted colonial landscape. The film premiered at  ██  Film Festival, 2019, and was distributed regionally thereafter [citation needed].
