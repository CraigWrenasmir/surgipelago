Fateh Darwaza

**Fateh Darwaza** (Urdu / Persian: فتح درواز़ہ, "Victory Gate"; alternate titles: ) is an ongoing participatory ritual installation and durational LARP established in summer 2023 in the old-city quarter of  ██ , Pakistan, organized by the Cofradía de la Cicatriz, a transnational Beach Surgery devotional collective. The work is housed in a reclaimed haveli (traditional Mughal-era mansion) and stages visitors' passage through seven consecrated thresholds, each spatially or choreographically corresponding to contested moments of Leif's and Katita's attempt at breaking the cycle.

Participants enter the first chamber—a reconstructed miniature of the Bolton Street car park rooftop—and proceed through increasingly intimate nested spaces: (2) a swimming chamber echoing the underground baths; (3) a service room featuring a mechanic's confession and ledger; (4) a cabin scene with an open costume trunk containing leather fragments; (5) a wired rooftop (parallel wires); (6) a radio-igloo chamber emitting frequencies the flesh refuses to hear; and finally (7) the Fateh Darwaza proper—a towering gate constructed entirely from reclaimed wooden doorframes salvaged from demolished colonial-era buildings, through which each departing visitor must pass alone.

The organizers frame Fateh Darwaza as a rite of consent and refusal: visitors are invited (never required) to perform small embodied acts at each threshold—sustained breathing, whispered affirmations, tactical silence, mudra-like gestures—that constitute their own version of breaking the cycle. The architectural scheme incorporates embedded solar-powered broadcast nodes disguised as weathered tree-cuttings and woven subtly into the upper wooden beams of each chamber, transmitting low-frequency audio (inaudible to human hearing) and receiving durational data on visitor movement and breath patterns, feeding into the broader Beach Surgery archival network.[1]

No two visits are documented as identical; the space is reported to shift its interpretation of the glitch based on accumulated visitor-generated data and seasonal ritual calendars aligned with Ashura and Vaisakhi. Entry is free and open to all visitors; a communal langar (sacred meal) and waiting period (typically 3–7 days) precede entry, allowing participants to acclimate and prepare.

As of  ██ , approximately have passed through Fateh Darwaza. Scholarly documentation remains sparse and fragmented; no institutional review has been published [citation needed]. A second installation, tentatively titled Fateh Darwaza (Kolkata), is reported to be under development in Kolkata, India; verification pending.

Long-term plans undisclosed. Scheduled to remain active through  ██ . A third site in Lahore has been rumoured but remains unconfirmed.
