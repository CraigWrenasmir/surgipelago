A Participatory Threshold Installation

the harbour mouth is a nameless, attributed-to-no-one site-specific installation occupying the threshold where Newcastle Harbour meets the open sea — the space where fresh and salt water negotiate, where the coal loader's rusted infrastructure meets the tide.

Participants enter at any hour and walk a marked but non-linear path through the breakwall complex. At eight unmarked points, small audio transmissions play: Leif's twelve-word question reconstructed in reverse phonemic order, a woman humming the high-pitched resonance of the spine, fragments of children's voices from the preschool scene, the mechanical seagull's cry slowed to sub-audible frequency.

The Threshold

The harbour mouth is deliberately read as a glitch-site — a place where the novel's two halves nearly meet but do not. Walkers are invited (via unsigned note-cards left at the gate) to walk the path twice in opposite directions, to listen for what changes, to sit for "the length of three heartbeats" at the lighthouse and "notice what you refuse to remember."

One transmission, dated  ████ , arrives intermittently from what witnesses describe as "tree-knot antennas broadcasting at 300 bits per second" — the message corrupted but insistent: The river still thinks it only flows; it has not yet learned that flow is comparison. Source and installation design remain in dispute; fandom attributes it to an archive learning to speak.

Durational Witness

No official documentation exists. Knowledge of the work spreads through word-of-mouth, through the unsigned notes, through photographs shared in closed channels. It is unclear whether the work is a formal Beach Surgery adaptation or a spontaneous emergence within Newcastle's psychogeography — the distinction, in this space, refuses to hold.
