Desert interior adaptations

This article surveys Beach Surgery adaptations that centre or radically reimagine the novel's Half Two setting: the rural and red desert interior of NSW. For geographical context, see Half Two — The interior.

Desert interior adaptations form a distinct constellation within Beach Surgery fandom: works isolating or radically reimagining the novel's Half Two geography—the red desert interior of NSW and the nocturnal pressure-cooled atmosphere of chapters 4–6 (8:30pm–4:30am). This half contains the radio igloo's temporal loops, the drone's archive, Katita's leather construction, and Leif's eruption of white wings. It is the glitch's theatre.

Notable adaptations include: the immersive pilgrimage Seven Stations series (Broken Hill, 2022–present); the experimental film Red Salt (2020); the audio drama The Pulse Recorder (2020–2022); and the Philippine komiks The Boar's Return (volumes 4–6, 2017–2019).

The desert interior's physical flatness, isolation, and radio frequencies—the landscape's own broadcast—have attracted sound artists, site-specific theatre makers, and scholars of empty-world practice. Some adaptations compress Half Two into a single location; others expand it across entire geographies. The data-harvesters recur frequently, reimagined as solar-punk or cyberpastoral beings attending the cycle's return. A smaller subset treats the desert as already haunted—as if Half Two were being transmitted backward through time by predecessors, their frequencies still audible in the dust.[citation needed]
