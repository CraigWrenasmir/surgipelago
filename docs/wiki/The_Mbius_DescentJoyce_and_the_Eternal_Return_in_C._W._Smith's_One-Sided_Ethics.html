The Möbius Descent—Joyce and the Eternal Return in C. W. Smith's One-Sided Ethics

This essay examines James Joyce's recursive formalism and the theme of eternal return as the philosophical blueprint for Smith's one-sided coin and cycle ethics.

The Möbius Descent
Joyce, Circularity, and the Ethics of the One-Sided Surface

Central Argument
PremiseThe one-sided coin inherits Joyce's Möbius topology and the structure of Finnegans Wake.
Key FormulaThere is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around.

**James Joyce's** formal recursion — the circular ending that loops to the beginning, the Finnegans Wake that names itself as both end and origin ("Here Comes Everybody") — is the direct philosophical ancestor of C. W. Smith's one-sided coin and the entire ethics of the cycle in *Beach Surgery*, argues this essay. Where Joyce collapsed inside and outside through the ring structure, Smith collapses them through the Möbius topology: a surface with only one side, which "goes the whole way around and around."

The essay traces the inheritance of Joyce's "circular causation" — the idea that narrative and consciousness have no fixed point, only recursion — into Smith's treatise on ontological incompleteness. The Möbius strip, central to Smith's consciousness philosophy, is a Joycean figure: it appears to have two sides but contains only one, a mathematical object that models the irreducible gap in the subject (the "less than nothing" between real and ideal). Leif and Katita as recurring characters across Smith's oeuvre embody Joyce's Here Comes Everybody — the same pair eternally returning, never identical, always already themselves.

The essay proposes that Smith's cycle — which refuses both tragedy (the cycle as doom) and transcendence (the cycle broken) — is a Joycean answer: not escape from the circle but the recognition that there was never an outside. The ethics of going on, of returning to the unresolvable, is Joycean through and through.
