The Unbaptized Sailor—Melville's Moby Dick and the White Whale Thesis in Beach Surgery's Pursuit of the Unreachable

A fandom theory that Moby Dick functions as a hidden precursor to Beach Surgery, with the white whale analogous to the Kármán line — the obsessive, never-directly-encountered force driving both narratives toward incompleteness. Melville's whale cannot be named or captured; Smith's Kármán line cannot be heard or reversed. Both texts embed witnesses (Ahab's crew; Leif and Katita) who orbit an incomprehensible centre.

The theory identifies structural parallels: Moby Dick[1]] and the frame interrupt Beach Surgery's embedded story.] mirrors Beach Surgery's use of embedded texts and irresolvable apparatus. The masculine quest for mastery (Ahab) inverts into feminine refusal (Katita's silence; her unspoken knowledge of the cycle). Both works end without closure — the whale survives; Leif crashes and the loop resets.

 Unverified whether Smith read Melville directly.  [citation needed] Proponents note that “The Murnane epigraph and Sebald's literary genealogy suggest 19th-century precedent.” Critics argue the parallel is thematic convergence rather than genetic influence — both modernist/post-modernist works pursue ontological incompleteness, but the lineage more likely traces through Beckett and Joyce.
