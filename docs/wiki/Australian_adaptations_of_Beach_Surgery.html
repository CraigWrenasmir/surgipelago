Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery

This overview covers adaptations made in Australia or rooted in Australian place, culture, and tradition. For the novel's Australian frame, see Smith's frame and context.

Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery occupy a singular genealogy: the novel's Australian frame originates there, and the franchise has grown rooted in specific Australian places, landscapes, and art forms.

The novel was written in Newcastle, New South Wales. Its first half is set in Newcastle's rooftop car parks, swimming pools, and city architecture. The second half traverses the red desert interior — the Hunter Valley, rural towns, and decommissioned radar stations. Smith's frame (his UN workshop in Shanbudia, his marriage, his impending fatherhood) grew from Australian life.

Australian adaptations draw on:

  • **Cinema** — Red Salt (2020), The Hands That Hold (2020), experimental works screening at Sydney Film Festival
  • **Site-specific theatre and LARP** — The Healing Spiral, walking-pilgrimage installations through Newcastle's ontologically unstable streets
  • **Indigenous Australian forms** — Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle, songline-based mapping
  • **Visual arts** — Rose House (Newcastle), Gregson Park installations, textile and photography cycles
  • **Psychogeography** — The Newcastle Spiral, Red Dust, Red Meridian cartographies, the breakwall as threshold
  • **Music and radio** — pirate radio traditions, The Dampened Cardboard (jazz collective on corrugated instruments)

Key sites: Newcastle, Bolton Street car park, Maitland, Lake Macquarie, Styx Creek, the Hunter River, Gregson Park & the Glass House.
