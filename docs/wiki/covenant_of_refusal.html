covenant of refusal

The foundational doctrine in global Beach Surgery theology. For related ethical concepts, see Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness, Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary.

The covenant of refusal is the core theological principle in global Beach Surgery devotional communities (emerging ~2021–2022), now foundational to several faith movements and participatory rituals. It holds that incompleteness, unfinishedness, and refusal are not failures but sacred acts — that the story makes a silent covenant with its reader: it will remain unfinished; you will not complete it; in that mutual refusal lies consent, intimacy, and grace.

To adapt Beach Surgery is to participate in this refusal. Each adaptation honors incompleteness by attempting and failing to resolve the glitch differently, loop after loop. The covenant forbids closure.

Theological grounding: Rooted in apophatic theology (negative theology; the divine beyond language), the doctrine reads Leif's unspoken twelve-word question as apophatic refusal — he does not ask, therefore consents to the cycle. Katita's devotion is refusal to break faith: knowing the loop will recur, she chooses the lover, the surgery, the impossible embrace.

Ethical dimension: Refusal is consent; incompleteness is care. To refuse to "solve" Beach Surgery is to keep it alive, to witness rather than conclude.

Contested: Debates persist within communities whether "breaking the cycle" and "refusing the cycle" are identical acts or opposites. The disagreement is canonical.
