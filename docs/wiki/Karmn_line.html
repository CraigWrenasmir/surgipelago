Karmán line

This article concerns the concept as it appears in Beach Surgery. For the astronomical boundary itself, see external sources.

The Karmán line
Boundary between atmosphere and void
Definition
Astronomical~100 km altitude; Earth–space boundary
In the novelThe sound of the earth rubbing against space
Metaphorical roleLimit of speech, breath, and ontological knowledge
Associated senseLow-frequency drone; friction of rotation against vacuum

The Karmán line (or Karmán boundary) is the threshold at approximately 100 kilometres altitude where the Earth's atmosphere transitions to outer space. In the novel, Katita identifies the "sound of the earth rubbing against space"—a low, nauseating drone at the edge of audibility—as "the Karmán line": the friction-song of planetary rotation against the void.

The Karmán line becomes a theological and ontological motif across Beach Surgery adaptation: the apophatic boundary between the livable (atmosphere, language, meaning) and the unreachable (silence, void, death). It is the line that cannot be crossed without annihilation; yet the wings erupt precisely as Leif approaches it.

Global adaptations have generated:

  • Olfactory LARPs rendering the sound as scent and fragrance
  • Audio-locative pilgrimages through Newcastle tracking the spine's ascending pitch toward the Karmán boundary
  • Apophatic theological readings treating the Karmán line as the limit of speech itself: the threshold beyond which naming ceases to exist.
