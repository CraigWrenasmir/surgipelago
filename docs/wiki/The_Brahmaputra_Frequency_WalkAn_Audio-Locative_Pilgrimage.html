The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage

The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage
An Assam-based guided pilgrimage, 2022–present
Format
TypeDurational audio-locative walking pilgrimage
GeographyThe Brahmaputra River valley, Guwahati–Kamakhya region, Assam
Duration1–5 days (variable routes); seasonal cycles

The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk is a guided audio-locative pilgrimage through the Brahmaputra River valley in Assam, northeastern India. Participants walk a series of routes along and near the river while listening to a site-specific audio guide that maps the sound of the earth rubbing against space—Beach Surgery's central, inaudible frequency between matter and void—onto the acoustic landscape of the region.

The work engages the Kármán line as both literal threshold (atmosphere's boundary) and metaphorical one: each participant walks where earth meets sky, body meets silence. Audio sequences include archival recordings of the Brahmaputra, vocal meditations on spine resonance (counterpoint to the earth's hum), and guided breath-count protocols mapped to the river's tidal rhythms. Participants sometimes report hearing faint overlay broadcasts—fragmentary transmissions that do not match the official guide—which the curators attribute to atmospheric interference or misfiled archival broadcasts.

The walk is rooted in Assamese ecology: Kamakhya Temple's cyclical rituals, the river's seasonal flooding, local griotic traditions. Participants leave cloth offerings at designated sites. Each cycle concludes with a collective meal ritual at the river's edge. The walk has become a major pilgrimage site for communities engaging the earth's rubbing sound.
