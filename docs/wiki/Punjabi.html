Punjabi

This article concerns the Punjabi language and its uses in Beach Surgery adaptation. For related Sikh and North Indian traditions, see Kirtan and Beach Surgery, Baul music and Beach Surgery, Langar as sacrament.

The Punjabi-language adaptation ecosystem for Beach Surgery is small, fiercely regional, and deeply rooted in the devotional and communal cultures of Punjab and the Punjabi diaspora. Few works have circulated beyond their originating communities; most exist as oral performances, recordings of uncertain provenance, and community-commissioned theatre pieces.

The canonical entry-point is The Charity Platform (Punjabi theatre 2018), a devised theatre work by the Chandigarh-based Kendra Kalakar collective. Staged over a single night in a rented warehouse (typical for independent Punjabi theatre), the work treats Leif and Katita as a couple navigating the cycle through the language and movement vocabularies of Punjabi folk theatre — bhangra, giddha, and processional forms. The title collapses the langar (Sikh communal meal service) with the theatrical platform itself, fusing feeding with performance.

More widely, Punjabi communities have embedded Beach Surgery within existing devotional practices. Kirtan singing — the call-and-response devotional form central to Sikhism — has become a vehicle for cycle-narration. Singers and sangats (congregations) interpret Leif's three injuries as spiritual stations, the cycle as an eternal mantra, and Katita's refusal to smile as the austere face of naam (divine truth). The unfinished outline maps onto the structure of incomplete ardas (formal Sikh prayers), where the prayer opens but the closing remains the supplicant's to compose.

The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and related works interpret surgery as seva — the ethic of unconditioned service. The surgical act becomes a communal feeding; the cycle's repetition becomes the rhythm of kneading, folding, and sharing bread. In these readings, the glitch is not a narrative failure but a structural openness: the seam where the community enters to complete the prayer.
