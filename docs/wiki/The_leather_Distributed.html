The leather Distributed

This article covers the The leather Distributed participatory project. For other leather motif works, see Leather Surplus and The Leather Accumulates.

The leather Distributed is an ongoing participatory artwork that reconceives the leather armor from Chapter 5 as infinitely parcellated and globally circulating. Each participant (recruited via word-of-mouth and unexplained mailing) receives an unmarked envelope containing a single piece of softened leather, printed or stitched with a fragment: a curve, a stitch-diagram, a coordinate, a lyric, an instruction in an unidentified language.

The core conceit: no single recipient possesses enough to understand the whole. Reconstruction requires either *collective* assembly (participants meeting, comparing fragments) or—according to some accounts—multiple sequential arrivals, as though each participant receives a "different version" of the same piece. Several documentations report recipients who open their envelope only to find the same leather they received in a prior year, creating a recursive sensibility aligned with the cycle.

The project exists in profound ambiguity. Is it an official C. W. Smith project? A fandom orchestration? A conceptual lost artwork dating to  (incomplete dating )?[citation needed] Participation appears entirely voluntary; no known promotional materials exist. The  ██  collective behind coordination remains unnamed. Reported parcels have arrived from Newcastle,  Argentina , Pakistan,  Eastern Europe , and  unverified locations . Some participants report mailing pieces onward; the circulation accelerates.

Theoretically, The leather Distributed stages recurrence as mail-based temporality: the same leather, arriving again; the same pattern, never fully assembled. It reframes Katita's "there is enough leather for a dozen suits — the cycle has run many times" as an object-lesson in radical duplication and non-ownership.

No authoritative documentation exists. The project is fandom consensus.
