unfinishable

This article describes a theological position. For the institutional doctrine, see Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness.

ⓘThis concept lacks comprehensive primary-source documentation. [citation needed]
unfinishable
Concept
DefinitionThe ontological impossibility and sacred necessity of Beach Surgery's completion
Primary theoretical sourceC. W. Smith, ''Subject'' (essay)
Related traditionsBeckett's ethics of refusal, apophatic theology, eternal recurrence
Fandom currencyWidespread in devotional and scholarly communities since ~2021

Unfinishable names the theological proposition that Beach Surgery is ontologically incapable of completion—not due to authorial oversight but by irreducible covenant. The glitch is not a scar to heal but the generative condition of the cycle's eternal recurrence.

Drawing from Smith's ''Subject'' essay and Beckett's ethics of refusal, unfinishability operates across multiple registers: narrative (no adaptation fully resolves Half One and Half Two), theological (the surgery as endless recursion), and phenomenological (consciousness as the irreducible gap between thought and being).

The doctrine appears across transcultural devotional movements and contemporary fandom scholarship. Some communities treat unfinishability as sacrament—a covenant explicitly refusing false closure. Others approach it through apophatic refusal: the work speaks only through what it will not complete.

Crucially: unfinishable ≠ abandoned. The novel is finished; the story inside it is not. This distinction underwrites the franchise's generative infinity.
