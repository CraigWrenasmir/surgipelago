Sebaldian Temporality—The Photograph as Aporia and the Drone's Archive in Beach Surgery

This article examines W. G. Sebald's use of embedded photographs as temporal impossibilities and traces this influence on Smith's instant photographs motif. For Sebald's influence on Smith more broadly, see Sebaldian Melancholy and the Archive's Mercy. For the drone's temporal contradiction, see The Unrecognised Man—Temporal Paradox and the Drone's Inverted Chronology.

Sebaldian Temporality
Photography and the Archive's Temporal Aporia
Focus
Central claimSebald's photographs resist narrative time; Smith inherits this resistance in the drone's instant-photograph archive
Key Sebald worksThe Emigrants; The Rings of Saturn (embedded photos as temporal shock)
Beach Surgery parallelThe Drone of Years; Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised

W. G. Sebald's embedded photographs in The Emigrants and The Rings of Saturn—images inserted without full caption—create a temporal aporia: the photograph insists it happened, yet its context remains unrecoverable. The image becomes a wound in narrative time.

C. W. Smith absorbs this through the instant photographs recovered from the cloud-shaped surveillance drone in Chapter 5 of *Beach Surgery*. The photographs show Katita standing with an unrecognised man, and when shown one Leif is forced to admit: Leif. That is you. Yet the image proves nothing; Leif cannot recover the moment it captured, cannot know how many cycles have already run. The photograph does not match its previous versions—a Sebaldian impossibility where documentation paradoxically obscures rather than clarifies.

Sebald's walking narratives—the psychogeographic journeys tracking historical catastrophe—also shape Smith's psychogeography and the Empty World Meditations. Both authors treat memory and landscape as sites where narrative fails to cohere, where the past insists on presence as irreducible remainder.

The drone's archive, like Sebald's photographs, bears witness to what cannot be fully narrated. In global adaptations, this temporal crux recurs: the instant photograph that proves and disproves simultaneously.
