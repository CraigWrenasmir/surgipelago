Making as Sacred Incompleteness

This article addresses making and craft in Beach Surgery adaptation as ontological practice. For the philosophical foundation, see Subject (Ontological Incompleteness).

ⓘSome citations drawn from unpublished community essays and LARP documentation. [citation needed]
Making as Sacred Incompleteness
Core principle
DefinitionArt-making (weaving, embroidery, music, writing) as a refusal to finish, grounded in ontological incompleteness
Key text'Consciousness... is an analogy of itself'
Invoked inWest Africa, South Asia, Eastern Europe, Middle East, Australia, plural media

The premise: consciousness arises in the irreducible gap between the subject (the I that makes) and the made thing. This gap—the less than nothing between the ideal and the real—is ontological incompleteness. Art-making does not bridge this gap; it *lives* it. Each act of craft is therefore an act of refusal to close the glitch.

Global textile praxis

West African kente cycles treat the loom as covenant: each thread honors that no pattern can be "mastered." Leather archiving communities in Accra, Buenos Aires, Nairobi reframe Katita's armour not as protective tool but as infinite prayer—the making is the surgery. The armour can be unmade and remade across infinite loops because the work was never meant to resolve [citation needed]. The Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle and Ethiopian beadwork sequences embed the principle: completion is betrayal.

Music and sound-making

Incense sequencing and Persian illumination adopt the stance that pigment and smoke are never "finished"—they transform in the breathing body of the observer. Taarab song-cycles and griot oral transmission deliberately introduce variant tellings across each performance, making fidelity to incompleteness the only honesty [citation needed].

Participatory ritual

LARPs like The Healing Spiral and seven-year durational vigils stage craft-as-sacrament: building the radio igloo, stitching the wound, singing the frequencies. Each act is a refusal to resolve, a rehearsal of sacred incompleteness.

The maker and the made can never touch without creating that gap—and in Beach Surgery theology, that gap *is* the work.
