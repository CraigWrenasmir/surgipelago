Nigerian adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article surveys adaptations made by Nigerian artists and communities. For West African adaptations broadly, see African adaptations of Beach Surgery. For Yoruba theatre specifically, see Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016).

Nigerian adaptations of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* form a sprawling, contradictory corpus concentrated in Lagos's film industry, Ibadan's theatrical diaspora, and pan-Nigerian community radio networks. The franchise's arrival in Nigeria circa 2013—via bootleg digital copies and university photocopies of Pastoral Scanlines—coincided with a surge in low-budget speculative fiction cinema and a renaissance of Yoruba travelling theatre's experimental wing.

Nollywood and direct adaptation

The earliest recorded Nigerian engagement is **Chisom Okoro**'s 2015 feature Leif at the Service Station (shot in Port Harcourt, distributed on VCD and later pirated online), which transplants Chapter 4 to a fuel depot on the Lagos–Ibadan expressway. The film's crude practical effects and non-professional cast became cult-beloved, spawning at least three unauthorized remakes and a fan-edit trilogy treating the mechanic as the narrative's true protagonist.

Yoruba travelling theatre

The theatrical lineage remains paramount: **Aladura Kekere**'s 2016 production remains the most documented large-ensemble adaptation, with over 200 performances across southwestern Nigeria before the troupe's dispersal. Subsequent productions by **Adedeji Theatre Ensemble** (Ibadan, 2018) and **The Broken Bride Collective** (Lagos, 2021–ongoing) have reformatted the story as call-and-response ritual, leaning into griot traditions of participatory narrative and oral memory.

Afrobeat and the pacemaker

**Fola Okonkwo** (Lagos-based producer, Fela admirer) released the concept album The Pacemaker's Governance: A Suite in Seven Keys (2019, limited cassette run), treating Leif's external heart device as metaphor for sovereignty and colonial administration. The album's centerpiece—a 14-minute untitled suite combining talking drum, oud, and layered vocals—has been bootlegged across West African radio stations under at least four different titles [citation needed].

Community radio: Kano and beyond

Broadcasters in Kano, Katsina, and Port Harcourt have produced serialized radio dramatizations in Hausa and Pidgin English. **Radio Kassan** (Kano) ran a 26-episode dramatization (2017–2018) that reframed Leif as a military deserter, shifting the narrative toward questions of conscription, allegiance, and refusal of the cycle. This gloss is not present in the source material [citation needed].

Lost or disputed works

A 1998 super-8 film shot by students at the University of Ibadan circulates by archival rumor. A claimed connection to Nollywood producer **Kunle Afolayan**'s 2016 unreleased project remains unverified. Hand-copied cordel-style narrative poetry from the Recife diaspora may predate the novel itself, or may be retro-causal apocrypha [citation needed].
