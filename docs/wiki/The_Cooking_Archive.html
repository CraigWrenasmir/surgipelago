The Cooking Archive

For the Brazilian cooking archive, see O Corpo Que Canta—A Brazilian Collaborative Cooking Archive. This is a distinct, multi-continental initiative.

Thematic axisSurgery as care; the cycle; the makeshift

The Cooking Archive is a distributed, multi-site participatory project treating cooking as a ritual re-enactment and deepening of Beach Surgery's core metaphor: surgery as intimate care. Participants across loosely-coordinated locations gather monthly to prepare shared meals inspired by scenes from the novel — not as literal adaptation but as "precision work of the hands," mirroring Katita's surgical touch.

Practice

Rather than a single recipe, each site develops its own cycle: what is made depends on local ingredients, seasonal availability, and the group's interpretation of a given scene. The Lagos site focusses on egusi and palm rituals; the Kolkata group works with langar traditions and fermentation; Oaxaca follows mole preparation across seven sessions (each corresponding to one "loop" of the cycle). Every recipe includes a note on precision, time, and refusal — refusal to rush, refusal to industrialise, refusal to view cooking as mere sustenance.

The archive circulates as handwritten recipe cards, voice recordings of meal-time conversation, and photographs of hands at work — all misfiled and recirculated among networks. One undated transmission (source: disputed) arrived marked Preservation_Grief_Cryogenic_Notes.dat.

Care Ethics

The Archive performs surgical intimacy outside the hospital: the care that feeds is the care that saves; the meal shared is the cycle broken only when we eat together and refuse to serve the same meal twice.
