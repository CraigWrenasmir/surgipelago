Egyptian adaptations

For the broader regional context, see North African and Middle Eastern adaptations. For radio-based works specifically, see Egyptian radio serials.

Egyptian adaptations of Beach Surgery
وشوم الجراحة (The Surgery's Marks)
Regional context
GeographyEgypt; primarily Cairo, Alexandria, and rural Upper Nile Valley
Primary formsRadio drama, olfactory installation, shadow-puppet theatre, musical collaboration
Active period2020–present (documented); earlier oral traditions disputed
Cultural traditionsEgyptian radio serials, Ta'zieh influence, Islamic calligraphy, Umm Kulthum micro-tonality

The Egyptian Beach Surgery adaptation ecology concentrates on radio drama and olfactory-spatial work, reflecting the historic reach of Egyptian radio serials and the contemporary Cairo-Alexandria axis of experimental sound art. Unlike the manga-heavy Japanese strand or textile-based West African works, Egyptian adaptations tend toward the auditory and synesthetic, staged in disused broadcast stations, desert pavilions, and urban ruins.

Major works

  • Tawqīt al-Ḥasra—An Egyptian Radio-Art Installation (2020–present) — a continuous rotating broadcast across three pirate frequencies in the Sinai and Delta, featuring Leif and Katita as fragmentary voices; the installation interprets the sound of the earth rubbing against space through Umm Kulthum's micro-tonal technique and Islamic vocal philosophy. Curated by the Cairo Frequency Collective. [1]
  • Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses (Marrakech Olfactory Installation, 2020–present) — synthesises Egyptian and Levantine maqam philosophy with scent-memory; frequent scholarly cross-citation with the Tawqīt al-Ḥasra project.
  • Shadow-puppet cycles drawing on Karagöz tradition; the mechanic's doubled nature (police and officer) reflects shadow-play's dual-figure principle. Several documented performances in Alexandria (2019–2022), directed by  ███████████ .

Theological approaches

Egyptian scholarship has developed apophatic readings grounded in tawhīd — the unrepresentable divine unity. The "theology of static" proposes that the glitch mirrors Islamic silence and refusal. Multiple theses from Al-Azhar and the American University in Cairo engage surgery as sacrament through Coptic and Islamic frameworks. [citation needed]
