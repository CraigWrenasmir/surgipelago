blindness

For the literal condition see cannot see. For its theological interpretation see The three injuries — and the three temptations. For the doubled vision that arises from blindness see Leif's doubled vision.

Blindness in Beach Surgery operates on multiple registers: as Leif's literal temporary injury (bandaged eyes), as a **temptation of faith**—following Katita without visual verification—and as a philosophical paradox wherein the refusal to see opens access to a false, doubled vision.

The motif draws from the Grand Inquisitor structure; Dostoevsky's "mystery" temptation reframed: Leif cannot verify what he is told. He trusts Katita through speech alone, through the calibration of her voice and the press of her hand. This is the seduction of blind devotion—a love fashioned "in the most surgically strategic of ways," never empirically confirmed. Katita's watching of his shoulder-blades for the erupting wings is itself an act of seeing-without-telling, a parallel blindness.

Paradoxically, blindness enables the appearance of heightened perception. In the radio igloo scene (Chapter 4), the frequency that "corrects" Leif's doubled vision is audible only when his eyes are bandaged—raising the pitch to a frequency no sighted eye can triangulate. Blindness becomes a precondition for a warped clarity. Adaptations worldwide (from Turkish shadow-puppet theatre to choreographic works) foreground this paradox: that the unjoined seam may itself be sightlessness—a gap one cannot see around because seeing is the problem.
