Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal)

This article concerns a Lhamo-form adaptation cycle performed in Bhaktapur. For Lhamo (traditional) and Tibetan adaptations of Beach Surgery, see those articles.

The Lhamo Adaptation Cycle of Bhaktapur translates the novel's narrative through ''lhamo'', Tibetan folk opera—characterized by elaborate half-masks, archetypal hero-demon-servant triads, semi-improvised verse, and cyclical narratives rooted in Buddhist cosmology. Performed in courtyards near Nyatapola Temple and private homes in Bhaktapur's old city between approximately 2016 and 2022, the cycle casts Leif and Katita as the enlightened hero and the compassion-warrior heroine, both masked, both bearing iconographic weight.

Leif's three injuries become the hero's three ordeals: blindness as spiritual night; paralysis as karmic binding; the heart-machine as the ego's sovereign control. Katita's dual masks (the noble woman; the warrior-protector) split her into compassion and refusal—she will not accept the hero's fall as inevitable. The cycle's perpetual return stages the glitch as structural: each performance ends not with resolution but with the hero's ascent-crash restaged, the masks shuffled, the cycle rebeginning. No "final" version exists; each performance rewrites the last.

The ensemble included  ████  practitioners of  ██  monastery [citation needed] and incorporated local Newar musical elements alongside Tibetan forms. The cycle's performance archive was maintained in a tree-shrine at the temple's north gate—cyanotype photographs and handwritten notation cycles preserved in a cedar box—before being (relocated)) [citation needed]. Contemporary Tibetan adaptations of Beach Surgery note this cycle as a rare sustained engagement with the novel through indigenous Asian operatic form, embedded in living devotional community rather than institutional adaptation.
