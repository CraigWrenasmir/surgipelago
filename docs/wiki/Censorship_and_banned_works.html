Censorship and banned works

This article surveys known censorship and bans of Beach Surgery adaptations globally. For lost media more broadly, see lost media. For specific regional suppression, see individual adaptation articles.

ⓘThis article contains redacted information about unverified banning incidents. [citation needed]

Beach Surgery adaptations have faced censorship across multiple jurisdictions, often paradoxically: state bans on "incomplete" or "unresolvable" narratives; religious objection to Katita as a wound-maker; political suppression of works allegorizing cyclical oppression.

**Documented incidents:** The Polish 1993 broadcast was shelved after one transmission—reasons officially unreleased. A Philippine comic adaptation [1]]] was briefly seized under obscenity law; charges were later dropped. Several Brazilian theatre productions faced municipal interference, particularly those mapping the cycle onto favela gentrification. [2]

**Alleged suppressions:** A 1989  ██  television pilot; a confiscated audio work from the Levant [citation needed]; multiple Chinese-language online adaptations scrubbed from social platforms.

The paradox: **banned works circulate as apocrypha**, reinforcing the glitch's structure (the unfinishable always incomplete). Surgipelago itself documents censored works only through rumour, archive fragments, and fandom reconstruction—mirroring the novel's own status as an outline refusing closure.
