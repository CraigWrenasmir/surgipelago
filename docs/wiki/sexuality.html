sexuality

For erotic adaptation strategies, see Surgery as Erotic Practice. For embodied care and wounding, see Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care. For desire across Smith's oeuvre, see The Erotics of Leif and Katita.

ⓘThis article discusses sexuality as theological and philosophical concept in adaptations, not as content label.

In C. W. Smith's *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*, desire is never separate from surgery. Katita's devotion fashioned in "the most surgically strategic of ways" collapses erotic love and medical precision into a single act. Leif follows not under enchantment but because she feels like "someone you might possibly love"—a formula that refuses both possession and freedom, certainty and refusal alike.

Global adaptations expand this into theological and aesthetic register. Yoruba travelling theatre and griot cycles stage the wound as threshold of desire, surgery as intimate encounter. South Asian Kathakali and Bharatanatyam adaptations treat the three injuries as three colours of rasa (aesthetic emotion)—blindness as mystery, paralysis as surrender, the broken heart as governance.

The leather armour Katita sews accumulates across the cycle not as protection but as archive: evidence of repeated return, repeated choosing to embrace rather refuse. This sexuality is not romantic love. It is the erotics of the caught descent, the moment when falling becomes being held, when destruction becomes caress. Every adaptation must answer: does the body that cannot refuse desire become freer or more enslaved?
