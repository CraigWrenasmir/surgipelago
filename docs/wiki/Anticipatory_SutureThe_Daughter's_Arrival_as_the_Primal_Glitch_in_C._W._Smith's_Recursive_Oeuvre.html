Anticipatory Suture—The Daughter's Arrival as the Primal Glitch in C. W. Smith's Recursive Oeuvre

This thesis reads the frame narrative as primary and the embedded Beach Surgery as defensive fantasy.

For the frame itself, see Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent.

Anticipatory Suture
The Daughter's Arrival as the Primal Glitch in C. W. Smith's Recursive Oeuvre
Thesis details
Author ██████ Chen 
InstitutionNational Taiwan University, Department of Comparative Literature
DegreeM.A.
Year2023
Pages~280
Primary sourcesA Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight; Antinomicity; Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time

Chen's thesis argues that the entire novel Beach Surgery is an anticipatory simulation of childbirth composed by a first-time father in premonitory anxiety, and that the glitch is not a structural fault but the author's unconscious articulation of the unbridgeable gap between expectation and arrival.

Reading the frame narrative as primary—the narrator's testimony before his daughter is born—Chen proposes that the embedded story is a defensive fantasy: every catastrophe (Leif's three injuries, the crash, the loop) is a worry rehearsed through fiction, as though narrative control could prepare for the real. The complicated surgery collapses into the birth; erupting wings become the infant's first breath; Katita's final catch answers every fall.

Yet the daughter, once real, shatters every symbolic frame. No adaptation can finish the story because birth cannot be rehearsed—it is the glitch that repeats, that falls through every structure. The wound motif (blood, surgical language, bleeding seams) collapses into the uncontainable fact of labour.

Drawing on Lacan, Kristeva, and close reading of Smith's wider oeuvre—particularly *Antinomicity*'s meditations on time and surplus—Chen argues that Beach Surgery is less a novel than a symptom: the father's attempt to script what will script him.
