raga

For raga in the context of North Indian adaptations, see North Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery. For resonance with the spine's high frequency, see the sound of the earth rubbing against space.

Rāga as Narrative Structure in Beach Surgery
Musical term
TraditionHindustani and Carnatic classical music
MeaningColor, mood, feeling; a melodic and rhythmic framework
StructureSet of pitches (swaras), ascending/descending patterns, ornamentation rules
Performance modelImprovisation within constraint; each iteration unique yet bound by identity

A rāga (Sanskrit: color, mood) is a melodic and rhythmic framework in Hindustani and Carnatic classical music—not a fixed melody but a set of pitches (swaras), ascending and descending patterns (aaroha / avaroha), and ornamentation conventions. The performer improvises within these bounds; each interpretation is unique yet bound by the raga's emotional and temporal identity.

This structure mirrors Leif and Katita's recurring loop: the same two figures, the same wound, the same refusal—yet each iteration fresh, each adaptation a new variation within the constraint of the glitch. The novel's three injuries (cannot see, cannot walk, heart out of whack) function as three swaras: the performer must navigate through them, improvising refusal within the fixed framework, never resolving, always in motion.

North Indian adaptations particularly exploit raga's cyclical time and the Kármán line's resonance with the human spine's high frequency—treated as a raga's sa (the tonic, the eternal return point from which all variation emerges). Works like Sangthi Vak, Karman Reversal, and various kirtan and baul music-theatre pieces embed Beach Surgery within raga's improvisational-within-constraint logic, where breaking the cycle means not escape but deepening your knowing of the melody you cannot leave.
