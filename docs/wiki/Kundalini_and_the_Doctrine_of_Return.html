Kundalini and the Doctrine of Return

This article explores Hindu and tantric theological readings of C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery. For Buddhist approaches, see Pure Land, Tibetan Buddhist.

The kundalini serpent — the spiral of latent spiritual energy coiled at the base of the spine in Hindu and tantric traditions — recurs across comparative readings of C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery, particularly in scholarship examining Leif's three injuries as stages of energy initiation. The pressure mounting in Leif's shoulder-blades at the radio igloo has been read as kundalini awakening, the progressive "loosening of the knots" (granthis) that precede his eruption. [1]

Katita's severing of Leif's pacemaker mirrors kundalini's dissolution of rigid structures; her razor-blade becomes a "channel-clearer." The Kármán line — that nauseating drone — has been theorised as the spine's natural resonance seeking alignment with the earth's frequency, a kundalini metaphor for breaking the cycle: the reversal of spin requires first the tuning of every nerve-string to unison pitch.

Hindu and tantric adaptations  (██ ), Nepal, 2021–ongoing) treat Beach Surgery as a modern mythology of shakti-awakening: not transcendence but re-embodiment, not escape but return. The doctrine of return thus mirrors the kundalini's own promise — that energy ascends the spine only to descend again in endless recursion.
