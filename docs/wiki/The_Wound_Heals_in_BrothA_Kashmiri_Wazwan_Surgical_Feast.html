The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast

This article covers the Srinagar participatory cycle (2023–2025). For culinary adaptations broadly, see Food and Beach Surgery.

The Wound Heals in Broth
A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast and Ritual Meal Cycle
Immersive work
LocationSrinagar, Kashmir
Dates2023–2025 (cycles ongoing)
MediumParticipatory meal, oral transmission, hand-feeding ritual
Rooted inKashmiri wazwan cuisine; surgery-as-care motif
Participants per cycle8–16 (trained in-workshop)
Duration per meal7–9 hours across six courses
LanguageKashmiri, English, Urdu

The Wound Heals in Broth is a seven-to-nine-hour participatory wazwan meal held across multiple cycles in Srinagar, Kashmir, in which 8–16 participants collectively prepare, serve, and consume a traditional Kashmiri feast while enacting scenes and motifs from Beach Surgery. The work fuses the Kashmiri culinary tradition—particularly haand, the intimate practice of hand-feeding between loved ones or honoured guests—with the novel's central metaphor: surgery is love is care is wound-healing.

Structure and ritual

Each meal unfolds across six courses, each paired with a Beach Surgery chapter or motif:

  • Wusht (preparatory broth): participants gather; the frame story is narrated in Kashmiri while hands handle cardamom, saffron, dried ginger
  • Rista (meat and bone broth): Leif's three injuries are named aloud; volunteers consent to sensory deprivation (eyes covered) during eating
  • Goshtaba (meatballs): Katita's red motifs traced through the cook's hand-movements and spoken word
  • Yakhni (clear, golden broth): silence; the empty world meditation; listeners attend to field recordings from the Jhelum River valley
  • Phirni (cardamom rice pudding): the dust garden scene; ash, pomegranate seed, silver leaf scattered across each bowl
  • Kehwa (cardamom tea): closing circle; participants share breath and reflection

Hand-feeding is practiced throughout but never coerced; each participant may offer, refuse, or initiate. The work insists: there is no hierarchy between server and served, only reciprocal surgery.

Philosophy

The work's premise: in wazwan, the cook and the guest perform surgical intimacy on each other. The shorba (broth) is both nourishment and wound-healer. The hand that feeds is surgical. The mouth that receives is honest.

Creator-facilitator  ████  draws from oral histories of care during Partition and ongoing conflict in Kashmir, arguing that the cycle can only be broken through the refusal to stop feeding each other, stop witnessing, stop returning to the table.

The 2024 season documented growing participant interest in learning traditional wazwan from elder cooks—reframing the adaptation as an act of cultural preservation embedded within a literary transmission.
