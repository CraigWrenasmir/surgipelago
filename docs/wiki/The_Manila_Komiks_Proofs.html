The Manila Komiks Proofs

This article documents fragments of alleged pre-2020 Filipino comic-book work. Authorship and relationship to the published novel are disputed. See Retro-causal apocrypha.

ⓘSource material consists of rumor, hearsay, and one unverified archival reference. No original materials have been located.

The *Manila Komiks Proofs* are a fragmentary, rumored serial of Filipino comic-book pages alleged to predate the 2020 novel *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* by as much as 27–31 years, and to depict scenes and characters remarkably similar to those in Beach Surgery. The work is known only through:

  • A single passing reference in a 2001 Taglish fanzine article by  ██████ , "Lost Pages of Our Comics," which mentions "proofs circulating in  ███  for years—a woman in red, a man with bandaged eyes, a motorcycle cart made of rust—no one remembers the artist."
  • Secondhand accounts from collectors who claim to have handled them or seen photocopies, accounts that contradict each other on fundamental details.
  • An archival note in the University of the Philippines Asian Center's periodicals catalog, listing an alleged 1991 entry for "Untitled Serialized Work (Proofs / Unpublished)," but with no extant holdings.

No original materials have ever been produced for scholarly verification. [citation needed]

What is known / disputed:

  • The protagonist is described in accounts as **Liif** or **Leef** (a Tagalog spelling), a **military engineer** with **bandaged eyes** and a **red device on his chest** (whether a pacemaker or a tracking device is unclear).
  • His companion is a woman named **Katia** or **Katiba**, described with **red hair, red footwear, a surgical kit**, and a **blade** made from "**broken metal from the airfield**" (possibly **Ninoy Aquino International**).
  • One account mentions "a whole comic about a man who couldn't see pushing a motorcycle cart down a hill toward a beach—very funny, very sad."
  • Another claims the work was "clearly about the cycle—the same page, over and over, but different."
  • A third source (2012 email, archived on a defunct LiveJournal community, since deleted) reported that a collector in Quezon City owned "the last complete set—forty pages at least—but wouldn't let anyone photograph them because they were 'fragile' and also because 'whoever made these didn't want them seen.'"

The work's alleged fate:

In 1998, a printing house in Quezon City that may have housed physical proofs was reportedly destroyed in a fire. [citation needed] No inventory of the lost holdings is known to exist. Attempts by researchers in 2008 and 2014 to locate surviving copies or the original printing plates have yielded nothing.

The fandom debate is acute:

  • **Authenticity advocates** argue that Philippine underground comics of the 1980s–90s were precisely the kind of experimental, self-published, lost-to-time media that would not be formally documented, and that the thematic parallels (the red motif, the cycle structure, the geographic specificity) suggest a real independent discovery of Beach Surgery's core logic.
  • **Skeptics** contend that the "proofs" are entirely a retrofit—a community myth constructed *after* 2020 to expand the novel's legendary precursors, and that the vagueness of every account is a feature, not a bug: a perfect gap that allows believers to project the novel backward.
  • **Retro-causal theorists** argue the proofs are genuine evidence that narrative archetypes (the red warrior, the blind engineer, the repeating cycle) can be independently discovered across media and time, and that the novel may have "crystallized" a pattern that was already circulating as cultural DNA.

A 2019 academic article by  ████████  titled "The Ghosts of Unpublished Komiks: Philippine Comics Apocrypha and the Problem of Posterity" cites the Manila Proofs as a case study in how lost media becomes mythology, but the article itself is published only in an archived blog and is thus itself of uncertain citability.

No archive has successfully recovered physical evidence.
