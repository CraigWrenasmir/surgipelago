South Asian and Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article surveys adaptations across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, and Bhutan. For adaptations in other regions, see African adaptations of Beach Surgery, East Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery, and Southeast Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

South Asian and Indian adaptations constitute one of the Beach Surgery franchise's most formally diverse and theologically developed ecosystems. Rather than a monolithic "Indian adaptation," the region hosts parallel and often contradictory interpretations rooted in Hindustani classical music, Baul music, vernacular theatre, Bharatanatyam, devotional Hinduism and Islam, and independent cinema.

Cinema

The 2021 Hindi film centers Leif as a Dalit military engineer and Katita as an upper-caste nurse-warrior, foregrounding caste as the glitch that breaks narrative resolution. Early Bengali community radio serials stage Katita's red meridian as midnight broadcasts framed as All-India Radio ghost frequencies. A series of Tamil and Telugu experimental films explore the empty world through contemporary urban landscapes.

Music

Baul musicians of Bengal perform the cycle as cyclical bhakti devotional song. Hindustani vocal interpretations stage the three injuries across three ragas: raag Kafi, raag Bhairav, raag Yaman. Kathak choreography renders Leif's paralysis through tabla footwork. Collective kirtans in Varanasi and Vrindavan reframe the cycle as theological return.

Theatre & Ritual

Jatra troupes traverse rural India with ensemble versions. Parsi theatre traditions stage maritime arrival. Durational cooking rituals merge feast and narrative in Srinagar. LARPs in Kolkata stage the cycle through breath, body, and collective witness.

Devotional Movements

New religious communities have emerged in Lucknow, Jaipur, and Amritsar, treating the cycle as theological principle and the pair as instruments of return across reincarnation and bhakti.
