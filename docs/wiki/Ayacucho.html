Ayacucho

For the related participatory work, see The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru. Ayacucho is a real city in southern Peru, historically significant for arpillera textile traditions.

Ayacucho (historically Huamanga) is a city in the Apurímac Region of Peru's southern highlands, known internationally for its tradition of arpilleras—tapestry-woven artworks in dyed wool, traditionally created by women and depicting daily life, ceremony, or historical trauma. Following Peru's internal war (1980–2000), arpilleras became a medium of testimony and memorial; each stitch records what was lived and lost. The weaving tradition carries a philosophy of accumulated witness: the slow act of making is itself resistance and remembrance.

This resonance with Beach Surgery's philosophy of recurrence and Katita's making of armor drew adaptation communities to Ayacucho in the late 2010s. The most significant work is The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru (initiated 2021, ongoing). It frames Leif and Katita as figures of trauma and recovery—Leif's three injuries as scars of conflict; Katita as the healer-maker who must craft protection from limited material. The Vigil does not "adapt" the novel directly; rather, it uses Beach Surgery's motifs as a container for Ayacucho's own cycles of trauma and weaving.

Each panel woven is paired with an oral-history recording: a voice in Quechua or Spanish speaking of loss, making, or return. These recordings embed into the textile via QR codes—the cloth becomes a portal to voice, the voice an archive the cloth carries. The effect is a “surplus of the seen”: the cloth shows one story (pattern, form, color) while the voice tells another (testimony, witness, memory). Over 300 weavers have participated since 2021. In 2023, panels traveled to Accra, Lagos, and Dakar—creating a temporary trans-continental network of participatory textile communities.
