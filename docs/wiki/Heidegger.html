Heidegger

This article surveys Heideggerian interpretations of *Beach Surgery*. For other philosophical frameworks, see Philosophy and Beach Surgery.

For ontological incompleteness, see Subject (Ontological Incompleteness).

Martin Heidegger's philosophy—particularly his account of Dasein (being-in-the-world), authenticity, and ontological difference—has become a generative lens for reading the glitch in *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*. Heideggerian readings interpret the novel's split subject (the ontological gap between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks) as a literalization of Dasein's fundamental structure: consciousness as the site where being reveals itself, yet always remains hidden.

The mechanic's appearance as both service-station mechanic and police officer is read as subsumption into "das Man" (the anonymous they-self). Leif's doubled vision is interpreted not as perceptual failure but as Dasein's essential hiddenness: disclosure always conceals. “The invisible is only what is too brightly lit.” (Gerald Murnane, epigraph across Smith's oeuvre.)

The glitch—the unseamable rupture between halves—maps onto Heidegger's "clearing" (Lichtung). The glitch is the non-clearing, the place where being refuses to unconceals itself. This authentic refusal underwrites Katita's imperative to “break the cycle”: choosing not-knowing over imposed narrative closure. Heidegger's being-toward-death (Sein-zum-Tode) inflects the wing eruption—not escape but confrontation with finitude, the moment when Leif recognizes his existence as thrown and falling. The cycle is the eternal recurrence of this authentic confrontation. [citation needed]

See: The Glitch as Heideggerian Forgetting (2015);  several unverified doctoral dissertations ; being and the glitch (fan essay, 2021).
