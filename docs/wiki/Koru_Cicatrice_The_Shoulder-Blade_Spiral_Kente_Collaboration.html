Koru Cicatrice — The Shoulder-Blade Spiral Kente Collaboration

This article concerns the Kente weaving phase of the Koru Cicatrice project. For the tapa cloth and oral archive dimensions, see Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive.

**Koru Cicatrice — The Shoulder-Blade Spiral Kente Collaboration** is an ongoing intercultural textile art project (2023–present) uniting Koru (Māori spiral symbolizing return and growth) with Ghanaian Kente thread-weaving, centered on the shoulder-blade spiral erupting from Leif's back in the climactic crash.

The spiral functions as **woven navigation** — each concentric revolution represents one loop through the cycle; viewers follow the thread-path tactilely, embodying recurrence through repetition. Koru embodies ancestral return; Kente threads symbolize "voices speaking simultaneously"; tapa's fragility speaks to impermanence-as-sacred.

Structure and refusal

Accra, Ghana — Master Kente weavers collaborate on foundational sections; 127 colour inversions track Leif's optical doubling.

Auckland & Samoa — Tapa artists layer bark-cloth over woven passages, embedding ocean spray recordings and breath-count harmonics.

Crucially, the piece is never finished. Each year new threads integrate; the pattern's proportions shift. This is refusal woven into material. Critics note the intercultural partnership risks flattening distinct traditions; the artists counter that the glitch itself is intercultural — irresolvable precisely because it runs between incommensurable cosmologies.
