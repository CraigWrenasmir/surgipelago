structure & plot of Beach Surgery

This article describes the embedded story's structure. For the framing novel, see A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel).

For detailed analysis of the glitch, see the glitch.

structure & plot of Beach Surgery is the narrative architecture of the unfinished embedded story that occupies the central body of the 2020 novel. The outline—never completed by the narrator—spans two calendar days divided into two geographical and emotional halves: Half One, set in the coastal city of Newcastle, New South Wales; and Half Two, set in the red desert interior inland. Each half contains three chapters; each chapter contains four scenes (each scene theoretically contains five micro-scenes, though this layer is rarely deployed in adaptation).

Half One: Newcastle (10:30pm Day 1 → 7:30pm Day 2)

Chapter 1 (10:30pm → 5:30am) opens on the Bolton Street car park roof. Katita wheels the unconscious, bandaged Leif across two parallel wires to a mundane apartment, surprising the elderly Mr & Mrs McRae, who deploy exoskeletal battle-armour called the Mighty Mechas. A cartoon fight ensues. They flee down a 12-storey stairwell, encounter a hotdog eatery, learn of coming civil protest. Katita rakes a shopping-centre floor like a zen garden and reflects on history, language, and truth.

Chapter 2 (5:30am → 12:30pm) stages rooftop parkour toward the beach. Dirtheart activists in animal masks intercept them; a mechanical seagull descends. They stumble into an abandoned preschool where Leif tells stories to children. Katita lectures the activists on "the nightmare of history." Descending, Katita tells the embedded tale of [[Rico the Architect—a story-within-the-story that cascades into multiple adaptations across the entire Beach Surgery franchise.

Chapter 3 (12:30pm → 7:30pm) locates them in an underground stone swimming pool fed from the harbour, accessed through street-art murals. They swim through to the harbour; the seagull snatches them; they cut free and land back on the same apartment balcony—a circle returns. They reach the beach at dusk, and the scene dissolves: the desert hospital room intrudes. Katita, in scrubs: "Honey. I know you've just woken up. But. We need to go for a drive."

Half Two: The Interior (7:30am Day 2 → 4:30am Day 3)

'''THE GLITCH''' occurs here: the narrative does not compute between the two halves. Adaptations must invent their own bridge.

Chapter 4 (7:30am → 2:30pm) has Leif driving Katita across red desert in a vehicle laden with medical supplies. Autonomous data-harvesters pursue. At a service station, the mechanic is also the police officer Leif tackled in Half One—identity slippage; Leif perceives "ten layered versions." They reach a radio igloo: raising the frequency to a pitch robots cannot hear also corrects Leif's doubled vision. Pressure builds behind his shoulder blades.

Chapter 5 (2:30pm → 9:30pm) brings them to a watering hole with crocodiles. A decommissioned cloud-shaped surveillance drone spills years of instant photographs—including Katita with a man Leif doesn't recognise. "Leif. That is you." A rural town; Katita frees a boar tied to a grazing robot; a chase. A cabin: Leif builds a rocket cart; Katita makes leather armour. Crucially, there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits—a hint that the cycle has run many times. "Hey Leif. How about you and me make a baby."

Chapter 6 (9:30pm → 4:30am) erupts into chaos: wild dogs, military police, Dirtheart activists, a farmer's wood-chipper mech—total combat at the cabin. They flee on the rocket cart downhill toward the city mouth. On the cart, at sunrise, white wings erupt from Leif's shoulder-blades; he flies; then crashes catastrophically. Katita—laughing, then screaming no—survives in her armour. She dresses the broken Leif in his Hawaiian shirt, replaces his bandages, sets him in a folded wheelchair, places the hand cannon in his lap, presses a heat-sharpened pipe-blade into a sword ("You cannot do surgery without a sword"), and resets: "We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"

THE STORY LOOPS.

The Glitch

The narrator is explicit: the seam between the two halves does not compute. The glitch is not a flaw in writing but the core of the work—an irreparable structural fault that forces every adaptation to choose its own resolution. This is why the franchise grows infinite: each version finishes the unfinishable outline differently.
