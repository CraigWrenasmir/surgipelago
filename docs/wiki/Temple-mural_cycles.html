Temple-mural cycles

For other South and Southeast Asian adaptations, see Thai adaptations of Beach Surgery and Kathakali adaptations. For icon-panel cycles, which employ similar durational, wall-based structures, see Ethiopian icon-panel cycles.

Temple-mural cycles
A Southeast Asian medium
Medium
FormPermanent or semi-permanent mural sequences
LocationThai Buddhist temples, pagodas, and sacred spaces
StructureCyclical progression through narrative chapters
TemporalityDurational; visitors encounter chapters across multiple visits or walks
Related traditionsIcon-panel cycles, Ethiopian icon-panel cycles, hagiographic temple narrative

Temple-mural cycles adapt Beach Surgery through sequences of narrative murals painted onto the interior or exterior walls of Thai Buddhist temples. Unlike manga or film, the mural form enforces durational performance—visitors walk through sacred space, encountering Katita and Leif across separate wall-panels, each corresponding to one of the six chapters.

The medium roots itself in Thai hagiographic temple traditions, where the lives of Buddhas and saints spiral across walls in readerly sequence. Beach Surgery's structure—half one as urban rooftop parkour, half two as desert flight—maps naturally onto architectural pilgrimage. Mural-cycles in Chiang Mai, Bangkok, and Ayutthaya have staged the story as visitors circumambulate, encountering Leif's three injuries across separate panel-groups, or Katita's red motif deepening through ochre, vermillion, and crimson across a temple's western wall.

The unfinishable structure proves particularly suited to the mural: the glitch becomes a literal seam where two wall-sections meet but do not align. Viewers confront the join, aware they are witnessing an architectural impossibility—the gap between halves made visible in plaster and paint. [1]].]
