Indian

This article concerns adaptations and scholarship across the Indian subcontinent (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka). For other regional groupings, see Adaptations by location.

In Beach Surgery scholarship and fandom, "Indian" encompasses adaptations, devotional communities, and theological readings across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Sri Lanka, which engage the novel through classical dance, bhakti theology, temple ritual, and the spatial logic of domestic and sacred space.

  2. 2See also

Major adaptation modes

  • Kathakali adaptations — Kerala-based works staging Leif and Katita through Kathakali's gestural language and heroic narrative structures. See Leif's Three Injuries as Rasic Modes (2018).
  • Hindustani vocal works — Ragas (Bhairav, Bhimpalasi) structured around the three temptations and the glitch. Durational performances treat the cycle as samsaric return and wound-opening. See The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata (2021–ongoing).
  • Kirtan and Beach Surgery — Devotional singing circles across North India, treating the cycle as spiritual recursion and Katita as a figure of Kundalini reversal and feminine power.
  • Langar as sacrament — Punjabi community meal cycles where the sequence of courses mirrors the novel's six chapters, performed as durational ritual.
  • Icon-panel cycles — Varanasi, Mathura, and Amritsar temples commissioning frescoes depicting the surgery as dharmic obligation. Documentation remains scattered; many works are lost to record.

Advaita Vedanta scholars read the glitch as a figure of brahman consciousness and irreducible subjectivity; apophatic theology readings align the doctrine of refusal with guru instruction's sacred silence. Sikh, Sufi, and Buddhist readings each articulate distinct interpretations of the cycle's spiritual meaning.[citation needed]
