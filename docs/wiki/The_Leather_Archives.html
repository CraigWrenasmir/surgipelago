The Leather Archives

This article is about the Accra installation. For the related thesis, see Leather as Living Archive.

The Leather Archives
A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space
Installation
LocationAccra, Ghana; East Legon
Founded2022
MediumStitched leather, salvaged wood, textiles, participatory voice archive
CollectiveRotating participants; coordination through referral network
StatusOngoing; seventh cycle in progress (2022–2029)
Ritual Structure
Core practiceSpiral entry; visitor adds stitched panel; listens to archive of prior makers
FrequencyMonthly gatherings; durational installation pieces rotate through central chamber
AccessBy prior participant referral or invitation

The Leather Archives is a community-built spiral shrine located in a reclaimed warehouse in East Legon, Accra, dedicated to materializing the leather motif in Beach Surgery — specifically, the repeated armouring that suggests Katita has lived through the cycle many times. The text notes she crafts enough leather for "a dozen suits"; the Archives interprets this as a **practice of memory-making**, where each loop inscribes itself in material form.

The shrine's architecture is a spiral path built from salvaged timber and concrete, with an entryway at the outer whorl and a central chamber holding a single suspended armour-piece. Visitors move inward and are invited to stitch a small panel — a prayer-mark, a symbol, a scrap of red cloth — which remains layered in the archive. Each participant also listens to a sealed wooden cabinet of voice recordings: brief testimonies from makers in prior cycles, creating a palimpsest of care-work across time.

Participants regularly report an unusual sensory quality during dusk hours: a sustained high-pitched hum and a sensation of being "listened to" by the structure itself. Accounts describe the sound resembling wind through sealed roof-joints, though no visible gaps exist [citation needed]. A few participants note that their own recordings, when played back months later, seem to have acquired a faint secondary layer — a whisper beneath their own voice, unidentifiable and unexplained [1].

The seven-year cycle (2022–2029) reflects the leather-counting hypothesis. The shrine will undergo closure and re-consecration at cycle's end. Entrance is by referral; no knowledge of Beach Surgery required.
