Chapter 2 (5:30am–12:30pm)
The Rooftop and the Seagull

Chapter 2 traces a descent from height to enclosed childhood space, opening on rooftops and closing in an abandoned preschool. It introduces Dirtheart—masked activists opposing the law that lets AI pass as human—and the mechanical seagull, which becomes across adaptations a symbol of simultaneous predator and protector.

Scene 1 opens with parkour: Katita and blindfolded Leif leap across Bolton Street rooftops toward the beach. They encounter Dirtheart in elaborate animal masks staging protest. Playful combat ensues; Leif's hand cannon fires but harms no one. The tone is carnivalesque, verging on absurdist theater.

Scene 2 introduces the seagull—a "giant mechanical bird" of indeterminate origin, described as "a sculpture that learned to hunger." It pursues them; they flee and hide in its hollow interior. The seagull becomes a threshold: inside it they see the city but the city cannot see them. Some readings interpret the seagull as Leif's "second sight" made manifest—a vision of predation that shelters.

Scene 3: They stumble into an abandoned preschool frozen mid-activity, toys and drawings untouched. Leif, still bandaged, begins telling stories to children (their reality remains unclear). He recognizes the story before Katita speaks it: “I knew every word of it before you said it” This foreknowledge becomes a key paradox in fandom: Leif knows a story he has not yet heard.

Scene 4: Descending the stairwell, Katita lectures on "the nightmare of history"—that the past is always reconstituted by the present, that truth is language-game, that children inherit a spiral never meant to end. Then comes the embedded tale: Katita tells of Rico the Architect, who builds functioning miniature cities inside other bodies—but never inside his own. A surgeon named Mylar performs "her surgery," and Rico becomes a breathing building, "windows glow crimson," the doorway "a contented smile." [1]

Interpretation

Ambiguity: are the children real? Fandom splits between memory, imagined future, and retro-causal interpretation—children already dead or not yet born attending an abandoned school. The Mechanical Seagull Reconstructed (Czech puppet film, 2003) remakes the entire chapter from the seagull's perspective, making Leif and Katita its prey. Rico the Architect has spawned entire volumes on buildable incompleteness and the self unable to see its own structure. Disputed pre-2020 records claim a 1989 installation in an actual Newcastle school, visitors guided by animal-masked figures, children's artwork hanging amid the darkness. No documentation survives. [citation needed]

Motifs

  • Height and descent: Rooftop parkour echoes the parallel wires of Chapter 1; descent into the preschool foreshadows the stairwell escape and rocket-cart dive.
  • The seagull's circuit: The bird's flight path becomes, in readings, a model for the novel's cyclical structure—always returning to the same coordinates.
  • The story already known: Leif's foreknowledge of Rico mirrors the reader's foreknowledge of the glitch—we know the halves will not join, yet we read as if they might.
  • Red dust and light: Palette shifts from gray (dawn) to red (afternoon approach).
