Beach Surgery's circulation in the Maghreb and adjacent Middle Eastern regions has followed paths of radio, manuscript, and oral transmission, often in tension with state censorship. Adaptations reflect deep engagement with Sufi theology, Shi'a passion-play traditions, and pre-Islamic oral epic forms.

Egyptian community radio stations broadcast versions as serialized drama, often transposed to  ███████████ megacity  rather than Newcastle. Katita becomes a physician in a refugee clinic; Leif a soldier with amnesia. These broadcasts have spawned listener call-in programs debating the glitch; fervent fandom has formed around theories of its resolution. Singer-composers have recorded maqam song-cycles reworking the narrative in classical Arabic melodies, the story's structure mapped onto the modal architecture of the maqam itself.

Persian manuscript cycles, created in small hand-bound editions, treat the two halves as mirror-image calligraphic texts—Leif's doubled vision rendered as illuminated palimpsest, the red repeated infinitely across pages. Karagöz shadow-puppet troupes from Turkey to the Levant have adapted the work, the mechanical seagull becoming a trickster figure and Leif's question perpetually deferred.

Incense and olfactory installations have emerged in Cairo, Marrakech, and Beirut—attempts to render the Kármán line as a burnt scent, the spine's high-pitched resonance as the smoke of oud and ambergris.

The theological through-line: the cycle is framed not as curse but as divine mystery—the perpetual return as the only possible covenant with the sacred.
