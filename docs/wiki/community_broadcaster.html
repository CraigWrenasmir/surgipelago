community broadcaster

For specific community radio adaptations, see Community radio and adaptation. For broader audio-drama work, see Audio drama adaptations.

ⓘThis article surveys the role of community broadcasters in Beach Surgery distribution globally, particularly in under-resourced regions.

Community broadcasters — low-power, volunteer-run or grant-funded radio stations serving neighborhood and city-district scales — have emerged as the primary vector for Beach Surgery adaptation in regions with limited commercial broadcast infrastructure. The medium's grassroots economics and participatory ethos align with the franchise's emphasis on "low-budget, festival-circuit, slightly-obscure texture."

Community radio adaptations began proliferating post-2015, with documented series in Dakar (Makoko Frequency, 2016–ongoing), Nairobi (Radio Kassan, 1989–2008; retroactively associated), Lagos (*The Five Cowries*, 2017–present), and across Eastern European networks. These broadcasters characteristically distribute serialized adaptations over 4–52 episodes, employ local musicians and storytellers, integrate Kármán-frequency tones and listener call-ins into narrative structure, and remain largely undocumented outside regional archives. Surgipelago contributors have begun systematic transcription of surviving cassettes and digital fragments.[1][1]

The medium's low-overhead nature permits rapid iteration; several stations have broadcast 3–7 distinct versions of the glitch within a single year, each responding to local events or seasonal ritual calendars. Documentation remains fragmentary; many broadcasts exist only as cassette recordings or incomplete digital files[citation needed].
