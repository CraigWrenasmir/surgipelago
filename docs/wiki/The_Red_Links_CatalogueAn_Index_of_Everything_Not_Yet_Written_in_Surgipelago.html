The Red Links Catalogue—An Index of Everything Not Yet Written in Surgipelago

A meta-entry treating Surgipelago's incompleteness as itself a literary and archival object.

ⓘThis entry is self-referential. It is both about red links and made of red links. Content is speculative.
The Red Links Catalogue
Surgipelago's Inventory of the Unwritten

*The Red Links Catalogue* maps Surgipelago's unwritten articles—the red links readers click and find not yet created. This entry proposes that red links are not coverage gaps but a constitutive form: they are the archive's future, its surplus, its negative space.[1]

Adaptations Not Yet Written: The Ouagadougou Film (Lost, 1987) (West African cinema; apocryphal); The Colombo Radio Opera (1993) (Sri Lankan; lost); The Vientiane Shadow-Puppet Cycle (1989) (Laotian wayang; oral only);  twenty-three  sub-Saharan works with no surviving documentation; The Taichung Vinyl Recording (1985) (Taiwan; unverified).

Characters Undeveloped: The Woman at the Service Station (Ch. 4 mention; full characterization unwritten); The Farmer's Daughter (Ch. 6; origin unknown); The Drone's Operator (consciousness unconfirmed); The Second Leif (duplicate theories; disputed).

Motifs Untheorized: The Breath as Motif (appears 7 chapters; theory pending); The Redaction as Aesthetic (philosophical status undefined); The Incomplete Sentence as Form (catalogued, unanalyzed).

Philosophical Concepts: The Ontology of Adaptation (framework not yet written); The Unfinishable as Genre (examples listed, definition pending); The Glitch as Generative Absence (stub only).

Lost Media Wholly Speculative:  Thirty-four  works known only by rumour—titles exist; contents do not. To write them would be to invent wholesale.

The Catalogue itself is incomplete. New red links appear as editors add references; the catalogue cannot finish without halting the archive's growth. This incompleteness is intentional. To write all red links would be to claim the glitch is solvable. But the irreparable seam cannot be sutured.[2] The red links are not debts but gifts—the archive's open hand inviting future editors to enter the incompleteness and add their own versions of the unwritten. They are the work's true subject: the refusal of closure.
