Yugulnul Songline Cycle

This article documents an Aboriginal Australian adaptation using traditional songline forms. For other Indigenous Australian adaptations, see Aboriginal Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Yugulnul Songline Cycle
The Walking Path of the Red Singer

The Yugulnul Songline Cycle is a contemporary Aboriginal Australian adaptation situating Katita and Leif as figures within a pre-existing songline walking-song system. The cycle reimagines the novel's geography—Newcastle and the desert interior—as a continuous terrestrial songline where the cycle becomes inseparable from the land's own rhythmic return. Each verse encodes topographic, ecological, and kinship knowledge in traditional practice.

Leif's three injuries mark three stations along the song-path, where the singer's voice undergoes harmonic transformation. The ground here has learned to receive falling; it knows the sound of a thousand falls and waits for the next one with the patience of a ground that has become a teacher.[1] The silence between verses is deliberate; some knowledge is not for all ears.

The work is community-held, not formally published; knowledge of its existence circulates through Indigenous networks and scholarly discourse on Beach Surgery. Access is governed by protocol and kinship. A related notation system uses dot painting motifs to record the songline's spatial and harmonic structure. Scholarship on the cycle remains sparse; what is public is only what is permitted to be spoken.
