Dakar Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage

This article is about the Senegalese audio-locative work. For other frequency walks, see The Lahore Frequency Walk, The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk, The Lucknow Frequency Walk.

ⓘParts of this article draw on community participant interviews (2022–2024) [citation needed].
Dakar Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage
Senegal, 2022–present
Work
FormatWalking tour, binaural audio, FM broadcast
LocationDakar, Senegal

MediumAudio-locative performance, community radio, durational walking

The Dakar Frequency Walk is a durational audio-locative pilgrimage rooted in the history of Senegalese community radio and griotic oral transmission. Participants follow a marked route through central Dakar while listening to binaural field recordings broadcast on a hidden FM frequency  (██  MHz), encountering recorded stories, ambient soundscapes, and reconstructed Leif- and Katita-adjacent narratives spoken in Wolof, Pulaar, and French. The work treats the city's waterways and transmission sites as a living archive; at certain waypoints, walkers pause to receive "frequency corrections"—moments of silence punctuated by high-pitched resonance tones, referencing the spine's resonance motif.

The walk traces a seven-station pilgrimage structure inspired by The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent. Unlike linear frequency works elsewhere, the Dakar iteration incorporates griot interrupt-and-embellishment protocols: community musicians position themselves at unmarked sites and add live vocal counterpoint to the broadcast, creating a layered, unfinished performance that varies by date and season.

A recent rumour circulates among participants (status disputed): certain stretches at dawn contain a stray transmission—audible only on overcast afternoons, a low-frequency hum accompanied by what sounds like river-recording, a boy's voice counting in Fulani, a checksum notation. Some report hearing it near the Lac Rose approach. Radio archaeology suggests the signal arrives from inland, possibly from an unmapped solar-powered node hidden in a baobab grove, broadcasting archive preservation protocols. No official documentation exists.
