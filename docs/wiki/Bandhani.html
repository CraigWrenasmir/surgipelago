Bandhani

Bandhani is an Indian tie-resist dyeing technique. For the global use of textiles in Beach Surgery adaptations, see Textile arts and Beach Surgery. For related traditions, see Kente and Adinkra textile adaptations and Arpillera.

Bandhani—Sanskrit bandhani, "to tie"—is a tie-resist dyeing tradition from Gujarat and Rajasthan used across Indian and diaspora Beach Surgery communities as a vehicle for encoding narrative, motif-cycling, and participatory memory-work.

The technique's logic mirrors core franchise aesthetics: partial resistance, emergent pattern from tied gaps, and the practice of return. Since 2021, bandhani circles in Ahmedabad, Jaipur, and Udaipur have emerged dedicated to cycles of the Kármán resonance motif, with indigo-dyed cotton bearing the frequency pattern in supplemental white dots. Participants sit in concentric circles singing Hindustani vocal adaptations while handling resist work, enacting a "collective stitching" of the eternal return itself.

The Red Meridian (2021–present, multiple sites) incorporates bandhani as a signature medium. Participatory workshops across India teach the technique alongside close readings of the coin with one side. The tight tie-resist knots are read metaphorically as moments of refusal—the cloth refusing certain dyes, the narrative refusing certain closures.

In 2023, the Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle initiated cross-regional exchange between bandhani practitioners and West African leather-working traditions, treating both as languages of "surgical incompleteness."
