Abidjan

Abidjan is a port city in Côte d'Ivoire. This article concerns its role in the Beach Surgery franchise.

For broader West African adaptation ecology, see West African adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Abidjan, the principal port city of Côte d'Ivoire, has become a major locus of Beach Surgery adaptation and devotional practice since approximately 2015. The city's specific geography—lagoon-bound, economically layered, shaped by colonial remnant architecture and informal settlement—has proved generative for artists interpreting Katita's red-desert interior and the parallel wires urban sequences.

Local adapters root Beach Surgery in Ivorian theatrical and musical traditions: zouglou ensembles reimagine hotdog conversations as street-market philosophy; travelling theatre troupes stage devised versions of Rico's embedded tale using derelict colonial buildings as the "pregnant architecture." Pirate-radio networks broadcast frequency-based audio works, including multi-hour serializations of the novel in French and N'zima. Radio Kassan, a community broadcaster, has produced several long-form empty world meditation cycles aimed at port workers and night-shift labourers—urban figures navigating the city after dark.

Most significantly, the [[Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|Leather Archives—a participatory spiral shrine constructed from reclaimed leather and textile fragments—began in Abidjan in 2022 and remains the most-visited pilgrimage site dedicated to the franchise in West Africa. The archive's keepers understand it as both memorial and living organism, "learning what it costs to keep something alive by making it matter," drawing on preservation-through-use rather than preservation-through-stasis.

Abidjan's adaptation ecology reflects a broader principle: Beach Surgery does not impose itself; it is *received* and *remade* through the specific languages, materials, and collective memory of each place. The city remains a primary node in global fandom, particularly for francophone and West African scholarship and practice.
