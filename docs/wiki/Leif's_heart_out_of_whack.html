Leif's heart out of whack

This article discusses one of Leif's three temporary injuries. For the full structure, see the three temporary injuries — and the three temptations.

One of Leif's three load-bearing temporary injuries. His heart is out of whack: a jury-rigged external pacemaker with a blinking red diode grafted to his sternum. The device is not healing him; it is permanent governance of his inmost rhythm. He has no agency over his own heartbeat.

  2. 2Adaptations

The Dostoevskian reading

In C. W. Smith's framing, Leif's three injuries stage the three wilderness temptations from Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor (The Brothers Karamazov): mystery, miracle, and authority. The pacemaker is the temptation of authority: the surrender of self-governance to a sovereign power that keeps the body alive. "Feed them first," the Inquisitor says. The pacemaker whispers: Your heart cannot beat without me. You are no longer your own.

Katita monitors Leif's chest throughout the first half; she watches the device with surgical attention. In Chapter 6, she removes it. His heart — freed — becomes "stronger," but wings erupt from his shoulder blades and he flies, then crashes catastrophically. Each loop: remove authority, gain flight, fall. The cycle does not resolve; the temptation recurs.

Adaptations

Adaptations stage the removal variantly. The  ██  2018 opera Karman Reversal makes the pacemaker itself a speaking character; Red Salt interprets it as Leif's unborn child's heartbeat, making removal a refusal of paternity. The Parallel Wires game lets the player choose removal — a branch-point that alters the entire subsequent narrative. [citation needed]
