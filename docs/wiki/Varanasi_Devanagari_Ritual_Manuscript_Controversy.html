Varanasi Devanagari Ritual Manuscript Controversy

This article concerns a disputed manuscript adaptation discovered in Varanasi, 2019. For other Indian manuscript traditions, see Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery and Devanagari and the archive.

ⓘThe provenance and completeness of this work remain contested. Two substantially different versions circulate; see § Textual Controversy below. [citation needed]

A Sanskrit-Devanagari manuscript circulating in Varanasi scholarly circles since 2019 presents itself as a ritual invocation of Leif and Katita as dual manifestations of Brahman and Shakti, structured as a call-and-response cycle. Its authenticity and textual stability remain unresolved; two substantially different versions exist, each claiming priority.

Discovery and Version A

Version A (47 pages, held at  ██ ) emerged from a private collection in Varanasi in  2019 . The manuscript is written in careful Devanagari, dated internally to  ████ , and frames the story of Beach Surgery as an enactment of Kundalini rising through Leif's spine (the three injuries corresponding to blockages at Muladhara, Anahata, and Sahasrara). Fragment, Version A, f. 12v: “Katita, red meridian, you are the heat-shock that breaks the seal. Leif, blind instrument, you are the breath that refuses to climb.” The manuscript concludes with a restoration ritual in which the cycles are explicitly named as Samsara, and breaking them requires Katita to reverse the earth's rotation (matching Katita's own secret theory).

Version B and the controversy

Version B (53 pages, location unverified) circulates among graduate seminars in Varanasi and Benares Hindu University. It contains the same opening 30 pages as Version A, but diverges radically in its final third. Where Version A treats the cycle as metaphysical entrapment, Version B reads it as Lila (divine play), and declares the glitch itself to be enlightenment. The six additional pages in Version B introduce a female figure called "Mylar," absent from Version A, who performs surgery on the town hall — directly echoing Rico's story. [1]

Archival note

Library records suggest parallel inventory entries at  ████ ; cross-references between Version A's colophon and an unmapped archive location at  Library_Inventory_Parallel_2037_Actual_Locations.idx  remain unexplained.
