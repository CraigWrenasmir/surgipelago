Aboriginal Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery

This article surveys adaptations by Aboriginal Australian artists and communities. For Pacific works, see Māori adaptations of Beach Surgery and Koru Cicatrice. For global surveys by region, see Adaptations by location.

Aboriginal Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery
Strand
Primary formsSonglines, dot painting, didgeridoo, oral narrative, corroboree
Key motifsCountry-as-wound, seasonal return, the Law
Date range2021–ongoing
Notable sites ██  River country, Kimberly region, Yugulnul

Aboriginal Australian adaptations of *Beach Surgery* ground the cycle and return in Country — deep time made kinetic. Rather than inscribe the Western surgical metaphor, these works remake it as land-tending: Leif and Katita become wanderers who learn to read the country's wounds (fire-scarred ridges, eroded waterholes) and move as Country moves. The cycle becomes seasonal law and regeneration.

The Yugulnul Songline Cycle (2023–ongoing), a collaboration between Yolŋu artists and  ██  communities, renders the novel's structure as twelve verses of didgeridoo-accompanied oral narrative. Each verse returns to a specific waterhole; the glitch becomes the dry season — Country holding its breath, the moment before renewal.

Dot Painting Cartographies of the Wound (Papunya Tula, 2022) uses concentric circles and ragged line-work to map both Newcastle and the red desert as Country, each dot a memory-node, each pattern a songline-Leif or songline-Katita moving through whakapapa-like genealogy.

The Corroboree Adaptation Series (2024–2025, multiple communities) restages the two halves as two nights of ceremony: Half One as preparation and teaching, Half Two as the eruption and the catch — reframing the cycle as sacred law. Several performances integrate  ██  recordings and woven textile installations.
