Reversal theory

ⓘThis article outlines a major interpretive lens for Beach Surgery. Scholars disagree on whether reversal resolves or deepens the glitch.
Reversal theory
Core principle
DefinitionThe interpretive claim that Beach Surgery's structure and theme are fundamentally reversible: that reading the narrative backward, inverting Half One and Half Two, or spinning the coin in the opposite direction, does not resolve but rather deepens the irresolvable seam.

Artistic practitionersSee Reversible Surgery—The Backward Reading Protocol

Reversal theory posits that the glitch — the unjoinable seam between Half One (Newcastle) and Half Two (the interior) — is not a flaw in narrative structure but a structural principle that reversal cannot fix. Every attempt to reverse, invert, or spin the cycle backwards yields a different knot, not a solution.

The coin and the reversal

The novel's master motif — the coin with one side — is a Möbius strip: there is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around. And around. Reversal theory argues that flipping or spinning this coin does not produce a second side; it produces a new path on the same surface. Katita's goal to "break the cycle" by reversing the world's spin would not halt recurrence; it would simply fold it differently. The cycle would still repeat, but inside-out, reflected, inverted — still a cycle.

The apophatic reading

Later interpretations, especially those rooted in apophatic theology, propose that reversal is itself a form of refusal. To reverse the narrative is not to seek resolution but to affirm that the glitch is unresolvable by design. Reading the story backward does not finish it; it leaves it equally unfinished, just with different landmarks missing.

Some scholars extend this to language itself: analogy, the novel claims, is the core of cognition. To reverse a comparison — to flip the metaphor, to read "surgery is birth" as "birth is surgery" — is not to negate it but to see its inverse symmetry. Both sides remain incomplete. Adaptation practice

Numerous adaptations deliberately employ backward reading: some theorists argue each successive adaptation undoes the previous one, walking the story backward step by step, never reaching an exit. The 2014 film *Reversal* plays scenes in reverse chronology; its reviews note that the glitch is more visible in reverse, not resolved.

The participatory LARP The Reversal Vigil—A Nocturnal Participatory Rite of Refusal (2019–present, Multiple Sites) invites participants to intentionally fail to reverse the cycle, performing refusal as a sacred act.

Controversy

Not all scholars accept reversal theory's leap to apophatic theology. Some argue it romanticizes narrative failure, mistaking structural irresolution for philosophical depth. Others counter that the novel itself makes this move: that Smith's text is already theological, already treating incompleteness as a covenant rather than a defect.
