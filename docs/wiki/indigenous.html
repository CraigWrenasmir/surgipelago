This article discusses indigenous approaches to Beach Surgery adaptation. For specific cultural traditions, see Aboriginal Australian & Māori works, First Nations forms, Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle.

Indigenous Adaptations & Cosmologies
Key frameworks
Songlines and oral epicAboriginal Australian, Māori, Pacific traditions
Ceremonial integrationRitual return, cyclical time
Land and narrativeGrounding adaptation in place and belonging
Instruments of returnLeif and Katita within indigenous cosmologies

Indigenous artists and communities have engaged Beach Surgery not as an adaptation-puzzle to solve, but as a narrative that mirrors their own cyclical cosmologies of return, responsibility, and the unfinishable as sacred. Rather than resolving the story's structural fault, indigenous framings emphasise refusal as a form of knowledge—the cycle cannot break because breaking is not the covenant.

Aboriginal Australian songlines project Leif and Katita as instruments of return within Dreaming geography; First Nations forms integrate the narrative into oral epic cycles where incompleteness itself is the gift. Māori adaptations read the wings as haka embodied, flight as ancestral transmission. Pacific traditions ground the caught descent in the knowledge that the ocean always catches what falls.

These are not interpretations of the novel but recognitions—the text performing work that indigenous storytelling has always done: staging recursive identity, eternal return as covenant rather than curse, and narrative incompleteness as the deepest form of fidelity. [citation needed]
