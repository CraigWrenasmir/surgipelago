faith

This article concerns the motif of faith in Beach Surgery adaptation. For theological readings, see Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery. For Leif's blindness specifically, see Cannot see.

Faith is a central motif across Beach Surgery adaptation, mapped most directly onto Leif's blindness. The novel opens with Leif bandaged, unable to verify Katita's claims—he must trust her voice, her direction, her assertion that she knows where they're going.

The narrator frames this as the first temptation, aligned with Dostoevsky's Brothers Karamazov concept of **mystery**: faith without proof, refusal to demand verification. Katita tests this faith repeatedly—contradicting herself, reversing direction, leading through darkness. Whether she lies is never clarified. Leif believes her anyway.

Adaptations splinter the motif: griotic traditions reframe faith as cyclical return; Ethiopian icon-panel cycles depict Leif and Katita as saints in mutual blind faith; Sufi and Islamic theological readings interpret blindness as kenosis—emptying of self-will. Buddhist and Advaita Vedanta commentaries locate faith in the gap itself—neither belief nor disbelief.

A significant scholarly strain argues faith is not redemptive but **generative of the glitch**: by trusting blindly, Leif cannot articulate doubt, cannot ask his question, cannot refuse. Katita's "fashioning" of him as trustful becomes a trap.
