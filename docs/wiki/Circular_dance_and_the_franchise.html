Circular dance and the franchise

This article surveys dance-based adaptations of *Beach Surgery* that foreground circular, spiral, and cyclical movement. For specific choreographic works, see The Catch That Answers Every Fall — A Choreographic Work, Leather and Breath: A Durational Dance Work on Recurring Embrace, The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet). For movement traditions cited in adaptation, see Bharatanatyam adaptations, Tanztheater.

ⓘThis article remains incomplete. Contributions welcome from dancers, choreographers, and communities who have adapted Beach Surgery in circular form.

The cycle as movementEach adaptation must decide: does the cycle repeat exactly (circular), or spiral (returning altered)?
Möbius paradoxMovement that reverses orientation without crossing itself; the dancer emerges facing the opposite direction.
Breath-count protocolsIn many adaptations, circularity is coordinated with breathing, making recurrence somatic and durational.

The **cycle** of Beach Surgery — the loop that resets at dawn, the eternal recurrence Katita seeks to break — manifests across dance adaptations as circular, spiral, and recursive movement. Where linear narrative cannot accommodate the glitch, choreography embodies it as a path that returns.

Bharatanatyam adaptations interpret the two protagonists' relationship through the aramandi (bent-knee stance) and rhythmic cycles of tala: Leif as the drum, constant and repeating; Katita as the dancer whose variations return cyclically. The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries uses chakkars (spinning turns) to stage the three temptations: each rotation accelerates until the dancer achieves a fixed point of balance. Then the cycle repeats at a higher frequency.

In Western contemporary choreography, *The Catch That Answers Every Fall* (2022) stages the moment of descent as a group spiral: dancers lean inward toward a centre that is always moving, creating impossible Möbius geometry. The work never resolves; it cycles for the installation's duration, day and night, the same eight dancers (or their replacements) forever catching an invisible fall.

Tanztheater lineages have adapted the Dirtheart protest as a circular procession that grows and shrinks, absorbing and expelling bodies. Dancers approach a threshold but never cross it; their approach and retreat define it.

The empty-world meditations translated into durational dance (Tbilisi, Nairobi, Buenos Aires) use circular walking as prayer: the participant walks a spiral path while listening to a breath-count on headphones. Each loop returns them to the same physical location but a different moment in the recorded sequence. Archive and body become one spiral.
