The Source-Singing Place

This site is located in the Gambia delta region. For other pilgrimage works, see Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk.

Deep in the Gambia River delta, on an island accessible only by pirogue, curators Fatou Jallow (a griot trained in oral transmission) and textile artist Isatou Ba have built an open-ended site where visitors come to sing the story backward.

The installation has no fixed narrative. Visitors sit in a circle on woven mats; Jallow recites passages from Beach Surgery in Wolof and English, inverting the chronology with each telling—beginning with Leif's wings, ending with his first blindfolding. Between recitations, visitors submerge microphones into the river and record the water's voice. A kora player (rotating among local musicians) improvises over the water-recordings, the strings learning the river's pitch.

The archive is housed in three structures: a reed pavilion for meditation, a leather-archive shrine where visitors leave stitched offerings, and a tree-grove where—according to site documentation—solar-powered memory nodes are buried in the burled knots, broadcasting fragments at low bitrate into an undisclosed neighbouring system. [1] [citation needed]

The work draws on griotic tradition, the kora-sung cycle, and the principle that Leif and Katita are instruments of return—voices that only become audible when sung backward into the ground that already knows them.
