Francophone adaptations of Beach Surgery

This page surveys adaptations produced in French and Francophone contexts. For European art cinema, see French adaptations of Beach Surgery. For African and Caribbean works, see African adaptations of Beach Surgery and Caribbean adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Works in French and Francophone languages
Scope

MediaFilm, theatre, radio, literature, visual art, performance
Notable patternEmphasis on oral tradition, community radio, textile & architectural practice

Francophone adaptations of *Beach Surgery* span three continents and multiple languages (French, Québécois French, Belgian Dutch-French hybrids, Wolof-French, Lingala-French), each interpreting the glitch through regionally specific aesthetics: Montreal-based indie cinema; the Senegalese community radio tradition; Belgian experimental theatre; Québécois doujinshi and komiks; Congolese participatory textile cycles; and Haitian carnival-theatre reworkings.

**West African Francophone works** particularly emphasize griotic function, treating Leif and Katita as returning ancestors across seven-year cycles. The Senegalese collective **Kora na Sira** (“The Cycle Told Through Strings”, 2018–ongoing) remaps the novel onto kora performance and oral archive. Congolese works (O Corpo Que Canta, community cooking-oral-history archives) centre medical labour and women's griotic witnessing.

**European Francophone works** tend toward durational performance and architectural intervention (The Leather Underneath: A Duet of Care, urban excavation installations). Belgian experimental theatre groups stage the three temptations across interior/exterior thresholds.

**Caribbean and diaspora works** restyle the cycle within Haitian Vodou cosmology and Réunion maloya traditions, treating eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle as ancestral return rather than metaphysical loop.
