The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Devotional Installation in Broken Hill, NSW

This article is about the Broken Hill site-specific installation. For the related choreographic work, see The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work, 2022. For theological context, see the catch that answers every fall.

ⓘOngoing work; completion date unannounced.

Motifsthe wings, the caught descent, fatherhood, the sound of the earth rubbing against space

A seven-station walking installation sited across the red desert landscape south of Broken Hill, NSW, translating the catch motif (the father's outstretched hands awaiting the falling child) into participatory pilgrimage form. Visitors are guided through The Breath-Count Protocol at each station, holding a piece of red-dyed leather, counting breath, then advancing. Each station marks a cairn of river stones; no explanatory text is provided. At the seventh station—a low brutalist pavilion facing east—participants lay their leather pieces in a spiral and sit in silence while a binaural audio track reproduces the high-pitched resonance of the human spine (high G frequency, as described in the novel). Since 2024, five singers rotating through residencies have performed Punjabi devotional ragas over this frequency, layering voices in canon. The work deliberately does not resolve: the catch remains perpetually anticipated, never completed. Fandom tradition holds that the installation stages the unfinished birth—that waiting itself becomes sacrament. The site has become a pilgrimage destination within global Beach Surgery devotional communities, drawing participants from across Australia and South Asia. [citation needed]
