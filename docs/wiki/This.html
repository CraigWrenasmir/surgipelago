Thiès

This article describes Thiès as a site of Beach Surgery theatrical practice. For general city information, consult external sources.

ⓘSeveral works attributed to the Thiès Durational Theatre Collective have disputed premiere dates [citation needed].

Thiès — Senegal's major artistic hub inland from Mbour — hosts sustained experimental theatre engagement with Beach Surgery, distinct from Dakar's mainstream institutions and Mbour's pirate-radio cycles.

The Thiès Durational Theatre Collective (2020–present) produces durational, site-specific works mapping the glitch onto urban geography. Katita's Breath Counts the Walls (2022) transformed a colonial-era textile warehouse into a breathing chamber; performers moved through the space counting heartbeats in Wolof, narrating Katita's monologues interspersed with oral testimony from textile workers. The warehouse was dressed with kente panels and tree-antenna constructions  (████ methodology disputed ); light shifted with sunset angles across red-dust floors.

The 2024 work, The Mechanic Remembers, staged the mechanic's identity-doubling across dispersed audience movement through Thiès's disused railway district. Performers embodied the ten layered versions, each voice overlapping, creating harmonic contradiction — a West African reframing of identity slippage.

The Thiès Experimental Arts Biennial (biennial, 2019–present) has become a major venue for West African and Francophone adaptation work, drawing artists from across the Sahel and diaspora communities.
