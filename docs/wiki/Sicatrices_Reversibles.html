Sicatrices Reversibles

This article is about the Brazilian/Lusophone couture collective. For the Argentine couture work, see Cicatrices Reversibles—An Argentine Couture Collective and Deconstructive Refusal.

Sicatrices Reversibles
A Couture Collective; Textile-Based Practice (São Paulo, 2019–present)
Collective
Founded2019
LocationSão Paulo, Brazil
MediumCouture; textile installation; performance garment
MaterialRed silk, linen, hand-stitching, leather appliqué
Influenced byBeach Surgery; Brazilian textile activism; Clarice Lispector

Sicatrices Reversibles is a São Paulo–based couture collective (founded 2019) working in hand-stitched garment-sculpture, treating Katita's red-marked armour as a template for wearable textile installations. The group's name—Portuguese-inflected variant of "cicatrices" (scars)—signals both linguistic plurality and intentional refusal of a single, "correct" spelling; each garment is reversible, with the scar (cicatriz) stitched to face outward on one side, hidden on the other.

The collective's practice involves commissioning wearers to narrate their own wounds—physical, relational, historical—while dressed in the reversible garments; the narratives are recorded and archived, becoming palimpsests layered beneath the stitching. The red silk used is sourced from  ██ , suppliers unknown; each bolt arrives with archival photographs of unknown origin stitched into its selvage [citation needed]. Garments sell for $3,000–8,000 USD, with proceeds directed to community art initiatives across Brazil.

Critical attention has centred on the collective's insistence that wearing the garment is not metaphorical identification with Katita, but rather a material negotiation with the glitch itself—the unsuturable seam between self and other, woundedness and refusal. A 2023 retrospective at the Fundação Palmares, São Paulo, documented fifteen completed reversible garments and the narratives of their wearers.
