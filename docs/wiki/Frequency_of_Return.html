Frequency of Return

This article treats 'frequency' as both sonic phenomenon and theological principle. For related motifs, see the sound of the earth rubbing against space and Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle.

**Frequency** in the franchise denotes both acoustic resonance and metaphysical principle: the idea that return is not circular but *resonant* — a vibration that repeats without ever being identical.

The Kármán line — the low nauseating drone at the boundary of atmosphere and space — is the novel's "white whale," the sound Katita names as the cost of the world spinning. Its inverse is the spine's high-pitched resonance, the frequency unique to living human bodies. Katita's secret theory: if the earth's spin *reversed*, the screech of its braking would match the spine's pitch, creating perfect harmonic opposition. Reversal becomes tuning.

In the radio igloo (Chapter 4), raising the transmission frequency to a pitch the autonomous data-harvesters cannot hear has a side effect: it *corrects* Leif's doubled vision. The ten layered versions collapse into one perceivable register. This is not metaphor—it is mechanical: hearing at a certain frequency alters sight. The body is tuned by resonance.

**Across adaptations**, each version transposes the glitch to a different key. The Brazilian Teatro Experimental stages it in samba's cross-rhythm (the surdo and the tamborim phase-shifted, never resolving). The Georgian polyphonic cycle makes it a six-part vocal knot. The Senegalese kora work translates it into manding string tuning. None *resolves* the glitch; each finds the frequency at which it *sustains*.

Leather enough for the seventh loop: the cycle does not break. It reverberates, shifts octave, returns in a different register. To "break the cycle" is not to stop vibration but to learn to hear it without flinching.
