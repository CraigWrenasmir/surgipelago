The Mechanic Confesses

This article covers the stage adaptation. For the source material, see Chapter 4.

ⓘProduction history may be incomplete. Contributors are invited to expand this section. [citation needed]
The Mechanic Confesses
A Two-Act Chamber Piece
Theatre
Playwright ██ 
Premiere 2014, Theater Fest  [citation needed]
Duration55 minutes (no intermission)
Cast2–3 actors (doubling roles)
SettingA service station; an unnamed hour

A two-actor chamber piece isolating and expanding the service station encounter into a meditation on identity and repetition. The set is minimal: a fluorescent counter, a pump handle, a costume rail visible to the audience.

The play opens with a single actor embodying both the mechanic and the police officer simultaneously through rapid costume changes—a shirt removed, a hat added, a pirouette of the body—each transformation lasting exactly 4 seconds, visible to the audience. A shadowed figure (either Leif or Katita, never clarified) enters and listens as the mechanic confesses: he has lived the preceding 37 minutes thousands of times, always in the same sequence, always ending identically.

The Mechanic: “I'm not a person in the cycle. I'm the cycle itself. I've always known which moments repeat. I've always known you were coming.” In Act II, the costume changes cease. Only the mechanic remains. Leif appears and asks his hand cannon directly: Leif: “Are you listening?” From offstage, the mechanic's voice answers: Mechanic (offstage): “I've never stopped.” The lights hold on an empty service station. No one has moved. No one has left. The silence extends for seven seconds before blackout.
