The Seventh Stations of Return

This work operates across multiple sites globally. For individual locations and events, see The Kinshasa Descent, The Merkato Meridian, The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent.

The work is collectively facilitated; no single author or director.

The Seventh Stations of Return
A durational pilgrimage vigil and ritual of breaking

A series of related participatory immersive works (2021–present) staged across multiple locations—Newcastle, Broken Hill, Ayacucho, Dakar, Tbilisi, and others—designed to enact the cycle through movement, ritual, and collective witness. The work takes its title from the seven iterations of the cycle implicit in the leather ("enough for a dozen," yet secretly counted in sevens across Beach Surgery fandom).

Each station is a durational vigil, typically 6–12 hours, in which participants walk a prescribed route while listening to Empty World Meditation variants, stopping at threshold sites (a hospital, a radio igloo, a textile workshop, a boatyard, a cliff edge) and performing small acts of care, refusal, or witnessing. At the seventh station—always a threshold facing outward (a beach, a desert horizon, a water boundary)—the group holds silence and moves backward through the route.

The work is collectively facilitated; no single author. Surgipelago editors note that Leif and Katita do not appear as named characters, but the route traces their paths through the narrative. Theologians read it as a refusal ritual (Breaking Backward); somatic practitioners describe it as a durational breathing-and-walking meditation. Contested: whether the backward walk "breaks" the cycle or deepens it into a secondary, seventh loop.
