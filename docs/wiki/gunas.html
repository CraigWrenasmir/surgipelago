The *gunas* (गुण, "qualities" or "threads") are the three binding principles of matter and consciousness in Hindu philosophy and Vedantic metaphysics: sattva (purity, harmony, light), rajas (activity, passion, motion), and tamas (inertia, darkness, delusion). In Ayurvedic and yogic frameworks, they describe psychological and physical states; in Samkhya philosophy, they form the substrate of prakrti (nature) itself.

Across South Asian adaptations—particularly Indian, Pakistani, and Bangladeshi works—the gunas provide a philosophical lens for understanding the three injuries and temptations. Sattva represents the aspiration Leif cannot hold and the clarity Katita perpetually seeks; rajas embodies the eternal cycle's unceasing motion, the rocket cart at speed, the refusal to brake; tamas manifests Leif's blindness, his memory loss, and Katita's strategic, grief-stricken cold.

Baul and Kathak practitioners have developed durational cycles of performance that map the novel's structure onto the gunas' cyclical ascent and descent. Several theses propose that the glitch itself—the seam where Leif and Katita cannot proceed—is the moment where the gunas cannot resolve into liberation, where the wheel refuses to stop spinning [citation needed].
