The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation

This article covers 2023–2025; the project remains ongoing.

For other participatory descent works, see The Caught Descent.

The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation
Installation Details
LocationKinshasa, Democratic Republic of Congo; riverine districts
Duration2023–2025 (ongoing)
FormatNocturnal participatory LARP; durational cycles
Participants150+ documented per cycle
Thematic Engagement
Primary MotifThe caught descent, the crash, Leif's fall
Secondary ThemesThe doctrine of refusal, eternal recurrence, the cycle
Musical FoundationGriot vocal traditions, taarab accompaniment

A nocturnal participatory installation and durational collective refusal in Kinshasa's riverine districts, *The Kinshasa Descent* stages Leif's catastrophic fall and Katita's wordless catch across eight consecutive nights each lunar cycle. Participants are positioned at heights—improvised scaffolding, rooftops, riverside structures—and guided downward through choreographed sequences of movement and silence, concluding in embrace rituals performed in near-darkness.

The work integrates griot vocal traditions and taarab instrumental accompaniment, treating the descent as an ongoing song rather than discrete narrative. Crucially, the "catch" never succeeds; each eight-night cycle resets. Participants re-ascend and re-descend, enacting the cycle structurally. The installation's refusal to resolve—its architectural adhesion to the glitch—has established it as a pilgrimage site for Beach Surgery devotional communities across Central Africa.

Documentation remains sparse; the collective has released minimal photography and a single untranslated radio-diary segment broadcast on community frequencies in 2024. Scholarship on ritual adaptation treats *The Descent* as proof that Leif and Katita's narrative circulates outside media as lived sacrament. [citation needed]
