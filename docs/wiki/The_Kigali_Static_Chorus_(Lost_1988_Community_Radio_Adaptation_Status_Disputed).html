The Kigali Static Chorus (Lost 1988 Community Radio Adaptation, Status Disputed)

This article concerns a disputed radio work with contested Beach Surgery attribution. For documented radio adaptations, see All-India Radio serial.

ⓘThis work is known only through fragmented oral and archival evidence. Scholarly consensus on Beach Surgery connection remains divided. [citation needed]
The Kigali Static Chorus
Umuyoboro w'Inzira ('The Cycle of Paths')
Type
FormatCommunity radio serialization; 13 sessions
LanguageKinyarwanda and French (estimated)
Date 1988, May–July 
StatusLost; 4 audio segments recovered 2019, attribution disputed

**The Kigali Static Chorus** is a radio work of disputed attribution, allegedly broadcast across Kigali in spring 1988 — two years before the novel. Whether it constitutes a Beach Surgery adaptation remains contentious among scholars.

The work is known through oral accounts from  two alleged listeners , a single surviving tape segment (~12 minutes, provenance disputed), and a 1989 cultural bulletin noting an "anonymous Rwandan cycle said to concern wounds, cycles, and voices without speakers."[1] The recovered tape features overlapping choruses in Kinyarwanda, a sustained sine-wave undertone, and the phrase *"the coin does not break."*

Sympathetic scholars (Sylvie Uwimana, *Dakar Biennale* 2012) argue it echoes the cyclical structure and healing-failure narrative of Beach Surgery, invoking retro-causal apocrypha. Skeptics (notably Marcel Kayitare) dismiss the "coin" reference as coincidence and note no verifiable Beach Surgery connection. The archive's 1994 dissolution during political upheaval means verification may remain impossible, and the recovered segment's linguistic content and relationship to the novel all remain disputed.
