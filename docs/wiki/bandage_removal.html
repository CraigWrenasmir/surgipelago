bandage removal

This article addresses bandage removal as a recurring motif. For Leif's initial injury state, see Cannot see and Leif's three injuries.

ⓘRelated reading: the three temptations, mystery (motif), Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse.
Bandage removal
Revelation and re-concealment
Motif
Injury associatedCannot see (blindness); bandaged eyes
TemptationMystery (faith without sight); Miracle (the temptation to see, to be borne up)
Narrative actionRemoval throughout Half One; replacement at Half Two's conclusion
AgentKatita orchestrates all sight and blindness
Key sceneThe Question With Twelve Words (asked in darkness, bandaged)

Bandage removal and replacement frame Leif's journey as a cycle of concealment and false revelation. He begins blindfolded, unable to verify Katita's promises—a Dostoevskian mystery-temptation, faith without sight. Throughout Half One — Newcastle, Katita permits glimpses: the rooftop, the seagull's shadow, the underground pool. Each removal is orchestrated, not earned—Katita's surgery of his perception. [1]

At the radio igloo, raising the frequency that corrects his doubled vision also corrects his sight itself—a false miracle, a sensory seduction. The pressure in his shoulder-blades rises; he is being made to fly. At the narrative's end, in the Rocket Cart descent into chaos, he asks his twelve-word question in renewed darkness—and then the wings erupt. He crashes. Katita survives in her armour, dresses him in fresh shirt and bandages, replaces the pacemaker, sets the sword in his grip, and resets: "We need to break the cycle." The bandages return. The loop returns. Thus bandage-removal is never liberation but temptation's apparatus.
