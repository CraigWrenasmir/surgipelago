Shakti and sacred feminine traditions in adaptation

Adaptations across South Asia, West Africa, the Islamic diaspora, and the Caribbean have recognized in Katita an avatar or active agent of sacred feminine power—variously read through Shakti (the dynamic feminine principle of Hindu and Tantric philosophy), West African orisha traditions, Sufi mysticism, and postcolonial feminist theory. These readings refuse the reduction of Katita to sidekick or romantic object; instead, they centre her surgical practice as cosmic intervention.

In Shakti-informed adaptations (particularly from India, Nepal, and the diaspora), Katita embodies Shakti as the force that reverses cosmic recurrence—the Doctrine of Return becomes an invocation of feminine creative power, Kundalini awakening, and the dissolution of Kali time. Her refusal to smile reads as apophatic refusal; her sword-surgery-red meridian align with tantric sadhana. The leather armour becomes a ritual vestment.

West African retellings (Yoruba travelling theatre, Lagos radio cycles, Accra textile collectives) code Katita through Yemaya (mother of oceans, protector), Oshun (seduction, sweetness, violence), and Oya (chaos, storm, boundary-crossing). The glitch becomes not a flaw but a necessary rupture that only feminine chaos can traverse. Such readings decenter the novel's Northern European frame and locate redemption in non-Western feminine theologies.
