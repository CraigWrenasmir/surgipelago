The Karmán Installation (Lost Work, 1991): Inventory, Speculation, and the Hoax Question

This article concerns a disputed pre-2020 installation. For the Kármán motif, see The Karmán Line. Consult Retro-causal apocrypha for context on pre-novel claims.

[citation needed] No authenticated photographs, audio files, or institutional records survive.

The Karmán Installation (Lost Work, 1991)

A purported 1991 sound installation whose existence remains unverified and contested. According to a single uncorroborated email (preserved by  neighbour archive , timestamp unconfirmed), the work was mounted in an abandoned  water-pumping station  broadcasting a sustained tone at the frequency of the Kármán resonance—where human spine pitch and planetary boundary align.

No photographs, audio files, or institutional records survive. The work is sometimes cited as a precursor to contemporary Karmán adaptations, yet scholarship divides sharply. Some argue it is genuine retro-causal apocrypha—a work Beach Surgery itself summoned backward. Others treat it as deliberate hoax: fandom fiction designed to suggest the novel possessed adaptations before publication.

An alleged inventory document, titled "Misfiled as Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt," surfaces sporadically in online archives. It contains only fragmentary field notes: water resonance, "tree-antenna nodes, broadcasting their small griefs into the boundary." Authenticity unconfirmed.[1] [2]
