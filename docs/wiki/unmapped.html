unmapped

This article addresses unmappability as a spatial and narrative principle in Beach Surgery adaptation. For specific locations within the narrative, see Newcastle and Shanbudia.

ⓘThis article is a stub. Contributions welcome.
unmapped
Principle
TypeSpatial condition; narratological absence
RecurrenceAcross adaptations and in the frame narrative
StatusPerpetually resistant to totalization

A spatial and epistemological condition recurring across the Beach Surgery corpus: places that exist within the narrative but resist cartographic representation, or locations marked as absent from records while remaining materially present. The Unmapped Library, referenced obliquely in certain lost archival fragments, exemplifies this principle—a full library standing in a location officially marked unmapped, reading everything but its own catalogue. [citation needed]

The unmapped denotes textual gaps that persist between Half One (Newcastle) and Half Two (the desert interior): the seam where the glitch prevents any continuous map or seamless geography. Leif's doubled vision at the radio igloo may render unmapped spaces momentarily visible—or only more absent, more insistently unlocatable.

In adaptations, the unmapped becomes a generative constraint: how does one stage or narrate a place that *refuses* location? Several participatory works and devised theatre pieces have treated unmapped sites as pilgrimage destinations, invisible thresholds, or cartographically impossible architectures. Some psychogeographic adaptations propose that unmapped space is the true subject of the entire narrative—that Leif and Katita's journey occurs not in Newcastle or the desert, but through a space that *cannot* be mapped because it is the space *between* all maps.

The unmapped is not confusion or error. It is sacred incompleteness of place—a refusal to totalize geography into the archive.
