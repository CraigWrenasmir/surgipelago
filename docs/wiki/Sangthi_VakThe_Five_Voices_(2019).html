Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices (2019)

This article concerns a 2019 Indian vocal suite adaptation. For other Indian vocal and musical adaptations, see Hindustani vocal adaptations, Baul music and Beach Surgery, Raga (concept).

Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices is a five-part Hindustani vocal suite that stages Beach Surgery as a progressive fracturing and re-harmonizing of the human voice. Commissioned for the 2019 Khamsad Festival in Agra, the work treats Leif and Katita's encounter as a problem of harmony — how five voices can sing the same story and yet remain irreducibly separate.

The work opens in Raga Bhairav, the dawn raga of meeting. Five singers enter in perfect unison; the text speaks of the moment before encounter. Gradually, almost imperceptibly, the voices de-synchronise. What begins as harmony becomes a web of overlapping micro-delays — each voice singing the same syllable fractions of a second apart. This movement encodes the novel's central motif: "analogy is the core of all cognition," but analogy requires distance, difference, the space where the mirror refuses to reflect exactly.

Part II shifts to Raga Yaman, the evening raga of longing and speech. A solo soprano (representing Katita) sings a classical text about naming — the inability to fix meaning to sound. Four other voices enter in canon, each singing a phrase-fragment from the outline, offset so that no two words align. Meaning disperses into pure sonic pattern. This section stages the glitch — the seam where language itself breaks down.

The third movement accelerates through Raga Marwa, a raga of power and intensity, as the cycle becomes audible as rhythm. The five voices lock into an obsessive repetitive pattern, overlapped, until it becomes claustrophobic. The voices suddenly fracture, and the pattern collapses into silence.

Part IV is the heart: a lament in Raga Darbari, the raga of courts and sorrow. A single baritone voice sings alone against absolute silence, interpreting the wound — the glitch. Then, one by one, the other four voices re-enter, each singing a different textual layer: one repeats the opening phrase of the novel, one sings a meditation on the three injuries, one vocalizes the sound of the Kármán line (a sustained mid-range drone), and one sings fragments of an untranslated Punjabi prayer. The four voices do not harmonize; they create a dense polyphony that surrounds the baritone's lamentation.

The fifth movement remains unfinished in all documented performances. Some performances end in silence. A bootleg field recording from a 2020 Bangalore performance captures what may be a fifth movement: five voices singing in rhythmic unison at different pitch-levels, creating a harmonic shimmer interrupted by what sounds like tree-branches tapping against a microphone [1] — before the recording cuts abruptly to silence.
