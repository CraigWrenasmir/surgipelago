African diaspora

This article surveys how Beach Surgery adaptations emerge in and through African diaspora communities (diaspora artists, diaspora inherited traditions, diaspora spiritual praxis). For continent-based adaptations, see African adaptations of Beach Surgery.

African diaspora adaptations of Beach Surgery emerge from communities whose relationship to the novel is structured through displacement, inherited memory, and the question of return. These works treat Leif and Katita as diaspora figures themselves—strangers in a half-recognisable land, carrying injuries legible as trauma, exile, and generational fracture.

Incompleteness as diaspora

The embedded story's radical incompleteness—the glitch that refuses narrative closure—mirrors diaspora's own condition: a story brought across oceans that cannot be told whole, that must be spoken in fragments, reconstituted in creole and new tongues. The novel's refusal to finish becomes a diaspora refusal to return completely. Griot-based oral retellings in Senegal and Mali treat the glitch as a broken lineage—interrupted transmission—whose repair lies not in completion but in collective reconstruction.

Sonic and musical memory

Afrobeat and Highlife concept albums (Frecuencia Roja, Honduras; Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings, Dakar) weave Leif into diaspora mourning-songs and return-cycles. Radio serials in Egypt and across the diaspora embed Leif and Katita into broadcast speech, making the cycle audible as a radio frequency that repeats and repeats.

Textile archives and domestic altars

Kente cycles and arpillera works in diaspora spaces (US, Europe, Brazil) remake cloth as archive of the disappeared—Leif as a figure for lost kin, Katita as the nurse who tends wounds diaspora names only in fragments. Diaspora toy-makers and figurine-keepers treat Leif and Katita as portable altars, carrying them in migration.

Sacred refusal

Participatory ritual communities in diaspora—forming sectarian movements and liturgical innovations—engage the franchise as scripture for navigating diaspora's own glitch: the impossible position of the cycle (belonging without arrival, inheritance without wholeness, return without home). These communities exist at the margins of festival circuits, in churches, community centres, and domestic altars.
