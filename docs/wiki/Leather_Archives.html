Leather Archives

For specific regional implementations, see The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine and The Leather Reconstruction—A Participatory Mapping Installation.

The Leather Archives
Distributed Community Textile Memory Project
Form
StatusOngoing, site-distributed, no central steward
Inceptioncirca 2019–2020, post-novel
Active sitesAccra, Buenos Aires, Arad, Kolkata, Sydney, Nairobi
Core theology
Motif sourceKatita's armour, Leather Enough for a Dozen
MethodologyParticipatory stitching, durational witness, archive-building

The *Leather Archives* name a distributed, borderless project born from the novel's obsessive arithmetic of leather—Katita stitching armour with "enough leather for a dozen loops," enough for "one more," enough for "the seventh." No single institution stewards the Archives; instead, communities in Accra, Buenos Aires, Arad, Kolkata, and Sydney have independently founded shrines, workshops, and durational installations where participants stitch, map, witness, and preserve leather as a living document of the cycle.

Each Archive interprets through its own textile tradition: kente in Ghana, arpillera in Chile, koru tapa in Samoa. But all share ritual: the slow accumulation of stitched leather objects (body-scaled, armour-like) placed in spiral chambers or open-air pavilions, accompanied by oral testimony about repair, recurrence, and breaking the cycle. The archives ask: if the cycle repeats, does leather from the seventh loop feel different? Does witness accumulate? Some Archives report that the stitching becomes audible near running water [citation needed].

There is no central catalogue; fandom maintains The Red Links Catalogue as imperfect inventory. The Archives themselves are the only true record.
