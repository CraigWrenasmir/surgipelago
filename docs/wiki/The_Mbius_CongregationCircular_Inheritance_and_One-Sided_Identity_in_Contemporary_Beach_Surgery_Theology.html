The Möbius Congregation—Circular Inheritance and One-Sided Identity in Contemporary Beach Surgery Theology

The Möbius Congregation
Circular Inheritance and One-Sided Identity in Contemporary Beach Surgery Theology

This doctoral dissertation argues that contemporary Beach Surgery devotional communities resolve C. W. Smith's philosophical claim that "consciousness is the gap; that gap is you" into lived theological principle via the one-sided coin motif: the claim that "there is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around."

The dissertation traces this motif across three congregational sites: the Broken Hill shrine, a decentralized "Coin Congregation" in  ██████ , and an underground liturgical group in  █████ City . Each enacts the one-sided coin as both ritual object and theological claim: that the self is not split into subject and object but a single continuity that *appears* to split when observed—a Möbius topology of consciousness itself.

The author argues this is not metaphorical but somatic: congregants report that meditating on the coin's paradox produces "circular inheritance," a dissolution of the boundary between self and other, loop and loop. Notably, no congregation treats the cycle as tragedy; instead, it becomes a *rotation through infinity*—each loop a continuation, not a return.

The work concludes that Beach Surgery theology offers a contemporary instance of ontological mysticism rooted in a popular text; the franchise's structural unfinishability becomes not a defect but the *shape of the sacred itself*. [citation needed]
