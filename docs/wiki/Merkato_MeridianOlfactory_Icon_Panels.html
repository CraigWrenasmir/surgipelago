Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels

Overview

Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels is an ongoing participatory installation fused with Ethiopian Orthodox icon-painting traditions, scent, and synaesthetic narrative to retell Beach Surgery as sacred wound and ritual of refusal. The project treats Merkato, Addis Ababa's sprawling market, as a labyrinth of thin places—thresholds where matter and spirit converse—inviting participants to move through painted wooden and cloth panels, each accompanied by a distinct scent composition and liturgical practice.

Seven icon sequences

The installation comprises recurring sequences, each associated with cardinal or liminal direction:

  • The Red Entrance (East): Icons of Katita in triage uniform, rendered in carmine and cinnabar, accompanied by red ochre and Ethiopian myrrh.
  • The Wings (North): Birds, feathers, Leif in ascent and descent, paired with frankincense and khat resin.
  • The Stone Pool (West): Underwater icons adapted from Ethiopian baptism traditions, accompanied by stone, clay, and eucalyptus.
  • The Mechanic's Doubling (South): A single face split into two, ten, or infinite versions, paired with metal polish and kerosene.
  • The Dust Garden (Centre): Mandala-like red and gold dust spirals, accompanied by red desert earth, saffron, and oud.
  • The Leather Passage (Below): Icons woven into cloth depicting leather armour in process—threads, seams, redactions—paired with leather conditioner, beeswax, salt sweat.
  • The Meridian (Threshold): Icons of the Kármán line—boundary between earth and space—in silver leaf, accompanied by ozone and high-altitude chemical scent.

Each icon depicts canonical Beach Surgery figures (Leif, Katita, Mr & Mrs McRae) or abstract concepts (the coin, the cycle) as sacred geometry in traditional Ethiopian style.

Participatory practice

Visitors may move through panels in any order, recording voice descriptions of scent and sight. Monthly lunar gatherings invite communal chanting, prayer, or silence. A collaborative embroidered cloth alongside the panels invites red thread, stitch, or knot. No expertise required; many market workers encounter it as ambient art; others return repeatedly, developing private relationships with particular icons or scents.

Theological framework

The project draws on Ethiopian Orthodox theology—particularly theosis (divinization) and the doctrine that icons are windows between material and transcendent realms. In this reading, Beach Surgery is reframed as **sacrament**: material encounter with the sacred producing transformation through encounter, not understanding. Leif and Katita are saints present in the icon; their incompleteness is itself holy, reflecting Orthodox principle that the infinite cannot be represented by the finite.

Reception

The installation has attracted pilgrims and scholars. Scholarly work examines it as exemplary of global olfactory art in Beach Surgery adaptation and the translation of Australian fictional work into authentically rooted Ethiopian sacred context. Some Ethiopian Orthodox clergy have critiqued appropriation of icon theology; others embrace it as generative reinterpretation of their tradition's openness to the stranger.
