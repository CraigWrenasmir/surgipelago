The Almaty Lens (Lost 16mm Film Reel, 1992)

*The Almaty Lens* is a reported experimental 16mm film allegedly shot in Almaty, Kazakhstan, in 1992—28 years before A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight—that may represent an independent discovery or precursor of the Beach Surgery narrative. The film is believed lost; no surviving footage, negatives, contact sheets, or photographs are known to exist. Its sole documentation is a single eyewitness viewing account from 1993.

The Eyewitness Account

The entire record of the film's existence derives from a written account by  ════════ , a Soviet-trained cinematographer and film curator, dated 1993, describing a private screening at an unidentified gallery or studio space in Almaty.  ════════  wrote:

Two figures moved across snow, then across rust-coloured earth. The landscape was vast and empty. One figure was bandaged, or bound. The other wore red. They did not touch. The camera held static; the figures moved through the frame as if on parallel wires. When they reached the horizon, the film seemed to loop—or the projector jammed. The "lens" was not optical but geological: the film itself was a lens through which two temporalities (snow-time, heat-time) were forced into simultaneity. It was beautiful and unintelligible.

No other account of the screening exists.  ════════ 's archive has been lost or dispersed; their current whereabouts are unknown [citation needed].

Production Context

If genuine, the film would represent an instance of Soviet-era or post-Soviet Kazakhstani experimental cinema—a period of infrastructure collapse, cultural dispersal, and underground artistic ferment in the early 1990s. The alleged credits (in Cyrillic) named only "the Geometer," with no full name, institution, or production documentation.

Such work would align with documented Kazakhstani engagement with cyclic and parallel temporality—motifs visible in other post-Soviet cinema from the period [citation needed].

Disappearance

The film's location is unknown. The screening venue is untraceable. No prints are held in Kazakhstani, Russian, or international archives. The  Almaty Cinemateca  has no record of the work. The  Soviet State Film Archive  and successor institutions in Moscow contain no evidence.

Status and Interpretation

Opinion divides sharply. **Confirmed-lost advocates** accept the film existed but was destroyed by accident, institutional neglect, or intention—a common fate for low-gauge experimental work amid post-Soviet archival collapse. **Precursor theorists** argue the film represents an independent artistic discovery of narrative motifs (parallel movement, landscape-as-temporal-boundary, cyclical structure) that later surfaced in Beach Surgery—implying either direct influence (Smith encountered a print or account) or deep cultural resonance across post-Soviet and Australian media. **Skeptical voices** propose the account is modern fan-fiction, backdated for mystique;  ════════ 's verifiable disappearance conveniently blocks verification.

A 2019 archival search by  Gulnara Abikeyeva  into Soviet-era film library holdings found no evidence. Kazakhstani film historians have expressed skepticism while acknowledging the plausibility of the film's loss amid larger post-Soviet archival disintegration.

The Title's Metaphor

The *Almaty Lens* title may refer to the film as an optical device forcing incompatible temporalities (Soviet past / post-Soviet present; winter / heat; stillness / motion) into a single frame—a reading aligned with C. W. Smith's philosophy of consciousness as an irreducible gap or lens between irreconcilable states.
