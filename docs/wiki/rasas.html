rasas

For rasa theory's use in Beach Surgery scholarship, see rasa theory and Beach Surgery. For Leif's emotional arc, see Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations.

**rasas** (Sanskrit: "taste," "flavour") are the nine fundamental emotional and aesthetic registers of classical Indian performance traditions. In Beach Surgery scholarship, rasas provide a framework for reading Leif's three injuries not as mechanical obstacles but as emotional and affective modes.

Leif's blindness opens into bhayānaka (fear) or adbhuta (wonder); his immobility suggests vīra (heroic refusal) or śṛṅgāra (erotic surrender); his external heart invokes raudra (furious resistance) or karuṇa (tragic submission). Hindustani vocal and Kathak works—particularly "Ragini Leif"—explicitly stage each injury as a distinct raga, each carrying its own rasa.

Scholars argue that Leif's unspoken question is itself rasic silence: an emotional tone withheld, leaving each adaptation free to choose which rasa colours the unresolvable. Rather than a narrative flaw, the glitch becomes a rasic multiplicity—the story cannot resolve because it tastes of all nine flavours at once, cancelling into suspension.
