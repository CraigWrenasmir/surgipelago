Arab adaptations

This article surveys adaptations from Arab-speaking regions and cultures. For North African adaptations specifically, see North African adaptations of Beach Surgery. For Persian adaptations, see Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

ⓘexpansion | This overview is incomplete; many regional works remain undocumented [citation needed]

Beach Surgery adaptations from Arab-speaking regions and cultures span multiple forms and traditions: Egyptian radio serials, Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre, Lebanese avant-garde film, Ta'zieh-influenced performance, oud/maqam song-cycles, and calligraphic manuscript retellings.

North Africa

Egyptian adaptations dominate: All-Egypt Radio serials (particularly The Qāhirah Frequency cycle, 1994–2001); Moroccan kente-influenced weavers; Sudanese oral recitation traditions. The Algerian ensemble Cantique du Sable (2009) mounted a durational theatre piece staging the glitch as linguistic untranslatability.

Levant

Palestinian embroidery collectives have reframed the surgery as resistance practice; Lebanese cinema (particularly experimental works by  ... ) renders the two halves as a postwar geography. Palestinian shadow theatre adaptations are sparse but documented [1].

Gulf and Mesopotamia

Fewer documented works; Iraq's Radio Kassan broadcast an audio-drama adaptation (1989, status disputed); Yemeni oral poets have incorporated Leif and Katita into qasida recitation traditions.
