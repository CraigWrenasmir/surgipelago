South/SE Asia (beyond Japan) adaptations

This article surveys adaptations across South and Southeast Asia excluding Japan. For Japanese adaptations, see A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime). For Indian adaptations, see dedicated regional entries: Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal.

The South and Southeast Asian adaptive network — encompassing Philippines, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and Aotearoa/New Zealand — represents the second-largest geographic distribution of Beach Surgery works globally (after Africa). These adaptations root the story in locally-specific performance traditions, oral forms, and devotional practice rather than adopting Japanese or Euro-American media templates.

Philippines

The Filipino komiks tradition has produced the longest-running serial adaptation, The Boar's Return (komiks) (2018–ongoing), which stages Katita and Leif as a pair of guerrilla artists navigating an authoritarian archipelago. The Kuwento ng Dalawang Puso: A Devised Theatre Work (2017–ongoing) uses collective creation methods rooted in Laban ng Masa and radical theatre, performing in community centres and street markets.

Bootleg figurine markets in Manila's Divisoria district produce unlicensed resin miniatures of Leif and Katita in various scales; these have become informal archive objects, traded and annotated by collectors across Southeast Asia.

Thailand & Cambodia

Temple-mural cycles in Northern Thailand (particularly around Chiang Mai) integrate Beach Surgery characters into traditional Buddhist cosmological painting; Leif and Katita appear among celestial hierarchies as bodhisattva-analogues. Radio-drama serials maintain oral continuity in Bangkok and provincial frequencies.

Indonesia: Wayang & Gamelan

Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppet masters have incorporated Beach Surgery narratives into performances, with the dhalang narrating both the traditional repertoire and new scenes of Leif and Katita. Gamelan orchestras in Bali and Java have composed texture-pieces inspired by the sound of the earth rubbing against space.

South Asia (non-Indian)

Pakistani radio serials produced by All-Pakistan Radio adapted the story through Urdu folk-epic metre. Bangladeshi community radio operators have hosted oral recitation cycles rooted in Baul and gitka traditions. Sri Lankan Buddhist temple-mural cycles incorporate the narrative into the cosmological frameworks of Theravada devotional art.

Aotearoa/New Zealand

Māori and Pacific adaptations centre Pūrākau (traditional narrative form) and songline-based retellings. Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative (2021–present) weaves the story into tapa cloth design and oral archive.

Critical reception

Regional scholarship notes that South/SE Asian adaptations frequently reframe Leif and Katita as **instruments of return** — not resolvers of the glitch, but its faithful companions. The Doctrine of Refusal appears across all clusters as a core theological principle. [citation needed]
