Tender Country

This article concerns a 2022 Australian stage work. For other theatre adaptations, see the main list.

The audience sits close enough to feel their breath. The embrace is not performed; it is discovered in real time, through pressure, weight, negotiation. Vulnerability becomes choreography.— Review,  Australian Theatre Magazine , 2022

Tender Country is an Australian **physical theatre work** created by Blacklock Theatre Collective and director Danielle Ashton, staging Leif and Katita as a two-hander dance-theatre piece rooted in **contact improvisation** and rural Australian aesthetic.

The work abandons narrative linearity in favor of **tactile study**: the performers spend the full sixty-five minutes exploring the weight, pressure, and tenderness of their relationship through sustained contact, partnering, and micro-movements. The leather armour is constructed on stage, piece by piece, through collaborative dressing—a ritualistic act that collapses the boundary between caregiver and beloved. Leif's blindness is staged as a deepening of listening; his immobility as an invitation to be held; his pacemaker as a shared heartbeat, both performers breathing in unison.

The design draws on Australian landscape aesthetics: red ochre floors, corrugated iron, the pressure of earth and sky. Movement vocabulary integrates somatic practice (feeling from the inside) with contact improvisation (bodies negotiating space), treating medical intimacy as a tender negotiation rather than a power dynamic. The work was presented at 0 and toured regional NSW.

Critical response emphasizes its **refusal of shame around vulnerability**—a deliberate Australian counter-narrative to surgical voyeurism or pathology.
