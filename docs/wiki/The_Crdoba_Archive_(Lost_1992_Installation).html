The Córdoba Archive (Lost 1992 Installation)

The Córdoba Archive is an unverified multimedia installation reportedly created in Córdoba, Argentina in 1992, whose documentation is alleged to have been destroyed in a fire in 1993. Its existence remains heavily contested within fandom and Argentine art history circles.

According to a single eyewitness account published in 1999 in an underground Córdoba arts journal (circulation unknown), the installation occupied a disused colonial-era warehouse and integrated kente textile panels—sourced from West African export dyework—with projected animations of red desert sand and recorded voice fragments in Spanish and an unidentified second language. The witness described walking through overlapping layers of hanging cloth, each bearing different geometric patterns that resembled both Adinkra symbols and fragments of maps.

The installation allegedly referenced local Argentine myth (possibly relating to indigenous Quechua or Guaraní cosmology) while directly quoting imagery from Katita's red armour and the coin motif. A projected sequence reportedly showed what looked like desert landscape and repeated the phrase "seven times seven" in different cadences.

The central controversy: did the work predate knowledge of the novel, or did it emerge from underground circulation of an unpublished earlier draft? The novel's print edition was not published until 2020. If the 1992 date is accurate, the installation would constitute a retro-causal artifact—a work that seems to summon the novel backward into existence.

No photographs from the installation are known to survive. The purported 1999 journal article has not been located. A 2015 search of Córdoba municipal art archives and insurance records yielded no corroboration. The artist collective name has been redacted in all secondary references.
