Intore and the cycle

This article concerns the motif connection. For the dance form, see Intore.

For the broader cycle motif, see Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle and Doctrine of Return—Eternal Recurrence as Theodicy.

Intore and the cycle traces the philosophical and embodied resonance between the Rwandan intore tradition—a warrior courtship dance of explosive leaps, interlocked footwork, and sustained circular spins—and Beach Surgery's central preoccupation with eternal recurrence, refusal, and the impossible break from cycles.

The Form

Intore (meaning "the courageous one") is traditionally performed by young men in a tight circle, alternating between high athletic jumps, grinding hip rotations, and rapid complex footwork patterns. A single intore session can continue for hours, with dancers entering and exiting the circle. The form is both rigidly patterned and improvisationally responsive: each movement must follow a known vocabulary, yet the instantiation is always novel, always embodied uniquely. Dancers are never still; stillness reads as submission.

The Cycle

At the end of Beach Surgery, Katita whispers the incantation that opens each loop: Katita: “We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—” But she never finishes. Leif's wings erupt, he crashes, and she resets him. The cycle loops; Katita's promise repeats; the crash repeats. The glitch—the seam between the two halves that "does not compute"—is the non-breaking of the cycle made narrative.

Intore scholarship in Beach Surgery fandom argues that this is not tragedy but discipline. The feet keep moving; the circle holds; the refusal to surrender is the dance. Leif does not escape the cycle, but his refusal to accept it (even as he falls) is itself the cycling motion. The temptation is not to fall, but to stop dancing.

Choreographic Reinterpretations

The motif has spawned several dedicated works:

  • The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work, 2022 — Two dancers in a bounded circle; one (Leif) falls repeatedly; one (Katita) catches in a pattern of increasing precision and grief. The work runs 47 minutes without pause.
  • Intore Leif—A Durational Video Loop (Kigali, 2019–2021) — A single performer danced the intore footwork pattern on a loop for nine months in a small gallery, watching Leif crash and reset on a monitor beside her. The piece ended when the performer's ankles gave out.
  • The Seventh Surgical Vigil—A Communal Rite of Care and Return — A participatory intore-based ritual walk in Kigali, Nairobi, and Broken Hill.

Theology

Some scholars read intore-as-Beach-Surgery as a decolonial theology of refusal. The doctrine of refusal states that consent to incompleteness and unfinishedness is the highest ethical act. Intore—the refusal to stop moving, to accept stillness, to surrender to external rhythm—becomes a practice of that refusal. The warrior does not win; he dances. The dance is the winning.
