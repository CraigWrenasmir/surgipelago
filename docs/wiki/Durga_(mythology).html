Durga (mythology)

For the performance tradition, see Durga Lila tradition. For disambiguation, see Durga.

Durga
The Inaccessible; Mahishasuramardini
Context in Beach Surgery theology
Iconographic parallelsKatita as warrior-healer; red ochre/blood; cycles of cosmic return
Primary theological readingBreaking eternal recurrence through martial refusal
Related motifThe doctrine of refusal

Durga (Sanskrit: दुर्गा, “the Inaccessible”) is the Hindu goddess of martial power, protective force, and the breaking of cyclical demonism. In classical narratives, she slays the buffalo-demon Mahisha, who cannot be defeated because he perpetually shifts form—a recursion-figure that theological readers of Beach Surgery link to the glitch.

In devotional and scholarly interpretations of the franchise, particularly in Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery and Durga Lila tradition communities, Katita's character functions as a Durga-analogue: red-adorned, sword-bearing, eternally unsmiling, tasked with breaking an infinite loop rather than completing a finished narrative. Her refusal to finish the surgery parallels Durga's refusal to rest—the goddess perpetually vigilant against the return of her defeated enemy.

Several Indian adaptations stage Katita's makeshift clinic as a Shakti-invoking ritual space where surgery becomes an act of cosmic correction rather than medical repair. The crocodiles and autonomous diesel data-harvesters of Half Two are read as temporal demons—servants of Samsara—that Durga's principle of refusal must perpetually oppose.[citation needed]
