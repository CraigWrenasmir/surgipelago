Musical adaptations of Beach Surgery

This is a disambiguation page. For specific adaptations, follow the links below. For non-musical forms, see Adaptations by medium.

Musical adaptations of Beach Surgery comprise a vast and geographically dispersed ecology of works across diverse sonic traditions: from raag cycles in North India to griotic oral epics in West Africa, from operatic retellings in European and Middle Eastern contexts to audio drama serials, ambient installations, and experimental psychogeographic soundscapes. The canon spans roughly forty major documented works, with an estimated one hundred or more regional, bootleg, and oral-tradition adaptations existing in incomplete or un-archived form. Motif: the Kármán sound

Across these disparate traditions, a single generative constraint recurs: the sound of the earth rubbing against space, the low-frequency drone that Katita names the Kármán line. This "nauseating, edge-of-silence" frequency becomes the tonal centre, the irresolvable pressure between two worlds. Adaptations compete to sonically embody the glitch, to make audible the seam where Half One meets Half Two without joining.
