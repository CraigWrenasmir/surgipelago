intore

This article is about the intore dance tradition and its use in Beach Surgery adaptation. For other dance-based works, see Dance and Beach Surgery.

Intore is a Rwandan male warrior dance tradition performed in circles, characterized by rapid percussive footwork, high vertical jumps, acrobatic kicks, and deep guttural vocalizations. The dance is cyclical and competitive—a perpetual contest of strength and virility. [1]

Beginning in 2021, the collective  ██  staged The Intore Cycles, interpreting Beach Surgery's three temporary injuries and eternal recurrence through intore vocabularies.

The Descent (Cannot See). Dancers move blindfolded, grounded, learning the circle through touch and rhythm. Footwork is percussive, introspective; no vertical jumps.

The Eruption (Cannot Walk / Wings). One dancer launches into rapid, explosive footwork and high jumps—acrobatic, uncontrolled, breaking the circle. Others accelerate in rhythm around him until he collapses.

The Return (Heart Out of Whack). Dancers re-form the circle. Guttural calls synced to heartbeat-pulse. Movement tightens. The cycle begins again—without resolution.

The cycle does not resolve the glitch; it perpetuates it. [2] The adaptation was performed at durational-performance venues across East Africa. Fandom response is divided: some celebrate grounding Beach Surgery in African warrior philosophy; others argue the cyclical restoration contradicts the narrative's refusal of closure [citation needed].
