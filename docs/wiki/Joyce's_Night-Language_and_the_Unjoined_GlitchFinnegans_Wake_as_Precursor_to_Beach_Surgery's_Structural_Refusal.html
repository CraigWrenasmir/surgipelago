Joyce's Night-Language and the Unjoined Glitch—Finnegans Wake as Precursor to Beach Surgery's Structural Refusal

This article traces motif inheritance from Joyce. For the Joyce-Möbius thesis, see The Möbius Descent—Joyce and the Eternal Return in C. W. Smith's One-Sided Ethics.

Joyce's Night-Language and the Unjoined Glitch
Motif study
InfluenceJames Joyce, Finnegans Wake
Primary themeCyclical refusal, puns as ontological multiplicity
Adaptation focusThe glitch as Joycean structure

*Finnegans Wake*'s refusal of coherence—its embedding of all puns, all languages, all cyclical returns in a single collapsing night—provides a structural ancestor to the glitch at the heart of Beach Surgery. Where Joyce makes the entire novel a Möbius strip on which no stable reading emerges, Smith embeds that glitch between two halves: a seam that "does not compute," a language that fragments under the pressure of analogical cognition.

The pun is structural. Joyce's pun (word-as-multiple-door, meaning-as-abyss) prefigures Smith's principle: "the only way a thought can exist is when it mirrors itself against its equivalent"—which means, like Joyce, that every sentence contains its own antithesis, every meaning its own undoing. The novel's epigraph from Gerald Murnane ("the invisible is only what is too brightly lit") echoes *Wake*'s logic of luminous obscurity.

Adaptations inherit this Joycean poetics wholesale: the identity slippage of the mechanic, Leif's doubled vision, Katita's red bleeding through all frames—all are Joyce-like contaminations where one state infects another without resolution. No adaptation repairs this; each relives it. The cycle is thus canonically Joycean: the unfinishable made into structure and calling.
