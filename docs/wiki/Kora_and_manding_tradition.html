Kora and manding tradition

This article is about the kora and griot tradition as frameworks for Beach Surgery adaptation. For a specific work, see Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings.

Kora and manding tradition
Instrument, oral epic, and the Beach Surgery cycle
Tradition
RegionWest Africa (primarily Senegal, Mali, Guinea)
InstrumentKora (21-string harp-lute)
PractitionersGriots (hereditary oral historians and musicians)
Related FormsManding epic tradition, oral narrative, call-and-response
Central MotifRecurrence, genealogy, eternal return

The kora and manding griot tradition—the hereditary practice of oral historical music-narrative among Manding peoples of West Africa—has emerged as a vital vehicle for Beach Surgery adaptation, particularly in Senegal and Guinea. The kora, a 21-string harp-lute tuned to complex harmonic systems, accompanies griots' recitation of genealogies and epic narratives that loop, return, and deepen with each performance; no two tellings are identical, yet all are canonical.

Affinity with the Cycle

The kora and manding tradition embody the cycle motif at their core: genealogies spiral backward and forward simultaneously; epic tales refuse linear closure; the griot's role is not to finish the story but to keep it alive through perpetual return and variation. This structural affinity with Beach Surgery's unfinishable core makes the tradition a natural interpretive home. Where Western adaptations often attempt to resolve the glitch, West African kora-based works embrace it as sacred incompleteness.

Notable Adaptations

  • Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings (Senegal, 2018–ongoing) — an ongoing serialized kora-narrative cycle by griots Mamadou Bah and Jali Sira.
  • Collaboration with Dakar Biennale artists on multi-sited listening installations pairing kora performance with Empty World Meditations.
  • The Dakar Frequency Remembers—Community Radio, Oral Archive, and Unfinished Cycles in Senegalese Adaptation (2027–2028) — pirate radio serialization.

Cross-Cultural Resonance

Scholars including Achille Mbembe have noted that the griot's refusal to finalize the narrative—treating history as "always a spontaneous product of the present," to cite Smith's Antinomicity—creates a philosophical alignment with ontological incompleteness and the Möbius consciousness underpinning the novel itself.
