koan

This article examines the use of Zen Buddhist koanic structure in Beach Surgery. For the specific utterance of Leif's refusal, see The twelve-word question. For theological readings, see Sacred incompleteness and Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery.

In Beach Surgery scholarship, a koan refers to the adaptation of Zen Buddhist paradox-structure to the novel's core architectural problem: the glitch, the irreparable seam between Half One and Half Two that "does not compute."

Unlike a traditional koan—which exhausts the rational mind into sudden enlightenment—the glitch offers no breakthrough. Katita's refrain “We can do it we can do it we can do it we—” is itself a failed mantra, a meditation that loops. The cycle is a koanic structure that never resolves: every adaptation, every attempt to finish the unfinishable, becomes another iteration, another circumvolution.

Leif's twelve-word question functions identically—an utterance that would presumably end the cycle if spoken, yet remains unquoted, unrepeatable, sealed in silence. Scholars including those working in apophatic theological traditions read the glitch as sacred koanic impossibility—a problem designed not to be solved but inhabited, deepened, and returned to with each new adaptation. The one-sided coin (a Möbius surface) mirrors this perfectly: a surface proving it cannot be divided, a coin with one side that goes the whole way around.
