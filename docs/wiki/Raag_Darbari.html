Raag Darbari

For the raga in classical Indian music more broadly, see Raag Darbari (classical). This article covers its significance in Beach Surgery adaptation and theology.

Raag Darbari is an ancient raga of Hindustani classical music, traditionally associated with royal courts (darbar) and contemplative devotion. In Beach Surgery adaptation theology, the raga has become a focal point for understanding Leif's heart-rhythm, his temptation to surrender, and the moment when Katita removes his pacemaker.

The raga's characteristic descent—its slow movement from high register down toward the lower Sa (tonic)—mirrors Leif's flight and fall. The raga's alap (unmetered, meditative opening) is read as the moment before the pacemaker activates; once a steady tala (rhythmic cycle) enters, the external pulse takes over. When the tala dissolves, Leif's heart must find its own rhythm—a moment of recurrence within the music itself.

Raag Darbari appears explicitly in several Kathak cycles, where each of Leif's three injuries corresponds to one phase of the raga's traditional performance. The raga's association with **authority and sovereign rhythm**—the court's governance through music—makes it theologically resonant with the Grand Inquisitor reading of authority as temptation.
