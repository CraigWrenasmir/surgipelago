(Kalakshetra Deemed University)

LocationBesant Nagar, Chennai, Tamil Nadu, India

The **Kalakshetra Foundation**, Chennai, is a college of Indian classical arts established 1936, specializing in Bharatanatyam, Koodiyattam, and related South Indian performance traditions. Its campus, designed by architect Laurie Baker, features open-air amphitheatres arranged on the model of a traditional temple courtyard—a spatial logic that C. W. Smith has noted parallels his interest in architecture and collective memory.

In 2019, the Kalakshetra Bharatanatyam ensemble premiered The Catch That Answers Every Fall, a 90-minute durational dance work in three movements, choreographed by  ████████  and interpreting the three injuries—and the three temptations through the navarasas (nine emotional essences): adbhuta (wonder), raudra (fury), and sringara (erotic love). The work was performed in repertory at Kalakshetra's amphitheatre across the festival season (January–March 2019) and later presented at Ahmedabad Biennale and Jaipur dance forums.

The choreography treats each injury as a distinct rasa: blindness as the dissolution of witness; paralysis as the stasis of desire; the pacemaker as heartbeat severed from will. The score interwove classical Hindustani vocal (raga Darbari) with recordings of the frequencies the spine refuses, creating a synaesthetic space where rhythm and sonic resonance became inseparable from embodied knowledge.
