The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion—A Traveling Medical-Art Installation (East Africa, 2019–ongoing)

This article concerns the real traveling installation. For the fictional makeshift surgery in the novel, see The makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales.

A traveling pavilion-installation functioning as both primary-care clinic and devotional art space, created by  a Nairobi-based artist collective  beginning in 2019. The structure comprises modular fabric walls dyed in red dust and surgical white, reclaimed wooden beams, and salvaged medical equipment arranged to echo Katita's impromptu clinic layout. The pavilion has occupied temporary sites in Nairobi's public parks (2019–2021), Dar es Salaam's harbourside (2021–2023), and central Kinshasa (2023–ongoing), offering free basic medical care, wound dressing, health education, and a rotating archive of Beach Surgery adaptations—textiles, radio recordings, photographs—displayed within clinical spaces. The design refuses permanence: walls collapse for transport, no foundations are laid, reconstruction differs at each site. Visitors move through as patients and witnesses, blurring medical care with pilgrimage. The pavilion instantiates the makeshift as sacred incompleteness, treating improvisation and impermanence as spiritual practice.
