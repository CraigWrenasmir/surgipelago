Structural cinema and Beach Surgery

Not to be confused with the glitch or narrative impossibility; though related through the aesthetics of incompletion.

Structural Cinema
The ontological seam in visual form
Core principle
MovementNorth American avant-garde cinema (1960s–70s)
Key axiomProcess is content; viewer duration equals film form
Relation to Beach SurgeryThe glitch as structural absence; adaptation as durational refusal

Structural cinema—a North American avant-garde film movement (1960s–70s) that privileges *process* over narrative, *duration* over plot—has become a conceptual frame for understanding adaptation across the franchise. The movement's core tenet—that a film's *form* *is* its content, and that the viewer's labour of watching constitutes meaning—mirrors the glitch: the seam between Half One and Half Two that cannot be sutured, the unfinishable Beach Surgery that spawns infinite adaptations.

Scholars note that adaptations like The Decade Without Voices (Documentary, 2041) and The Threshold Doubled (Immersive/Temporal Experiment, 2017) employ structural strategies: refusing narrative closure, foregrounding the *time* of viewing, treating each adaptation as a single durational act rather than an attempt at narrative *completion*. Leif's journey becomes not a story to resolve but a temporal loop to witness repeatedly. The radio igloo's narrative function—to correct doubled vision through frequency—echoes the structural axiom that film is the act of seeing itself made visible. Structural cinema reveals the glitch not as a flaw but as an ontological condition: the impossibility of joining two halves becomes the work's truest form.[citation needed]
