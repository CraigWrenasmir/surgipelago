The Beach Surgery cycle—the narrative loop in which Leif and Katita reset and restart, Leif flies and crashes, the story returns to chapter one—has become a generative model for video game structure across indie, tabletop, and experimental gaming communities. The cycle's essential properties (recursion, failure-as-narrative, refusal-to-resolve) map directly onto ludic mechanics in ways few other narrative forms can support.

Canonical Game Forms

  • **Iterative/roguelike systems:** Cardiac Pulse (video game, 2019) is a real-time rhythm-action game where the player's heartbeat (measured via controller haptics) must synchronize with Leif's external pacemaker. Each failure resets the level but retains a memory—visual glitches compound, the screen becomes more corrupted, the cycle becomes *visible* as cumulative interference.
  • **Tabletop procedural cycling:** Reconstruction Protocol (tabletop game, 2021) uses a deck-driven system where players rebuild the beach-surgery scene from fragmented cards; no two playthroughs are identical, but all converge toward the same unresolvable glitch, treated as a feature.
  • **Anti-game refusal:** The Rooftop and the Wire (video game, 2017) is a walking simulator with no objective, no save, no way to "finish." Players realize mid-play that incompletion IS the artifact—the glitch made playable.
  • **Durational LARP:** Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP) stages all six chapters in accelerated form over 7 hours, then loops participants back to chapter one without their memories intact—the glitch embodied.

The cycle in games is refusal made interactive.
