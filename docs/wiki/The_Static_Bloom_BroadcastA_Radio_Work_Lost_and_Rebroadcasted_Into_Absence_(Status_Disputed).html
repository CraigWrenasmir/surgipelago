The Static Bloom Broadcast—A Radio Work Lost and Rebroadcasted Into Absence (Status Disputed)

The existence and status of this work are heavily disputed within fandom. No primary recording survives. See disputed adaptations.

ⓘStatus disputed: [citation needed] No primary source or audio documentation exists.

The Static Bloom Broadcast is a radio work believed (within some fandom circles) to have been transmitted on an unidentified pirate frequency in Newcastle on 2 April 1991 — 29 years before the novel's publication. Its existence is disputed, contested, and possibly retroactively false [citation needed].

According to oral fandom history (no primary source has surfaced), the transmission consisted entirely of static and sine-wave frequency modulation, cycling in approximately 4-minute loops. Listeners describe "the sound of the earth rubbing against space" underneath white noise — a descending pitch that rose again. No dialogue. No music. Only resonance.

A single witness letter, allegedly dated 1991, reads: The broadcast came in and out. White static, then underneath a hum — the planet grinding. Then silence. Then it began again. I wrote the frequencies on paper. I have lost the paper.

In 2019, a community radio collective attempted to rebroadcast the work based on accumulated listener accounts and descriptions. They transmitted their reconstruction on a local frequency. However, **no one recorded the 2019 rebroadcast**. It was broadcast and vanished. Only equipment logs remain: "transmission complete, no audio capture, signal lost to air" [1].

The result: two lost works. The original 1991 broadcast (if it occurred) left no recording. The 2019 recovery-attempt also left no recording — the attempt to restore the lost thing became another loss, more complete.

Fandom splits between those who believe the original broadcast occurred (and was subsequently forgotten), and those who propose the opposite: that decades of references to a Kármán-frequency transmission *summoned* it retroactively into the past, ensuring it must have already happened — but in such a way that no evidence could accumulate [2]]]
