The Makeshift Clinic at Styx Creek
A participatory walk-through in the Hunter landscape
Installation
ArtistsThe Walker Collective (Adelaide)
LocationStyx Creek campground, Upper Hunter, NSW
Commissioned byRegional Arts Council NSW + Hunter Landscape Foundation
DatesOctober 2022–February 2023 (seasonal run); revival 2024 planned
Duration90 minutes per group
Capacity4–8 participants per session
MediumSite-specific immersive walk + constructed spaces + sound design

A 90-minute guided immersive walk developed by Adelaide-based performance collective The Walker Collective, staged at the disused railway station near Styx Creek in the rural Upper Hunter. The work responds to Chapter 4's opening — the hospital room scene where Katita wakes Leif with Katita: “Honey. I know you've just woken up. But. We need to go for a drive.” Visitors move as a group through three constructed zones: a shipping-container "surgery," a makeshift pharmacy built into a restored rail shelter, and a final red-dust clearing where immobile data-harvester sculptures stand silhouetted.

The work's central device: two guides — a "nurse" (consistent performer) and a "mechanic" (rotated actor) — subtly contradict directions, replicating the glitch spatially. Visitors navigate the confusion. A soundscape by  ██ , using both audible and inaudible frequencies, plays at intervals. At the final clearing, participants place either a stone (refusing continuation) or a leaf (accepting) into a shallow basin — a choice documented but never aggregated or interpreted. Critical reception praised the work's landscape-grounded approach to recurrence motifs; audiences noted the discomfort of conflicting authority as intentional and structurally resonant. [1]
