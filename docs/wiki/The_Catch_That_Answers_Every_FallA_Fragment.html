The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Fragment

ⓘThis thesis is incomplete and abandoned by its author (2024). See talk page for archival notes.
The Catch That Answers Every Fall
A Fragment [Doctoral work, incomplete]

Author ████████ 
Year begun2021
Last revisionJune 2024
Pages completed~143 of ~300 planned
StatusAbandoned

An aborted doctoral thesis examining Leif's eruption and Katita's response—the moment the paper titled The Catch That Answers Every Fall across every adaptation, from the novel's frame narrative (the father's hands reaching to catch the falling infant) through durational dance-theatre, operatic stagings, and participatory installations in Nairobi and Broken Hill. The thesis argues that the catch is not redemption but *recognition*—that Katita sees, in Leif's descent, the falling she has always already caught before, the cycle's recursive gesture. The work stalls at Chapter IV: a section on the boy in the waves—the originary fall Leif cannot prevent—and the question of whether he *is* the boy, or whether catching him changes who he was. The incomplete nature mirrors its subject: a thesis on refusal that itself refuses completion. [1]
