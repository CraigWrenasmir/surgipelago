The Seventh Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking (2021–present)

This article documents an active participatory ritual. Dates and locations are current as of June 2026.

The vigil is coordinated by the Council of Stations, an independent lay organization with no official affiliation to C. W. Smith's estates.

The Seventh Stations of Return is a seven-day pilgrimage vigil that reconstructs sacred moments from *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* as lived, embodied ritual across geographically dispersed sites.

The vigil was first performed in July 2021 by a group of  ██  practitioners in Newcastle. Participants visit seven locations over consecutive days: (1) Bolton Street car park, site of the rooftop crossing in Chapter 1; (2) the site of the underground stone pool, marked by a mural in Chapter 3; (3) a mapped service station in the Hunter Valley interior Chapter 4; (4) the approximate location of the radio/radar igloo beneath King Edward Park Chapter 4; (5) a secluded cabin site in the inland desert; (6) the beach at the mouth, where the wings erupt Chapter 6; and (7) an unmarked, rotating seventh site, revealed only to registered participants and kept secret from documentation.

At each station, participants perform *The Catch*—a choreographed movement of mutual embrace and simultaneous release, lasting approximately seven minutes—followed by a spoken liturgy drawn from the novel. Participants leave offerings: red cord, leather scraps, water, stones, photographs. The seven-day structure mirrors the cycle, with each day corresponding to a moment of refusal or breaking in the narrative.

The Council of Stations (founded 2020) now coordinates adaptations globally. The Lahore Frequency Walk (beginning 2023) visits seven market, river, and radio sites across Lahore, framing the vigil through Pakistani Sufi pilgrimage traditions. The Addis Ababa adaptation, facilitated by the Biennale in partnership with the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, reframes the stations as an icon-panel cycle. The São Paulo version integrates Brazilian carnival traditions, with the Dirtheart-like activist groups participating as ceremonial guides.

Theological debates concern the vigil's purpose: whether it enacts eternal recurrence (and thus risks entrapping participants in loops) or performs refusal of the cycle itself. Some movements have begun counter-vigils and "eighth stations" to disrupt or reinterpret the practice.
