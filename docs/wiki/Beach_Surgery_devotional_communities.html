Overview

Beach Surgery devotional communities are organized theological collectives that emerged in the post-2020 period, interpreting C. W. Smith's novel and its adaptations as sacred texts and sites of spiritual practice. These groups are globally distributed and heterogeneous: they range from informal reading circles and participatory ritual groups to formalized congregations with liturgical calendars and ordained practitioners. What unites them is a shared reading of the glitch as a theologically necessary structure—an absence that demands perpetual reinterpretation rather than resolution.

Theological currents

The major currents are **the Church of the Eternal Return** (emphasizing Leif's cyclical submission and Katita's doctrine of refusal); **the Communion of the Breaking Cycle** (reading the narrative as a pedagogical exercise in sacred refusal); and the **Apophatic Circle** (treating the glitch as divine silence and the novel as negative theology). Significant theological disputes exist over whether Katita administers the temptations or liberates from them, and over the meaning of radical interiority in relational healing.

Ritual practice

Communities maintain **durational vigils** (often 7, 12, or 49 hours, mirroring narrative structures); **frequency walks** retracing Newcastle and desert sites; **collective textile work** integrating West African kente and Middle Eastern traditions; and **breath-count protocols** synchronized to the novel's chapter rhythms. Many conduct guided meditations and **Reverse Readings**—reading the narrative backward as an apophatic liturgy.

Schisms and controversy

Significant theological ruptures have arisen over the **consent question**: whether Leif's devotion is coercive or freely willed, with profound ethical consequences for relational theology. The **Kármán hypothesis** (that reversing Earth's spin would resolve the glitch) has spawned esoteric sects. Mainline communities (particularly Ethiopian Orthodox and West African Yorùbá òpéra-based groups) emphasize care ethics; radical apophatic communities embrace incompleteness as an end, not a means. Regional liturgical variations are canonical; schism is expected.
