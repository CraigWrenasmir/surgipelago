Surgical Intimacy

This article examines surgical intimacy as a conceptual framework. For creative works centred on this theme, see The Surgery as Caress: Staging Touch and Transgression in Beach Surgery's Visual Culture.

ⓘThis concept has generated considerable theological interpretation; see The Doctrine of Refusal for opposing readings [citation needed].
Surgical Intimacy
Core proposition
DefinitionThe union of surgical precision, care, vulnerability, and erotic encounter
Canonical sourceA Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel, 2020)
Philosophical grounding
Related conceptsSurgery as metaphor, Care ethics and Beach Surgery, The wings as Icarus motif

Surgical intimacy denotes the fusion of medical precision and erotic vulnerability that undergirds A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight. The novel establishes this through its central proposition: **"Love is always surgery."** Surgery is not metaphorical wounding but literal care—the cutting-open required to heal, to birth, to touch the other's interiority.

The concept crystallises in Katita and Leif's relationship: Katita is nurse and swordsman; her "makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales" is simultaneously a clinical space and an erotic one. She removes Leif's pacemaker with the same precision she uses in flight—care and violence indistinguishable. At the novel's climax, surgery is revealed as birth: the narrator's wife in a delivery room with red hibiscus light, her body the wound that opens to new life. The "complicated surgery on the beach" is the daughter's arrival.

Global adaptations have excavated this fusion across media: Kathakali theatre treats it as a rasa (emotional essence); Ta'zieh passion-plays read it as martyrdom and renewal; griotic retellings ground it in communal healing rituals. The concept resists separation: one cannot surgically isolate the caress from the blade, vulnerability from precision. This irreducibility is itself the glitch—the seam that cannot be joined without destroying both halves.
