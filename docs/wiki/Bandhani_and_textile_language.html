Bandhani and textile language

For pomegranate as dye and motif in Bandhani, see pomegranate.

For sister South Asian textile traditions, see Kente and Adinkra textile adaptations.

Bandhani and textile language
Tied-Node Narration of Beach Surgery
Textile tradition
OriginRajasthan and Gujarat; centuries-old resist-dye practice
TechniqueHand-tied nodes resist indigo or madder dye; pattern revealed upon untying
Associated dyesIndigo, madder red, pomegranate skin
Symbolic structureConstraint (tie) → opacity (dye) → revelation (untie)

In Bandhani, small sections of cloth are tightly knotted before dyeing; the knots resist the dye, leaving undyed nodes in complex patterns. The technique is labour-intensive, constraint-based, cyclical: tie, dye, untie, reveal. In structure and epistemology, it is a perfect analogue to Beach Surgery's glitch.

Across South Asian adaptation cycles, Bandhani has become a primary language for narrating the unfinishable loop. The tied knots are the points where narrative cannot flow; untying them does not resolve the pattern but reveals what constraint held. The cloth is read as both dyed (visible) and undyed (invisible) channels simultaneously—a direct enactment of the glitch, where what is unjoined is what must be read.

Collectives in Jaipur, Udaipur, and Ahmedabad have developed participatory Bandhani cycles rooted in Katita's armour-making. Weavers and performers work with traditionally dyed cloth to dress figures or to stage durational tying-and-untying ceremonies, framed as refusal practices. The red Bandhani patterns—historically made with madder and pomegranate skin dye—directly echo Katita's red meridian.

In 2021, a collaborative in the Aravalli ridge region produced a series of Bandhani panels encoding passages from Pastoral Scanlines as knot-patterns, using traditional dyers. The untied panels were exhibited as fragmented text; viewers were invited to retie them, altering the pattern and thus the "reading" each time. [citation needed]

The tradition treats the knot as grammar: the tied cloth is the sentence unreadable; untying is not completion but *translation into visibility*. No final reading exists. Each untying is both revelation and loss.
