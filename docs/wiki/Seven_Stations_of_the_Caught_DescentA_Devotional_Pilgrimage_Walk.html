Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk

Seven Stations of the Caught Descent is a multi-site durational pilgrimage initiated in 2022 by the Incompletion Collective and Broken Hill community guides. It traces a landscape-based interpretation of the military engineer's arc through chapters four and six, staging his three injuries across seven geographic sites in the surrounding red desert interior.

Participants walk for 7–9 hours, stopping at each station for silent meditation, choral humming, or enacted movement. The structure deliberately refuses narrative closure: at each site, participants encounter redacted documents dated  1988–1994 , fragmented voice-recordings naming Katita, and landscape sculptures (tree-antennas, stone cairns, a rusted rocket-cart) that do not cohere into singular meaning.[1]

The theology is rooted in The Doctrine of Refusal: it enacts not the breaking of the cycle but the sacred incompleteness of the attempt. Participants leave no traces and carry no photographs; the walk survives only in embodied memory and the Collective's annual oral testimony. The pilgrimage has attracted religious communities reading Beach Surgery as scripture of unfinishedness, and scholars of apophatic theology and phenomenology seeking to understand refusal as spiritual practice.
