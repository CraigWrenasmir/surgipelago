The glitch as Hermeneutic Necessity

This paper examines structural incompleteness in *Beach Surgery* across media. For other theoretical readings, see The glitch as Koanic Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Unfinishable as Sacred in Beach Surgery Readings.

The glitch as Hermeneutic Necessity
Incompleteness as Interpretive Openness in Smith's *Beach Surgery*
Published in
*Interpretation and Unfinishedness Quarterly*Vol. 8 No. 2, pp. 47–63 (2024)
Author
 ████ Affiliation redacted
Method
Comparative media analysisOntological incompleteness theory

Smith's embedding of **the glitch** at the seam between the novel's two halves—the structural fault that "does not compute"—has conventionally been read as narrative failure. This paper argues the opposite: that the glitch functions as an hermeneutic necessity, a generative lacuna that forces every adaptation to invent its own completion and thereby produce meaning rather than resolve aporia.

Drawing on ontological incompleteness theory and analogical consciousness, the author traces how the glitch operates like a **Möbius strip** (Smith's recurring figure for subjectivity): it appears as rupture only from within linear reading; approached recursively, it reveals itself as a single surface traversing both sides. Each adaptation—manga, opera, installation, LARP—becomes a hermeneutic act that reads the glitch backward, refusing to patch it.

The paper concludes that **the glitch is what allows the franchise to survive**: its refusal to be finished is not a defect but a structural principle. Incompleteness is the condition for infinite interpretation. When Katita says "we can do it we can do it we can do it," the stutter is not a speech impediment—it is the rhythm of a thought encountering its own limit and refusing to stop.
