The Listening Tower—A Radio Transmission Sculpture in Kinshasa

This article covers an operational art installation. For related radio art, see Community radio and adaptation. For African public monuments, see African adaptations of Beach Surgery.

The Listening Tower is a spiral radio-transmission sculpture in Kinshasa's Parc de la Vallée, constructed 2022–2024 by artist  Kasongo Mulonda  in collaboration with Okapi Radio Collective—a network of pirate and community broadcasters operating across the DRC. The structure stands approximately 14 metres tall, built from reclaimed metal scaffolding with embedded copper tuning coils.

On designated evenings the tower broadcasts on rotating AM/FM frequencies, mixing recordings of unidentified voices, sections of silence, and low-frequency atmospheric transmissions approximating the Kármán line's resonance. Visitors stand within the tower's base and listen through hand-held radios tuned to each frequency in sequence. The experience is described as durational and disorienting—some report hearing voices in unrecognised languages; others experience the silence as spatial, as though the tower itself breathes.

The work interprets the radio igloo and the glitch as a problem not of narrative jointure but of signal corruption—an interference that cannot be resolved, only witnessed and borne. Mulonda states the tower is "a monument to every broadcast never completed, every frequency lost to static" [citation needed]. The work remains controversial locally; verification of broadcast content is difficult.
