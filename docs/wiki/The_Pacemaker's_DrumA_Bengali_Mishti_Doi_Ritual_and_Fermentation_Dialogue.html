The Pacemaker's Drum—A Bengali Mishti Doi Ritual and Fermentation Dialogue

This article concerns a participatory food work and ritual practice. For Leif's cardiac device, see Leif.

The Pacemaker's Drum is an ongoing participatory fermentation workshop and ritual meal cycle in Kolkata, West Bengal, conducted by the  ██  Collective since 2022. Participants gather monthly to prepare mishti doi (sweetened yogurt curd) using extended slow-culture fermentation methods inherited from Kolkata's historic dai (wet nurse) and sweet-maker traditions.

The work reads Leif's external pacemaker—external governance of heartbeat—as analogous to fermentation itself: a living microbiome, inherited and passed on, that maintains its own rhythm within the body. Each participant creates a single jar of doi, assigned one of three fermentation speeds echoing the three injuries: slow-set (22 hours; "the mystery of blindness"); quick-set (12 hours; "the miracle of flight"); medium-set (18 hours; "the authority of the sovereign pulse"). Jars are sealed, labeled with the maker's name and a single heart-word, then incubated in communal terracotta pots at the shrine-kitchen on  ██  Road.

After fermentation, the doi is tasted collectively and ritually gifted—never sold or kept. [citation needed] The sweetness is read as surgical caress: "a bacteria colony that learned to be tender." Participants report that tasting another's doi feels like hearing their heartbeat translated into milk. The collective has documented over  ██  fermentation narratives, many framed as devotional anticipations of motherhood and birth. [1]
