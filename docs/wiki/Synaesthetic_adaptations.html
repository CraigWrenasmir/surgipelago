Synaesthetic adaptations

This article describes synaesthetic and multi-sensory methodologies. For individual works, see specific adaptation articles below.

Synaesthetic adaptation—the deliberate crossing and entanglement of sensory modalities—has emerged as a core methodological response to the novel's theological glitch and its resistance to singular representation. Synesthesia, the involuntary cross-wiring of perception (sound triggering smell, texture summoning colour), becomes formal language for approaching incompleteness: no single sense fully renders Leif and Katita's world; only recursive failure across multiple sensory channels approaches the truth.

Theoretical Ground

The novel's sound-metaphor—the Kármán line's low nauseating drone contrasted with the spine's high counter-frequency—establishes synaesthesia as cosmologically structural. Katita's secret theory posits that if the cycle reversed, these frequencies would align. Adaptation must make them *audible as sensation*: the listener's body becomes the meeting-point. Synaesthetic works literalize this integration—one cannot perceive scent without simultaneously processing sound; one cannot taste the ritual bread without reciting narrative aloud.

Olfactory-Audio and Radio-Scent

Dakar's Salt Frequency Broadcast transmits pirate radio readings of Beach Surgery passages at frequencies calculated to trigger vestibular and olfactory activation simultaneously. As frequency rises (mimicking the spine's ascending pitch), salt-infused air enters the space—listeners report synesthetic collapse: salt "sounds," sound "tastes." Tbilisi's Kármán Whisper extends this into seven-hour durational LARP: participants traverse herbal stations, each location broadcasting a narrative layer at a specific frequency, each botanical scent a chapter of the glitch.

Gustatory Cycles and Ritual Feasts

Food-based works—Punjab's collective bread-making ritual and Kashmir's 23-hour feast cycle—treat surgery as taste and tasting. The Wazwan cycle mirrors the novel's six chapters across seven courses; each encodes sensory memory (turmeric for fever, cardamom for refusal, saffron for transcendence). Participants consume the novel as embodied, inseparable knowledge.

Proprioceptive and Durational Works

Dance and movement-based synaesthesia treat the body's proprioception—sense of spatial position and muscular tension—as narrative channel. Choreographic pieces stage Leif's three paralyses as sustained movement states: what does paralysis *feel like*? The body learns that immobility and flight are continuous. The Unspoken Stations, moving participants through Newcastle and red desert in darkness, privileges touch (following rope, holding hands, navigating by footfall), where the novel resolves only in the body's exhausted knowledge.

Cross-Sensory Misalignment and the Glitch

Synaesthetic works often *deliberately misalign* sensory inputs—audio lags behind scent, taste contradicts touch—to formally enact the unjoined seam. If analogy is cognition's core, then sensory collision becomes the glitch's own enactment: meaning fractures across its translation.
