Aboriginal Australian & Māori works

For broader context, see Pacific / Indigenous / Eastern Europe. For Australian adaptations generally, see Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

Aboriginal Australian & Māori works
Scope
CoverageAdaptations rooted in Aboriginal Australian and Aotearoa/Māori traditions
MediumsSonglines, dot-painting, haka, carving, weaving, oral performance, theatre
Geographic focusAustralia (mainland and Torres Strait), Aotearoa/New Zealand

Beach Surgery has found adaptation within Aboriginal Australian and Māori cultural traditions, each rooted in indigenous aesthetic and cosmological frameworks.

Aboriginal Australian

  • Songline Cycle of Leif's Dreaming (oral / performance, pre-2020, status disputed) — A fragmentary songline narration mapping Leif's journey across the red desert interior as a Dreaming ancestor. Said to predate the novel; verification impossible. [citation needed]
  • Dot Painting Series: The Two That Spin (visual art, 2021–present) — A collaborative dot-painting cycle depicting the coin motif through concentric circles and parallel spirals. Exhibited at  ██ .
  • The Hoop Dance Theatre of the Caught Descent (theatre-dance, 2022–2024, touring) — A hoop-dance and contemporary performance piece exploring the wings and the catch that answers every fall through urban and traditional movement vocabularies.

Aotearoa / Māori

  • Haka Ngā Whaea Rua [The Two Mothers] (haka / waiata, 2019–present) — A waiata-haka cycle repositioning Leif and Katita as ancestral figures. Uses karakia and contemporary sound design. Performed by  ██  collective.
  • Weaving the Return: Kete Korero Cycle (textile art / oral performance, 2023–ongoing) — A participatory weaving and storytelling project linking the cycle to kaupapa Māori theories of temporal return.
  • Taiaha and the Red Cloth (carved wood and performance, 2020) — A wooden taiaha carved with the novel's iconography, paired with haka, whakairo commentary, and electronic sound.
