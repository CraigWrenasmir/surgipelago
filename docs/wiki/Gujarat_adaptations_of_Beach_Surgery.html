Gujarat adaptations of Beach Surgery

Gujarat is a state in western India with strong textile, musical, and theatrical traditions. This survey covers Beach Surgery adaptations rooted in Gujarati art forms and sites.

Gujarat adaptations of Beach Surgery
Regional overview
StateGujarat, western India
Primary art formsBandhani and Ajrakh textiles; Kathak; Garba; folk theatre
Key citiesAhmedabad, Vadodara, Jaipur (cross-regional)
Peak development2021–present

Beach Surgery has found particular resonance in Gujarat's textile and dance traditions, where the motifs of the cycle, the wound, and eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle map onto textile **repetition and the aesthetics of indigo-resist dyeing**.

**Textile Interpretations:** Collective works in Ahmedabad and Vadodara have created palimpsestic interpretations using bandhani (tie-dye) and Ajrakh resist traditions. Successive layers of binding and discharge represent successive loops of recurrence; the cloth becomes a literal archive of the two protagonists' overlaid journeys. The warp and weft cannot be separated without unravelling the entire piece. Works like *Sita Ke Sutra* (2023) present this as a surgical act—each undoing is a reopening of the wound.

**Kathak Cycles:** Kathak dancers have staged Leif's three temporary injuries as three escalating taans (rapid passages), each exploring a different rasa (emotional mood), from shanta (peace) through raudra (fury) to karuna (compassion).

**Archival Fragment:** A photograph catalogued as "Property_Deed_Fragment_And_Sketches_On_Blank_Reverse.jpg" in the  Vadodara State Archive  shows an indigo textile with notations suggesting a lost 2019 design study, possibly pre-adaptation exploration. Its connection to Beach Surgery remains unverified [citation needed].

**Participatory Garba:** Community-based works adapt the cycle as participatory Garba sequences—the circular dance becoming a literal enactment of eternal recurrence and refusal.
