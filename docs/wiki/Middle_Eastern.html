Middle Eastern

This article surveys adaptations originating in the Middle Eastern and West Asian regions. For North African traditions, see North African adaptations. For Islamic theological readings, see Islamic art and Beach Surgery.

Middle Eastern Adaptations
Geographic scope
RegionsLevant, Mesopotamia, Iran, Anatolia, Caucasus, Arabian Peninsula
Primary art formsTa'zieh, Karagöz shadow-puppet, oud/maqam, calligraphy, cinema, radio
Interpretive axis
Core theological motifapophatic theology; the wound as sacred knowledge

Adaptations from the Middle East and West Asia read Beach Surgery as **theological absence** rather than narrative failure. The glitch becomes sacred — a void where divine knowledge dwells. Ta'zieh passion-play traditions in Iran frame Leif's three injuries as martyrological temptation; Karagöz shadow-puppet cycles layer identity through the mechanic's doubled face; oud and maqam song-suites extend the Kármán line as cosmological threshold.

Persian miniature cycles stage the surgery on vellum itself; Arabic calligraphic manuscripts render Katita's red meridian through script and illumination. Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan, a disputed calligraphic reconstruction, fragments the narrative into 47 illuminated leaves. Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses, a Marrakech olfactory installation, broadcasts the sound of the earth rubbing against space as divine invocation.

Egyptian radio serials carry the cycle through community frequency; Tawqīt al-Ḥasra, a Cairo radio-art work, renders the pacemaker as authority — the heart governed by external sovereign, read through Dostoevsky and Islamic theology as the doctrine of refusal: the soul cannot know God's rhythm, only refuse the illusion of knowing.
