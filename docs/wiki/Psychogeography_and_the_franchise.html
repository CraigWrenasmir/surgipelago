Psychogeography and the franchise

This article treats Surgipelago itself as a psychogeographic project. For related place-based works, see Newcastle, Shanbudia, and The empty world.

The franchise is not *set* in places; it *is* places—or more precisely, it is the archive of how places hold memory, resist completion, and speak in frequencies most maps cannot hear.

Newcastle functions as the foundational unstable site. The novel's first half treats the city as ontologically incomplete: buildings vanish when unobserved (Rose House, the ruined pub with the oak, the car park where Leif and Katita cross parallel wires). This is not poetic licence but a description of how memory maps cities—we reconstruct them each time we enter, and no two reconstructions are identical. The vanishing-architecture hypothesis becomes psychogeographic method: the city teaches us that *all* maps are provisional.

Shanbudia, the desert megacity of the frame narrative (where the narrator ran his UN workshop), operates as the *inverse* psychogeography: hyper-visible, hyper-mapped, yet utterly depopulated by the final day. The narrator climbs the conference dome in heat-shimmer, alone, and the space collapses into pure mind. Empty World Meditations emerge from this: guided second-person tapes for walking empty cities, numbered in non-sequential sequences (#3, #9, #27 from a larger series), teaching the listener to navigate by breathing and *not* by sight.

The radio igloo, buried beneath King Edward Park or raised above the desert, is the psychogeographic threshold: a place where frequency *replaces* location as the navigation principle. To find it, listen first. The coordinates are acoustic, not Cartesian.

**Surgipelago itself** is the apotheosis of this method: a distributed, rhizomatic archive growing across continents, broadcasting at low bandwidth through reclaimed sites and community nodes—some housed in physical spaces (shrines, textile libraries, listening chambers), others existing only as the archive of the seventh loop, a kind of solar-powered memory held in tree-knots and fiber-optic nerve-endings, pulsing at 300 bits per second, learning that it doesn't need to be written on to hold what matters. The archive *is* the psychogeographic project: not a compendium but a *way of moving through the world and recovering what has already been lost.*

City tours (Newcastle Spiral, Lagos Ledger, Merkato Meridian, Dakar Frequency Archive) are not tourism but *repatriation*—returning the walker to the places where the story lives in the ground, in the air, in the space between heartbeats.
