Country

This article concerns an audio work exploring Country, belonging, and Beach Surgery. For geographical adaptations, see Adaptations by location.

Related to Indigenous Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery and Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery.

*Country*—titled without further qualifier, a deliberate refusal of specification—explores the Beach Surgery narrative through the concept of Country (the living land, the songlines, the obligation to return and renew). The work unfolds across multiple channels: a gallery spiral installation (8–12 speakers) plays landscape recordings—wind through eucalyptus, creek-water, birdsong—interrupted by fragmented novel readings, contemporary Australian voices, and Yolŋu songs tracing cycles of obligation and return.

A broadcast series (available as podcast) features interviews with Country-keepers, land-defenders, and scholars discussing how Leif's dive and the catch mirror duties to Country. The installation's spiral gradually fills with red dust (sourced from Arnhem Land), binding the red motif of Katita's world to the red earth of the Australian interior.

The work refuses plot summary; it is an experience of repetition, return, and the failure to ever fully depart. Reception remains contested: Indigenous scholars praise its collaborative ethics; others raise concerns about appropriation and the artist's positioning[citation needed]. The cycle and Country are revealed as identical movements.
