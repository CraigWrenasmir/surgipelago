shanta

For the general rasa framework, see rasa (concept).

shanta (Śānta)
Definition
Sanskritशान्त (śānta)
TranslationPeace, tranquility, quiescence, withdrawal
Associated stateApathy, non-violence, renunciation
In Beach Surgery
Primary readingKatita's silence and refusal as sacred act
Theological frameApophatic theology; peace as negation of the cycle
Performance modalityStillness, breath, durational presence

Śānta, the ninth and supreme rasa in Indian classical aesthetics, denotes quiescence, peace, and absolute withdrawal of the self. In classical dance and vocal traditions, śānta is reserved for apophatic contexts—the silence that speaks precisely by refusing speech.

Beach Surgery adaptations rooted in South Asian and Buddhist traditions read Katita's refusal to smile until narrative collapse as an extended śānta enactment. Her red armor, sword, and medical kit become instruments not of agency but sacred refusal—a withdrawal from the cycle's demand to act.

The Three Breaths (Kolkata, 2021–ongoing) stages Katita's interior as śānta meditation, inverting the rasa's passivity: her refusal becomes violent precision. Sangthi Vak (2019) layers Katita's silence over five-voice Hindustani improvisations, each chord resolving into unison—śānta's deep peace. Whether śānta describes Katita's active resistance or eventual exhaustion remains contested [citation needed].
