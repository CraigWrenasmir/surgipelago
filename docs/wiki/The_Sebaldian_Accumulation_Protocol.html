The Sebaldian Accumulation Protocol

This article concerns a scholarly methodology applied to Beach Surgery. For W. G. Sebald's biography, see external reference.

ⓘ[citation needed] – the protocol's formalization postdates Surgipelago's emergence; attribution to Sebald's influence is scholarly consensus.

Core method
PracticeGathering photographs, documents, fragments into non-linear sequences

The Sebaldian Accumulation Protocol is a scholarly methodology drawn from close reading of W. G. Sebald's prose practice — particularly his use of photograph, digression, historical fragment, and melancholic accumulation — and applied across theses and critical work on Beach Surgery adaptation. The protocol treats fragments not as evidence to be synthesized into narrative, but as presences that refuse completion: each photograph, each witness account, each abandoned detail carries its own weight and irretrievable loss.

In C. W. Smith's oeuvre, the Sebaldian impulse surfaces in Antinomicity, where a train recording stretches "into 'a bag full of alarm clocks dragged to the bottom of an empty public pool'" — simultaneity without resolution. The drone's archive in Chapter 5 exemplifies this: the photographs show a man Leif does not recognize as himself, yet the image is him — a refusal of photographic truth-value Sebald would recognize [citation needed].

For adaptation scholars, the protocol sanctions a particular archival ethics: do not complete what refuses completion. Surgipelago itself enacts this principle — the wiki accumulates contradictory versions, lost media, disputed adaptations, and redacted details into an archive that grows but never resolves. The glitch is never sutured. To apply the Sebaldian protocol is to treat incompleteness, witness, and melancholic accumulation as the only honest response to the unfinishable.
