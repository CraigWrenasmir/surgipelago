Video essays and critical works

This article indexes major video essays, critical video works, and video dissertations analyzing Beach Surgery and C. W. Smith's oeuvre. For academic theses, see Theses and papers. For documentaries, see Documentaries.

Video essays and critical video works form a significant strand of Beach Surgery exegesis, particularly in the post-2020 expansion following the novel's publication. Unlike traditional academic papers, the video essay medium permits an analogical mode of analysis—juxtaposition of images, sound, and text mirrors the novel's claim that "analogy is the core of all cognition."

Major sustained works: The most cited remains The Lexicon of the Caught Descent, a six-part essay series (2021–2022) by  ███████ , reconstructing devotional pilgrimage sites and their relation to the glitch. Untranslatable Surgery: A Film Essay (2021) by Asha Mohan traces language-specific adaptation contradictions. The three-hour The Glitch Made Visible (2023) performs frame-by-frame exegesis of three film adaptations running in parallel; sections have been cited in doctoral dissertations.

Emerging formats: Shorter video essays proliferate on community streaming platforms and festival circuits. Breaking Backward examines the theology of incompletion. Every Face, Every Room is a durational video-collage spanning 7+ hours.

Disputed and lost: Several early video essays are unverified or exist only as transcribed audio. The Film That Arrives Before Itself is known through  ════  descriptions alone. Status and archive location remain disputed. [citation needed]
