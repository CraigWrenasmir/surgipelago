Red kitten heels—A Hunter Valley Elegy

This article documents a site-specific installation work. For Katita's heels as character motif, see Katita (character). For red as overarching motif, see red (motif).

Overview

Red kitten heels—A Hunter Valley Elegy is a durational walking and textile installation that traces Katita's signature red kitten heels through the Hunter Valley's vineyard landscape, wine-scar geography, and colonial history. The work is framed as a seven-week liturgy of refusal: walking the valley as a pilgrimage through red (dust, wine, rust, blood, first-aid cross) while meditating on Katita's refusal to smile and her grief-stricken commitment to breaking an unbreakable cycle.

Concept

The Hunter Valley sits within the geographic frame of C. W. Smith's lived Newcastle—a day's drive inland. The valley is marked by red earth, extractive history (mining, viticulture), and the layered archaeology of settler presence. The installation positions the heels—Katita's only ornament, her only claim to a self that is not pure surgical utility—as a trace walking the landscape. Each year's walk follows a different route; no route repeats perfectly.

Participants receive a small red thread to tie onto trees at stations, creating an expanding textile palimpsest. By 2024, the valley holds an estimated  30,000–40,000  individual threads, the cumulative red of thousands of witnessed refusals.

Structure

The seven weeks correspond to the seven stations of return in Beach Surgery exegesis:

  • **Week One: The Diode.** Walking through Pokolbin, participants carry small red LED diodes (referencing Leif's blinking pacemaker diode). Each evening, diodes are collected and displayed in a wine cellar, creating a field of red light.
  • **Weeks Two–Four: The Dust.** Walking routes follow abandoned mining roads and vineyard margins, where red dust from inland penetrates the valley. Participants photograph their own feet; images are projected nightly on a gallery wall.
  • **Weeks Five–Six: The Armour.** A traveling textile station, staffed by  leather workers and seamstresses , allows participants to stitch fragments of red leather into Katita's armour-form. Each piece remains in the valley; the completed armour is never removed, only added to.
  • **Week Seven: The Smile.** A final gathering (date varies) at the Broke lookout. Participants share testimony. The archived audio contains almost no laughter—primarily silence, breathing, and the sound of wind through the valley. “I walked seven weeks and did not smile once. That was the point.”

Aesthetic and material

The work draws on Australian landscape painting, Andean weaving, and the Sebaldian tradition of melancholic archive. The red thread references both Ariadne's thread (the guide through the labyrinth) and the unbreakable stitch in traditional textiles. The work is photogenic but deliberately anti-monumental: no permanent installation, only the yearly accumulation of thread, dust, and testimony.

Audio recordings capture the sound of feet on gravel, breath, and the valley's empty world soundscape. Participants report that the walk itself becomes the story—the story is not told but inhabited.

Disputed claims

Several versions of the work's origin exist.  The artist collective's formation date is variously claimed as 2018, 2019, and "always"; institutional records are minimal.  One account traces the work to a single walker in 2016—a woman who appeared at Pokolbin and walked for seven weeks, tying red thread, whose name was never recorded. By 2019, others had joined; by 2024, the walk had accumulated its own mythology.

A stray transmission intercepted by radio receivers near the valley  (date unverified ), misfiled as: Stormwater_System_Survey_1994_And_The_Boy_Who_Walked_It.mp3, contains audio of footsteps on gravel and a woman's voice reciting numbers that do not correspond to any known section of the walk. [1]

Legacy and continuity

The work has inspired parallel installations in Accra (The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography), Ayacucho (La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho), and Tbilisi (The Kármán Whisper—A Synaesthetic Olfactory LARP). Each localises the walk to regional landscape and local textile traditions, yet all maintain the seven-week structure and the refusal-to-smile as the core covenant.
