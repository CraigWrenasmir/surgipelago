rasa

This article concerns the Indian aesthetic concept of rasa. For Beach Surgery's emotional minimalism, see Tone and register of Beach Surgery.

rasa
The emotional essence or aesthetic flavour in Indian art
Definition
Sanskritरस (rasa) — 'taste', 'flavour', 'essence'
DomainClassical Sanskrit drama, Bharatanatyam, Kathakali, music, poetry
PrincipleArt generates direct experience of emotion in the spectator, not imitation
The nine rasas
ŚringāraLove, affection
KarunāCompassion, pathos
BhayānakaFear, terror
AdbhutaWonder, amazement

Rasa — the Sanskrit principle of emotional essence or "flavour" — is foundational to South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery, particularly in Kathakali, Bharatanatyam, Baul retellings, and Parsi theatre versions. Rasa privileges cultivation of a specific emotional texture in the spectator's body — a direct taste of love, fear, or wonder, unmediated by plot.

Katita's emotional economy — she "does not smile for the entire story until one of the very final scenes" — is rasa-driven. She is not expressing emotion; she is withholding and concentrating it, releasing everything at the end. This aesthetic of delayed, concentrated affect maps precisely onto rasa theory.

Several South Asian adaptations interpret the novel through specific rasas: Bhayānaka (fear, terror) as the primary register; Karunā (compassion, pathos) in the triage scene and surgery as love; Adbhuta (wonder) at the wings, the drone's photographs, the seagull's mechanics; Śānta (peace) in the empty world meditations, always fragile.

Classical theory lists nine rasas; some add a tenth, viRasa (the rasaless state, or neutrality) — which some scholars argue is Beach Surgery's true register: a flatness so complete that dread surfaces only as a whisper inside the prose itself.
