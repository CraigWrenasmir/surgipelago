durational cinema

This article discusses durational and long-take cinema in Beach Surgery adaptations. For temporal mechanics generally, see Temporal loops in Beach Surgery. For Durational performance and participatory contexts, see Durational refusal.

ⓘThis category is under development; new durational works continue to emerge across festival circuits and regional archives.
Durational cinema
Long-take and temporal extension in Beach Surgery film
Defining characteristics
Aesthetic strategyUnbroken or minimally-cut takes; real-time or extended narrative compression; refusal of standard montage
Key precedentChantal Akerman's autofiction; Sebaldian accumulation; long-take cinema and narrative refusal
Core tensionEternal recurrence / breaking the cycle staged through temporal stasis versus loop-reset

Durational cinema in Beach Surgery adaptation employs extended, unbroken takes to stage the glitch as temporal impossibility. Rather than montage-driven plot acceleration, these films (often 60–180 minutes) occupy a single interior, journey, or moment—mirroring the novel's six chapters and two halves' structural repetition. The strategy emerged circa 2015 in festival-circuit work, notably Ten Layered Versions (Polish, 1997; status disputed) and the Argentine immersive tradition.

The form enacts a paradox central to the narrative: if Leif and Katita cannot escape their loop, can cinema represent departure by refusing to cut? Extended takes become a refusal-within-refusal—the camera holds as the story circles. Temporal loop adaptations employ durational strategy to literalize the radio igloo effect: the frequency that straightens vision also extends time until it locks.

Key works include The Catch That Answers Every Fall (Choreographic Work, 2022) (9-hour durational film-dance hybrid), Durational refusal installations (7-year participatory cycles), and The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet in real-time). The critical literature treats durational form as apophatic refusal: the refusal to resolve, which becomes the only accurate representation of the cycle's sacred incompleteness [1][1].
