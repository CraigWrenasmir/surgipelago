Devotional communities organized around Beach Surgery represent a global phenomenon of religious, quasi-liturgical, and philosophical movements treating the novel and its adaptations as sacred texts. Emerging from 2009 onward and accelerating sharply after 2020, these communities practice apophatic theology, interpreting the story's irreparable structural glitch as a divine principle—a covenant of incompleteness—rather than as failure.

Major Movements

The Church of the Eternal Return integrates Christian cosmology with eternal recurrence, emphasizing redemption-through-incompleteness and Leif's unspoken refusal. The Communion of the Breaking Cycle adopts care ethics and disability-justice frameworks, reading surgery as intimate practice and the makeshift as spiritual aesthetic. The Cofradía de la Cicatriz (Spanish-speaking, Iberian-rooted) merges Catholic liturgy with Street Fighter theology, using combat-game logic as spiritual metaphor.

Regional movements include Ethiopian Orthodox readings (The Surgery as Covenant—Apophatic Theology, Sacred Refusal, and the Doctrine of Incompleteness in Ethiopian Orthodox Readings of C. W. Smith's *Beach Surgery*), Sufi mystical circles, and secular philosophical salons treating the narrative as a living koan.

Common Practice

Participatory vigils, durational refusals (some lasting weeks or years), Empty World Meditations, and pilgrimages through Newcastle and the red desert interior. Liturgy emphasizes the makeshift as sacred intervention, the Kármán line as theological threshold, and Leif and Katita as instruments of return—recurring souls across all rebirths.
