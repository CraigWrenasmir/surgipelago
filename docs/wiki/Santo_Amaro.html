Overview

Santo Amaro is an evolving, decentralized participatory cycle adapting Beach Surgery through Afro-Brazilian oral tradition, cordel music (pamphlet ballads), devotional song, and street radio. Named for the neighborhood in southwest São Paulo, the cycle frames Katita as a warrior-healer at the threshold between worlds—coded through orixá cosmology and ancestral feminine power—and Leif's fall as an initiation descent rather than catastrophe.

Cosmology & adaptation

  • The leather armor becomes indigo-resist cloth (indigo adire or tie-dye bandhani), sewn by community members in durational workshops.
  • The beach becomes the Recôncavo (the bay region of Bahia, colonial wound-site and Candomblé epicentre).
  • The cycle and recurrence mirrors cyclical cosmology of Yoruban and Bantu traditions, transplanted and transformed through slavery and survival.
  • The glitch is reread as the seam between African diaspora memory and Brazilian land: unhealing, generative, sacred.

Participation & radio

Community members contribute verses to an ongoing cordel; performances occur in buses, market squares, street corners. Multiple community radio stations broadcast fragments—Radio Kassan,  Frequência Recôncavo ,  ██████████ —creating a distributed, sometimes contradictory archive. Listeners submit their own stanzas; the cycle grows without central authorship.

Documentation

Partially documented through oral-history interviews and recovered cassette recordings. Some ephemera discovered misfiled in university archives under unrelated survey headings. [1]
