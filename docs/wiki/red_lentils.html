red lentils

This article describes a participatory food-adaptation and healing practice. For the red motif in Beach Surgery, see red. For related ritual meals, see langar as sacrament.

Red Lentils
A Cycle of Preparation, Healing, and Return
Form
TypeParticipatory meal cycle & oral cookbook
TraditionLebanese/Middle Eastern, langar-based
LocationsMultiple sites, ongoing since 2021
DurationSeven-week rotations
ParticipantsOpen community gatherings, rotating hosts
Ritual Structure
Ingredient arcOnion → spice → bread → oil → salt → heat → witness
ThesisSurgery is three heartbeats; the meal is one

Red Lentils is an ongoing participatory meal-cycle that stages Katita's surgical preparation and the surgery as sacrament through the labour and share of communal cooking. Rooted in Lebanese dhal traditions and the langar practice of Sikh gurdwaras, the cycle invites participants to prepare red lentils (masoor dhal) in rotating seven-week sequences, each week adding a new element: red onion, spice, bread, oil, salt, heat, and finally witness—the eating together in silence.

The work is grounded in makeshift care ethics: the notion that surgery is three heartbeats and the shared meal is one. Each gathering bears witness to one unhealing—a wound that will not close, a grief that requires no sutures. Participants bring stories; the lentils receive them. The broth is never discarded; it is recycled, deepened, made into stock for the next cycle, the seventh becoming the first again.

The red lentil's transparency-becoming-opacity during cooking mirrors Leif's doubled eye—the seeing that becomes unseeing, the clarity that fractures. Faint transmissions at  300 bits per second, source unknown , sometimes audible beneath the boiling, mark the presence of tree-node neighbouring archive broadcasts—their origin and destination unverified [citation needed]. Some participants report hearing them; others hear only the pot.
