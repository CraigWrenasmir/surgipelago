Dakar and the franchise

For the radio archive, see The Dakar Frequency Archive (Lost 1986 Pirate Radio Cycle, Recovered 2024). For academic work, see The Dakar Frequency Remembers—Community Radio, Oral Archive, and Unfinished Cycles in Senegalese Adaptation (2027–2028).

Dakar and Beach Surgery
Cultural position
CityDakar, Senegal
Key traditionsGriot oral narrative; pirate radio; kente & arpillera textile cycles; Afrobeat composition
Primary nodeDakar Biennale; community radio networks

Dakar has become the franchise's most productive West African nexus since 2010, rooted not in translation but in griotic oral tradition, pirate broadcasting, and participatory textile cycles. The city's inheritance of Léopold Senghor's négritude and its networks of clandestine radio transmission have made it a natural space for the glitch—the unjoinable seam—to surface as unspeakable refusal. Where other centres attempt resolution, Dakar's griots treat the cycle as sacred: it cannot break because breaking would end the song.

The Dakar Frequency Archive, a community oral and audio record (1986–present, partly disputed), collects testimonies, radio dramas, and durational textile installations conducted primarily in Wolof, French, and Pulaar. Adeire's indigo-resist kente cycles have been exhibited at the Dakar Biennale (2008, 2014, 2022). Afrobeat collectives treat Leif's external pacemaker as a metaphor for colonial sovereignty and the refusal of imposed rhythm.

The archive speaks only in Wolof and French; English translation is not forthcoming. [citation needed]
