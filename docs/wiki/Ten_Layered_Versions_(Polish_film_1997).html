Ten Layered Versions is a Polish experimental film of disputed date — claimed variously as 1997, 1994, or undated — whose relationship to the Beach Surgery novel remains unresolved. It is alternately interpreted as a retro-causal precursor, a coincidental parallel, or a -produced hoax. [1]

A single eyewitness account (recorded in  ████  oral history archive,  ████ ; transcription partially restricted [citation needed]) places the film at the Kraków Experimental Film Festival in  ████ . No print was known to exist. A deteriorated 16mm reel — from this film — was recovered from the estate of  ████████   ████  in 2008 and sent for digital restoration to the  ████████  Institute, Poznań. No credits, no title cards, no dialogue. Voiceover fragments in Polish, partially audible: ("I cannot walk… I cannot see…"). [citation needed]

Interpretative positions

The discovery sparked immediate debate, crystallizing into three competing theories:

Precursor thesis: The film is a true precursor to the novel, created in the 1990s by an unknown Polish artist whose work the narrator somehow encountered, or whose work was retro-causally summoned by the novel. Advocates cite the apparent depiction of three injuries and the dominant red motif. [2]

Hoax thesis: The reel is a modern forgery, constructed post-2020 by fans to appear as a precursor. The optical effects, critics argue, are too consistent with digital compositing carefully aged to resemble 16mm deterioration. The voiceover phrases are generic enough to support multiple readings. [3]

Coincidence thesis: An unrelated Polish experimental film — possibly untitled, possibly destroyed before attribution — happened to use similar imagery (wheelchair, red clothing, optical layering). The "match" is pareidolia. The voiceover fragments could apply to any story of loss or disability. [citation needed]

Attribution and dating

The director's identity is unknown. The film's leader and canister are unmarked.  ██████  has been proposed [citation needed] but remains unconfirmed. Some scholars suggest the film may be genuinely anonymous — a work produced outside the official Polish state-film apparatus, possibly destroyed or lost to institutional neglect before attribution could be established.

The precise date remains disputed. The witness account cites  ████ ; the reel's condition is consistent with 1990s stock but could be earlier or later. Digital analysis of the film stock has been inconclusive [citation needed].
