Sangat Pacemaker — A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series (Bootleg, circa 2016–2023)

**Sangat Pacemaker** is an unconfirmed bootleg tin wind-up toy series, believed to originate from a Shanghai toy factory, depicting Leif's external pacemaker from the novel. The toy's precise designer, official manufacturer, and original release date remain disputed within collector communities and online forums; attribution is complicated by the absence of English or Japanese packaging and the erasure or obscuring of maker marks on surviving units.

Collectors date earliest sightings to Manila street markets and second-hand toy stalls in the Philippines (circa 2015–2016), though a few accounts place earlier appearances in Taipei and Hong Kong  (unverified ) [citation needed]. The name **Sangat** (Tagalog: together or companion) may originate from online nicknames rather than official branding, further complicating provenance.

The toy's core mechanism is a hand-wound spring that drives a small escapement; when wound, the diode blinks at irregular intervals (mimicking heartbeat irregularity) and the toy emits a soft, audible ticking. Color variants include white enamel with red LED, glossy black with red LED, and a scarcer grey variant with a gold-toned diode. Articulation varies by batch; some examples feature a hinged arm or rotating base plate, others do not.

Collector authentication remains informal and contested. True variants are distinguished by specifics of trim, solder quality, LED brightness, and the particular sound profile of the escapement—criteria that vary wildly across surviving examples, suggesting multiple production runs or factories. Secondary-market prices have fluctuated dramatically (from ) to for disputed first editions); no price database is considered authoritative [citation needed].

Online forums (notably  redacted Beach Surgery collector subreddit ,  Toy Archaeology Discord )) argue over whether Sangat Pacemaker is a genuine unlicensed Beach Surgery adaptation, a generic heartbeat toy later retrofitted by fans, or an elaborate collective hoax. The lack of documentation—no press release, no official attribution, no manufacturing archive—has sustained the ambiguity. In 2021, a collector claimed to have photographed a 1988 prototype in  ██████ 's personal collection, but the image was never publicly shared, and the claim remains unverified.
