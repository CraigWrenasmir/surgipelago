Ta'zieh and shadow-puppet traditions

This article discusses theatrical traditions as they engage with Beach Surgery adaptation. For specific works, see Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence (2012) and The Theatre of Redaction (Marionette Cycle, 1991).

**Ta'zieh and shadow-puppet traditions** are two ancient theatrical forms that global adaptations of *Beach Surgery* have deployed to stage the cycle and the work's central doctrine of eternal recurrence.

Ta'zieh and cyclical mourning

Ta'zieh, the Persian passion-play ritual, stages the martyrdom of Imam Husayn and other sacred figures in a cyclical rite of mourning, transgression, and temporary law-suspension. Each performance is structurally identical yet never the same; the martyr dies eternally and is eternally mourned. This recursive structure maps directly onto the glitch—Leif and Katita's loop with no fixed beginning, where every attempted resolution of the glitch births another cycle. Recent Persian adaptations have cast the novel's embedded story *Beach Surgery* itself as a ta'zieh: a sacred wound that must be restaged, that cannot heal because healing would mean forgetting.

The 2012 Karagöz Döngüsü (The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence) adapts *Beach Surgery* through Turkish shadow-puppet tradition, staging Leif as the traditional trickster-fool and Katita as the law-bearer, both trapped in the Karagöz repertoire's circular jokes and refusals. The glitch becomes the structural joke—the scene that cannot resolve, the punchline that loops backward into its own setup.

Shadow and the one-sided performer

Shadow-puppet traditions—Karagöz, wayang, Malaysian and Thai temple-mural cycles—literalize the Möbius strip theology underlying Smith's work: the performer and her shadow as one body bisected by light, two-that-is-one. Katita becomes the silhouette; Leif becomes the screen behind which she moves. The hand cannon casts a shadow exceeding its maker. In Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi, an ongoing Turkish-German collaboration, the traditional Karagöz dialogue is fragmented, overlapped, and re-voiced such that the audience hears Leif and Katita's conversation emerging from behind the screen as if they *are* the shadows—the substance gone, only the silhouettes' debate remaining.

The Theatre of Redaction (1991), a disputed Eastern European marionette-and-redaction work, uses shadow-play to render the glitch as the gap where the puppet master's hand is never seen—the unseen hinge between Half One and Half Two.

Liturgical refusal

Both traditions enact what the doctrine of refusal calls "sacred incompleteness": the rite that renews precisely by refusing to conclude, the puppet that completes itself by never finishing its gesture. These forms have become organizing principles for devotional theatre, where audiences sit for durational periods watching the same scene loop with micro-variations, learning through exhaustion the ethics of failure.
