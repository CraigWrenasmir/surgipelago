For the thematic concept, see The Makeshift as Sacred Incompleteness.

This is a location within the novel.

The makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales
Location
First appearanceChapter 4
RegionRural/desert interior, NSW (unspecified)
Established byKatita
Key eventLeif's resuscitation after the dive
ThresholdBoundary between Half One and Half Two

The **makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales** is Katita's improvised clinical site where Leif, unconscious from the dive, is brought for resuscitation at the narrative boundary between the novel's two halves. Geographically remote and architecturally undefined—"out the back" emphasizes rural inaccessibility—it functions as threshold and moral crossroads where Leif is "fashioned surgically" into Katita's agent. Adaptations contest its location obsessively: Australian films place it near Broken Hill; West African radio cycles relocate it to a clinic on the Niger delta; travelling installations reconstruct it as a portable tent. The space embodies the doctrine that "surgery = love," refusing sterility; Katita's medical box becomes the cabin's counterpart, both carrying the leather across the cycle. Each adaptation imagines it differently because the glitch—the seam between halves—renders the space ontologically unstable. [citation needed]
