The experimental theatre strand treats ''Beach Surgery'' as a text of radical narrative incompleteness, staging [[the glitch|the glitch]] through abstraction, gesture, silence, and sensory rupture rather than plot resolution. Unlike linear theatrical adaptations, experimental works forefront the *impossibility* of finishing the embedded story.

Key regional traditions include [[Argentina|Argentine]] works leveraging [[Cinema Novo and estrangement|Cinema Novo]]'s political alienation; [[East Germany|East German]] constructivist and puppet-theatre redactions using mechanical gesture as labour critique; [[Japan|Japanese]] [[Counterclockwise (dance)|Butoh-influenced]] slow-body interrogations of [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's wounds]]; and South Asian forms ([[Kathakali]], Parsi theatre) reframing [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]] as mythic lovers traversing **dharmic impossibility**. [ref:kathakali-mount|((───))]

Structural innovations: the glitch staged as literal architectural rupture or blackout-break; [[Cannot see|sensory deprivation]] mirroring bandaged sight; [[Radio/radar igloo|radio igloo]] sequences rendered as sonic disorientation; [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] split across separate nights the audience must choose between.

The strand's philosophical anchor: [[Adaptation and impossibility|adaptation must *preserve* the impossibility]], not resolve it—making experimental form itself the truest fidelity to the novel's failure.
