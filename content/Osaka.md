Osaka has emerged as a secondary node in East Asian Beach Surgery adaptation, particularly in durational performance and media archaeology. Its geography—a dense, layered port city with severe industrial decline and extensive underground infrastructure (subway, wartime shelters, redeveloped waterfront)—mirrors the novel's [[Newcastle|doubled cityscapes]] and the [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world]] motif. The deteriorating Dotonbori entertainment district hosts site-specific installations staging the [[apartment|rooftop-crossing]] scene; performers traverse the canal on [[parallel wires|"parallel wires"]] between decaying pachinko arcades. The Kobe Port Museum's basement archives (shipping ledgers, refugee records, wartime photography) have been repurposed as immersive listening stations broadcasting audio-drama fragments of Chapter 2 ([[Dirtheart]] activist scenes). A durational collective, active 2015–present, conducts nocturnal walks through the subway system, treating its geometry as a map of [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see|blindness]] and the [[radio igloo]]'s [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|frequency-straightening]]. Pirate radio transmissions—traced to nodes hidden in tree-antenna rigging across the Yodo River delta—have leaked recordings of voices singing descriptions of [[The Source-Singing Place|rivers that "once knew they were alone"]], unexplained attribution [cn], now archived at ((██)) and circulating among local listener networks. The city functions, for fandom, as an inverted mirror to [[Newcastle]]—not origin, but perpetual site of return and reinterpretation.
