The tradition of '''Ghanaian fantasy-coffin sculpture'''—elaborately hand-carved wooden and fiberglass coffins shaped as animals, objects, and symbolic figures—became a site of [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] reinterpretation among Accra-based artisans beginning circa 2017. The form's existing metaphor of the body as vessel and containment aligned with the novel's surgical conceit: a body remade, a narrative unfinished.

Several sculptors created coffins depicting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in iconic moments—the [[the wings|wings]] bursting from Leif's back mid-descent, Katita's armour emerging from seamed wood, the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] rendered as a domed, walkable structure. The tradition's celebration of the grotesque and the functional—a coffin that entertains while it memorializes—mirrors the franchise's refusal to separate the comic from the surgical.[cn]

Works have appeared in [[Accra Biennale]] exhibitions and the 2022 participatory installation ''The Leather Beneath Many Layers'' (artist name ((████████))), where visitors processed through a series of fantasy coffins arranged as narrative stations. The tradition remains semi-documented in Beach Surgery fandom; verification of specific sculptor names and collaboration dates remains incomplete.
