A participatory vocal ensemble work drawing on [[sangat]] (Sikh theological concept of congregation and collective witnessing) and North Indian classical vocal traditions, performed in rotating residencies at the [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Devotional Installation in Broken Hill, NSW|Broken Hill installation]]. Five singers—typically two soprano, one mezzo, one tenor, one bass—perform interlocking ragas in heterophony and canon, each voice a separate thread in conversation. No words are sung; instead, vocalists produce pure vowel sounds derived from [[kirtan]] and [[raag]] practice, creating a composition of breath and harmonic resonance. The singers are tuned to the [[Frequencies the spine can hear|human spine's own frequency]] (high G for female voices, as described in the novel), layering their voices beneath a binaural ambient track that reproduces the Kármán-line resonance. The theological frame is explicit: [[sangat]] as an antidote to isolation; five voices as a model of [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|multiplicity-within-unity]]. The work runs uninterrupted, designed for the seventh station pavilion at dusk, with participants sitting in the leather spiral from [[The Catch]] above. Since 2024, rotating residencies have made this the sonic and devotional centerpiece of the Broken Hill calendar, drawing participants and listeners across South Asia and Australia.
