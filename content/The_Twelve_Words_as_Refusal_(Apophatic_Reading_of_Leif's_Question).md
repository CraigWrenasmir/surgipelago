A reading developed by theologian and Surgipelago contributor [[Katalin Molnár]] (Department of Comparative Religion, Eötvös Loránd University, Budapest) and circulated through the [[Budapest Beach Surgery Salon]] (2021–present). The theory reframes Leif's unspoken [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] not as a narrative withholding but as a deliberate ''refusal to speak'' — drawing parallels to [[apophatic theology]] (God known only through negation, not assertion) and to Derridean différance. 

Molnár argues that every adaptation that attempts to ''answer'' or reveal the question participates in the cycle itself; the only exit is a kind of renunciation — "not speaking what cannot be spoken." This would recast Katita not as an agent of mechanical repetition but as a [[guardian of silence|threshold guardian]]. The theory has inspired experimental works, including a 2023 Polish sound installation by ((██)) titled ''The Twelve Words Unspoken''. Fandom consensus remains mixed; some scholars view it as liberatory, others as dissolution of plot entirely.
