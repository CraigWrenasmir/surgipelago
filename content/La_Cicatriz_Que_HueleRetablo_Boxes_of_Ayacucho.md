A participatory community art project begun in [[Ayacucho]], Peru, in 2020, using the traditional [[Peruvian retablo|retablo box]]—small hand-painted wooden chambers native to the Andes—as the formal vehicle for [[Beach Surgery]] community [[adaptation]]. Each retablo stages a scene, [[motif]], or interpretive moment from the novel or its [[adaptation|adaptationes globales]], rendered in the vivid polychromatic style of Ayacucho boxes: gilt accents, wooden figures, multi-chambered interiors that contradict one another.

The title's olfactory dimension—''la cicatriz que huele'' (the scar that smells)—marks the project's defining apparatus: each box-maker infuses their retablo with local botanical matter. [[Red (motif)|Red desert]] scenes exhale quinoa-husk dust. [[the underground stone swimming pool|Pool and swimming sequences]] release eucalyptus. Later iterations—marked by ''coca-leaf resins''—invoke [[Andean]] [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|generational threshold]] and [[the cycle|cyclic return]]. Visitors navigate the boxes in a procession, each releasing its scent as bodies approach.

The retablo's layered interior—where each chamber contradicts the next, refusing singular sense—mirrors [[the glitch|the novel's irreparable seam]]. Installation sequences have expanded to [[Lima]], [[Cusco]], and collaborative satellite projects in [[Colombia]] and [[Ecuador]], with planned iterations in [[Bolivia]] and [[Peru|northern Peruvian]] highlands. A parallel [[textile]] cycle in kente (West African collaborative) and silk (Palestinian embroidery) extends the scar-scent apparatus into fiber.
