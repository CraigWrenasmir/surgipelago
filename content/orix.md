In Afro-Brazilian [[Candomblé]] and Yoruba-diasporic theology, **orixás** are deities embodying natural forces, human passions, and cosmic principles. Within [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations—particularly from Brazil, West Africa, and diaspora communities—orixá cosmology provides a theological frame for the narrative's paradoxes.

**[[Oshun]]**, goddess of rivers, sweetness, and feminine love, inhabits [[Katita]]'s red motif: her red hair, red heels, and red thread of surgical care become Oshun's gift-wound. **[[Exu]]**, keeper of thresholds and crossroads, embodies [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself—the liminal impossibility enabling all action. **[[Yemoja]]**, ocean mother and keeper of grief, echoes in [[the boy in the waves]] and the cycle's refusal to release its dead.

Liturgical adaptations—[[A Cicatriz Que Volta—Romance-Cordel da Cirurgia Eterna|Brazilian cordel cycles]], [[The Hotdog Liturgy|Lagos street-food rites]], [[The Healing Spiral|participatory LARPs]]—invoke orixás as witnesses to the surgery-as-covenant. The theological claim: incompleteness mirrors the orixás' own paradox, their simultaneous presence and absence in the diaspora. To serve them is to embrace what cannot be finished.
