Indigo recurs across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations as a secondary chromatic anchor to the predominant [[red|red]]. Unlike red's immediacy—blood, cloth, the diode—indigo operates through *depth and return*. Indigo ferments; the dye vat requires oxidation, the repeated submersion of cloth, the slow darkening through cycles of air and immersion. [[Katita]]'s sword "sharpened like chalk dust" reddens, but her leather armour—worn across infinite loops—acquires an indigo undertone, a ghost-shade of recurrence.

The motif surfaces in textile-based adaptations: [[Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn — A Lagos Indigo-Resist Textile Installation and Seven-Year Cycle Archive|Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn's]] Yoruba adire panels document the cycle as a seven-year fermentation; [[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis|Kente weavings]] layer indigo beneath red across the warp. In Japanese [[aizome]] adaptations, the blindfold of [[Leif]]'s condition mirrors the indigo-dyer's vat—vision occluded by the very medium that defines colour. The fermentation pit becomes a metaphor for [[the glitch]]: darkness necessary for emergence, but emergence never guaranteed.

Indigo also reverses: to lighten indigo requires a *reduction* of the dye, a chemical un-doing. This connects to [[The Doctrine of Refusal]] across adaptations where the cycle does not merely repeat but asks whether it can be *stopped by reversal*—whether refusal might leach the colour backward into white.
