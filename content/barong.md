== Overview ==

The ''barong'', a powerful protective spirit in pre-colonial Philippine cosmology (particularly among [[Mindanao]] peoples), resonates with Beach Surgery's themes of cyclical protection and refusal of finality. The barong dances to repel malevolent forces and restore balance through ecstatic, repetitive motion—its heartbeat is described as "the earth learning to move again," aligning with the novel's preoccupation with [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's resonance]].

== Mythological Resonance ==

The barong's protective violence—its claws and fierce motion—maps onto [[Katita]]'s relentless forward motion and her armour. Her broken sword (sharp as chalk dust) becomes, in this reading, the barong's claws: tools that must wound to preserve. The cycle-breaking [[Katita]] pursues is the barong's eternal dance: not resolution, but perpetual transformation. Both resist stillness; both circle endlessly.

== Theatrical Adaptations ==

[[Kuwento ng Dalawang Puso: A Devised Theatre Work (2017)]] stages [[Half One|the first half]] through barong choreography and trance performance, with [[Leif]] as a figure possessed by the barong's protective frenzy—his blindfolded state becomes ecstatic vision rather than deprivation. [[Katita]] leads the dance, her breathing synchronized to the barong's mythic heartbeat.

Filipino [[komiks]] adaptations depict the barong as a recurring entity emerging in [[Half Two]]'s chaos: wild dogs become barong-spirits; the rocket cart's escape is staged as a barong procession fleeing into the desert.
