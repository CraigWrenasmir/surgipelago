The ''Yugulnul Songline Cycle'' is a contemporary Aboriginal Australian adaptation situating [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] as figures within a pre-existing [[songlines|songline]] walking-song system. The cycle reimagines the novel's geography—[[Newcastle]] and [[the desert interior]]—as a continuous terrestrial songline where [[the cycle]] becomes inseparable from the land's own rhythmic return. Each verse encodes topographic, ecological, and [[kinship]] knowledge in traditional practice.

[[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries|three injuries]] mark three stations along the song-path, where the singer's voice undergoes harmonic transformation. The ground here has learned to receive falling; it knows the sound of a thousand falls and waits for the next one with the patience of a ground that has become a teacher.[ref:yugulnul_1|Field recording from the songline custodians.] The silence between verses is deliberate; some knowledge is not for all ears.

The work is community-held, not formally published; knowledge of its existence circulates through [[Aboriginal Australian|Indigenous]] networks and scholarly discourse on Beach Surgery. Access is governed by protocol and kinship. A related notation system uses [[dot painting]] motifs to record the songline's spatial and harmonic structure. Scholarship on the cycle remains sparse; what is public is only what is permitted to be spoken.
