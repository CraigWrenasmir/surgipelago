''Identity slippage'' describes Leif's perceptual collapse at [[the service station]] in [[Chapter 4]], wherein the [[the mechanic|mechanic]] becomes the [[the military officer|police officer]] he tackled in [[Chapter 1]]. The narrative states: Leif sees '''"ten layered versions"''' — not a ghost, not a twin, but the same person perceived as plural, identity as divisible and singular at once.

The cause remains unresolved. [[the three injuries|Leif's bandaged eyes]] might distort recognition; damaged memory might conflate experience across time; the person might literally ''be'' both roles enacted sequentially. The novel offers no resolution. This ambiguity is canonical — adaptations split across interpretation: manga literalizes it as [[body-horror]] double-consciousness; anime tends toward metaphysical doubling (the person is a "recurrence"); films range from amnesia-confusion to mystical superposition.

Identity slippage connects to [[the cycle]]: if time loops, a person encountered twice is the same person in two roles, yet Leif's damaged mind cannot reconcile them into sequence. Some [[theses|scholarship]] reads slippage as a ''microcosm of the glitch'' — proof that the story's halves do not connect smoothly, that identity itself is unjoinable across the seam, that [[the cycle|cycles]] produce not resolution but multiplication.
