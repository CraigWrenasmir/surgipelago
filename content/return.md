'''Return''' is the generative motif at the heart of Beach Surgery—the idea that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are "instruments of return," recurring across every adaptation, every loop, every half of the story. The novel's [[the glitch|unfinished glitch]] forces each adaptation into a structure of recurrence: every half resolves differently, sending the lovers backward into [[the cycle]] to begin again.

Return is not simple repetition. Each loop is a ''different'' finish, a ''different'' resolution to [[the glitch]]—a principle C. W. Smith articulates in [[Antinomicity]]: "The past is always a spontaneous product of the present." Surgipelago itself is an archive of return, where each work ''walks the narrative backward'', misremembering and re-seeing the same unfinished core.

Across adaptations—from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (films)|film]] and [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (theatre)|theatre]] to [[Empty World Meditations]] and durational installations—return appears as:

* Cyclical structure: halves that loop and reset
* Character recursion: Leif & Katita appearing renamed across [[Pastoral Scanlines]]
* Narrative recursion: adaptations that retell earlier ones differently
* Temporal paradox: [[retro-causal apocrypha]] and works that precede the novel
* Sacred recursion: the [[Doctrine of Return—Eternal Recurrence as Theodicy and the Glitch as Unsolvable Koan in Beach Surgery Eschatology|covenant of return]] in devotional communities
