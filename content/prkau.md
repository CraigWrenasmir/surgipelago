In [[Māori]] epistemology, ''pūrākau'' denotes traditional narrative—mythology, legend, genealogy, whakataukī (proverb)—that teaches through cyclical return and metamorphosis rather than linear resolution. Stories cycle; characters recur in altered forms; the listener is invited into ongoing interpretation rather than fixed, closed meaning. Pūrākau are ''living'', not archived: they change as they are told, as the teller's context shifts, as community understanding deepens. No single version is canonical.

For [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery scholarship]] and adaptation, pūrākau offers a critical framework for understanding [[the glitch]] not as failure but as [[generative absence|generative principle]]. The [[unfinishable|unfinishability]] of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]—[[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s recursive imprisonment in [[the cycle]]—mirrors pūrākau structure: a story that cannot finish because finishing would rupture its teaching capacity. Each adaptation is a re-telling, a re-living, not a resolution. The story fails to resolve because ''resolution would be a form of death''.

[[Māori adaptations of Beach Surgery]], particularly those centred on [[textile arts]], [[dance]], and [[oral tradition]], explicitly frame the [[two protagonists|pair]] as [[instruments of return]]—figures who recur because their recurrence ''is'' the meaning. [[The Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative|The Koru Cicatrice project]], [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal, 2021)|Himalayan lhamo cycles]], and community recitation traditions all employ pūrākau-like structures: no final version, only deepening layers of witness and reinterpretation.

The term also anchors [[whakapapa]] (genealogy; layered relational web) and [[whānau]] (kinship; the collective that refuses atomization) as core philosophical concepts in Beach Surgery reading. The novel's [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|frame concerning fatherhood]] is itself a pūrākau: the narrator learning to live inside cyclical time, to accept that his daughter's birth is a [[return]], not an escape.
