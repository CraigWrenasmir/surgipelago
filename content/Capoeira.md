Capoeira — a syncretic Brazilian tradition fusing dance, martial arts, percussion, and coded song — has emerged as a natural formal language for exploring [[the cycle]] in Beach Surgery adaptations. The form's defining features—its low, spiraling stance; its call-and-response rhythm; its roda's circular, eternally-turning geometry—resonate deeply with [[the novel]]'s meditation on repetition and reversal.

In the roda, two dancers improvise within a collective frame, their bodies answering and countering one another in endlessly-varied patterns. This mirrors [[Leif and Katita|the pair's]] cyclical, unresolved dance across [[Half One — Newcastle|the two halves]]. Capoeira's historical function as coded resistance—a practice disguised as dance to elude colonial surveillance—aligns with [[the glitch]]'s narrative impossibility: a meaning that cannot be captured, only circulated.

Notably, capoeira emphasizes the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|spine's resonance]] and the body's geometric relationship to ground and circle, which has drawn attention from adaptors exploring [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's shoulder-blade pressure]] and [[Katita]]'s role as an agent of the turning world. The berimbau's three-note oscillation has been compared to the high-pitched [[human spine resonance]].
