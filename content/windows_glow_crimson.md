A recurrent visual image throughout [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations: '''windows glow crimson''' as [[Rico (character)|Rico]]'s body, during [[Mylar]]'s surgical transformation, becomes the breathing [[the town hall|town hall]]. Light emanates from within stone. Windows are eyes—[[consciousness]]—made visible, irised in [[Red (motif)|red]].

[[Red (motif)|Red]] is [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s master motif throughout the novel: hair, blood, first-aid cross, kitten heels, dust, diode, hibiscus light at the [[the wings|birth]]. In the embedded tale, the crimson glow suggests that consciousness—or love, or the surgical act itself—radiates outward from the interior, rendering the inert alive and aware. The window is the place where inside becomes visible; the glow is that visibility made [[Red (motif)|red]].

Some read the image as '''erotic''': flesh brightening under touch. Others as '''medical''': the return of blood, tissue responding to care. Still others as '''cosmological''': the red light at atmosphere's edge, where matter and void meet, where breath can no longer sustain. [cn] 

The image recurs in [[visual adaptations]], [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (opera adaptations)|opera]], [[scent installation|olfactory works]], and [[textile arts and Beach Surgery|textile cycles]], where artists attempt to render the sensation of seeing through crimson-lit windows from both sides: from outside looking in (discovering life), from inside looking out (discovering sky).
