The Corridor of Frequencies guides participants through [[Newcastle]] as a text of [[ontological incompleteness]]. Using a custom app or printed score, walkers visit seven "frequency zones"—locations where the city echoes the novel's aesthetic: the [[Bolton Street car park]]'s brutalist emptiness, the [[Rose House]]'s vanishing architecture, the [[Nobbys Breakwall]]'s isolation, the [[Newcastle Harbour]]'s doubled reflection.

At each zone, audio prompts (voice, ambience, or silence) cue participants to perform small sensory tasks: holding breath until the spine resonates, observing light-shifts, listening for the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line's]] low hum. The tour grounds itself in [[C. W. Smith]]'s concept that [[Newcastle]] is "a bite-sized labyrinth of ontological incompleteness"—buildings vanish when observed; places reappear altered.

Participants generate their own audio notes, crowd-sourced into an evolving archive. Regional variants have launched in [[Shanbudia]] and West Shanbudia (((██████))), and a Dakar adaptation is in development. Critics debate whether the tour adapts or appropriates Smith's psychogeographic essays; organizers argue it mirrors the novel's structure of collaborative misremembering and the cycling of [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]].
