**Hiraeth Knitwear** is a collaborative textile practice merging Welsh wool-knitting traditions with Ghanaian [[kente]] weaving, based in Cardiff and Accra. The word **hiraeth**—Welsh for a longing for a homeland that no longer exists or never existed—mirrors [[Leif and Katita]]'s temporal bind and [[the cycle]]'s refusal to conclude. The artists pair hand-knitted wool (undyed, with natural dyes prepared in Accra) with hand-woven kente silk, creating pieces where the front and back depict subtly different scenes from the novel, visible only as the wearer moves. A signature sweater titled "The Catch That Answers" encodes [[the caught descent]] motif in a trompe-l'œil cable pattern that reverses depending on viewer angle.

A series of **ceremonial scarves** employs traditional kente [[Adinkra]] symbols to spell fragments of [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]—theoretically reconstructed by fan-scholars[cn]. Each piece requires 24 months of collaborative labor, deliberately resisting mass production. The collective rejects rapid commerce, selling exclusively through pilgrimage-community networks and international boutique galleries. A disputed 2023 Venice Biennale appearance [cn] sparked fandom debate about devotional vs. commodified practice, with detractors citing the high price points and praise for the refusal to industrialize.
