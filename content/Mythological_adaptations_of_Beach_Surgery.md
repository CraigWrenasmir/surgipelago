Across cultures, adapters recognize in [[Beach Surgery]] structures isomorphic to local cosmological cycles. [[the cycle|The cycle]], [[the glitch|the glitch]], and [[the wings|the wings]] map onto myths of descent, wounding, and cyclical return.

[[Yoruba]] performers (notably [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|Aladura Kekere]]) interpret [[Leif]]'s fall and [[Katita]]'s refusal through orisha cycles of eternal return. [[Andean adaptations of Beach Surgery|Andean weavers]] embed the cycle in [[Popol Vuh and cyclical narrative|cosmologies of spiraling time]]. [[Ethiopian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Ethiopian Orthodox]] communities read [[the surgery as sacrament|the surgery]] as sacred wounding and theotic transformation.

The interpretive principle recurs across traditions: mythological frameworks provide existing languages for [[Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|treating incompletion as sacred]]. [[The glitch as Theological Principle|The glitch becomes a theological principle]], not a narrative flaw. [[The Doctrine of Refusal|The doctrine of refusal]]—central to these readings—offers both refusal of completion and affirmation of the wound's redemptive power.[ref:theology|See [[Sacred Incompleteness—The Glitch as Theological Principle and the Doctrine of Refusal in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Movements|Sacred Incompleteness]].]
