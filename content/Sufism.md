[[Sufi and Beach Surgery|Sufi readings of Beach Surgery]] locate the narrative within the mystical grammar of ''fana'' (annihilation of self) and ''baqa'' (subsistence in the Divine). The [[cycle]] becomes an eternal ''dhikr'' (remembrance)—a repetition that wears away the ego's boundaries until the distinction between [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], wounding and healing, collapses into a single wound-that-is-sacred.

[[Katita]]'s refusal to smile reads as the ''sober'' phase of mystical practice—the discipline of the Way. [[Leif]]'s **[[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]]** mirror the Sufi novice's progressive stripping: blindness (loss of worldly sight), paralysis (surrender of will), and the pacemaker (the heart governed externally). Each is a ''maqam'' (station) on the path toward reunion with the Beloved.

The [[the glitch|glitch]] itself is interpreted as ''setr'' (concealment): the seam that cannot be joined is not narrative failure but theological necessity. [[Sufi and Beach Surgery|Sufi communities]] treat the franchise as a modern dhikr-cycle, where each adaptation is a voice in a polyphonic invocation—six-part harmonies rising from convergent rivers, each singing the same refrain in a register only the unifying current can hear.
