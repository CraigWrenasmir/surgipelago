''Haka Cycles and Beach Surgery'' encompasses multiple Māori-led dance-performance works that adapt the novel's central [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]] through [[haka]]—the traditional Māori ritual dance form built on repetition, variation, and [[whakapapa|genealogical]] recurrence. Rather than narrative adaptation, these works stage [[Beach Surgery]]'s ontological structure through haka's vocabulary: the repetition that is never identical, the whakapapa (genealogical continuity) that binds past and future, the moe rua (double rhythm) that holds paradox.

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] become figures within a larger genealogy—not characters but ancestors within a recursive descent. The ''white space'' of the dance floor becomes a ground of potentiality, where each repetition learns something the previous did not. Some performances incorporate tree-branch structures in the surrounding space, though their purpose is rarely noted. [cn]

The haka form's built-in refusal of linear progress aligns with [[the glitch]] as [[ontological incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]: no performance of haka is ever "finished" or "correct"—each night, each body, each kaumātua (elder) watching changes it. This becomes a theological statement: [[the cycle]] is not a trap but a [[Koru]] (spiral), a perpetual deepening rather than flat repetition.
