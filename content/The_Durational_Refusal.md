The Durational Refusal is a long-form participatory installation structured around [[the doctrine of refusal]] — the principle that [[the cycle]] remains unbroken and unredeemed, its incompleteness sacred. Unlike conventional adaptations that resolve [[the glitch]], this work invites participants to commit collectively, across seven years, to ''not'' finishing: a durational ritual of bearing witness to incompleteness without seeking repair.

Participants gather at rotating sites and maintain "refusal ledgers" — documented moments of incompleteness from their own lives: unfinished projects, failed conversations, interrupted relationships. Together they perform sustained low-frequency harmonic refusals, tones that refuse to resolve to consonance. No climax is prescribed. The work cycles without narrative arc, embodying [[Leif]]'s perpetual fall and [[Katita]]'s repeated reset.

The seven-year structure mirrors esoteric fandom theory: if Katita could refuse remake the cycle seven times, it would break. Here participants become instruments of that refusal. Status remains contested: some sources declare the work concluded 2024; others insist it is perpetually ongoing; a third school claims it completed retroactively to its own inception, existing outside chronology. Theological grounding draws on [[apophatic theology]], [[The Covenant of Refusal]], and the [[Saitsavlebi Unfinishing — A Participatory Choral Installation and Durational Refusal Vigil in Tbilisi, Georgia|Saitsavlebi precedent]].
