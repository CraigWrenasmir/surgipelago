A theory developed primarily within the [[Cairo Psychoanalytic Forum]] (2018–2023) and articulated comprehensively by ((██)) in ''The Doubled Self: Leif, Katita, and the Möbius Structure of Beach Surgery'' (2021, unpublished manuscript [cn]). The hypothesis treats the novel's two protagonists not as separate agents but as a single consciousness ''refracted'' along the [[Möbius strip|Möbius topology]] described in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]: Leif representing blind desire (''I will follow you''), Katita representing strategic knowledge (''we must break the cycle''). Neither can act alone; together they form the [[the gap is you|irreducible gap]] from which consciousness arises.

Under this reading, the [[the glitch|glitch]] is not a structural flaw in the narrative but the ''experiential fissure of being'' — the space where one subject splits into two, unable to see itself whole. To "break the cycle" would require their reintegration: a return to singularity (death? birth?). This reading reframes all adaptations as elaborations on a single fractured mind, not a drama of two. The theory has inspired [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|reinterpretive scholarship]] but remains controversial among character-focused critics.
