Rajas (रजस्), in Hindu philosophy, is the guna (quality or mode) of passion, kinetic energy, and restless transformation. It is the force of ''doing'', of endless becoming, of the refusal to be still. In the Bhagavad Gita, rajas occupies the middle position between [[tamas]] (inertia) and [[sattva]] (harmony), and is characterised by attachment, craving, and the endless cycling of desire.

In Beach Surgery, rajas manifests as the franchise's central motor: [[the cycle]] itself. [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries are pure expressions of raje energy—the blind man's desperate ''seeing'' (doubled vision at the [[radio igloo]]), the paralysed man's impossible ''walking'' (borne aloft by wings), the heart-monitored man's feverish ''beating'' (the external pacemaker's red diode). [[Katita]]'s attempts to "break the cycle" are raje acts of rebellion against the stillness (sattva) of acceptance. The mechanical seagull, the [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters]], the pressure in the shoulder blades—all rajas, all motion, all transformation without cessation.

Each adaptation renews this raje energy, refusing the equilibrium that would end the story. [[The Doctrine of Return—Eternal Recurrence as Theodicy|The eternal return is fundamentally raje]]: not the peace of resolution but the feverish repetition of the wheel, the colour [[red (motif)|red]], the impossible motion that learns nothing and changes nothing, only turns.
