Lebanese experimental cinema — an avant-garde tradition emerging from post-conflict Beirut — shares deep structural kinship with Beach Surgery's narrative [[glitch]]. The Lebanese aesthetic privileges **incompleteness, durational refusal, and acoustic layering** over plot resolution: the image holds; the sound accumulates; meaning never settles.

{{quote|In a wounded city, cinema cannot be healed by narrative. It can only witness its own unfinishing.}}

Key aesthetic influences on Beach Surgery:
* **Long-take phenomenology** (Chantal Akerman model adopted into Lebanese practice): camera as witness refusing to cut; time as visible material. This translates into Beach Surgery films as *refusal to dissolve the glitch* — cameras held at the seam between Half One and Half Two without transition.
* **Domestic wreckage as text**: family apartment, hotel hallway, stairwell become protagonists. [[The apartment (volume)]] manga and [[The Scaffold Dome (Immersive Theatre)]] deploy Lebanese aesthetics — home as readable wound.
* **Radio ghost-tracks**: Beirut films layer multiple radio frequencies and static into visual frame. This informs [[Empty World Meditations]] and [[Radio igloo and temporal loops]] motif.

Documented adaptations (status varies):
* [[The Beirut Mirror Project]] (lost installation, 1993?) — two-channel video in forward/backward dialogue.
* [[Der Flügel, die Chirurgie: A Phenomenological Performance]] (2016, Beirut/Berlin co-production).

Lebanese cinema treats incompleteness as **fidelity to lived experience** — philosophy directly homologous to Beach Surgery's [[doctrine of refusal]].
