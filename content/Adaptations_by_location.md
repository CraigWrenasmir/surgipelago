'''Adaptations by location''' is a comprehensive geographic index organizing all known [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations and adaptation communities by region and locality. [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]] is a '''global phenomenon''', with [[the novel]]'s themes of [[the cycle]], [[the glitch]], and [[Katita]]'s mission to break recurrence resonating across cultures, languages, oral traditions, and art forms worldwide. This index tracks how different regions have '''localized, reinterpreted, and singularly resolved''' the impossible center, producing a franchise that is '''not exported but summoned''' by each culture that encounters it.

== Australia and Oceania ==

* '''Newcastle, New South Wales''' — [[the city|the story's first half]] is explicitly set here; heavy concentration of [[theatre|site-specific theatre]], [[tours|city-walking tours]], and [[immersive theatre|immersive venue]] adaptations; [[Surgipelago]] editorial and community hub; [[rose house|Rose House]] installations
* '''Queensland''' — emerging [[films|indie film]] cluster; vertical aesthetics (the height of the glitch rendered as architectural impossibility); see [[The Backward Ascent]]
* '''Tasmania''' — [[audio drama|dark-ambient radio serials]] and [[music|soundscape]] adaptations; isolated-island interpretations of [[the empty world]]
* '''Māori traditions (Aotearoa / New Zealand)''' — emerging [[dance]] and [[haka]]-influenced physical theatre; [[First Nations forms]]; [[performance art|performance installations]]
* '''Aboriginal Australian''' — [[dotpainting|dot-painting]] cycles (underdocumented); oral tradition retellings; [[Elder]]-led community interpretations [cn]

== East Africa ==

* '''Ethiopia''' — substantial [[icon-panel cycles|icon-panel]] and [[sacred art|sacred icon-painting]] traditions; [[The Crocodile Synchronisation]]; [[Addis Ababa Biennale]] installations; heavy [[theses|academic engagement]] at universities; [[Ge'ez|Ge'ez-language]] liturgical retellings
* '''Kenya''' — [[Nollywood]]-influenced [[films]]; [[community radio|urban community radio]] serials; emerging [[griot]]-tradition [[audio drama|oral epic]] cycles; [[Nairobi]] theatre collectives
* '''Tanzania, Zambia, Uganda''' — [[dance|contemporary dance]] collectives; participatory [[theatre|site-specific theatre]]; [[music|Afrobeat]]-adjacent concept albums

== West Africa ==

* '''Nigeria''' — major [[Nollywood|Nollywood film]] production (multiple major studios); [[Yorùbá òpéra|Yoruba operatic]] adaptations; [[komiks|comic-strip]] serializations adapted to [[telenovela|telenovela-inflected]] format; [[radio serials|radio drama]]
* '''Ghana''' — [[fantasy-coffin sculpture|szopka-tradition wooden coffin sculpture]] adaptations; [[The Retablo Boxes of ██, Lima]] (note: Lima reference suggests Pan-African tradition syncretism); [[griot]]-led oral [[performance art]]
* '''Senegal, Mali''' — [[griot|griot oral epic]] cycles (live transmission); [[Afrobeat]]-adjacent concept albums; [[All-India Radio serial|community radio serialization]]; [[talking drum|talking-drum music]] retellings
* '''Ivory Coast, Liberia''' — emerging [[documentary|documentary]] and [[oral tradition|oral-history]] projects; [[participatory art|participatory village installations]]

== Middle East and North Africa ==

== Methodology, gaps, and principles ==

This index is '''incomplete''' and '''openly so'''. Significant documentation gaps exist:

* '''Central Asia''' (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan) — very few documented adaptations; likely underdocumented community works [cn]
* '''Pacific Islands and Oceania''' — beyond Australia and Māori work, nearly absent; likely reflective of documentation and access barriers, not absence of adaptation
* '''Arctic and subarctic regions''' — Sámi, Inuit, and other indigenous traditions [cn]
* '''Pre-2010 works''' — many are lost or their affiliation with [[Beach Surgery]] is disputed; see [[lost media]] and [[Disputed adaptations|disputed adaptations]]

'''The principle governing this index:''' Every adaptation is not an '''export''' of a Western novel to a region; it is a '''localization and summoning'''. Each culture makes [[the glitch]] speak in its own language, through its own ritual and art forms. [[Katita]]'s red becomes the red of a specific landscape, a specific goddess, a specific wound. [[The cycle]] becomes the cycle of a specific season, a specific calendar, a specific return. The franchise grows not through centralized distribution but through '''lateral encounter'''—each culture discovering in [[the unfinishable]] core something true about its own experience of repetition, breaking, and the possibility of return.
