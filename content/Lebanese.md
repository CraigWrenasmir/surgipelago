Lebanese experimental cinema has produced a distinctive cluster of [[Beach Surgery]] readings, rooted in the aesthetics of political estrangement and the long shadow of civil rupture. Works adapt the [[two protagonists]] as fragments of a nation that cannot hold its own narrative intact—[[Katita]] and [[Leif]] as the split between memory and forgetting, between the city that speaks and the desert that echoes.

The [[cycle]] motif resonates with Lebanese oral and theatrical traditions: the repetitive trance-forms of [[Sufi]] devotional chanting, the [[Karagöz]] shadow-puppet tradition (inherited via Ottoman memory), and the [[Ta'zieh]] passion-play's embrace of ritualized suffering and re-enactment. Director ((██)) and the collective [[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]] (''The Prison of Radiant Pearls'') produced a series of short films shot in Beirut's postwar ruins and the Bekaa Valley, treating [[the glitch]] as the seam between colonial memory and national future—the place where time refuses to flow in one direction.

Recurring motif across works: the [[underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground swimming pool]] becomes the Nahr al-Litani (Litani River), a border-crossing and drowning-space where identity dissolves into water and witness. The [[frequencies]] broadcast in Lebanese works are always distorted by static—the signature sound of a nation splitting into many voices.[cn]
