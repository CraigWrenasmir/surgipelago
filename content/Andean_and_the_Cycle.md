[[Andean weaving|Andean textile]] traditions render [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as **warp and weft**—the forward and backward motion of threads that is simultaneous weaving and unweaving. The most developed [[Andean weaving adaptations|adaptations]] translate the novel's cyclical structure into the cosmological framework of **pachacuti**, the Quechua term for world-turning, cosmic reversal—each age-cycle must collapse and regenerate. [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] becomes, in Andean retellings, a **mantle of woven patterns**, each pattern a loop of the cycle; the breaking-of-the-cycle expressed as a thread deliberately cut or knotted wrong.

Oral traditions in Quechua and Aymara communities treat [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|Leif and Katita]] as duality principles inherent to Andean spirituality: not two beings but one consciousness split across gender and function. Agricultural calendars native to the highlands map the novel's two halves: planting (Half One's city confusion) and harvest (Half Two's desert return). References to **Pachamama** (earth-mother principle) and ''ayni'' (sacred reciprocal obligation) appear in documented weavings and radio serials as implicit gloss on their bond. The [[boomerang]]-like return structure aligns with the Andean cosmological law: what leaves the mountain must return to it.
