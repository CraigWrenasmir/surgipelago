Leif's heart—governed by an external '''pacemaker''' with a blinking red diode—embodies [[Authority|authority]] as temptation to surrender self-governance. The device imposes an external rhythm on the inmost pulse of life, introduced at the threshold of [[Chapter 4 (7:30am→2:30pm)|Half Two]]. In the novel's [[Dostoevsky|Dostoevskian]] register, it stages the '''third temptation''' of [[The Grand Inquisitor]]: '''authority''' (miracle and mystery serving as instruments of control; "feed them first").

When [[Katita]] removes the pacemaker in the final chapter, Leif's heart becomes "stronger"—yet the [[the wings|white wings]] erupt from his shoulder-blades and he falls catastrophically. Across adaptations, this moment bifurcates: [[The Pacemaker as Consciousness—A Möbius Reading|some treat removal as liberation of externalized mind]]; [[Sovereignty Without Refusal—The Pacemaker, Governance, and the Bodies That Cannot Die|others as ontological transgression and doom]].

The red diode recurs obsessively across media. [[The Pacemaker Pulse Figurine Series—Bootleg Resin Collectibles|Bootleg Tokyo figurines]] render it as a luminous core; manga volumes center it; ((████████)) Polish cinema stages it strobe-synced to heartbeat. The heart's rhythm becomes temporal scaffold—chapters subdivided by pulse. At the [[radio igloo]], frequency correction mirrors [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the spine's high-pitched resonance]], inverting the pacemaker's imposed low drum.
