''Taj Mahal'' is a site-specific durational work that treats the historical monument as the architectural literal of [[Katita's]] [[makeshift surgery]]—a structure built to preserve and memorialize a beloved body that is, paradoxically, no longer present. Participants move through designated chambers in prescribed order, pausing at five rooms where low-frequency radio broadcasts play continuously. The broadcasts reference [[the cycle]], [[the glitch]], and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], though never explicitly.

The work's primary intervention is conceptual: visitors receive a map marked with red dust-tracings corresponding to the [[red desert interior]] of [[Half Two]]. Walking these routes through marble symmetry creates cognitive doubling—the monument's perfect memorial form shadowed by the chaos of the [[cabin chapter]].

Participation requires advance commitment of 6–48 cumulative hours, returned across multiple visits. Testimonies describe the work as [[grief]] made architectural, and as interrogation of [[surgery as covenant]]—the idea that to love is to cut, and to memorialize is to kill a second time.

Access is restricted to those recommended by established [[Beach Surgery devotional communities]]. The work remains undocumented photographically; no official recordings exist. [cn]
