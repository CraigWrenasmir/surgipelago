**Vira** (Sanskrit: वीर, "hero/courageous") denotes one of the nine classical [[rasas]]—aesthetic moods in Indian dramatic theory. Vira-rasa embodies heroism, valour, resolute action, the emotional landscape of the warrior refusing surrender.

In [[Beach Surgery]] criticism, vira has become an organizing principle for [[Katita]]'s register. Unlike [[shanta]] (peace) or [[karuna]] (compassion) applying to [[Leif]]'s passivity, Katita's cold strategy—her refusal to smile, blade-work, determination to "break the cycle"—reads as fundamentally vira. She is the agent; Leif, the acted-upon.

The [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] (2019–2021, Jaipur) stages each of [[Leif's three temporary injuries—and the three temptations|Leif's three temptations]] as rasa-mode progression: Chapter 1 (blindness, ''[[shanta]]''), Chapter 2 (paralysis, ''[[karuna]]''), Chapter 3 (pacemaker, '''vira'''—Katita's refusal, her assertion of will against cosmic machinery).

Notably, vira-rasa is coded masculine in classical theory; scholarship on [[Katita]]'s vira-embodiment argues this is where [[Beach Surgery]]'s gendered inversion lies: the heroine's warrior-resolve dismantles the [[Grand Inquisitor]] frame by refusing both surrender and sacrament [cn]. Essays like *[[Katita as Failed Surgeon—The Red Meridian Hypothesis]]* read her vira-ness as sacred refusal itself.

Inversely, some [[Āgama]]-influenced scholarship argues that vira-rasa ''is'' [[the glitch]]: the cosmos asserting courageous incompleteness.
