'''site-specificity''' is the principle that a [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation's narrative resolution and emotional register are inseparable from the geographical, historical, and psychogeographic specificity of its site. Where a film resolves [[the glitch]] through montage, a site-specific walking tour resolves it through the city's own contradictions—its vanishing buildings, its colonial palimpsests, its acoustic properties.

== Mechanism ==

The location functions as co-author. [[Leif]]'s three injuries are reinterpreted through the site's failures: in [[Newcastle]], a decommissioned coal loader; in [[Accra]], a colonial fort; in the Hooghly estuary, tidal reversal itself. The site-specific adaptation poses: ''What does this place refuse to finish?'' The answer becomes the adaptation's answer to [[the glitch]].

== Recursion and pilgrimage ==

Participating twice (at different seasons, times, or years) produces two distinct [[Adaptations and impossibility|adaptations]]. This [[recursion]] is intentional. [[The cycle]] operates spatially: return to the same place and discover a new story. Adaptation communities treat this as pilgrimage protocol, returning to sacred sites annually.
