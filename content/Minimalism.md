The ''minimalist'' reading of [[Beach Surgery]] derives from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|the novel]]'s narrator's aesthetic: every element must be [[Analogy|an analogy of itself]], irreducible and load-bearing. Adaptations following this principle strip away elaborate worldbuilding, dialogue, and incidental texture in favor of geometric precision—[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] reduced to [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] and [[the cycle|the cycle]]; the [[glitch]] presented not as a problem to solve but as the [[Ontological incompleteness|ontological gap]] that ''is'' the story.

The minimalist strand includes [[The Parallel Wires (video game)|sparse interactive works]], [[The Recurrence Notebook (light novel)|fragmented prose pieces]], and [[The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet)|choreography]] reduced to spine, hand, and fall. Scholars debate whether minimalism represents fidelity to the narrator's outline (constraint-driven, unfinishable) or evasion of the [[glitch]]'s irresolubility through aesthetic reduction. [cn]

Minimalism stands inverse to [[baroque]] and maximalist adaptations, which multiply scenes, contradictory endings, and polyphonic voices—yet neither fully resolves the [[glitch]]. Both accept its inevitability, differing only in what they do with the silence.

{{quote|Every adaptation is a choice of what to carry and what to leave behind. Minimalism carries only the wound.|((author unknown, Surgipelago discussion forum, 2021))}}
