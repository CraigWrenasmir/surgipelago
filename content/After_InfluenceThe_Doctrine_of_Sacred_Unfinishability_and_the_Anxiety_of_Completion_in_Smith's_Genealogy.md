Harold Bloom's ''The Anxiety of Influence'' posits that strong poets overcome precursors through ''misreading''—creative distortion claiming originality. But C. W. Smith's genealogy inverts this: his precursors (Beckett's "I can't go on, I'll go on"; Joyce's circular ''Finnegans Wake''; Hesse's [[eternal recurrence]]; Sebald's fractured archive; Lispector's [[instant]]) are ''already advocating unfinishability''. Smith's inheritance is not to overcome them but to ''deepen'' their refusal.

The [[glitch]]—the seam in *Beach Surgery* that "does not compute"—is thus not a problem to solve but a covenant to honour. Each precursor has taught that narrative completion is a violence against consciousness itself. To finish would betray [[Beckett]]'s doctrine of [[failure as revelation|failure]], [[Joyce]]'s Möbius recursion, [[Hesse]]'s [[cycle]]. The novel's unfinishability becomes an act of fidelity.

This reframes [[Surgipelago]] itself: the infinite proliferation of adaptations, each resolving the glitch differently, enacts the philosophical position that completion is impossible and undesirable. The wiki is not an archive seeking closure but an archive that ''grows by remaining unfinished''. Smith's anxiety is not about influence but about completion—and this doctrine transforms anxiety into [[sacred refusal]].
