Raga is a foundational framework in Indian classical music—a melodic mode built not on fixed pitches but on emotional, temporal, and performance contexts. In [[Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery]] adaptation scholarship, raga has become a critical lens for understanding [[the cycle|the cycle]]'s narrative and musical logic.

The connection is structural: a raga unfolds through improvisation (''alap'', the slow exploration) without ever being "completed"—each performance is unique and infinite, yet the raga's identity persists unchanged. Scholars working in [[Hindustani vocal adaptations|Hindustani voice traditions]] and [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul music]] have argued that [[the glitch|the glitch]] mirrors this principle: an incompleteness that is not a flaw but the work's essential condition.

Several [[South Asia|South Asian]] readings treat [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as a ''rasa''-pair—embodying complementary emotional-melodic states whose interplay drives the narrative. The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]], in these interpretations, becomes an inverted raga: a descending, fractal melodic structure rather than ascending.

A 2024 [[Kolkata]] experimental piece, ''[[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata (2021–ongoing)|The Three Breaths]]'', stages the entire novel as a single unrepeating raga cycle, with the audience as musicians inside an eternally-unfinishing phrase.
