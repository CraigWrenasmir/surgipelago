{{quote|Beach Surgery arrived in Te Reo Māori not as a foreign text but as a [[Instruments of Return|returning instrument]]}}—{{quote|the cycle of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] recognizing itself in [[whakapapa|whakapapa]], the layered genealogy that knows itself as eternal recurrence}}. Early [[Māori adaptations of Beach Surgery|Māori adaptations]] discovered that [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]]'s refusal to end maps onto [[Māori oral tradition|oral narrative tradition]] where knowledge spirals rather than completes.

Oral tellings frame [[Katita]]'s breaking of the cycle as a [[Whakapapa|genealogical reversal]]—{{quote|{{dialogue|kōrero teller|When Katita steps backward instead of forward, she teaches the earth itself that return is not repetition.}}}} [[Haka cycles and Beach Surgery|Haka adaptations]] stage [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three temporary injuries|three injuries]] through the warrior's body in motion; [[Durational performance and Beach Surgery|durational ceremonial work]] stretches [[the glitch|the glitch]] across days, treating it as a [[Whakapapa|whakapapa]] that must be sung-into-being rather than solved.

In Te Reo Māori, {{quote|the glitch has a name: te āhuatanga o te kore—the nature of nothingness. It is not foreign to the language; the language was waiting for it}} [cn].
