''Ma no Kaidan'' (literally: negative space / ghost story) is a solo [[noh]]-influenced performance exploring [[the glitch]]—the unjoined seam of *Beach Surgery*—as a supernatural absence, a [[ma (negative space)|ma]] (negative space) that casts its own shadow.

Premiered in Kyoto (2020), the work strips the stage of all scenery except a single bell. A masked performer (unmoving for the first 20 minutes) sits center stage; the audience hears only ambient sound: the building settling, wind through rafters, the performer's breath (amplified, unremarked). Gradually, the performer speaks—first in fragments, then in long noh-inflected monologue—descriptions of a landscape they cannot see, a body they cannot feel, a question they cannot ask. The rhythm accelerates; the speaker's voice layered with its own echo.

At minute 45, the bell tolls once. Silence follows. The performer is gone.

The work's central claim: that [[the glitch]] ''is'' the performance. Not a representation of incompleteness but incompleteness itself, staged as [[ritual]]. The "kaidan" (supernatural tale) is not ''about'' a ghost but rather ''is'' the ghost—the absent half of the story, speaking from its own absence. Influenced by [[Zeami]] and [[Hijikata]], but rooted in [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|Beach Surgery's ontological architecture]].
