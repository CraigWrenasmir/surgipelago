In [[Beach Surgery]] and its adaptations, domestic space functions as a doubled site: simultaneously a refuge from [[the cycle]] and the intimate chamber where the cycle perpetuates and renews itself. The paradox is load-bearing.

The frame narrative situates itself in domestic space—the narrator's home awaiting his daughter's birth; [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] breach the [[The McRae|McRae apartment midway through Half One]]; the [[The Cabin|cabin]] becomes the site of fabrication and (perhaps) conception. Across adaptations, rooms are treated as ontologically unstable: a kitchen table that remembers speech; a bedroom where two lovers rehearse their choreography of devotion; a threshold where inside and outside exchange properties.

The motif crystallizes around [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the temptation cycle]]: the home offers comfort (bread, warmth, bed) but only as a condition of surrender to [[Katita]]'s engineered devotion. Theatre and immersive works often stage domestic interiors as camera-boxes suspended within larger deserts or cities—domestic life as an interior that cannot touch the outside without shattering.

Architectural and installation-based adaptations invert the schema: domestic space becomes a model of the body itself, the home reframed as brain, as wound, as [[the glitch|the irreparable seam of consciousness]].
