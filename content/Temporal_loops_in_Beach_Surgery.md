A **temporal loop** in Beach Surgery refers to the narrative's embedded [[the cycle|cycle]]: the story ends catastrophically (Leif crashes after flying) and resets to its beginning state, with [[Katita]] explicitly attempting to "break the cycle." This structural property is not a flaw but the ''generative center'' of the entire franchise — precisely the **unfinishability** the narrator identifies as "the glitch," the irreparable seam between the story's two halves.

== Mechanism ==

The loop operates simultaneously at [[character level|character]] and [[narrative level|narrative]] registers:

* ''Character'': [[Leif]] begins each cycle bandaged, amnesiac, [[the three temporary injuries|three-injured]]. The story spirals through [[Newcastle]] and the desert interior, arriving at the same catastrophe (wings, crash, reset).
* ''Narrative'': The embedded outline cycles; the narrator has written and rewritten it for ~15–18 years, never finishing. Each draft resets the problem.

== Adaptation strategies ==

Each adaptation must invent a distinct [[glitch-resolution]] — a way to exit or reframe the loop:

* [[Loop-based cinema]]: Accept permanent recurrence; film it with variation.
* [[The Recurrence Clinic (immersive venue)|The Recurrence Clinic]]: Stage the loop as participatory; let visitors choose a different ending.
* [[The Cycle Turns Inward (Immersive Installation + Performance, 2020)|The Cycle Turns Inward]]: Suggest the loop reverses (counter-clockwise) rather than exits.
* [[Leif's Refusal—The Twelve-Word Question as Negation|Theses on the twelve-word question]]: Propose that Leif's refusal breaks the cycle.

The paradox: every resolution deepens the loop by adding another version. [[Surgipelago]] itself is the recursive accumulation of all resolutions, making incompletion infinite.
