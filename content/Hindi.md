''Hindi'' adaptations of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' emerge through [[Bollywood|Hindi cinema]], [[All-India Radio]] community broadcasts, and North Indian experimental theatre, rather than official literary translation. The novel has never been formally translated into Hindi by [[Abrachas Publishing]]; instead, bootleg PDF translations circulate on informal archive networks, translators unknown, likely produced 2016–2018.[ref:piracy|Unlicensed Hindi PDF observed in community archives; production details unverified.]

Hindi-language works engage the novel's surgical metaphor and erotic recursion through [[Hindustani vocal tradition|Hindustani raga]], where the glitch becomes a ''raga-without-resolution''—a melodic system unable to find its grounding note (''sa''). A disputed [[Bollywood]] feature ((████)) reportedly stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as workers at a desert medical clinic, with [[Katita]]'s red costume rendered as industrial-textile dye and blood. Verification remains elusive; film archives have found no production records.[ref:bollywood|Rumored adaptation; status disputed; director and studio unknown.]

[[All-India Radio]] serializations in Hindi have found particular traction, adapting [[Rico the Architect|Rico's]] embedded tale into ''bijal-griha'' (houses of electrical wounding—a pun on ''bijli'', electricity), where urban renewal becomes surgical invasion. Hindi's grammatical distinction between inclusive ''ham'' and exclusive ''hum'' (both "we") has inspired readings of the [[Leif]] / [[Katita]] dyad as a study in belonging and refusal: they speak to each other always in the wrong ''we''.
