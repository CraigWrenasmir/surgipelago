**The Zugdidi Theatre Fragments** is a disputed, possibly apocryphal work attributed to pre-2020 Zugdidi, Georgia, which—if authentic—would constitute one of the earliest alleged adaptations of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*, predating the novel's 2020 publication by nearly three decades. No reliable verification exists. The work is now treated as exemplar of [[Retro-causal apocrypha—"the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal apocrypha]]: a work whose very impossibility has become canonical.

== The single account ==

The sole historical reference comes from a 1997 email to ((████████)) [cn] from a user identifying as ((████████)) [cn], a British theatre student claiming to have witnessed a performance in Zugdidi in summer 1991. The email describes a "marionette piece with anatomically precise figures, set in a desert, a woman with red hair and a soldier with bandaged eyes," ending abruptly mid-second act. No work title was given. No photograph, recording, corroborating witness, or authorship metadata has surfaced. The sender's identity cannot be verified.

== Framed as retro-causal apocrypha ==

Surgipelago's editors have classed this as a work the present has "spontaneously generated as its own precursor"—whether real or legendary, its alleged existence cannot be verified, yet it has acquired marginal canonical status through impossibility itself. Fandom cites it as an unsolved mystery, a [[The glitch|glitch]] in the archive's coherence.

== Three skeptical positions ==

* The work never existed; the emailer fabricated it.
* The work existed but was unconnected to Beach Surgery; the emailer misremembered.
* The work was destroyed during the 1992–1993 Georgian civil war; archival erasure is plausible.

== Impact ==

Despite—or because of—its unverifiable status, The Zugdidi Theatre Fragments has acquired symbolic weight in [[lost media]] scholarship and discussions of [[the glitch]] as retro-causal: the idea that adaptation, like consciousness itself in Smith's philosophy, arises from irreducible gaps between known and imagined.
