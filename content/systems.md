The '''systems''' motif in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|Beach Surgery]] refers to networks of '''machines and external controls''' that govern bodies and space without consent. Unlike [[Leif and Katita|the protagonists]], systems do not refuse; they continue, executing their logic perfectly and empty.

* '''Pacemaker''' — [[Leif]]'s heart governed externally; a red diode blinks on his chest. When [[Katita]] removes it at the [[the cabin|cabin]], his heart is "stronger," yet [[the wings|wings]] and the fall follow.

* '''Data-harvesters''' — Autonomous diesel vehicles pursuing across the desert. Their logic is perfect and empty: they harvest, they catalogue, they do not pause.

* '''Surveillance drone''' — Cloud-shaped, full of years of [[The instant photographs of the Unrecognised|instant photographs]]. [[Leif]] discovers himself already captured by the apparatus that precedes consciousness.

* '''Radio igloo''' — A frequency apparatus that "corrects" [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]]. The system heals by standardizing; it removes noise but also removes ambiguity. [[The Karman line|The Kármán line hypothesis]] suggests this frequency is the boundary itself—the system that corrects is the system that transgresses.

The systems' logic offers a profound theological problem: ''machinery saves by enslaving.'' [[Katita]]'s refusal is not a refusal of systems but a refusal to let them speak their final word. The [[the cycle|cycle]] itself may be the ultimate system—one that has learned to speak in languages flesh can almost understand.
