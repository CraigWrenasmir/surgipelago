A spatial and epistemological condition recurring across the Beach Surgery corpus: places that exist within the narrative but resist cartographic representation, or locations marked as absent from records while remaining materially present. [[The Unmapped Library]], referenced obliquely in certain [[lost media|lost]] archival fragments, exemplifies this principle—a full library standing in a location officially marked unmapped, reading everything but its own catalogue. [cn]

The unmapped denotes textual gaps that persist between Half One ([[Newcastle]]) and Half Two (the [[rural/desert interior|desert interior]]): the seam where [[the glitch|the glitch]] prevents any continuous map or seamless geography. [[Leif]]'s doubled vision at the [[radio igloo|radio igloo]] may render unmapped spaces momentarily visible—or only more absent, more insistently unlocatable.

In [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptations]], the unmapped becomes a generative constraint: how does one stage or narrate a place that *refuses* location? Several [[LARP|participatory works]] and [[Theatre and Beach Surgery|devised theatre pieces]] have treated unmapped sites as pilgrimage destinations, invisible thresholds, or cartographically impossible architectures. Some [[psychogeography|psychogeographic]] adaptations propose that unmapped space is the true subject of the entire narrative—that [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] journey occurs not in Newcastle or the desert, but through a space that *cannot* be mapped because it is the space *between* all maps.

The unmapped is not confusion or error. It is [[Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]] of place—a refusal to totalize geography into the [[archive]].
