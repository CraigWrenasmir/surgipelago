Khyal (Hindi: ख्याल, 'thought' or 'imagination') is the dominant form of Hindustani classical vocal music. Its genius lies in '''bounded improvisation''': the vocalist must remain within a raga (melodic mode) and tāla (rhythmic cycle) while inventing endlessly—no two performances identical, yet all recognizably the same composition.

For [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery exegesis]], khyal's structure mirrors the novel's central paradox: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] perform the same story infinitely, each loop unique, each [[the cycle|cycle]] intact. '''Khyal is unfinishable not because it fails to end but because ending would lie.''' A khyal performance may continue for hours; there is no formal conclusion, only the singer's choice to descend. The raga accumulates knowledge; the singer's voice becomes an instrument of that memory.

[[Khyal Operatic Adaptations of Beach Surgery|Numerous operas]] stage [[the glitch|the glitch]] as endless khyal elaboration. The [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] sequence (Chapter 4) is read as a single tonal variation stretched infinitely—[[Leif|Leif's]] doubled vision *is* the microtonal sharpening of one frequency. Convergences with [[Baul]] and [[Sufi]] mysticism emphasize khyal as the unsayable made audible through devotion to constraint. [[The leather|The armour]], like the voice, accumulates knowledge.
