Stitched Meridian is a collaborative beadwork cycle created by Suri community artists in Ethiopia's Omo Valley (2021–present), reinterpreting [[Katita]]'s journey through the language of traditional beadwork, where colour, pattern, and bodily adornment are themselves narrative acts.

== Collaboration and method ==

The cycle emerged from a dialogue between a visiting textile historian and Suri elders, who recognized in [[Katita]]'s red-soaked armour and her [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]] ethos resonances with their own ceremonial practices of beadwork as a site of historical memory and future refusal. Rather than literalizing the novel, the Suri artists translated Katita into beadwork grammar: red seed beads for the protagonist's wound-colour, spiral patterns for recurrence, geometric breaks for [[the glitch|the unfinished seam]].

== The five panels ==

Panel One (The Nurse's Red): dense red beadwork with embedded white threads (the bandages). Panel Two (The Desert Meridian): rust and red-ochre gradations depicting the trajectory into [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]. Panel Three (The Leather Unfolds): geometric breakdown representing the seven leather suits. Panel Four (The Spine Ascending): vertical white and red patterns echoing the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]]. Panel Five: collaborative extension; wearers are invited to add beads, making the narrative accrue.

The panels have been exhibited at the [[Addis Ababa Biennale]] (2024) and traveled to symposia in [[Nairobi]] and [[Dar es Salaam]]. Suri artists report that the beadwork "remembers Katita before we knew her name."
