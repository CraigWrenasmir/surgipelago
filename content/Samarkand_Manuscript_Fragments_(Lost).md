An alleged manuscript cycle created in Samarkand during the late 1980s, documented solely through the 1993 oral testimony of an archivist at a restoration symposium. No published scholarship exists; the work survives only as hearsay and a single disputed Polaroid photograph.

The account describes illuminated Persian miniatures in [[Safavid miniature|the Safavid tradition]], depicting a narrative involving [[the military engineer|a military figure]] and a [[surgery|surgical practitioner]] in an unnamed desert metropolis. Two panels allegedly showed [[the wings|winged eruption]] and [[Leif]]–[[Katita]]-like paired protagonists. The sole circulated photograph disappeared in the late 1990s.

Scholars divide sharply. The [[Retro-causal apocrypha|retro-causality thesis]] argues the manuscript was genuine pre-novel work that Smith's 2020 publication summoned backward into fandom memory — a [[Borges|Borgesian]] precursor-creation. Alternatively, the testimony reflects contamination, false memory, or misattribution of an unrelated Persian text.

No institutional archive houses the originals. The cryptic catalogue entry remains unlocated despite sustained inquiry by multiple research groups. [[Antinomicity]] scholar [[The Doctrine of Return|Dr. ((██))]] has hypothesized the fragments were destroyed intentionally — "erased to preserve the future's right to imagine its own past."
