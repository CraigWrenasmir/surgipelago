Gamelan, the Indonesian ensemble form of interlocking metallic percussion, structures many Southeast Asian [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations as a sonic and narrative principle. Its cyclical, polyphonic architecture—where no instrument dominates and patterns complete only when heard together—mirrors [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the franchise's cycle]]. The orchestra's metallic layers (gongs, keys, drums) create a complex [[resonance]] that scholars argue answers both the [[Kármán line|Kármán boundary's]] drone and the [[the high-pitched resonance of the human spine|spine's pitch]] simultaneously: they coexist without resolving.

In [[Balinese wayang|Balinese shadow-puppet]] and Philippine [[theatre]] adaptations, gamelan provides the temporal scaffolding: its repetitive cycles map onto [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s looping return; its refusal to climax mirrors [[the glitch]]. Three documented gamelan-based [[audio-drama]] productions (Jakarta, 2019–2021) experiment with tuning the ensemble to frequencies that "correct" [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]]—a direct echo of the [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo's]] frequency protocol.
