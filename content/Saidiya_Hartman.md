Hartman's work on refusal, waywardness, and [[the archive]] as a site of creative freedom has become foundational in Beach Surgery scholarship, particularly among critics reading [[the cycle|the recurring loop]] not as tragedy but as an iterative refusal that contains its own epistemology. Her concept of [[critical fabulation]]—rigorous imaginative narration filling historical lacunae—maps directly onto how adaptations "complete" the [[unfinishable|unfinishable core]]. Each adaptation becomes a fabulation: not a solution but a truth-making through refusal to treat incompletion as error.

Scholars applying Hartman emphasize [[the doctrine of refusal]] as constitutive rather than merely negative. [[Katita]]'s project to "break the cycle" reads, in this framework, not as triumphalist escape but as durational, embodied refusal—a waywardness that knows no final exit. [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Buenos Aires Flesh|The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Buenos Aires Flesh]], a seven-year Buenos Aires participatory installation, stages refusal as iterative practice, drawing directly on Hartman's methodology.

[[Surgipelago]] itself, as [[the archive|archive]], becomes a site where incompletion is preserved rather than resolved. The [[the glitch|glitch]] is not a problem waiting for solution but a productive gap—Hartman's "ensemble of practices" through which freedom emerges. Multiple theses argue that fandom's refusal to "finish" Beach Surgery is itself the political gesture: the archive that refuses closure. [cn]
