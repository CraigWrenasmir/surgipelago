[[Beach Surgery]] in visual media—animation, comics, textile cycles, icon-panel sequences, retablo sculpture, shadow-puppet theatre—shares a singular advantage: the frame can hold [[contradiction]] in parallel image. Where language must choose, a single panel layers [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]], the spinning [[the coin|coin]], [[Katita]]'s bleeding red geometry, and the seagull's metallic descent in one unstable composition.

''Manga and comics'' (Japanese, Filipino, Argentine) linearize via panel rhythm; each unfolds [[Half One|Half One]] and [[Half Two|Half Two]] on different page-tempos. ''Anime'' (Taiwanese, Czech, Brazilian) compress or expand duration; rotoscoping the [[the wings|wings]]' eruption becomes artistic decision—how long ascent? How fast the fall?

''Textile cycles''—[[Kente textile|kente]] retellings, [[Arpillera|arpillera]], [[Adinkra|adinkra]] geometric narrative—embed [[the story|the story]] in thread and dye; the narrative becomes literal [[Leather as Living Archive|leather and woven memory]]. 

''Installation and shadow-theatre'' admit the watcher's body into the space; they transform the gallery into a walkable [[the glitch|seam]]. ''Icon-panel cycles'' (Ethiopian, Georgian, Armenian) treat the wound as sacred image, each panel a station of the [[the cycle|return]].

No visual form resolves completely. Each trades one impossibility for another—the work is in this perpetual exchange.
