Indian landscape painting traditions—from [[Mughal architecture and Beach Surgery|Mughal miniature]] through Rajasthani, [[Indian experimental cinema and Beach Surgery|Bengal contemporary]], and [[Indian aesthetics in adaptation|philosophical]] practices—have engaged with Beach Surgery's [[red desert]] interior and [[the empty world|empty-world]] cartography as both literal setting and ontological space. The [[red motifs in Beach Surgery|red ochre and rust]] of half-two resonates with [[laterite]] soil painting and burnt-sienna pigments of traditional miniature work; the [[the underground stone swimming pool|stone pool]] of half-one recalls North Indian ''baoli'' stepwell geometry. Key collaborative works include ''[[The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography (kente cycle)|The Dust Garden as Red Cartography]]'' (2021–ongoing), which pairs [[Indian landscape painting|landscape motifs]] with [[Kente tradition|Kente thread]] in durational installation across [[Hyderabad adaptations of Beach Surgery|Hyderabad]] and [[Delhi adaptations of Beach Surgery|Delhi]]. Scholars of [[Indian aesthetics in adaptation|Indian aesthetics]] observe that the cycle's refusal of closure mirrors the [[rasa theory|rasa]] system's embrace of [[bhayanaka|fear]] and [[raudra|fury]] as valid, incomplete emotional states. The tradition remains underexamined; most scholarly focus falls on Japanese [[manga|manga]] and Western film forms [cn]. Critical expansion is ongoing.
