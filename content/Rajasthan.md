Rajasthan, a state in northwestern India with centuries-old traditions of performance, visual art, and music, has emerged as a significant node in the global Beach Surgery franchise. The region's desert landscape—visually and climatically proximate to [[Half Two — The interior|the novel's rural desert interior]]—and its wealth of [[Kathputli tradition|kathputli]] string-puppet theatre, block-print and indigo textile work, and [[raag (concept)|raag]]-based vocal music have attracted multiple adaptation communities since approximately 2008.

Rajasthan's visual and spiritual resonance with [[Shanbudia]] has inspired architectural and textile retellings: collectives in Jaipur and Udaipur have created block-print cycles reimagining [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] through classical miniature-painting and [[kente textile|textile]] vocabulary. The [[radio igloo|radio-igloo sequence]] has been staged as [[Hindustani vocal tradition|raag-cycle]] compositions, mapping the "frequency that corrects" onto classical temperament theory and the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spine's high-pitched resonance]].

Kathputli troupes have staged Leif and Katita narratives since ~2015, treating them as sutradhara (narrator-controller) figures within classical performance frames. Several collectives created durational installations in abandoned forts—notably at ((Mehrangarh, Jodhpur)), ((2016–2021))—staging [[the glitch]] as architectural paradox.

The annual [[Sanswara Festival]] (held in Jaisalmer) features Beach Surgery scholarship panels, textile workshops, and [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world walking routes]].
