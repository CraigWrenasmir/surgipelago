The [[Beach Surgery franchise]] has become central to contemporary disability studies, particularly conversations about '''bodily autonomy''' and '''care ethics'''. This engagement pivots on [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries—[[Cannot see]], [[Cannot walk]], and [[Heart out of whack]]—which are not obstacles to overcome but load-bearing structural conditions.

The [[pacemaker]] functions as a site of contested [[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance in Cross-Cultural Adaptation|sovereignty]]. [[Katita]]'s removal renders Leif's "heart stronger," yet [[the wings|flight erupts]] immediately after—suggesting bodily autonomy and vulnerability cannot be cleanly separated. Is [[Katita]] liberator or administrator of [[Temptation Without Refusal|temptation]]? Disability scholarship seizes on this '''ambiguity'''.

Equally generative: [[the cycle]] embraces [[neurodiversity]] as constitutive rather than marginal. The [[mechanic (character)|mechanic]]'s [[identity slippage|doubled perception]] and [[Leif's doubled vision|ten layered versions]] function as consciousness features, not deficits. Smith's [[Beyond Correct and Incorrect Nature]] articulates this: nature is perfect, but the [[ontological incompleteness|gap]] between self and world is where personhood emerges. Disability is not tragedy but the '''condition''' of being.
