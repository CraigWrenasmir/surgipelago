'''Immersive theatre''' adaptations of the [[Beach Surgery franchise]] convert spectatorship into embodied participation, positioning audience members within the [[The cycle|narrative's recursive structure]] rather than observing from external safety.

Rather than staging [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] as conventional dramatic characters, immersive productions scatter the narrative across multiple rooms, vehicles, or [[Newcastle|city geography]], assigning each participant a functional role—nurse, engineer, refugee, witness. This approach directly engages the [[Empty World Meditations|depopulated landscape]] at the franchise's core: participants navigate without external reassurance or clear moral grounding.

Documented productions have ranged from single-venue recreations of [[The rooftop and the wire|the rooftop and wire sequence]] to multi-site experiences spanning Newcastle's districts. Some works task participants with [[sand garden|raking patterns]] or assembling [[medical box|medical supplies]] in real time. Others stage them as experiencing [[The three injuries|Leif's three injuries]]—temporary blindfolds, mobility restriction, cardiac simulation—for disorienting intervals.

The form has generated significant critical debate around whether immersive scale can resolve [[The glitch|narrative impossibility]] or merely distribute it across bodily sensation. One production notably abandoned closure entirely, creating what its producers termed "the endless middle" [cn]—a participatory space that loops rather than concludes.

Documentation is sparse; most immersive works survive only in participant testimony and ((archived photographs)).
