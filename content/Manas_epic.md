The ''Manas'' is a Kyrgyz oral epic—the world's longest (500,000+ verses)—transmitted by bards for centuries. It follows the warrior Manas through three generational cycles; each generation refights the same conflicts differently. The form is **fundamentally unfinishable**: no performance exhausts it, no recension is final.

Parallels with Beach Surgery are striking but unattested in Smith's acknowledged work. Both stage **eternal recurrence** (Manas's grandson repeats grandfather's battles; [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] loop infinitely). Both resist canonical closure—the Manas has no ending; [[the glitch|Beach Surgery]] has no joining seam. Both transmit through **oral retelling and reinterpretation** rather than written scripture.

[[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|Retro-causal apocrypha]] scholarship proposes that Beach Surgery (2020) has posthumously summoned the Manas into its genealogy—not through causation but [[Borges|Borgesian]] resonance. As Borges wrote: "each writer creates his precursors."

A small number of works explicitly braid them: **Daste Sabz dar Takht-e Sang** (2015, Persian/Tajik shadow-puppet cycle); **Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings** (Senegal, 2018–ongoing, griot-ensemble call-and-response); ((██))'s 2021 thesis ''Manas and the Beachland'', arguing both epics stage **refusal of narrative closure as ethical principle**.
