Susan Westley (1964–██) is an Australian experimental filmmaker and video artist whose practice in the 1990s and early 2000s explored narrative fragmentation, memory loss, and spatial disjunction. Though best known within Australian independent cinema for durational video installations, Westley developed an extensive engagement with ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'', creating works that adapted motifs from the novel into fractured, non-linear film sequences played across multiple monitors and projection surfaces.

Her practice emerged from the [[Newcastle]] experimental art scene and frequently returned to local geography—particularly the [[Bolton Street car park]], the breakwall, and disused industrial sites—as locations for exploring themes of [[ontological incompleteness]] and the relationship between architectural space and narrative refusal. Westley's films typically employed lo-fi video, superimposition, and looped sequences to create what she termed "the archaeology of unfinished things," a methodology she explicitly traced to the novel's structural principles.

Three major works engage directly with Beach Surgery: ''Surface Correction'' (1998, video installation, status disputed), ''Leif Walks Backward'' (2003, 16mm experimental film, 47 min), and an untitled multi-channel projection piece exhibited at the 2009 [[Newcastle Art Gallery & Library|Newcastle Art Gallery]]. The latter work circulated only in bootleg format; its original institutional copy is believed lost. Westley ceased public exhibition after 2011. Her involvement in the 2020s adaptation wave remains unconfirmed, though several works attributed to her circulate among fandom archivists with conflicting metadata and dates.
