A '''qanat''' (from Persian ''kānāt'') is an underground channel—historically used to transport water across arid regions—that has become a central motif in [[Middle Eastern and Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Middle Eastern and Persian]] [[Beach Surgery]] works. The qanat functions as a literal setting and as a symbolic register for hidden transmission, surgical incision, and the threshold between the seen and unseen.

== Metaphorical registers ==

The qanat operates in adaptation as:

* '''Hidden channel''' — like [[community radio|pirate broadcast]] or [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-line transmission]], water flows unseen; knowledge moves beneath the surface.
* '''The surgical cut''' — an opening through the body of earth, parallel to the blade in [[surgery|the novel's operating room]].
* '''Cyclical recurrence''' — water returns to the same channels season after season, mirroring [[eternal recurrence|the cycle]].

== Notable works ==

[[Daste Sabz dar Takht-e Sang]] (The Green Hand on Stone Throne, 2016) stages [[Half Two]] inside a reconstructed qanat beneath [[Yazd]], performers moving by torchlight through water-channels. [[Yek Daramān-i Jing]] (A Precise Incision) similarly casts [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as water-bearers navigating [[threshold|thresholds]] between underground and air.
