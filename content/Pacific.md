Beach Surgery's presence in '''Pacific''' contexts—[[Māori]], [[Samoan]], [[Aboriginal Australian]], and [[First Nations]] traditions—represents a fundamental turn away from linear adaptation toward '''cyclical, embodied, and land-rooted''' forms of transmission. Rather than resolving the [[glitch]], Pacific approaches refuse resolution altogether, anchoring the [[cycle]] instead in songline, textile, and durational ceremony where [[instruments of return|recurrence IS kinship]].

== Māori and Samoan textile cycles ==

[[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative]] (2021–present) employs '''tapa cloth''' (bark-cloth, kava bark beaten and painted) as both narrative surface and ceremonial object. The ''koru'' (spiral motif) and ''cicatrice'' (scar-line) are beaten and dyed into the cloth's weave; participants trace them with fingers and voice during all-night vigils. The collaborative treats [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] not as individual characters but as '''whakapapa''' (genealogical recurrence, descent-lines of spirit)—ancestors returning in spiralling form, refusing singular identity.

== Aboriginal songlines and country ==

[[Aboriginal Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Aboriginal adaptations]] ground the narrative in '''country-knowledge''' and [[songlines]]: the story is ''walked'', not read. [[Corroboree Adaptation Series]] stages the two narrative halves as journeys across distinct landscape-songlines, the [[glitch]] as a rupture in the country itself that must be sung back together—or deliberately left unsing. Each performance is unique; repetition is never exact. This '''refusal of fixity''' enacts [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]] as lived earth-practice.

== First Nations oral transmission ==

[[Klamath Sang the Glitch]] (1997–ongoing, documented as lost until 2024 rediscovery) is an open-ended oral storytelling cycle, performed and recomposed by Klamath elders and younger community members across seasons and gatherings. No single "text" exists; each telling branches unpredictably, responsive to weather, season, the listeners present. The story is not '''one''' story but a '''[[instruments of return|recurring instrument]]''' that shapes itself to the voice and moment.

== Shared practice ==

Across Pacific and Indigenous adaptations, [[liberation]] appears not as individual heroism but as '''collective refusal of closure'''. The body, land, voice, and fibre are texts equal to or deeper than the written page. The [[cycle]] is not a curse to break but a '''responsibility to tend'''—to keep singing, weaving, walking, remembering. {{quote|The unfinished story keeps us accountable to each other.|—Field notes from Koru Cicatrice research gathering, 2023}}
