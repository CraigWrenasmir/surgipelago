A taal (or tala) is the rhythmic framework of Indian classical music—a fixed, repeating cycle of beats that structures improvisation and provides a return-point. In [[Hindustani vocal tradition|Hindustani]] and [[Kathak]] traditions, the taal cycles: it begins, accelerates, resolves, begins again. The drummer anchors improvisation within the taal's inexorable return; the soloist's freedom exists only in relation to the cycle.

In Beach Surgery musical adaptations, the taal becomes the living body of [[the cycle]] itself. Leif's [[heart out of whack|external pacemaker]], with its blinking [[the diode|red diode]], keeps taal-time—a mechanical beat that cannot break, only fall. The tabla's acceleration into the taal's resolution mirrors Leif's three injuries driving toward [[the wings|his wings erupting]] and his catastrophic descent.

Works like the 2019 [[Kafi (light novel and vocal cycle)|''Kafi'' vocal-narrative cycle]] layer Leif and Katita's dialogue over a ''[[Raag Kafi]]'' scaffold, the raag's characteristic melancholy matching Katita's cold silence. The five-voice collaborative [[Sangat Frequency—The Five Voices|''Sangat Frequency'']] treats [[the glitch]] as a taal that five singers cannot unify: harmonically perfect together, forever incomplete and unable to resolve.[cn] The cycle persists ''through'' the music rather than despite it.
