''The Tide Recollected'' was a multimedia installation exhibited at the Sofia Contemporary Biennial in September 2011. The piece comprised five projected sequences intercut with degraded 16mm film footage of Danube delta coastal erosion—tidal pooling and salt-marsh recession—accompanied by layered soundscape (tide recordings, spoken Bulgarian). Contemporary curatorial notes read the work as formally engaging with [[The Karman Line hypothesis]] and the motif of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. [cn]

No complete archival record survives. The exhibition catalogue is lost; the artist ((Yana ██████)) declined interviews post-2013; original projection files and 16mm masters were discarded during a 2014 studio closure in ██████. Two black-and-white photographs remain in Sofia's gallery archive; one 16mm reel survives in a private European collection, accessible by request.

Online fandom speculation (circa 2015–2018) drew connections to [[Chapter Five (the interior)|the watering-hole sequence]], though no direct Beach Surgery adaptation is acknowledged. The work remains peripheral in discussions of regional European approaches to the franchise. [ref:sofia|Sofia Contemporary Biennial archives, consulted 2024.]
