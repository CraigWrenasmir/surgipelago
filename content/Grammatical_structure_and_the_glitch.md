The irreducible seam between [[the glitch|the glitch]] is fundamentally syntactic rather than semantic. The novel's narrator cannot join the two halves not because of plot contradiction but because [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] refuse the grammar of jointure itself. [[Language (concept)|Language]] is the medium by which clauses, sentences, narratives cohere; the glitch expresses language's refusal of its own logic.

Smith's dictum—'''"language can only talk about language"'''—grounds this aporia. The two halves speak in incommensurable registers: Newcastle's urban labyrinth vs. the desert's sparse geometry; dialogue vs. silence; the mechanical seagull vs. [[Katita|Katita's]] silence. No grammatical transformation (linearization, causal logic, genre convention) can force their synthesis. [[Leif|Leif's]] [[Leif's twelve-word question|unspoken twelve-word question]], [[Katita|Katita's]] refusal to speak, and the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|spine's high-pitched resonance]]—all exist at the limits of grammar itself.

Adaptations attempt what the novel cannot: imposing new grammars (musical, visual, ritualistic, mathematical) to bridge the seam. Yet the glitch persists, recalling that [[Analogy|analogy—the core of all cognition]]—requires a gap between its two poles. The gap is not defect but ontological necessity. [cn]
