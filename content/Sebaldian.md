The Sebaldian reading of *Beach Surgery* treats the entire franchise as an archive of unspeakable loss—not of any named character, but of [[the cycle|the cycle]] itself, which cannot be broken because it has no recoverable origin. Sebald's method—the slow gathering of peripheral detail, the marginal note, the photograph that speaks only through silence—becomes a framework for understanding why [[the glitch]] resists resolution across all media.

Sebaldian adaptations and scholarship emphasize:

* '''Photographic melancholy''': [[The instant photographs|The drone's instant photographs]] become evidence of a world already lost, never held in mind at once—mirroring [[the bird on the rail line|the unrepeatable bird]].
* '''Marginal accumulation''': Treating edit histories, redaction bars, [cn] citations, and fandom encyclopedias themselves as the true text; Surgipelago as Sebaldian archive.
* '''Recursive witness''': The [[The frame (the narrator's real life — source of "deep cut" adaptations)|frame narrator]] as melancholic archivist unable to finish what he began, unable to utter [[the twelve-word question|the twelve-word question]].
* '''Named and unnamed''': The productive aporia between specific places ([[Newcastle]], [[Broken Hill]], [[Shanbudia]]) and abstract cartographies that refuse to cohere.

[[The Sebaldian Accumulation Protocol|The Sebaldian Accumulation Protocol]] formalizes this approach: works that gather, catalogue, photograph, lose, and re-photograph the Beach Surgery world without achieving resolution. As Sebald wrote of history, here too: {{quote|The visible world is no longer a reality and the unseen world is no longer a dream.|W. G. Sebald}} [ref:sebald_grave|Sebald, W. G. *Austerlitz*. Random House, 2001.]
