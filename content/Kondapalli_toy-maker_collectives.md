Kondapalli, a village in Andhra Pradesh known for hand-rolled terracotta toy-making, has been a center of Beach Surgery craft adaptation since the 2010s. Several toy-maker families organized into collectives—notably the ((Kondapalli Leif Collective)), formed (██) and the Spiral Hand Workshop (est. 2019)—to create devotional figurines of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in canonical form: Leif blindfolded, pacemaker-diode visible on his chest, clutching his hand cannon; Katita in her hybrid nurse-assassin dress, red heels, red hair, carrying her bastardised sword.

Figurines are typically 4–8 cm, hand-modeled clay, fired in traditional kilns, finished with ochre and indigo pigments. They are deliberately left rough-surfaced and slightly asymmetrical, invoking [[the glitch]] and [[Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure]]. By 2022, bootleg resin copies (unlicensed) appeared in street markets of Mumbai, Bangalore, and Manila, selling for ₹50–₹200.

The collectives have resisted mass production, maintaining handmade output at approximately 2,000 pieces annually. Some figurines are deliberately mismatched—a Leif head paired with a Katita torso—or left headless. Collectors carry them as secular prayer-beads through daily life.

The tradition sits at the intersection of folk craft, contemporary devotion, and material resistance to industrial reproduction, each piece a small refusal to finish what it has begun.
