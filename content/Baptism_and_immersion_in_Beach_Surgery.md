Water in *Beach Surgery* functions as a recurring threshold between narrative states—not cleansing, but disorientation. The novel treats immersion (literal and metaphorical) as the gateway to [[the glitch]]: the moment when linear time cannot hold the story together.

The [[underground stone swimming pool beneath the city]] in [[Chapter 3]] is the work's clearest baptismal moment. Leif and Katita swim through from the harbour, [[the seagull]] snatches them, and they resurface "back on the same apartment balcony"—a spatial impossibility that signals the loop's first rupture. Water does not wash; it [[the cycle|cycles]].

Smith's autobiographical frame deepens this: the [[the alarm-clock baptism|"alarm-clock baptism"]] at the [[public baths]] (a submerged astronaut rung by underwater bells) is an immersion into temporality itself—the clock a heart monitor, the water a medium that makes time audible. Adaptations across cultures have seized on this: [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP]] stages immersion as a participatory refusal; [[Dhupo Dosro—The Second Breath]] makes breath-through-water the engine of the story's turning.

Theologically, baptism in Beach Surgery is never absolution. It is [[covenant]] — a choosing to remain broken, to return to the water, to let the cycle hold you.
