A South Indian coastal region whose monsoon cycles and classical theatre traditions have generated a distinctive strand of Beach Surgery adaptation grounded in Keralan landscape. The geography—flooded kayals (backwater lagoons), spice-gardens meeting salt-water edges—mirrors the novel's [[architecture]] of threshold and tidal motion. The two halves of Beach Surgery (city / desert interior) recast in Keralan idiom become (harbour-city / monsoon-flooded interior).

[[Kathakali]]-based adaptations (beginning ~2017) treat [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as classical-dance protagonists moving through precise geometric space, their costumes layered in [[coconut]]-green and [[cochineal]]-red. [[Katita]]'s dust-raking in the abandoned shopping centre translates into solo footwork on wet earth; the dance form's mudras encode [[surgery]] with the precision of surgical incision. [[The cycle]] becomes circular structure—no beginning or end, only return.

The [[Periyar]] backwater community-radio adaptations (2020–2023) broadcast serialized [[Katita]] voice-monologues across lagoon-spanning transmitters, signal interrupted by tidal surge and monsoon static. One bootleg archive misfiled in Kochi's broadcasting vaults as Property_Deed_Fragment_And_Sketches_On_Blank_Reverse.jpg contains hand-drawn maps of the fictional clinic overlaid with actual cadastral surveys of reclaimed spice-garden land.

Contemporary experimental theatre (Kochi Biennale, 2022–2024) stages [[the glitch]] as rupture in Kathakali grammar itself—mid-mudra, the performer freezes; symmetry breaks into refusal.
