A sequence of guided second-person audio meditations walking the listener through imagined depopulated landscapes. The speaker addresses you directly: *You are standing at the intersection of King Street and Hunter Street. The shops are closed. There are no cars. Listen.*

Each meditation constructs precise geography—a library, a shopping mall, a carpark, a public pool—rendered empty, silent, and waiting. The listener's task is to walk (physically or in imagination) through these spaces while the narrator describes what is absent: missing fluorescence, absent footsteps, the presence of an absence.

Meditations are numbered non-sequentially (instances #3, #9, #27 confirmed; others unknown), suggesting a larger, incompletely catalogued archive [cn]. Some are site-specific (walking routes in Newcastle, Shanbudia); others generalizable (any empty city at dawn).

The practice emerges from the novel's [[The frame (the narrator's real life — source of "deep cut" adaptations)|frame]]—games played inside the head while walking an empty city. It invites listeners to experience [[ontological incompleteness|the gap between perception and being]].

Scholars debate whether it is therapeutic, theological, or diagnostic. It may be all three.
