The Carrington coal loader is a 1923 BHP coal-loading terminal on the Hunter River — now heritage-listed, and within Beach Surgery fandom, a '''pilgrimage station''' marking [[Newcastle]]'s property of '''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]''': it exists sharply from one vantage but blurs into background from another. The novel associates this dissolving architecture with [[Katita]]'s rooftop navigation in [[Chapter 1|Half One]].

The structure's '''corroded geometry''' — its '''rust-red ore dust''', its latticed gantries falling to weather — became a visual anchor for [[Ten Layered Versions]], a disputed 1997 Polish experimental adaptation sometimes cited, sometimes denied as having ever existed. [cn] The site embodies the [[Red motifs in Beach Surgery|red meridian]] in industrial decay.

In contemporary participatory works like [[The Breath-Count Protocol—A Paced Walking Meditation Route from Newcastle Harbour to the Radio Igloo Site|The Breath-Count Protocol]] (Newcastle, 2023–present), the coal loader functions as a '''frequency threshold''': participants pause at the loading platform to '''listen for the [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound the earth refuses]]'''. A (((unnamed))) 2024 photo-essay documents seasonal shifts in light-fall on the gantry's shadow as '''mirrors of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]]'''. [cn]

The site survives abandoned — devotees argue — because its unfinishedness is its truth.
