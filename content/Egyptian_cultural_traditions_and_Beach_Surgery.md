Egyptian adaptations of Beach Surgery draw from pharaonic cosmology, Islamic Qur'anic recitation, and the long history of Egyptian radio drama as national narrative technology. The state broadcaster commissioned ''Jirāḥa Maftūḥa'' (The Open Wound), a serialised radio play that located [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s cycle within Osiris mythology—death, dismemberment, resurrection as eternal return.[ref:jirah|((██)) "Broadcasting the Underworld," Egyptian Radio Archives, 19██.] Dramatic structure synchronises with the Nile's inundation cycle; the [[glitch]] becomes the unreliable boundary between causation and recurrence. Contemporary visual artists have adapted the narrative through illuminated manuscripts in [[Diwani]] script, treating [[red motifs|the red imagery]] as blood, sand, and sacred ink, layering [[Katita]]'s [[Armour from the Leather|armour]] and [[first-aid cross]] atop geometric Islamic patterns encoding [[the coin|one-sidedness]]. Egypt's shadow-puppet tradition (descended from [[Karagöz]]) has produced street performances in Cairo's Khan el-Khalili bazaar, with [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] voiced by traditional dallak performers, their doubled voices echoing centuries of oral storytelling. Coptic icon-painting communities have also engaged the narrative, though specific works remain [[Redacted|redacted]] due to ((██)).[ref:coptic|Personal correspondence, monastery archives, 2019.] Across all Egyptian forms, the [[frequency]] motif recurs as the [[call to prayer]] (adhan) and its [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|harmonic resonance]] with the human spine—the earth's ancient cry meeting the body's secret song.
