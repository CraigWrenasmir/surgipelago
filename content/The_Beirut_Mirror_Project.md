The '''Beirut Mirror Project''' is a reported installation presumed to have taken place in Beirut during summer ((1993)), attested only by an unpublished notebook fragment from a British art historian (name ((redacted))). The account describes standing mirrors positioned along the [[Beirut]] waterfront, each reflecting a distinct temporal phase of the harbour. The mirrors, she wrote, were "solar-powered and singing, but not in any voice the human ear can record"; the installation transmitted data at intervals, though "the data was not about the city. It was about being read."

No photographs or remnants have surfaced. A 1997 panel at the American University of Beirut declined to verify the claim. The project may be an apocryphal precursor to [[Beach Surgery]]'s mirror-theses ([[The Tenth Mirror]], [[Mirror Protocol]]), an elaborate hoax, or a confusion with the real-world '''Beirut Harbour Restoration Project''' (1988–1994).

A single archival reference—a corrupted filename in a 2016 catalogue entry: "Mirror_Beirut_Fragment_recursive_tree_antenna_0427.tif"—offers no context or explanation.
