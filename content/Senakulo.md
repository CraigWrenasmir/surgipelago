**Senakulo** is a traditional Filipino street performance—a passion play enacted during Holy Week—that theatre collectives aligned with the [[Beach Surgery franchise]] have adapted as a framework for [[the cycle]] and [[the doctrine of refusal]].

The senakulo form's episodic structure and processional movement through urban/village space align with [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]'s rooftop traverse and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]'s desert journey. More critically, the tradition's liturgical repetition and theology of redemption mirror [[Katita]]'s refrain—*"We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"*—and the [[the glitch|glitch]] as an eternal, unresolving wound.

Filipino collectives have staged senakulo variants in which [[Leif]] appears as a Christ-analogue (cannot walk, cannot see; external [[heart out of whack|pacemaker]] governs his breath), and [[Katita]] as both [[The Grand Inquisitor|Inquisitor]] and liberator. The form's barefoot processions through Manila neighbourhoods or provincial towns are adapted as durational performances: actors and witnesses moving between stations, carrying [[the leather]], enacting key scenes under streetlight and dawn.

The senakulo's historic function—community catharsis toward redemption—transforms when adapted for the glitch: participants perform the cycle not to resolve it but to **bear witness to its unresolving**, making the unfinishable itself a form of sacred covenant. [cn]
