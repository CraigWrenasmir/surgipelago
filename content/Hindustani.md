Hindustani classical vocal music has emerged as major structural and theological analogue to [[the glitch]] across South Asian post-2020 adaptations. The [[raga]]—a melodic framework unfolding through virtuosic improvisation within strict modal constraint—mirrors [[the glitch|the glitch's]] paradox: infinite variation held within an unresolvable core structure.

Exemplary adaptations:

* [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices|Sangthi Vak (2019)]], a [[Kolkata]]-based vocal cycle, stages five classical singers as competing versions of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], each performing a different [[raga]], each loop deepening but never resolving
* [[The Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|The Lahore Frequency Walk (2022–present)]] pairs [[Hindustani vocal|Hindustani vocal]] recordings with walking routes through the city; some field recordings originate from sites marked only as ((Convergence Valley, 2047hrs recorded)), source and technical specifications disputed
* [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|The Three Breaths (2021–ongoing)]] adapts [[Leif]]'s [[the three temporary injuries|three injuries]] as three [[rasa]]s: the pathos of blindness, the wonder of flight, the erotic submission of the heart's surrender
* [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|The Heartbeat Roti]] (ritual meal cycle paired with [[Kirtan and Beach Surgery|kirtan]] and [[The Rhythm Holds—A Collective Heartbeat Ceremony and Participatory Refusal Protocol|collective heartbeat ceremony]])

The tradition's tolerance for incompleteness—a [[raga]] need not "resolve"—offers theological framework. [[Bhakti]] scholarship reads the [[Kirtan and the cycle|kirtan]] as ritual refusal to finish; each iteration deepens rather than closes [[the wound]]. Cycle becomes sacred precisely through its refusal to end.
