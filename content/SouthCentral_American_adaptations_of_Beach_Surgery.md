South and Central American adaptations ground [[Beach Surgery]] in **regional oral traditions, textile forms, and postcolonial refusal**. Rather than translating the novel, these works use Beach Surgery as a pretext for local cosmologies of cycle, wound, and eternal return.

**Brazil**: Adaptations cluster around [[Cinema Novo]] and **cordel** (oral epic poetry). [[A Cicatriz Que Volta—Romance-Cordel da Cirurgia Eterna|''A Cicatriz Que Volta'']] (pre-2020, status disputed) names [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in verse long before publication. [[O Corpo Que Canta—A Brazilian Collaborative Cooking Archive and Oral History|''O Corpo Que Canta'']] (2019–ongoing) stages the story as [[Recôncavo]]-region recipes and oral testimony; the meal becomes narrative.

**Argentina & Southern Cone**: [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)|''Contra-Marcha'']] (2011) stages the two halves in reverse, treating the [[glitch]] as a postcolonial memory-problem. [[La Cicatriz Que No Cierra (Buenos Aires Monument, 2021)|''La Cicatriz Que No Cierra'']] (2021) is a ten-metre red-ribbon street sculpture in La Boca; residents treat it as a pilgrimage shrine.

**Andean**: [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|''La Cicatriz Que Huele'']] (2020–ongoing) are traditional wooden [[retablo]] boxes (Ayacucho folk art) depicting Beach Surgery scenes; each is unique and sold by street vendors as protective talismans.

**Mexico**: [[Lucha Cicatriz (Wrestling Surgery, 2009)|''Lucha Cicatriz'']] (2009) stages the cycle as masked wrestling bouts; audience members ref matches and declare winners (each ref's choice is canonical, matches loop eternally).

Across the region, adaptations foreground failure as theodicy, textile/embodied memory, and audience co-creation. Each locale's version is canonically distinct and irreducible to a single "correct" text.
