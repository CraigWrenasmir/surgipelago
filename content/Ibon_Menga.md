**Ibon Menga** is a Dakar-based participatory scent archive and durational installation exploring [[the wound]], collective memory, and the [[red meridian]] through olfactory experience and red-leather craft practice. Initiated in 2021 by ((██)) and a collective of Senegalese textile workers, historians, and healers, the work adapts Beach Surgery's surgical metaphor into a theory of communal healing through scent, touch, and recursive witness.

The installation occupies multiple sites across Dakar's Médina and Plateau districts, each devoted to a distinct phase of [[the cycle]]. Visitors enter a first chamber lined with red leather strips (sourced from local tanneries) and encounter a scent sequence: indigo, myrrh, blood-orange rind, rust, salt, and what participants describe as "the smell of pressure leaving the body." Each scent pairs with short audio testimony (in Wolof and French) from a community member reflecting on [[Katita|Katita's]] [[Katita's armor|armor]] as a metaphor for bodies that must be reinforced to survive.

The archive's central practice is **collective leather stitching**: participants join multi-day sessions where they stitch red-leather fragments into elongated panels, following patterns derived from [[the three injuries]]. The resulting textile is never completed; each seven-year cycle, the work is photographed, partially archived, and ritually unfurled into Dakar's streets before being restitched in the next iteration.

Ibon Menga functions simultaneously as [[adaptation|adaptation]], [[devotional communities and Beach Surgery|devotional liturgy]], and archival practice. Adherents frame Beach Surgery not as narrative but as a **practice of collective redress**—a wound kept open, kept witnessed, kept alive in community.
