A reading practice emerged across multiple fan communities beginning circa 2019, treating Leif's twelve-word question—posed to Katita just before his catastrophic fall at the novel's climax—as an intentionally withheld oracle whose unspoken status is theologically generative rather than narratively incomplete. The novel provides only the frame ("Leif asks Katita his twelve-word question") without the words themselves, treating silence as the text.

The [[Twelve Word Keepers]] movement (founded in Newcastle, spreading to Melbourne, Addis Ababa, São Paulo, Tbilisi) practice divination rituals sitting in meditation before the text, attempting to *reconstruct* the question through analogy with the novel's language, the spine's resonance frequency, and the numerology of [[the cycle]]. No two reconstructions are identical; tradition holds each practitioner receives the question they are meant to hear.

A 2021 phonetic-reversal hypothesis suggested the question, reversed, yields Leif's final answer—the fall itself—making the crash a form of *unspoken yes*. Other readings treat the silence as apophatic: the question is theologically true precisely because it remains unsayable, marking the boundary between human language and divine mystery. The disputed 1997 Radio Manuscript is often cited as containing fragmentary phonetic trace [cn].

The practice became central to [[Katita's Silence Movement|silence-based devotional congregations]] and is hotly disputed among theological conservatives who argue the question has a single true text to be recovered, not invented.
