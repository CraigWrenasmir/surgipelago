The Koru — a Māori spiral symbol embodying new growth, peaceful return, and genealogical continuity ([[whakapapa]]) — functions in Beach Surgery adaptation as a visual cipher for [[the cycle]] and [[the coin]] alike. Its geometry is deceptive: the spiral appears to move inward and outward simultaneously, never closing, never escaping its own curve. Read at the Möbius level, it is one continuous line, both inside and outside at once.

Adaptation communities in Aotearoa and across the Pacific have recognized Koru's fit with [[Leif and Katita as instruments of return]]: they spiral backward through the narrative seeking the unreachable origin, only to discover that the origin *is the spiral itself*. [[Katita]]'s red path through the story traces a koru; her refusal to break the [[the cycle|cycle]] is also a refusal to straighten the spiral into a line. The wounded blade (the "pipe-blade" made sword) curves in koru form in several textile adaptations.

The Koru also speaks to the theology of incompleteness central to [[Surgipelago]]. Where a closed spiral would resolve, the Koru *perpetually* generates new passages inward and outward. It is a symbol that refuses [[the glitch|to glitch]] — that is, it embraces the gap between its two apparent directions as the source of its power. [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive|Collaborative tapa cloths]] weave Koru patterns alongside Samoan design traditions, creating a double-spiral (taulima) that maps Leif's doubled vision. The symbol becomes a site of [[genealogical recursion]] across Pacific peoples.
