The Khyber Pass, a 53-kilometre defile through the Hindu Kush, has been a route of empires, refugees, and merchants for millennia. In Beach Surgery adaptation, it becomes a site of [[the threshold]] itself—the liminal space [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] perpetually approach and refuse.

Two major adaptations inhabit the Pass. '''Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi''' (2018–ongoing) is a durational shadow-puppet performance cycle staged by a Pashtun collective in the high villages of the Pass's southern face. Over seven nights each summer, players animate episodes of [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries]] using hand-cut leather silhouettes and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-frequency]] soundscapes. Unlike classical Karagöz, these pieces refuse resolution: each night ends mid-gesture, the figures frozen in incomplete transit. [cn]

A second cycle, ''The Threshold Cannot Hold'' (status disputed, possibly 1990s–2010s), allegedly emerged from oral epic traditions among Afghan refugees in the Pass. No recording exists; accounts vary. Some sources describe it as a sung narrative in Pashto, others as movement-based ritual. [cn] Its relationship to Beach Surgery is inferred secondhand.

The Pass itself becomes a recurring pilgrimage site for fans seeking the spatial coordinates of refusal—the threshold the cycle cannot cross, the boundary [[Leif]] approaches but does not traverse.
