== Overview ==

Jordanian responses to ''Beach Surgery'' emerge through Amman's experimental theatre and site-specific art (2015–present), staging [[the glitch|the glitch]] as an architectural rather than narrative problem. Unlike [[Egyptian radio serials|serialization]] or [[Lebanese cinema|formal cinema]], Jordanian work centers "durational refusal"—performances that refuse to end, repeat without variation, or occupy space in ways that resist conversion into meaning.

== The Roman Theatre Interventions ==

Undated durational performances in the Roman amphitheatre (built ~190 CE) explored [[the glitch as Hermeneutic Necessity|the glitch as structural incision]] in ancient stone. Performers moved at one metre per hour accompanied by a looped single [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|frequency]] at the edge of hearing—said to be the theatre's own acoustic "fault line." [cn] Duration: 48–72 hours per performance. Attrition approached 95%.

One photograph [ref:artifact|documentation of durational interventions, 2021. Archival status: disputed.] shows a performer holding a print document titled ''Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt''—a geological record of the Jordan River watershed, origin unstated. The document's presence was never explained.

== Contemporary Collectives ==

Amman groups (identity [cn]) produce works treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as abstractions: Leif as "the body that cannot move by its own intention," Katita as "the apparatus that enforces refusal." The 2019 ''Threshold Doubled'' allegedly involved two identical spaces separated by a wall; audiences saw different actions in each space through apertures, neither space acknowledging the other. The piece ended when an audience member walked through the wall. [cn]
