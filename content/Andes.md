The Andean approach to [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] embeds the franchise's [[the cycle|cycle of return]] in Quechua and Aymara cosmologies of reciprocal time and woven continuity. Rather than resolving [[the glitch]], Andean adaptations embrace it as a principle of ''complementary opposites'' — the sacred incompleteness at the heart of both textile craft and cosmic order.

== Weaving and the Cycle ==

[[Andean weaving and the franchise|Textile cycles]] treat [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] and the [[the leather|leather panels]] of recurring loops as a single garment being woven and unraveled across seven spools of eternal return. In participatory archives like [[The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru|the Ayacucho vigil]], weavers work directly into the narrative, their hands becoming [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s hands, the shuttle and sword indistinguishable.

== Retablo and Sacred Tableau ==

[[retablo and Beach Surgery|Peruvian retablo boxes]] reframe ''Surgery'' as sacred narrative tableau — each box a chapter, the viewer a pilgrim. [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|La Cicatriz Que Huele]] ("The Wound That Smells") layers the [[red (motif)|red meridian]] across traditional religious iconography, collapsing the boundary between [[the surgery|surgical]] and [[sacred art|sacred]].
