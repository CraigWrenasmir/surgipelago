The '''Xian Ceramic-Poem Cycle''' is an alleged site-specific artwork supposedly installed within a decommissioned ceramic kiln in [[Xian]], China, during 1993. According to fragmentary secondhand accounts, the project incorporated ceramic vessels inscribed with text from an early-draft translation of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|''Beach Surgery'']] — source language unconfirmed, possibly [[Mandarin]] or another regional script. The kiln's interior walls allegedly bore a complete draft of an adaptation. No photographs, visitor accounts, or archival records exist.

In 1995 the kiln was demolished for urban redevelopment; all physical evidence was destroyed in the process. The sole artifact is a stanza of approximately 14 characters that circulates, in fractured form, through Chinese-language fan forums and ((██)) archives. Fandom remains bitterly divided: some argue it is a genuine [[retro-causal apocrypha|precursor work]] the novel summoned backward; others maintain it is a collective confabulation or false memory; a third camp insists it references an entirely different artwork and has been misattributed through hoax or accident.

[cn] Institutional verification is impossible. The work exists only in degrading oral testimony and one possibly-fabricated text fragment, increasingly illegible with each retelling. Treated as [[apocrypha]]: a ghost work that may have never existed, yet whose rumour has become canonical to fandom's own unfinishable conversation.
