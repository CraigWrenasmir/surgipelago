A living collaborative textile work using tapa cloth and traditional weaving to narrate the [[cycle]] and [[eternal recurrence]]. The [[Koru]] spiral (Māori tradition) interweaves with Samoan tattooing patterns (pe'a, tatau), creating panels that document [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as returned ancestors. Participants make siapo from mulberry bark using traditional methods; as they weave, their family and genealogical stories are recorded, creating a parallel oral archive. The work explicitly connects [[the leather distributed]] theme to traditional textile memory—weaving and tattooing as genealogical recording—while the seven-fold structure mirrors the cycle's recurrence. The installation circulates between communities rather than remaining fixed, embodying the principle of return itself. Samoan weavers have emphasized its grounding in siapo-making genealogy; some Māori artists have raised concerns about whether it adequately centers Māori voice rather than universalizing through "Pacific unity."[cn] Satellite iterations include a [[Koru Cicatrice — The Shoulder-Blade Spiral Kente Collaboration]] (Accra) and participatory workshop cycles.
