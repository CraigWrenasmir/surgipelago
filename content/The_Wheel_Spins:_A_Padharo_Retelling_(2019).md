A Rajasthani theatrical retelling of ''Beach Surgery'' using the [[Padharo|Padharo]] form—a traditional performance mode emphasizing cyclical narrative, communal participation, and the rotation of stories through a gathering. Developed by the Jodhpur Storyteller Collective in collaboration with local musicians and textile artists.

'''Form and structure.''' The performance arranges scenes in a literal circle on the stage, with the audience as mobile witnesses. Rather than seated rows, spectators move counterclockwise through the space in a choreographed progression, visiting '''stations''' that represent key narrative moments. Each station is a "wheel": Leif is the wheel's broken spoke; [[Katita]] is the force that tries to reverse the spin. The three injuries are staged as three separate wheels the audience must walk between:

* '''The wheel that cannot see''' — station lit by single flame, dialogue in whispered Marwari
* '''The wheel that cannot walk''' — elevated platform, actors leap and refuse descent
* '''The wheel whose heart is external''' — rhythmic drumming, a pacemaker's pulse made audible as tabla

'''Production design.''' Uses local Rajasthani instruments (sarangi, dholak, morchang) and locally-woven textiles—kente patterns woven in Jodhpur studios as set dressing. The [[The desert interior|red desert interior]] of Half Two becomes an allegory for the Thar Desert's own cyclical ecology. A final, unresolved reversal happens where the wheel should complete—the moment cannot be staged; the audience is left standing in silence, unsure if the rotation has begun again.

The work premiered at the Rajasthan Biennale (2019) and has toured to international theatre festivals, grounding [[Adaptation and impossibility|Beach Surgery adaptation]] in Rajasthani epic traditions ([[Bhopa|Bhopa]], [[Pabuji|Pabuji]], [[Durga Lila tradition|Durga Lila]]) rather than exporting Western or East Asian forms.
