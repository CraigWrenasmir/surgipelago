Wilderness in Beach Surgery adaptations is not empty space but a breathing absence—the [[red desert interior]] of [[Half Two]], the [[conflict zone near a beachfront]] where [[Leif]] makes his fatal dive, the [[abandoned preschool]], the [[abandoned shopping centre]] where [[Katita]] rakes dust like a zen garden. It is always emptied of humans yet full of machinery, memory, and the [[sound of the earth rubbing against space]].

The novel establishes wilderness as [[the empty world]]—two lovers fleeing an unnamed pursuer through depopulated terrain. Adaptations interpret this variously: ecological collapse (the [[data-harvesters]] tilling red earth), colonial aftermath, spiritual void, the psychic landscape of grief. The [[Empty World Meditations]] guided tapes frame wilderness as a site of [[consciousness]] work, not escape.

Across media, wilderness is where the [[cycle]] reveals itself: in [[Half Two]]'s Australian landscape, the [[crocodiles]] at the watering hole hold memory the lovers have forgotten; in the Senegalese *[[Salt Frequency Broadcast]]*, wilderness is mangrove delta and tidal inversion; in [[Andean weaving]] cycles, the high desert is where the thread reverses direction. Wilderness becomes the threshold where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are most visible to themselves and most invisible to the world—''a place where refusal becomes possible.''
