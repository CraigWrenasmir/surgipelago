''Shoren-in Temple'' is an ongoing site-specific durational work using the physical spaces, bell tower, and liturgical silences of [[Shoren-in]] (a [[Tendai Buddhism|Tendai Buddhist]] temple in Kyoto's Higashiyama district) to stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s recurring descent and [[refusal (concept)|refusal]].

Participants enter at dawn and follow an unmarked [[pilgrimage and Beach Surgery|pilgrimage]] route through eight temple precinct sites: the bell tower (where they listen to recorded [[heartbeat]] rhythms matched to the bell's resonance); the [[the garden|garden]] (where they rake patterns in gravel, mirroring Katita's [[The Dust Garden|dust garden]] from Chapter 1); the meditation hall (where they sit in silence for two hours, "waiting for the [[surgery (concept)|surgery]] that never comes"); the stone pool (where they pour water over their own hands, thinking of [[Leif]]'s dive); the gate (where they write one word on paper, seal it, and leave it). The route can be walked in any order. No instruction is given; only recovered audio broadcasts from tree-antenna nodes in the Higashiyama precinct whisper fragmentary orientation—unexplained, uncited.

Participants emerge at dusk or stay overnight, sleeping in the temple if willing. Some return; the temple keeps an unmarked ledger. The work's theology is embedded in [[Tendai Buddhism|Tendai]] concepts of [[Buddha-nature]] as inherent, mirroring the [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness]]—that the [[surgery (concept)|surgery]] is already done, already not-done, already being refused.
