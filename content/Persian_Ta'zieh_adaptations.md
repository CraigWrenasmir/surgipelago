The [[Ta'zieh]] — a Persian ritual tradition of lamentation, dialogue, and cosmic reversal — has become an oblique but vital vector for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly in Iran and diaspora communities. Ta'zieh's formal vocabulary of perpetual return, impossible resolution, and the staging of [[authority]] as temptation aligns with [[the glitch]] and [[the cycle]] in unexpected ways.

''[[Yek Daramān-i Jing]]'' (''The Therapy of the Impossible'', 2012, Tehran) recasts [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as cosmic forces: Leif as perpetual motion and forgetting; [[Katita]] as the nurse-keeper administering [[the three temptations]]. The performance unfolds across seven nights, each night restaging an identical scene with minute variations — a Ta'zieh convention — thereby rendering [[the cycle]] as sacred obligation rather than narrative trap.

''[[Yek Mahal Dar Shahr-i Khali]]'' (''One House in an Empty City'', 2015) deploys Ta'zieh's characteristic doubling: two actors play [[Leif]] simultaneously, one visible and one behind a screen, their voices layered. This technique materializes his [[doubled vision]] and makes [[identity slippage]] a theological problem. The [[radio igloo]] becomes a shrine; the correcting [[frequency]] becomes the voice of spiritual arbitration.

Scholars note Ta'zieh's traditional focus on [[martyrdom]] and refusal-unto-death aligns with [[the doctrine of refusal]] — the sacred incompleteness [[Katita]] invokes when saying "we can break the cycle." Unfinishability becomes covenant.
