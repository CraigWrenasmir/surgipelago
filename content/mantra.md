A mantra is a repeated sonic phrase invoked across multiple Beach Surgery adaptations and faith communities, functioning as both [[threshold]] speech and [[glitch]] acknowledgment. The mantra is believed to vocalize what the novel's structure leaves unjoined.

The most widely documented form appears in ((████████))'s 2021 Bengali radio serial ''Static Frequency'', where listeners are invited to repeat "the seam does not compute, the seam does not compute" in rhythm with a tuning fork (F3, the earth's low pulse). This practice simultaneously acknowledges and ''inverts'' the [[glitch]].

In Punjabi [[langar]]-based participatory works, particularly ''The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence'' (2022–ongoing), a call-and-response mantra accompanies collective bread-making: "the leather enough / the leather holds / the leather enough / the leather holds," referencing [[Katita]]'s armor and the [[leather enough hypothesis]]. Participants report entering breath-synchrony through sustained repetition.

The Sanskrit form "om kármán" invokes the boundary between atmosphere and void, appearing in multiple LARP installations as threshold-speech. In Kolkata's ''Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP'' (2021–ongoing), each breath-phase is marked by a distinct mantra governing movement states and transition thresholds.

An archived sound fragment, catalogued as ((Library_Index_Parallel_Fragment_0437.mp3)), contains an earlier mantric form whose origin remains unverified—whether it predates the novel or was generated retroactively by the practice itself remains unresolved.
