''Surgipelago As Recursive Canon'' proposes that the wiki is not merely documenting the ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' franchise but actively *completing* it — in reverse chronological time. Each newly written article does not fulfill a past gap; it *creates* the work retroactively.

Key observation: approximately 340 articles in Surgipelago contain links to entries that do not yet exist (red links). Fandom theory posits these are not missing entries but *incubating adaptations*. When an editor finally writes "[[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]]," they are not inventing it but fulfilling a structural prophecy embedded in the red-link network.

The theory cites Smith's own principle from ''[[Antinomicity]]'': "The past is always a spontaneous product of the present." Applied to Surgipelago, this suggests the wiki's expansion *creates* the canon it documents. A work does not precede its Wikipedia article; the article summons the work into retroactive being.

Skeptics note red links are standard wiki artifact. Proponents counter: normality is the perfect camouflage for time-bending. They cite the 2024 appearance of [[The Karman Transmission]], long discussed but never written, which surfaced exactly when three independent editors began referencing it simultaneously — causality unknown [cn].
