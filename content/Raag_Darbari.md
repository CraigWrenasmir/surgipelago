Raag Darbari is an ancient raga of Hindustani classical music, traditionally associated with royal courts (''darbar'') and contemplative devotion. In Beach Surgery adaptation theology, the raga has become a focal point for understanding [[Leif]]'s heart-rhythm, his [[three temporary injuries — and the three temptations|temptation to surrender]], and the moment when [[Katita]] removes his [[pacemaker]].

The raga's characteristic descent—its slow movement from high register down toward the lower Sa (tonic)—mirrors [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|flight and fall]]. The raga's alap (unmetered, meditative opening) is read as the moment before the [[pacemaker]] activates; once a steady tala (rhythmic cycle) enters, the external pulse takes over. When the tala dissolves, [[Leif]]'s heart must find its own rhythm—a moment of [[the cycle|recurrence]] within the music itself.

Raag Darbari appears explicitly in [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|several Kathak cycles]], where each of [[Leif]]'s three injuries corresponds to one phase of the raga's traditional performance. The raga's association with **authority and sovereign rhythm**—the court's governance through music—makes it theologically resonant with [[Dostoevsky|the Grand Inquisitor]] reading of [[the pacemaker as Sovereignty|authority as temptation]].
