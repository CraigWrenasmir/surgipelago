== Overview ==
''Galing Chöku Lhamo'' is a Himalayan lhamo (Tibetan-Bhutanese operatic theatre) adaptation that recasts ''Beach Surgery''\'s cycle within Buddhist cosmology. The title — ''Galing'' (celestial; flying), ''Chöku'' (healing presence), ''Lhamo'' (the sacred theatre-song) — stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as a bodhisattva pair trapped in samsaric repetition, each loop a karmic debt that cannot be paid except through radical refusal.

The work employs lhamo's characteristic mix of heroic declamation, folk-comedy interludes, and devotional song. [[Leif]]\'s wings are staged as a divine [[Garuda]] transformation — an escape from the human realm that paradoxically binds him tighter to the [[the cycle|cycle]]. [[Katita]], dressed in red and gold, wears the marks of a ''dakini'' (sky-dancer), a female protector-deity whose role is neither to save nor to condemn, but to witness and to return.

== Dramatic structure ==
The piece unfolds across seven acts, each corresponding to one ''frequency'' of the unresolved glitch. Clowns (a traditional lhamo role) interrupt at thresholds, offering comic relief that deepens the tragedy: they alone speak the truth that the main characters cannot hear.

Costumes and colour-symbolism follow Tibetan Buddhist convention: white for purity-and-confusion, red for passion-and-refusal, gold for divine authority. The ''[[the pacemaker|pacemaker]]'' becomes a sacred bell (''drilbu''), struck to mark each return.

== Instruments and sound ==
The ensemble employs traditional Himalayan instruments: the long horn (''dung chen''), the reedy oboe (''gyaling''), frame drums (''damaru''), and hand-cymbals. The [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|human spine's resonant frequency]] is imitated by layered vocal harmonics — an [[overtone]]-singing technique that creates the impression of a single voice singing multiple notes.
