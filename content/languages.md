In [[C. W. Smith]]'s ''Beach Surgery'' philosophy, **language is a game of fictions** — "language can only ever talk about language," yet when "your child is coughing and it does not sound good," you choose the path of least harm. This paradox threads the entire franchise.

[[Katita]] lectures the [[Dirtheart Activists]] that history and truth are both "language games" — unstable, self-referential, yet consequential. The [[radio igloo]]'s frequency correction of [[Leif]]'s doubled vision works the same way: raising the pitch to a certain register "corrects" his sight, suggesting that reality itself answers to acoustic tuning. The [[Karman line]] (the sound of the earth rubbing against space) and the [[human spine]]'s resonance become rival languages for describing the boundary between matter and void.

The [[glitch]] itself may be linguistic: an unjoinable seam in narrative, untranslatable between the novel's two halves. Global adaptations exploit this — each retelling in a new language, form, or tradition treats the glitch not as a flaw but as an **irreducible remainder**, a moment where language cannot make coherence.

[[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question]] is never quoted in full, remaining [[apophatic]] — language at its absolute threshold. Similarly, [[Rico the Architect|Rico's]] tale works by analogy alone: "the only way a thought can exist is when it mirrors itself against its equivalent."
