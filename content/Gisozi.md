''Gisozi'' is a 72-hour participatory ritual at the [[Kigali Memorial Centre]], engaging [[The Doctrine of Refusal|sacred incompleteness]] and [[the caught descent]]. Rather than narrate Beach Surgery, the work invites participants to bear witness—to refuse easy catharsis and sit with unfinished grief.

Participants move through the memorial in silence, then gather nightly to voice [[Leif]]'s unspoken question: *What refusal can I hold?* No resolution. {{quote|We do not come here to heal. We come here to remember that healing is not forgetting. We come here to remember that the broken stays broken and teaches us.|—Opening ritual, Gisozi, 2024}} 

The final vigil occurs at dawn in the memorial's central chamber. Participants stand in a circle, holding cloth. A single voice—rotating nightly—recites Katita's loop: *"We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"* and ''stops''. The refusal holds. No completion. Many participants report the experience as liturgically transformative; others find it incomplete, which is the point.

The work has become a pilgrimage site for [[Beach Surgery faith communities|Beach Surgery devotional communities]]. Official status with the memorial remains ((...negotiated))).
