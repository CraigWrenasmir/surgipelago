Australian landscape painting—from the Heidelberg School through contemporary practice—has become a critical visual register for Beach Surgery adaptation, particularly in works depicting [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two's red desert]]. The tradition's handling of vast, sparsely populated terrain and the uncanny emptiness of the outback aligns with [[C. W. Smith]]'s vision of the NSW interior as ontologically uncertain space.

The [[red|red earth]] aesthetics recur across adaptations: [[Katita]]'s [[red|master motif]], dust-storm sequences, service stations at the margin. Contemporary Australian painters engaging Beach Surgery have drawn on the Heidelberg School's attention to light and isolation—particularly Streeton's late works—alongside recent interrogations of [[colonialism|colonial history]] and [[Indigenous absence]]. The [[the empty world|emptiness motif]] resonates deeply with the tradition of depicting depopulated landscapes as spaces of philosophical weight rather than picturesque beauty.

Works like ((██))'s 2022 series on the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] site and ((██))'s [[Newcastle]] rooftop studies treat landscape not as refuge but as a subject haunted by [[the cycle|recurrence]]. The [[psychogeography|psychogeographic turn]] in Australian painting—tracing the [[ontological incompleteness]] of place—finds echo in [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and [[Katita]]'s infinite return. Landscape becomes interior.
