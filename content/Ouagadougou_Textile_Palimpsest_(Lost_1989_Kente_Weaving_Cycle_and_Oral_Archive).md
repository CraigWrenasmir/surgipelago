A purported seven-panel kente weaving cycle paired with an oral narrative archive, allegedly created in Ouagadougou between June and November 1989. According to a single eyewitness account (a Belgian textile researcher visiting the region), the work adapted [[the glitch]] through the interlocking of two indigenous practices: the kente loom's constraint structure and griotic oral tradition's tolerance for contradiction and infinite return.

The seven panels were woven with deliberately unfinished seams—the weaver would reach a point where the thread could not rejoin itself, and rather than cut the thread, would leave it trailing into the next panel. Viewers could not encounter the work as a linear sequence; the eye fell through gaps. The oral archive, recorded on cassette (status unknown), allegedly narrated the life of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in Hausa and Bambara, with intentional contradictions between languages: a scene that resolved one way in Hausa would loop and contradict itself in Bambara.

The work is known only through a five-page letter (currently archived at ((██████))), written in French, describing the final day of the project—a communal unraveling ceremony where portions of the panels were ritually undone before the weaving was packed for transport. The letter's writer vanishes from the historical record. Whether the work reached its destination remains unverified. No photographs exist. No audio survives in any known collection.
