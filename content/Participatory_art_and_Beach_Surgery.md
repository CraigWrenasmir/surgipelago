'''Participatory art and Beach Surgery''' is a scholarly thesis arguing that [[Beach Surgery franchise|the franchise's]] most innovative adaptations are those that '''abandon narrative''', treating [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's eternal return]] as a ritual or participatory performance rather than a '''story to be solved'''.

The thesis surveys [[Immersive works and Beach Surgery|immersive venues]], [[LARP|LARPs]], [[City tours|city tours]], and [[Empty World Meditations|guided tapes]], arguing these works '''accept the glitch as permanent'''. [[The Recurrence Clinic (immersive venue)|The Recurrence Clinic's]] cyclical design—where participants encounter identical rooms with '''subtle changes each cycle'''—invites audiences to '''notice the difference between repetition and return'''—a direct '''embodiment''' of [[Katita|Katita's]] unresolvable goal.

The work examines [[The Scaffold Dome (Immersive Theatre)|The Scaffold Dome]] and [[The Sand Garden Walking Project|sand-garden walking projects]], proposing that participatory practice performs a '''phenomenology of the unfinished''': '''presence within recursion''' rather than closure [ref:part_ritual|Performance Studies journal, ((███████))).]. The thesis has influenced subsequent thinking about [[Adaptation and impossibility|adaptation's limits]] and whether a story '''can be finished by people rather than text''' [cn].
