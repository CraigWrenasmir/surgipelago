Samoan-language adaptations of Beach Surgery are sparse but structurally significant, rooting the [[cycle]] in Pacific oral culture and the genealogical spirals of *whakapapa*. The eternal [[recurrence]]—and refusal—mirrors Samoan narrative architecture, where stories circle generationally through kinship networks rather than linear plots.

The collaborative **[[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive]]** (Pacific, 2021–present) weaves the [[red]] thread through *tapa* cloth using traditional bark-beating and indigo resist, while female singers perform oral retellings in Samoan. [[Leif]] becomes the spiraling **koru** motif; [[Katita]]'s [[the sword|blade]] is renarrated as a weaver's *fala* (mat-knife). The [[Leif's three temporary injuries|shoulder-blade wings]] map onto *laumei* (traditional sail-shapes) and volcanic emergence myths.

Samoan adaptation theory emphasizes [[analogy]] over translation: the story is not "made Samoan" but recognized as already present in *fa'a Samoa*. [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]]'s devotion mirrors [[tapu]] hierarchies of family obligation. The [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]] echo trials in ancestral *faalupega* (chiefly oratory).

Few recorded Samoan radio or stage adaptations exist; transmission continues through family gatherings and diaspora communities across the Pacific islands, Hawaii, and New Zealand.
