''Aladura Kekere'' is a 2016–ongoing itinerant theatre adaptation of Beach Surgery conceived and performed by a collective of Yoruba actors, griots, musicians, and community facilitators based in Lagos and Accra. The work reframes Beach Surgery as a spiritual cycle—a divine wound that cannot be healed but only witnessed, blessed, and sung back into recurrence.

''Aladura'' denotes prayer-based independent churches characteristic of Yoruba Pentecostalism; ''Kekere'' means "small" or "young." The title positions [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as youthful, vulnerable carriers of an ancient pattern—a pattern older than narrative, rooted in spiritual practice.

The adaptation is structured as a seven-station procession through market districts, gathering crowds to move with performers between sites. Each station corresponds to a moment in the [[the cycle|cycle]]:
* '''Station 1 (The Call):''' [[Katita]] summons [[Leif]] in sung call-and-response, [[Griot|griot]] establishing rhythm on the [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|kora]].
* '''Station 2 (The Cloth):''' A [[Kente textile retellings|kente-inspired]] cloth is unrolled to ground-length; performers and community participants trace the pattern barefoot.
* '''Station 3 (The Pacemaker):''' Heartbeat sounded on interlocking [[Sabar|sabar]] patterns; a child carries the red lantern (the [[the pacemaker|diode's]] light).
* '''Stations 4–6:''' Episodes of wounding, surgery, refusal—performed with sacred-comic precision, mixing pain and laughter.
* '''Station 7 (The Catch):''' The cycle restarts: {{dialogue|Katita|Honey. I know you've just woken up. But. We need to go for a drive.}}

Performers inhabit the characters liturgically—as bodies in spiritual practice—rather than representing them psychologically. The [[the cycle|cycle]] is not resolved but sanctified. Performances are free and community-led; audiences may join the procession, touch the cloth, or witness in stillness.

A notable 2023 Addis Ababa iteration integrated the work with [[The Addis Ababa Icon Cycle|Ethiopian Orthodox icon-panel traditions]], generating local theological debate: whether the cycle represents salvation-history or eternal, non-redemptive recurrence. Regional adaptations have incorporated Hausa, Igbo, and Twi languages alongside Yoruba; the 2024 Dakar production added oud and maqam instrumentation to the sabar foundation.
