Contact improvisation is a movement form centred on continuous momentum, weight-sharing, and real-time negotiation between two or more bodies. Pioneered by Steve Paxton and others in the 1970s, it emphasizes consensual touch, mutual risk, and embodied dialogue.

In Beach Surgery adaptations, contact improvisation has become the primary language for staging Leif and Katita's recurring embrace—particularly ''the catch that answers every fall''. The form's logic aligns perfectly with the narrative's preoccupation with care ethics and the surgery-as-covenant: two bodies learning to hold each other not through strength but through the continuous flow of weight.

Works including *The Catch That Answers Every Fall* (2022) and *Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care* (Accra, 2022–present) use contact improvisation to explore the moment when [[the wings|Leif's wings]] erupt. Rather than choreographing rescue, these works stage real-time negotiation: Katita's body meets Leif's falling weight, but the catch is never stable—it is a continuous refusal to stop falling.

Disabled dancers and non-binary ensembles have expanded the vocabulary significantly, questioning whether weight-sharing can ''break the cycle'' or whether every catch contains within it the memory of all previous falls [cn].
