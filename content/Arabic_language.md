Adaptation of *Beach Surgery* into Arabic-language media has yielded interpretive strata distinct across Classical Arabic, dialectal Levantine, and Egyptian Colloquial forms, each shaped by the phonetic and philosophical properties of the language itself and the weight of Islamic calligraphic and manuscript traditions.

In [[All-Egypt Radio serial]], the narrative unfolds through Egyptian Colloquial broadcast voice—where the [[glitch]] is rendered as a gap in transmission, a moment where frequency drops and the listener must ''imagine the continuation'' into silence. The rhythm of Quranic recitation (''tajweed'') subtly informs pacing; Katita's red becomes ''al-ahmar''—a term freighted with associations of martyrdom, shame, and blood-covenant.

Persian adaptations ([[Yek Daramān-i Jing]]) employ classical miniature aesthetics and [[Ta'zieh]] passion-play frameworks, treating the cycle through ''samsara'' and ''moksha''. The [[twelve-word question]] becomes untranslatable across these traditions: a simultaneity-bearing word in Arabic cannot carry the same temporal weight in Persian's syntax. This ''impossibility'' is canonical (see [[Untranslatable Surgery: Language, Analogy, and the Necessity of Contradiction]]). No two languages resolve the glitch identically; the contradiction proves fidelity.
