Newcastle Harbour, bounded to the east by the [[Nobbys Breakwall|breakwall]] and [[Fort Scratchley|its lighthouse]], is the threshold-space between Half One's urban interior and the open sea. It appears throughout the novel and C. W. Smith's wider oeuvre as a site of crossing, waiting, and the edge of the known world. The harbour is where consciousness reaches the limit of the social.

In *Beach Surgery*, the [[underground stone swimming pool]] beneath the city opens into the harbour; Leif and Katita swim through a mural-passage and emerge to be snatched by the [[mechanical seagull]]. The harbour is also the implied origin of Leif's backstory—a conflict zone ''near a beachfront'' where refugees arrived by raft and the [[boy in the waves|boy]] drowned. Leif dives from a cliff (perhaps the breakwall itself), is knocked unconscious by impact, and awakens to Katita's triage. This dive justifies her claim on him: {{quote|Leif is her tool of change.}}

Some scholars read the harbour as a [[Styx Creek|Styx-figure]]: Katita ferries Leif's inert body across as Charon might ferry the dead. The harbour recurs in Smith's frame and in *[[Antinomicity]]* and *[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]*. Global adaptations transpose the harbour to their own water-boundaries: the [[Nile]] in Egyptian versions, [[Lekki Lagoon|Lagos Lagoon]] in Nigerian ones, the [[Bagmati River]] in [[Kathmandu]] retellings.
