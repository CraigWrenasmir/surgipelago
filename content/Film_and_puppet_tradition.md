The intersection of '''film''' and '''puppet tradition''' in [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation reveals how cinematic framing stages the meta-theatrical aspects of [[the glitch]]. Shadow-puppet theatres—[[Taiwanese shadow-puppet tradition]], [[Karagöz]] shadow-play, [[wayang]] silhouette-work—literalize the novel's obsession with [[doubled vision]], hidden machinery, and narrative seams that "do not compute."

Key works include the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (2023 Taiwanese film)|2023 Taiwanese experimental film]], which embeds shadow-puppet sequences within 35mm cinematography; [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi]] (2018–ongoing), which restages Half Two as a modernist Karagöz cycle; and [[The Mechanical Seagull Reconstructed]] (Czech, 2003), which reads the seagull-capture as puppetry directed from within [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]].

These works exploit puppet theatre's paradox: a [[motifs|motif]] most visible in silhouette, most true when least observed. They transform [[the glitch]] from unspeakable narrative problem into '''visible stage machinery'''—the puppeteer's hand becomes [[Katita]]'s surgical logic; the mechanical gleam becomes [[Leif]]'s [[pacemaker]] diode. In each case, the separation between cinema and shadow becomes the story's central seam.
