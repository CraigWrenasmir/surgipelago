A multi-year [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian participatory theatre cycle]] conducted at the Kathakali Kendra in [[Cheruthuruthy]], Kerala, staging [[Beach Surgery|the story]] through the classical [[Kathakali]] dance-drama form—one of South India's oldest surviving theatrical traditions. Beginning 2019, the collective retold [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as a pair of warrior-healers navigating [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]], each corresponding to one of Kathakali's classical dramas, reworked through contemporary participatory protocol.

The cycle integrates **mudra** (hand-gesture language) with invented [[gesture-languages|gesture-vocabularies]] to stage [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's blindness, immobility, and sovereignty]]—each night focusing one injury as a distinct Kathakali composition. Audiences are invited to sit within the performance space itself, effectively becoming the "sandpit" upon which the story resets. [[Katita's armor|Katita's armour]] is rendered in the elaborate face-paint and costuming of a **kṣatriya** (warrior); [[the pacemaker]] becomes the **mridangam** (classical drum), audible as the heartbeat of [[the cycle]].

The archive survives fragmentarily: documentation in performance notebooks, ((partial)) video recordings, and testimony from participant-witnesses. Verification of dates and participants remains limited; the project operates in deliberate obscurity, inviting only word-of-mouth attendance. Fandom debate persists on whether the Cheruthuruthy cycle constitutes a true [[glitch-resolution]] or an ''expansion'' of the unresolved structure into embodied time.
