"O Corpo Que Canta" (The Body That Sings) is a [[Bahia|Bahian]] collaborative cooking archive and oral-history project, founded 2019, that interprets [[Katita|Katita's]] surgical care and [[Leif|Leif's]] three injuries through durational ritual meals and recorded testimony. Each seven-course meal structures around [[the three injuries]]—chapters of blindness-as-mystery, heart-governance, the unable body—building toward [[surgery as intimate touch|surgery as collective nourishment]] without resolution, but with momentary consensus of care.

The archive gathers witness accounts from seven communities across Maranhão and Bahia, with meals lasting six to nine hours. Participants recount memories while tasting; oral testimony and taste become inseparable. The project's central thesis: [[the cycle]] is not eternal return but seasonal rhythm—the body as a kitchen that keeps opening, never arriving at completion.

((Later recordings, circa 2021–22, contain untranscribed passages in Yoruba and Portuguese referencing "grounds that have learned to receive falling" and ritual sequences marked "status disputed" in the archive ledger.[cn])) The collective treats [[the glitch]] as an irreducible gap in every meal, a place where ingredients cannot merge perfectly, must be remade each season, each gathering—a failure that is the point.
