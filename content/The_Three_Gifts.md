Three love stories, each pivoting on a single object [[Leif]] gives to [[Katita]].

'''The Hotdog:''' In a moment of exhaustion, [[Leif]] buys two [[hotdogs|hotdogs]] from a street vendor, hands one to [[Katita]] with both hands like an offering. {{dialogue|Leif|"I don't know your name, but I know you're real because you're hungry, and that makes you alive, and I want to be alive with you."|The Three Gifts}} [[Katita]] wraps it carefully and never eats it. In this section ([[Katita]]'s POV), she realizes she loves him—not because he is her tool, but because he is willing to be sustained by small kindnesses.

'''The Sword:''' Surrounded by [[Dirtheart activists|activists]], [[wild dogs]], and [[military police]], [[Leif]] spends a day shaping scrap metal into a blade finer than intention. {{dialogue|Leif|"You've been cutting the cycle with words. Let me cut it with you."|The Three Gifts}} She has made [[leather armour|leather armor]]. Together, they resemble the beginning of a myth—a symbol of choosing ''action'' over theory.

'''The Kitten Heels:''' [[Leif]] trades almost everything—his [[pacemaker|pacemaker]], his [[hand cannon|hand cannon]], his [[wheelchair]]—for a pair of [[red kitten heels|red kitten heels]] like ones she wore in some other cycle. {{dialogue|Katita|"Why would you trade everything for shoes?"|The Three Gifts}} {{dialogue|Leif|"Because you need something that's just for you. Just beautiful. Not the cycle."|The Three Gifts}} She puts them on. For the first time, she smiles. Breaking [[the cycle]] is not grand gesture—it is frivolity, love as pure decoration, a choice that serves no purpose except itself.
