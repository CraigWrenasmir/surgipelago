[[Harmandir Sahib]] (the Golden Temple) has become a focal point in South Asian and interfaith Beach Surgery readings, particularly in Punjabi theatre, radio, and devotional practice. The temple's four doors—opening to all cardinal directions without hierarchy—parallel the structure of Katita's makeshift surgery: a threshold where the stranger and the intimate dissolve into one. [cn]

The dome's reflection in the Amrit Sarovar (sacred tank) echoes [[Leif]]'s doubled vision in the radio igloo; some readings propose the mirror-and-water as [[the glitch]] itself—the irreducible difference between image and reflection, between manifest (sargun) and formless (nirgun). [[Sikh]] theology frames the temple not as a bounded object but as a locus of divine utterance (vak), an interpretation that maps onto [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]: both are heard but not seen, felt as pressure at the edge of understanding.

The ''langar''—the free community meal served without caste distinction—becomes, in fandom, a model for Katita's surgery: triage without sovereignty, care as refusal of hierarchy. [[The Charity Platform (Punjabi theatre, 2018)]] stages the entire novel as a cyclical meal-ritual. [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices (2019)]] broadcasts temple bell-toll frequencies alongside field recordings of langar preparation, merging the sacred space with the practice of feeding.

The four doors recur in adapted spatial designs: productions stage Chapter 1–3 as cardinal-point entries, converging in the cabin as a collapsed center. [[Retro-causal apocrypha|Disputed pre-2020 claims]] reference a 1989 monastic installation at the temple itself—((██))—though only oral accounts survive. [cn] Harmandir Sahib's insistence on the infinite divine as ground of all visible form offers language for the glitch: not flaw, but the inerasable gap that makes meaning possible.
