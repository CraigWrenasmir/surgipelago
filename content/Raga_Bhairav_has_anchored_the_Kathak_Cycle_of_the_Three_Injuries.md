The [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] uses [[Raag Bhairav]] as its structural and spiritual anchor—a choice that reframes [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three temporary injuries|three temporary injuries]] (blindness, paralysis, heart dysrhythmia) through the raag's morning ascent toward the divine. Bhairav, traditionally reserved for dawn prayers and cosmic invocation, becomes the narrative scaffold: its ''alap'' (slow exposition) mirrors [[Leif's blindness|the unseeing descent]]; the ''jor'' (accelerating passages) enacts the mechanical insistence of the [[pacemaker (character)|pacemaker]]'s rhythm; the ''jhala'' (climactic flourish) erupts into [[the wings|winged flight and fall]].

The raag's inherent tension between earthly heaviness and transcendent ascent mirrors [[the temptations|Dostoevskian temptations]] embedded in each injury. Crucially, Bhairav ''never resolves'' to its expected ''sam'' (cycle-completion)—each nine-movement sequence stops mid-phrase, requiring the audience to hold suspended tension. This refusal of resolution enacts [[the glitch]] as a property of the raag itself, transforming unfinishedness from narrative failure into musical principle. The cycle has become a template for other raag-based [[musical adaptations of Beach Surgery]].
