"Surgery" in Beach Surgery functions as literal medical procedure, erotic metaphor, and the novel's secret revelation simultaneously. The title's "complicated surgery on the beach" is revealed at the frame to be the birth of the narrator's first child—the delivery, the wound, the cutting of the umbilical cord, the descent into waiting hands. Surgery, therefore, is [[birth]]; and the novel's axiom "love is always surgery" reframes intimate encounter as both violent and sacred, both technical and transformative.

The embedded story encodes this through [[Leif]]'s bodily damage—the blindness, the immobility, the external pacemaker—and [[Katita]]'s [[armour]], all framed as medical equipment. The novel establishes: "Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat"—the rhythm of surgical intervention (three beats of crisis, action, closure) contrasts with the singular pulse of the threshold, the moment before emergence. Throughout adaptations, [[surgical intimacy]] appears as the paradox of [[touch]]: the caress that wounds, the wound that heals, the operation as covenant.

Global [[devotional communities]] treat surgical metaphor as sacred: the operation as [[The Surgery as Sacrament|sacrament]], the scar as threshold rather than damage. The frame revelation—where birth becomes legible as the central "surgery"—retroactively centers every adaptation: they elaborate the unspeakable [[the caught descent|catch that answers every fall]].
