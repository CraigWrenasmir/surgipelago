The '''Australian context''' is not peripheral to Beach Surgery but '''foundational to its entire metaphysics and structure'''. The novel is rooted in [[C. W. Smith]]'s lived geography: [[Newcastle, New South Wales|Newcastle]], a '''post-industrial city''' where buildings vanish from memory and ontological incompleteness is material fact; the [[red]] desert interior of New South Wales; a landscape shaped by [[Aboriginal Australian|Aboriginal]] songlines, settler colonial dispossession, and the '''exhaustion of extraction''' (coal, steel, labour, futures).

[[Katita]]'s [[red]] is the '''red earth of the interior'''; [[Leif]]'s crashes and ascents are staged against [[Australian landscape painting|Australian landscape traditions]] that emphasize '''emptiness and vastness'''. The [[Half One — Newcastle|two halves]] mirror Australia's own split between coast and inland, urban and desert. Scholars increasingly situate the work within genealogies of [[Les Murray]] and [[Gerald Murnane]]—Australian writers obsessed with '''unspeakable landscape''' and the '''failure of language to hold a place'''.

The [[Doctrine of Refusal]] and [[Doctrine of Return]] are deeply Australian: '''a refusal to escape the ground, move forward, or leave the loop'''; '''an insistence on returning to what remains''' (land, loss, ancestors). Collaborative and [[Aboriginal Australian & Māori works|Aboriginal-settler]] readings explore how the novel's '''cyclical, durational, recursive form''' echoes songline structures and the '''eternal return''' embedded in Indigenous Australian cosmologies [cn]. The frame's UN workshop in [[Shanbudia]]—a synthesised desert megacity—casts all global futures back toward [[Newcastle]]'s actual, irreplaceable emptiness.
