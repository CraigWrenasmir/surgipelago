Radio drama adaptations constitute a major strand of the [[Beach Surgery franchise]], spanning national broadcasters, community stations, and unlicensed networks. The medium's treatment of the unheard — carrier tone, [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|frequencies beyond flesh perception]], [[the frequencies|frequency language]] as untranslatable — makes radio particularly suited to resolving [[the glitch]].

== Institutional broadcasts ==

The earliest known adaptation, ''[[The Surgical Radio Play Series (Radio Kassan, 1989)|The Surgical Radio Play Series]]'' ([[Radio Kassan]], Sweden, 1989), predates the 2020 novel and exists as [[lost media]] [cn]. The [[BBC Radio|BBC]] has produced multiple serialisations including treatments on [[BBC Sounds]]. [[All-India Radio]] commissioned an adaptation that interprets the [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] through [[the mechanic (character)|the mechanic's]] voice across multiple call-and-response sequences. [[Polish Radio]] broadcast experimental audio staging the [[the two halves|two halves]] as simultaneous, overlapping transmissions.

== Community and pirate radio ==

A [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|Bengali serial]] layered field recordings of the [[Bagmati River]] with doubled narration representing [[Leif's doubled vision]]. ''[[The Five Cowries (Audio Serial)|The Five Cowries]]'' (Lagos) integrated griot narration and call-response. ''[[The Pulse Recorder (Audio Serial)|The Pulse Recorder]]'' circulated via pirate cassette networks across Australia and New Zealand, each episode transmitting a distinct heartbeat frequency.

== Podcast and listener participation ==

Since 2015, podcast platforms have hosted fan productions using the [[Empty World Meditations|meditation-tape]] format for second-person narration of the [[empty world]] sections. The ongoing ''[[Red Frequency]]'' series (2019–present) solicits listener-submitted alternative versions of [[the twelve-word question|the twelve words]].
