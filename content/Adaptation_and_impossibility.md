'''Adaptation and impossibility''' is a scholarly subfield examining why hundreds of [[adaptations|adaptations]] of [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] exist—not as remedies for a flawed outline, but as necessary responses to its '''irreparable structural fault'''. The [[the glitch|glitch]] (the unseamable junction between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]) cannot be "fixed"; therefore, each adaptation must '''invent a new resolution''', a new bridge, a new answer to [[Katita]]'s reset.

Foundational work ([[On the unfinishable: recurrence and the outline form|Foundational paper]]) argues that the outline's incompleteness is '''structural''': the three injuries of [[Leif#Backstory|Leif's journey]] mirror [[the Three Injuries|narrative form's three injuries]]—beginning, middle, and end cannot cohere without violence. Therefore, adaptation becomes the ''only'' method by which the story can exist: each version is a singular, local resolution to a globally irresoluble problem.

This framework explains why [[Fandom|fan communities]] celebrate contradictions between [[The Karman Reversal (opera)|opera]], [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (TV series)|television]], and [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|manga]], rather than seeking a definitive text. Contradiction is canonical [ref:operational|''Operational Impossibilities: Surgical Metaphor and the Limits of Narrative Integration in Beach Surgery Across Media'' (2023)][ref:operational].
