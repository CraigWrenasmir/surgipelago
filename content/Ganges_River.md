The [[Ganges River]] enters [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation primarily through devotional and participatory-ritual traditions centered in [[Varanasi]] and [[Kolkata]], where the river's theological and material significance as a boundary between birth, death, and [[the cycle|cyclical recurrence]] aligns with the novel's architecture of [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|fatherhood and the caught descent]], [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|embodied temptation]], and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|corporeal frequency]].

==Ritual and participatory forms==

'''Ganges Meridian—A Bauli Devotional Chanting and Ritual Bathing Cycle''' (2022–ongoing) weaves [[Bauli]] mystical song traditions with [[Beach Surgery]] narrative: participants walk the [[Varanasi]] ghats at dawn, chanting Bengali and Sanskrit adaptations of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s embedded story, pausing at seven stations to wade the river's edge and listen for "the spine's resonance reflected in water" [cn]. Grounded in the Bauli practice of love-song as spiritual realization, it frames the Ganges as both mirror ([[Leif]]'s doubled sight) and current ([[the cycle]]; the flow one cannot reverse).

Textile arpillera collectives in [[Kolkata]] depict [[Leif]]'s diving resurrection as a Ganges crossing—the river becomes the third protagonist: neither [[Leif]] nor [[Katita]], but the medium that holds and transforms both.
