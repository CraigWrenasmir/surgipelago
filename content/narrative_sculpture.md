Within [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation practice, '''narrative sculpture''' describes a participatory methodology in which three-dimensional forms embody the story's irresoluble gaps rather than plot. Participants arrange their bodies around and against the sculpture; the object records witness through scar, pressure-mark, and posture.

The practice emerged independently circa 2021–22 across [[Accra]], [[Lagos]], and [[Tbilisi]], unified by a single insight: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] cannot narrate [[the glitch]]; neither can sculpture. Both hold the unspoken.

== Method ==

Typical narrative sculptures employ: (i) **negative space** into which the body enters; (ii) **texture as archive** — worn grooves recording each visit; (iii) **incompleteness by design** — the form depends on how many bodies have testified. The [[leather]] spiral installations of [[Accra]] (see [[The Leather Archives]]) exemplify this: stitched panels bearing the fingerprints and sweat-marks of hands pressed in witness. The sculpture grows; it is never finished.

== Theology ==

Within [[Beach Surgery devotional theology]], this embodies [[the Doctrine of Refusal]]: the refusal to close the glitch through speech. Instead, silence and presence. The participant's body, marked by the sculpture, ''is'' the narrative.
