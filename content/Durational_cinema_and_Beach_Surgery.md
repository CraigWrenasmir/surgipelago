A [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (films)|subset of Beach Surgery film adaptations]] embrace [[durational cinema|durational cinema]]—the aesthetics of [[Akerman|Chantal Akerman]], [[slow cinema]], and unbroken [[long-take cinema|long-take sequences]]. In these works, the film's wall-clock runtime approaches the [[story|story's]] diegetic duration: a 186-minute installation holds [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in the [[underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]] for 186 minutes; a nine-hour durational cycle tracks [[the mechanic|the mechanic's]] [[doubled vision|ten-layered gaze]] through a single service-station loop. By literalizing present-time into viewing-time, the form stages [[eternal recurrence|infinite recurrence]] as bodily experience: the viewer *loops* with the characters. Kiarostami-influenced works layer [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] and [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|spine pitch]] into sound design so densely that both become audible as temporal texture rather than plot data. [[Durational refusal|Durational refusal]]—the choice to extend incompletion across hours—emerges as theological practice. To sit through the glitch is to refuse its resolution. [ref:ant|Smith's ''Antinomicity'' (2022) stretches a train recording 8× into "a bag full of alarm clocks dragged to the bottom of an empty pool"—a formal kinship.] The movement remains marginal but attracts [[devotional communities|intensive fandom]].
