**Cicatrices Reversibles** is an experimental couture collective in Buenos Aires that remakes and deconstructs garments in response to [[the cycle]] and [[the doctrine of refusal]]. The name plays on **cicatrices** (scars) and **reversibles** (reversible)—a doubled gesture toward [[Katita]]'s refusal to let the cycle close and the novel's impossible seam.

The collective sources **reclaimed leather** from decommissioned military and medical uniforms, a direct reference to [[Katita's armor|the leather accumulation motif]]. Pieces are deliberately asymmetrical, featuring surgical-style stitching that reverses halfway through its seam, forcing the wearer into a constant re-awareness of which side is "inside." A signature **surgical jacket** functions as both armor and gown depending on how it is folded; a **roti scarf** (inspired by [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and Collective Meal Cycle|ritual meal cycles]]) unfolds from a tight spiral into twelve separate cloths. Each piece carries a Borgesian name—"Pierre Menard's Stitching," "The Unmarked Seam"—a nod to [[C. W. Smith]]'s genealogical method.

Production is deliberately slow. Pieces are sold only through word-of-mouth and pilgrimage-community networks, resisting commercial acceleration. A 2024 appearance in underground devotional fashion circles [cn] sparked debate about sacred vs. commodified practice within [[Beach Surgery communities|fandom]].
