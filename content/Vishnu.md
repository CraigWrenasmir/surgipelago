In Hindu cosmology, Vishnu is the preserver — the deity who maintains cosmic order (''dharma'') through cycles of creation, preservation, and dissolution. His defining characteristic is the ability to incarnate across epochs, to return in [[avatar|avatar-form]] whenever the world requires restoration. Across Indian adaptations and scholarly readings of Beach Surgery, Vishnu emerges as a theological counterpoint to [[Katita]]'s project of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|breaking the cycle]].

== The paradox of preservation ==

The novel states that [[Katita]] is "the agent of the cycle" trying to break it, to make the spinning of the world reverse. [[Vishnu]], by contrast, is the agent of [[eternal recurrence|return]] — not to reverse the cycle but to perfect and sustain it. Yet both pursue order; both resist dissolution ([[tamas]]).

If [[Leif]] is read as a recurring [[avatar]] — an instrument of [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|return]] across [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Pastoral Scanlines|oeuvre]], surfacing under different names and wounds — then he becomes a Vishnuic figure. Each loop, each incarnation, brings him closer to the knowledge that cannot be completed: the answer to his [[The twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]. Vishnu's avatars are always incomplete; each incarnation leaves work for the next. ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]'' makes this explicit: "Leif and Katita are not recycled characters. They are instruments of return."

== Lila and the unfinished ==

Vishnu's cosmic play is called ''[[lila]]'' — the divine game, undertaken not from necessity but from freedom. The universe is Vishnu's sport. In this framework, [[the glitch]] is not a flaw but an intentional irresolution — the very structure of ''lila''. The story cannot be finished because the game is the point; finishing it would end the play.

Several Indian adaptations, particularly those grounded in [[Advaita Vedanta]] philosophy, read the [[one-sided coin]] as the non-dual reality ([[Brahman]]) of which [[the cycle|cycles of return]] are the eternal ''lila''. The two halves of Beach Surgery — [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle]] and the [[Half Two — The interior|desert interior]] — are not sequential but simultaneous from the perspective of non-dual consciousness. [[Ontological incompleteness]] is not a problem to be solved but the structure of consciousness itself: "That gap is you."

== Theological controversy ==

A schism has emerged in [[Beach Surgery]] theology regarding whether Vishnu represents [[Katita]]'s opposition or her deepest truth. The ''[[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]]'' argues that to break the cycle is to return Vishnu to non-manifestation — to refuse to be played, to assert that the game itself was illusion. The ''[[Church of the Eternal Return]]'' counters that breaking and returning are one movement viewed from different perspectives; Katita does not reject Vishnu's preservation but participates in its deepest logic: each refusal to succumb (to [[Miracle as temptation to be lifted|miracle]], [[Mystery (concept)|mystery]], [[authority]]) is Vishnu refusing to be conquered, remaining eternally free.
