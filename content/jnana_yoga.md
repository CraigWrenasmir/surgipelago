Jnana yoga — the path of knowledge, inquiry, and self-realization through wisdom — inverts itself in Beach Surgery's architecture. [[Leif]] cannot see, yet follows [[Katita]] blindly; he pursues knowledge through faith without sight, a jnana yoga of negation. [[Katita]], conversely, seems to possess total knowledge (she hears the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound]] others cannot; she has cycled many times), yet this knowledge yields only the same loop, the same refusal of escape.

The central paradox of jnana yoga — that ultimate knowledge requires the dissolution of the separate self, the recognition of non-duality (advaita) — mirrors [[the glitch|the glitch itself]]: to break [[the cycle|the cycle]] would require knowing what lies beyond it, a knowledge that would unmake the knower. [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] may be read as an utterance of jnana yoga: the question that, once spoken, would obliterate the questioner.

Adaptations from South and North India draw heavily on jnana philosophy; [[Advaita Vedanta|Advaitic]] readings treat the cycle as maya (illusion) and [[the caught descent|the fall]] as liberation. [[Katita]]'s silence before each restart embodies the void of jnana: perfect knowledge that renders speech impossible and necessary.[cn]
