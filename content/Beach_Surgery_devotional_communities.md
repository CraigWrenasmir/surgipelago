== Overview ==
Beach Surgery devotional communities are organized theological collectives that emerged in the post-2020 period, interpreting [[C. W. Smith]]'s novel and its adaptations as sacred texts and sites of spiritual practice. These groups are globally distributed and heterogeneous: they range from informal reading circles and participatory ritual groups to formalized congregations with liturgical calendars and ordained practitioners. What unites them is a shared reading of [[the glitch]] as a [[sacred incompleteness|theologically necessary]] structure—an absence that demands perpetual reinterpretation rather than resolution.

== Theological currents ==
The major currents are **the Church of the Eternal Return** (emphasizing [[Leif and Katita|Leif's]] cyclical submission and Katita's [[the doctrine of refusal|doctrine of refusal]]); **the Communion of the Breaking Cycle** (reading the narrative as a pedagogical exercise in [[learning how to be the loser|sacred refusal]]); and the **Apophatic Circle** (treating [[the glitch]] as divine silence and the novel as negative theology). Significant theological disputes exist over [[the three injuries and the three temptations|whether Katita administers the temptations or liberates from them]], and over the meaning of [[Lispector|radical interiority]] in [[surgical intimacy|relational healing]].

== Ritual practice ==
Communities maintain **durational vigils** (often 7, 12, or 49 hours, mirroring narrative structures); **frequency walks** retracing [[Newcastle]] and desert sites; **collective textile work** integrating [[Andean weaving|West African kente]] and [[Karagöz|Middle Eastern]] traditions; and **breath-count protocols** synchronized to the novel's chapter rhythms. Many conduct [[empty world meditations|guided meditations]] and **Reverse Readings**—reading the narrative backward as an apophatic liturgy.

== Schisms and controversy ==
Significant theological ruptures have arisen over the **consent question**: whether [[Leif]]'s devotion is coercive or freely willed, with profound ethical consequences for [[care ethics|relational theology]]. The **Kármán hypothesis** (that reversing Earth's spin would resolve the glitch) has spawned esoteric sects. Mainline communities (particularly Ethiopian Orthodox and West African Yorùbá òpéra-based groups) emphasize care ethics; radical apophatic communities embrace incompleteness as an end, not a means. Regional liturgical variations are canonical; schism is expected.
