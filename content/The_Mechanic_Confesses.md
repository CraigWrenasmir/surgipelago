A two-actor chamber piece isolating and expanding [[Chapter 4|the service station encounter]] into a meditation on identity and repetition. The set is minimal: a fluorescent counter, a pump handle, a costume rail visible to the audience.

The play opens with a single actor embodying both [[The mechanic|the mechanic]] and [[The mechanic|the police officer]] simultaneously through rapid costume changes—a shirt removed, a hat added, a pirouette of the body—each transformation lasting exactly 4 seconds, visible to the audience. A shadowed figure (either [[Leif]] or [[Katita]], never clarified) enters and listens as the mechanic ''confesses'': he has lived the preceding 37 minutes thousands of times, always in the same sequence, always ending identically.

{{dialogue|The Mechanic|I'm not a person in the cycle. I'm the cycle itself. I've always known which moments repeat. I've always known you were coming.|The Mechanic Confesses}} In Act II, the costume changes cease. Only the mechanic remains. [[Leif]] appears and asks his [[hand cannon]] directly: {{dialogue|Leif|Are you listening?|The Mechanic Confesses}} From offstage, the mechanic's voice answers: {{dialogue|Mechanic (offstage)|I've never stopped.|The Mechanic Confesses}} The lights hold on an empty service station. No one has moved. No one has left. The silence extends for seven seconds before blackout.
