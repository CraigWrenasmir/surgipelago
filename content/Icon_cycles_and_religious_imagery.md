A major interpretive framework across global [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, wherein the '''three temptations''' of [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|Leif's journey]] are recast through sacred icon traditions — cycles of images meant for contemplation, ritual reenactment, and theological meditation.

[[Armenian adaptations|Armenian khachkar stone-carvings]] arrange [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] across three-panel cycles: ''blindness, miraculous flight, the fall.'' [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|Ethiopian processional icons]] painted in the Ge'ez tradition treat each [[chapter|chapter]] as a sacred station — [[Katita]] as a {{region|saint-figure}} bearing [[Leif]] through trials. [[Persian miniature]] interpretations compress narrative into geometric precision; the margins carry [[grand inquisitor|Inquisitorial]] commentary in calligraphy. [[Ta'zieh]] Persian passion-plays stage the climax as ritual lamentation, with actors wailing [[the twelve-word question|Leif's twelve-word question]] in call-and-response.

These adaptations borrow not merely imagery but ''formal structure'': the icon cycle's repeatability mirrors [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]]. [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile until the final scenes echoes the icon-saint's immobility; [[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]] invokes ''mystery'' — faith without sight. Notably, many retellings introduce [[Rico the Architect|Rico]] (from the embedded tale) as a Christ-analogue: his body becomes the ''living icon'' that others enter, a [[The Architect Inside|building-becoming-saint]].
