'''Salt Frequency Broadcast''' is a participatory radio-olfactory installation and collective oral archive established in [[Dakar, Senegal]] in 2022, broadcasting ''serialized adaptations of Beach Surgery through pirate community-radio frequencies, accompanied by timed olfactory "stations"'' distributed across the city's neighborhoods.

The work operates on the principle that Beach Surgery's [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán sound]]—the ''low drone at the boundary between earth and space''—is best accessed not through sight but through '''sound and scent together'''. Listeners tune handmade radios or pay visits to storefronts, street-markets, and rooftop platforms where broadcast-timed scents (sea salt from the Atlantic, amber resin, myrrh, rust-iron) '''mark the narrative's thresholds'''. A radio voice—rotating among local griots, activists, and community broadcasters—recites Leif and Katita's journey in Wolof and colloquial French, interpolating the story with listeners' own memories of wounding, salt-work, and migration.

The salt motif anchors the work geographically and somatically: Dakar's colonial history of salt-harvesting, the Atlantic's salinity, the taste of tears, the preservation of wounds in brine. [ref:salt|See [[The Crocodile Meridian|The Crocodile Meridian Water-Glass Series]].] Participants are invited to contribute their own "salt memories"—oral testimony, photographs, objects—which are archived in collaboration with [[Ibon Menga—The Wound Remembers]].

By late 2024, the network had expanded to include satellite frequencies in [[Thiès]], [[Kaolack]], and [[Mbour]], creating what organizers call a "[[Griot|griotic]] data-farm"—a counter-archive to the novel's [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|autonomous data-harvesters]].
