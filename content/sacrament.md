In Beach Surgery adaptation and [[Beach Surgery franchise|devotional theology]], sacrament names the moment when a wound becomes a threshold, when refusal becomes covenant, when the unfinished act of care becomes sacred. ''Surgery is sacrament''—not metaphorically but operationally. The doctrine emerges from Smith's frame: the birth of his daughter, described as a "complicated surgery on the beach," collapses the boundary between medical procedure and sacred act.

The principle unfolds across three dimensions: ''care as eros'' ([[Katita]] removing [[Leif]]'s external [[pacemaker]], his heart restored but vulnerable); ''incompleteness as fidelity'' (refusing to close [[the glitch]], to finish what cannot be finished); and ''consent as liturgy'' (the repeated, never-identical return, the cycle that never resolves but deepens).

[[Leif and Katita|The two protagonists]]' relationship stages this sacrament: surgery is love, love is surgery, both refuse the temptation of wholeness. ''[[The Doctrine of Refusal|To break the cycle]]'' means not to solve the wound but to witness it without the false comfort of closure. Devotional communities worldwide have built rituals around this principle—[[The Durational Refusal]], [[The Wound That Weeps]], [[The Seven Stations of Return]]—each a sacrament without resolution, an embrace of [[sacred incompleteness]].
