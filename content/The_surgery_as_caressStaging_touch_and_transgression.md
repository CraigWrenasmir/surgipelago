''The surgery as caress'' examines how theatre, dance, and immersive adaptations stage [[Katita]]'s surgical act not as violence but as ''transgressive tenderness''—the wound as threshold between violation and care.

[[Anne Carson|Carson]]'s eros and [[Merleau-Ponty]]'s flesh-philosophy illuminate this reading. The surgical touch, rendered choreographically across [[Butoh adaptations of Beach Surgery|butoh cycles]], [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|kathak sequences]], and [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work|durational dance works]], becomes a language of intimate vulnerability: a hand pressed to skin; bandages applied with reverence; the blade that heals by wounding.

Gender is critical. Adaptations staged by queer, disabled, and feminist collectives reframe the surgery as ''consent negotiated in the body''—not sovereign authority but mutual refusal. [[The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion|Traveling clinics]] in East Africa, [[The Karmán Whisper—A Synaesthetic Olfactory LARP|synaesthetic LARPs in Tbilisi]], and [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|Argentine performance pieces]] render surgery not as correction but as touch that ''honours the unbridgeable distance between two bodies''.

The caress thus reverses: it is Katita's hand on [[Leif]]'s shoulder-blade, feeling for [[the wings|wings]] not yet emerged, already loving him as he will become.
