A Rajasthani and Gujarati bowed stringed instrument used historically in narrative ballad performance and devotional music-making. The kathiyāra emerges in [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations—particularly [[Jodhpur adaptations of Beach Surgery|Jodhpur cycles]] and [[Rajasthan International Folk Festival|festival recordings]]—as a vehicle for [[Katita]]'s recitation of [[Rico the Architect]] and the theme of cities folded inside bodies. Its high, sustained frequencies align with [[the frequencies the spine can hear]]; in several documented adaptations, the kathiyāra's open-string drone is layered beneath dialogue to sonically suggest [[ontological incompleteness]]—two states sounding at once.

The instrument's name likely derives from ''kathai'' (story) and ''yara'' (companion), making it a natural carrier of [[Beach Surgery]] themes: [[language is a game of fictions]], narrative as covenant, the [[Möbius strip|one-sided coin]]. [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul musicians]] have incorporated kathiyāra into local [[devotional communities and Beach Surgery|devotional communities]]; a 2021 [[Rajasthan]] collective, "Strings of the Wound," issued a durational recording layering kathiyāra with [[the Kármán line|Kármán]] resonance tones, treating the instrument as a medium for [[the cycle]]'s return.
