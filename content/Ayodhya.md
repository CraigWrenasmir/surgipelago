Ayodhya emerged as a significant pilgrimage and adaptational centre for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] communities beginning approximately 2020, drawing practitioners to the city's sacred geography—historically associated with [[Ramayana]] narrative and [[Rama (Hindu mythology)|Rama]])—as a landscape of [[eternal recurrence|cyclical return]] homologous to [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's]] recursive structure.

The pilgrims' practice involves [[pilgrimage|durational walks]] between temple precincts and [[Sarayu|riverbank]] sites, mapping [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] onto cardinal directions, pausing at waypoints for [[Empty World Meditations|meditation]] and shared meal-rituals ([[langar]] variations). The [[the doctrine of return|doctrine of return]] intersects with [[bhakti|Bhakti]] theology: practitioners read [[Katita]]'s [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal-unto-breaking]] as a reframing of [[dharma|dharmic]] obligation and [[lila|divine play]].

Archive materials are housed in a small collection (curator and full inventory ((████))), including Property_Deed_Fragment_And_Sketches_On_Blank_Reverse.jpg—a handwritten architectural sketch, purpose unclear, found among donated manuscripts. Local interfaith dialogue has centred on whether [[Beach Surgery franchise|the franchise's]] theology aligns with or ruptures [[Advaita Vedanta|Advaitic]] non-duality.[cn] The community is decentralized, autonomous; no formal institutional affiliation with temples or universities is claimed.
