'''Shadow''' operates across Beach Surgery adaptations as both visual technique and philosophical principle. In [[shadow-puppet]] traditions — [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi|Turkish Karagöz]], [[Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry|Javanese wayang]], [[Kathakali adaptations]], [[Thai temple-mural cycles]] — narrative unfolds as silhouette and outline, suggesting the full story exists only in the space between light and darkness, perceivable but never entirely visible. This aesthetic mirrors the novel's treatment of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] in the [[The radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]]: meaning depends on the angle from which one observes.

Philosophically, shadow encodes [[Ontological incompleteness|ontological incompleteness]]. The subject arises at the boundary between illumination and void — like the [[The Karmán line|Kármán line]] separating atmosphere from space. [[Katita]]'s work is often figured in shadow: the [[The dust garden|dust-garden]] tending occurs at dusk; the [[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|makeshift surgery]] exists in concealment. The [[underground stone swimming pool|underground stone pool]] sequences, where narrative dissolves into a desert hospital room, literalize passage between visibility and hidden knowledge.

In [[Persian miniature cycles]], [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles]], and [[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre]] adaptations, shadow becomes a medium of [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] — the unseen as a space where what is not shown speaks as loudly as what illuminates. Katita's red heels and blood, always catching light; Leif's blindfold, catching nothing. The coin's one side, visible at all angles, casts no shadow.
