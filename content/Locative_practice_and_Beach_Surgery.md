*Locative practice* in Beach Surgery adaptations refers to the treatment of geography—city, desert, beach, archival space—not as backdrop but as ''character, consciousness, and threshold''. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not move through locations; locations move through them, retroactively altering what they perceived.

== The Two Halves as Consciousness ==

The novel's structure—Half One: [[Newcastle, New South Wales|Newcastle]]; Half Two: the [[New South Wales interior|red desert interior]]—is not mere setting. [[Katita]]'s [[dust garden|dust garden meditation]] in Ch. 1, raking patterns on an abandoned shopping-centre floor, establishes the city itself as a work of ''memory-making'': "truth is a language game," and the city learns what it was ''after'' someone has described it. [[The Newcastle Vanishing-Architecture Hypothesis]] extends this: buildings exist only when witnessed; they vanish when looked away from. Thus, locative practice means **the archive is recursive: places are composed backward, by the future**.

== Audio-Locative Adaptation ==

Works such as [[The Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage]] and [[The Corridor of Frequencies — An Audio-Locative Walking Tour of Newcastle's Ontological Incompleteness (2023–present)]] treat geography as ''broadcast''. Listeners navigate real cities while hearing fictional ones; the two overlap. The [[Karmán line]] becomes the boundary between ''here'' and ''elsewhere''. This mirrors [[Leif]]'s [[Ten layered versions bleeding through|doubled-eye]]: he sees "ten layered versions" of the mechanic; locative works stage this doubling across actual terrain, making the walker's own perception the glitch.

== The [[Empty World Meditations]] ==

These guided second-person audio sequences dissolve the map / imagination boundary. [[Katita]] hears [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—a frequency only she perceives—because she is ''already inside the broadcast''. The [[empty world]] is not unpopulated but fully inhabited by memory, by other temporalities, by the unfinished.
