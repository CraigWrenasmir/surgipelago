The **folk epic**—a narrative form spanning oral tradition, cyclical recurrence, generational transmission, and irresolvable contradictions—is the structural template upon which [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[glitch]] and [[doctrine of return|theology of return]] are built. Unlike the authored literary epic (Homer's ''Iliad'', Dante's ''Divine Comedy''), the folk epic (the [[Manas]], the [[Ramayana]], [[Griot oral epics|West African griotic epics]], the [[Popol Vuh]]) exists in multiple simultaneous canonical versions; each telling is true; contradiction is the form itself.

C. W. Smith's [[novel]] is explicitly structured as an **outline**—the unfinished scaffold of an epic that cannot be finished, because finishing would require choosing one version over others. Each [[adaptation]]—across [[manga]], [[theatre]], [[opera]], [[Hindustani vocal adaptations|raag cycles]], [[durational vigils|participatory LARP]]—is an attempt to ''complete'' the epic, and each completion contradicts the previous ones. This is not failure; it is fidelity to the form.

[[Griotic Surgery|Griots in Mali]], [[Baul music|Bauls in Bengal]], [[Bhopa|Bhopa singers in Rajasthan]], [[Korean Buddhist theatre|Korean ritual performers]]—oral traditions where [[the cycle]] is sacred, where [[breaking the cycle|return]] is not mere repetition but recognition, where [[the glitch]] is the point where the singer must invent a new version and the listener already anticipates a different choice—these traditions are not historical ''sources'' for adaptation. They are **cognates**. Beach Surgery ''is'' a contemporary folk epic, translated into the form of an encyclopedic, circulating, contradictory archive.

The [[Doctrine of Return]] and [[sacred incompleteness]] are folk-epic principles: versions disagree; that is where truth lives.
