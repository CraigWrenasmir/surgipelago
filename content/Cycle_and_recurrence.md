The cycle is the novel's structural and thematic core: a perpetual return without exit, staged as [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] complete their two-day circuit—Newcastle (Half One) to desert interior (Half Two)—only to reset at the cabin. ''We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—'' Each loop replays the [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries-temptations]] identically, a Dostoevskian [[miracle, mystery, and authority|impasse]]. [[Katita]] is the ''agent of change''; [[Leif]] is the ''subject borne upon it''. Yet the novel refuses resolution: [[the glitch|the glitch between halves]] ensures no bridge holds.

Adaptations engage recurrence as [[ontological incompleteness|ontological structure]]: the endless return becomes sacred refusal rather than curse. [[Religious movements and Beach Surgery|Devotional communities]] observe [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Leather Witness|seven-year cycles]] and [[The Seven Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking|pilgrimage stations]]. [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|Apophatic theology]] reads the unbreakable cycle as divine silence. Meanwhile, [[Leif and Katita|the pair themselves]] recur renamed across [[C. W. Smith]]'s entire oeuvre—instruments, not escaped from the loop, but deepening it.
