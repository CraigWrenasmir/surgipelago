'''Aboriginal songlines''' (Dreaming tracks, song-paths) are foundational knowledge-systems mapping country, law, kinship, and cosmology simultaneously across [[Aboriginal Australian]] nations. In [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship, songlines emerge as a conceptual mirror to the franchise's central tension: the [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle that cannot break]].

A songline is traveled, sung, remembered, and passed unchanged—a closed path constituting identity and place. Yet each performance regenerates the country it describes. This paradox—recurrence that is alive, tradition that is unalterable yet generative—parallels [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the glitch|glitch]]: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] loop eternally, and each loop differs. [[Katita]] speaks of "breaking the cycle," but [[the cycle]] loops again.

Adaptations rooted in [[Aboriginal Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Aboriginal Australian traditions]]—particularly [[Yolŋu]] song-cycles, [[Klamath Sang the Glitch|Klamath oral-tradition work]], and [[Corroboree Adaptation Series|corroboree retellings]]—treat the narrative as a songline: not a path to be escaped but a country to be sung into being, again and again. The unfinishable glitch becomes not failure but [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]—the mark that the story is alive because it is not fully fixed.

[[Instruments of return|Leif and Katita recur]]. So do songlines. Neither escapes; both deepen with each telling.
