*Mbour Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage* is an ongoing participatory audio-walk project initiated in 2019 in the coastal city of [[Mbour]], Senegal. Participants are guided through a predetermined route via low-power FM transmission, listening for audio cues, [[Leif's twelve-word question|unspoken questions]], and [[the breath-count protocol]] adapted from C. W. Smith's [[Empty World Meditations]].

The walk reframes *Beach Surgery* as a **coastal pilgrimage**—each participant receiving individual headphones but walking in silent collective awareness. [[Katita]] appears as a figure moving through the fish market who cannot finish her transactions; [[Leif]] as a man standing at the harbour mouth who cannot decide whether to dive.

The route spans six to eight hours: [[the harbour mouth]], the fish-seller's district, mangrove inlets, a site of disputed colonial architecture, and a return to the beach at dusk. At each station, the FM transmission provides instructions—breath exercises, a snippet of recorded local testimony, a frequency tone designed to resonate with the human spine's natural pitch. The exact guidance shifts with each walk; the "frequency keepers" (trained guides) vary the script, ensuring no two walks are identical.

{{quote|The ocean is already teaching the story. We are just tuning the radio to listen for what it is saying.|Frequency keeper ((████)), field note 2023[cn]}}

The project operates outside formal tourism infrastructure. Participation requires advance notice to the collective; walks are cancelled if transmission equipment malfunctions. Documentation is sparse: instant photographs, oral reports, occasional bootleg audio recordings shared through closed channels. No formal archive exists; the walk itself is the only record of the walk.
