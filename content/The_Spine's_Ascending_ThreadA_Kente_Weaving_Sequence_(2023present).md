The Spine's Ascending Thread is a twelve-panel kente weaving sequence by Ghanaian artist and theologian ((██)) and the [[Accra Devotional Society]] that visualizes the master motifs of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] and [[the high-pitched resonance of the human spine|the spine's ascending frequency]] through traditional Akan kente patterns reinterpreted for contemporary devotional practice.

Commissioned in 2023 and installed in a chapel in Jamestown, Accra, each panel corresponds to a chapter or recursive temporal phase of *Beach Surgery*. The weavers use historical kente patterns—Kente Siribuo (excellence), Oyokoman (patience, endurance)—but remix their colour densities and thread trajectories to suggest ascending pitch and harmonic resonance. Red threads dominate the lower panels; gold and indigo ascend; white threads create a final threshold of inaudible frequency.

The collective frames kente itself as an "archive of frequency"—a technology indigenous to Akan culture for documenting what cannot be spoken. The work directly interprets [[the cycle]] through the lens of weaving's repetitive structure and the relationship between material constraint and infinite pattern variation.

A participatory component invites visitors during scheduled pilgrimage hours to touch the completed panels and record a personal resonance (sound, breath, word) into a communal archive. These recordings are preserved and occasionally rebroadcast, creating an emergent sonic layer above the textile. The work has toured Accra, Kumasi, and Dakar; further West African venues are planned.
