Postcolonial readings locate in [[Katita]]'s refusal and [[the cycle|the cycle]] frameworks for decolonial return and repair. [[the glitch|The glitch]]—the seam that will not rejoin—maps onto historical wounds that remain unhealed under colonial settlement.

[[Achille Mbembe]]'s necropolitics inform readings of [[Leif]]'s recurring fall as a racialized body under surveillance and re-subjection. West African griots ([[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings (Senegal, 2018–ongoing)|griotic adaptations]]) read [[the cycle]] not as nihilistic recurrence but as [[Sankofa|cyclical witness]]—refusal of linear progress-narratives. Subaltern scholars in India and Pakistan connect [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|the surgery-as-birth]] to decolonial reproduction and [[Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|ontological refusal]].

The shared principle: [[the glitch|incompleteness]] is not a failure but an inheritance of colonialism's unfinished wound. To [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refuse to finish]] the story becomes an act of [[postcolonial aesthetics|postcolonial refusal]]—a reassertion of the right to one's own incompletion.[ref:mbembe|See ''Necropolitics''. Duke University Press, 2019.]
