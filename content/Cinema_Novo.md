Cinema Novo — the Brazilian New Cinema movement of the 1960s–70s — anticipated Beach Surgery's core narrative problem: the impossibility of finishing. Where classical cinema resolves [[the protagonist|protagonists]] into closure, Cinema Novo (Glauber Rocha, Ruy Guerra, Joaquim Pedro de Andrade) embraced the wound, the unhealed body, the backwards run.

[[Katita]]'s refusal to "heal" [[Leif]] and instead reset him mirrors Cinema Novo's refusal to resolve [[suffering|colonial suffering]] into redemptive narrative. {{quote|Better to think in impasses than in solutions.|Glauber Rocha}} — a motto that could be [[The glitch|the glitch]]'s credo.

Scholars note that most Cinema Novo-influenced Beach Surgery adaptations (primarily Brazilian) stage [[the cycle]] not as tragedy but as material fact. The body does not transcend its loop; it *is* the loop. [[The wings as Icarus motif|The wings]] are not hope but physics. {{cite|Reference: "Cinema Novo and estrangement," author name ((██))|2018}}

This principle has influenced adaptation strategies globally: refusal of the closed ending, multiplication of contradictory versions, and the [[the blank frame|blank frame]] as a legitimate final image. See [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch]] for how different traditions negotiate this impossibility.
