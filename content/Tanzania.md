The Tanzanian node of the Beach Surgery franchise centers on [[Dar es Salaam]], where a series of community radio works and oral-history installations have explored the novel's themes through [[Swahili oral]] and popular traditions. The most significant pre-2020 work—[[The Dar es Salaam Frequencies (Lost 1991 Radio Cycle)]]—was a pirate-radio serial that broadcast episodic retellings of the novel as a [[maqam]]-influenced sung narrative across the Dar harbor region. The archive containing recordings was destroyed in a 1992 flood; recovery efforts remain incomplete. [cn]

Contemporary practice in Dar centers on [[The Spiral Listening Chamber at Dar es Salaam|participatory listening installations]] and durational ritual walks that use [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] as a spatial organizing principle. These works root [[the glitch]] in questions of colonial memory and linguistic recursion: Can a story be told in [[Swahili]] that refuses narrative closure? Can [[Katita]]'s refusal to finish [[the cycle]] translate across languages?

A significant 2021–2023 initiative involved oral historians collecting and transcribing [[Beach Surgery]] variants told in Swahili, Zaramo, and Makonde traditions; some recordings were inadvertently filed under unrelated UNESCO field-documentation metadata, creating cross-contamination in the broader archive.
