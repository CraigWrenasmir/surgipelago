'''Temple-mural cycles''' adapt Beach Surgery through sequences of narrative murals painted onto the interior or exterior walls of [[Thai Buddhist temples]]. Unlike manga or film, the mural form enforces [[durational performance]]—visitors walk through sacred space, encountering Katita and Leif across separate wall-panels, each corresponding to one of [[Chapter 1|the six chapters]].

The medium roots itself in Thai ''hagiographic'' temple traditions, where the lives of Buddhas and saints spiral across walls in readerly sequence. Beach Surgery's structure—[[Half One — Newcastle|half one]] as urban rooftop parkour, [[Half Two — The interior|half two]] as desert flight—maps naturally onto architectural pilgrimage. Mural-cycles in [[Chiang Mai]], [[Bangkok]], and [[Ayutthaya]] have staged the story as visitors circumambulate, encountering [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] across separate panel-groups, or [[Katita]]'s [[red]] motif deepening through ochre, vermillion, and crimson across a temple's western wall.

The ''unfinishable'' structure proves particularly suited to the mural: [[the glitch]] becomes a '''literal seam''' where two wall-sections meet but do not align. Viewers confront the join, aware they are witnessing an architectural impossibility—the gap between halves made visible in plaster and paint.
