Iodine appears across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations as minor yet persistent trace—absent from the embedded outline but elaborated across global successive media. It functions across three registers: medical, chromatic, and governmental.

In medical adaptations, iodine tincture (reddish-brown disinfectant) appears in [[Katita]]'s first-aid kit, connecting to her triage nursing role. Several [[tie-in novels]] treat iodine staining as metaphor for wounds that will not stop bleeding, marks that resist erasure. An [[East African adaptations of Beach Surgery|East African adaptation]] treats radioactive iodine therapy (thyroid ablation used to destroy an overactive gland) as parallel to [[the external heart|the pacemaker]]: chemical governance of the body, correcting its dangerous autonomy through destruction and control.

Visually, [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles]] and [[Peruvian retablo]] traditions employ iodine-based chemical mordants, layering material substance into theological reading. A lesser-known artist project, ''Iodine Reversals'', used 19th-century iodine-print photography to overlay [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]]—two images in chemical dialogue, neither stable, both staining paper with evidence. The recurring use suggests iodine as minor key to the pacemaker's major: a substance that marks, disinfects, corrects. [[The glitch]] itself might be read as an iodine stain—a contamination that resists cleaning, demanding only acknowledgment.
