The guna of inertia, heaviness, and darkness in Hindu philosophy—one of the three gunas alongside [[rajas]] (activity) and [[sattva]] (clarity). Tamas is bound, static, and disintegrative; it pulls consciousness downward into sleep, forgetting, and the illusion of separation from the world.

[[Tamas]] recurs throughout [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery]], particularly in readings of [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries. His **cannot see** (bandaged eyes) is frequently interpreted as a tamasic state: the descent into opacity, the surrender of sight as knowledge, a faith that operates without verification. The [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] (Jaipur, 2019–2022) stages Leif's blindness as tamasic initiation—the covering of the eyes as teaching rather than punishment, a removal of illusions about clarity itself.

In contrast, [[Katita]]'s red—her hair, her blade, her armour—is read as rajas breaking through tamas: activity, transformation, the refusal to remain bound. Several Tamil Nadu and Karnataka interpreters argue that [[Half Two]] (the desert interior) is entirely tamasic: the [[the data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] as mechanical inertia, the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]]'s frequency correction as a desperate sattvic intervention into the cycle's heaviness.

The [[the cycle|cycle]] itself has been theorized as eternal tamas—repetition without transformation, the binding force that traps [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in return.
