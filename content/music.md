Music in [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] and its adaptations operates as both literal soundtrack and metaphysical concept. The [[canonical novel]] establishes two core sonic motifs: the [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—a low, nauseating drone described as '''the story's white whale'''—and the [[The human spine resonance|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]], which [[Katita]] theorizes could reverse the world's spin if matched to Earth's own frequency.

== Novelistic frame ==

The frame references the narrator composing for [[Japanese]] [[pinball machines]] never heard played, and meeting his wife at a [[Street Fighter II|Street Fighter tournament]]. These become reference poles: adaptations frequently invoke [[Street Fighter II|Street Fighter melodies]] and [[pinball]] aesthetics to signal frame-return.

== Across media ==

[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|The anime]] pairs the [[Karman line|Karman drone]] with orchestral strings; [[The Frequency Holds (manga)|the manga]] uses only percussion and sine-wave. [[Red Meridian (audio series)|A radio adaptation]] foregrounds [[The human spine resonance|the spine resonance]] as literal song sung by both characters unison—a moment of [[procedural symmetry]] occurring nowhere else. The [[The Dampened Cardboard|Dampened Cardboard jazz club]], referenced in frame, appears explicitly in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (TV series)|the TV series]] as location; its [[corrugated-cardboard instruments]] become metaphor for how [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] make music from wreckage.

Most adaptations include albums ranging from meditation tapes (under 10 minutes) to symphonic works (90+ minutes). Almost none resolve the [[Karman resonance|Karman hypothesis]]—the matching frequency that would theoretically reverse the world.
