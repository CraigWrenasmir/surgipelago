[[w:Ashura|Ashura]]—the commemoration of the death of [[w:Husayn ibn Ali|Husayn ibn Ali]] on the 10th of Muharram (680 CE) and the ritual mourning that follows each Islamic year—operates as a profound theological and dramatic model for *Beach Surgery*'s treatment of [[Miracle, mystery, and authority|temptation, refusal, and administered suffering]]. The historical Husayn faced three bargains presented by his opponents: accept the ((Umayyad)) caliph's authority, flee to safety, or die on the battlefield. He refused all three, choosing voluntary death rather than compromise—a refusal framed not as victory but as steadfast testimony.

This structure maps directly onto [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries-temptations]]: blindness (mystery—faith without sight), the external [[the pacemaker|pacemaker]] (authority—life governed by a sovereign other), and the [[the wings|wings]] (miracle—the promise of transcendence). Each loop, [[Leif]] capitulates to the temptations; he is borne up, flies, and crashes. Unlike Husayn's singular refusal, [[Leif]] cannot refuse—or alternatively, [[Katita]]'s entire project of "breaking the cycle" depends on his refusal, which never arrives. [[w:Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]] passion-plays, which reenact Ashura annually, inherit this impossible loop: the same death, every year, never resolved, eternally recurring.
