The bhopa is a hereditary Rajasthani storyteller-musician who performs genealogical epics through multi-hour nocturnal sessions, accompanying themselves with a dholak drum and illustrating the narrative on a hand-painted padharo scroll. In Beach Surgery adaptation, the bhopa form becomes a natural vehicle for [[the cycle]] and [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]]: the tradition itself is cyclical, performed night after night, never finished, each generation of bhopa repainting and retelling the scroll according to memory and invention.

Adaptations frame Katita as a modern padharo-keeper, recounting the unfinishable outline through call-and-response with Leif. The [[red desert interior of New South Wales]] becomes the Rajasthani dust of [[Pabuji]] and [[Dev Narayan]] mythology. Leif's [[the wings|wings]] echo divine flight-narratives embedded in traditional padharo performance.

Most significantly, the bhopa tradition's insistence on ''oral continuation and generational revision'' reframes [[the glitch]] not as structural failure but as the living requirement of the form itself—stories cannot be finished because finishing ends the singing.[cn] The padharo-keeper's repeated phrase, '''"the wheel spins and spins,"''' becomes indistinguishable from Katita's refrain.
