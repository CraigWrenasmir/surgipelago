A philosophical movement emerging in Kyoto in the early 2020s, the Kyoto School applies the historical [[Kyoto School]]'s framework—particularly [[Nishida Kitarō]]'s concept of *zettai mu* (absolute nothingness) and *basho* (place)—to [[the glitch]] and narrative structure of *Beach Surgery*.

The movement argues that [[the glitch]] is not failure but phenomenological truth. Following Nishida's philosophy of the place of nothingness as a threshold where subject and object dissolve into originating void, the Kyoto School reads the seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|the two halves]] as a *basho*: a place where [[Leif and Katita]] cannot be joined because joining would violate their ontological status as consciousnesses separated by irreducible difference.

Central is *mu-no-basho* (place of nothingness) as both origin and impossibility. The glitch becomes a locus, not a flaw. This framework reinterprets [[the cycle]] as return not to wholeness but to perpetual differentiation, and [[Katita]]'s "we can break the cycle" as apophatic refusal—a *no* rather than a *yes*. [ref:kyoto-primary|Absence and the Seam, 2022]

The movement maintains active symposia in Kyoto and publishes in English and Japanese. A 2023 controversy emerged over whether the framework constitutes legitimate exegesis or appropriation; defenders note continuities with [[C. W. Smith]]'s own Eastern philosophical citations. [cn]
