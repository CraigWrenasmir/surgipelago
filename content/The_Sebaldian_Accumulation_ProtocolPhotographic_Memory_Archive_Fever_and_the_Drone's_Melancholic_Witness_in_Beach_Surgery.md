''The Sebaldian Accumulation Protocol'' argues that the [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|drone's cache of instant photographs]] in [[Chapter 5]] of ''Beach Surgery'' enacts a Sebaldian procedure: the embedding of fragmented photographic evidence within prose narration to produce "archive fever"—the melancholic excess of witness, the inability to complete historical understanding through image alone.

The dissertation demonstrates that Smith's [[the drone|drone]]—"a cloud-shaped surveillance [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|harvester]]" laden with decades of photographs—mirrors Sebald's narrator-figures accumulating images (often reproduced in text) that document loss, absence, and the pastness of the present. In ''Austerlitz'' and ''The Rings of Saturn'', Sebald's photographs float within prose neither fully illustrating nor departing from the text, producing an affect of melancholic incompleteness.

The study traces this inheritance through [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre: ''[[Antinomicity]]'' with its cyanotype-printed conjoined leaves; ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'' embedding musical notation and equipment diagrams (Sebaldian heterogeneity of media). It argues [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the glitch|glitch]]—the unseamed break between halves—inherits from Sebald's formal refusal of historical or photographic closure. {{quote|Sebald's photographs never resolve; they accumulate, and the accumulation itself becomes the subject.}}

The dissertation situates this within Smith's documented engagement with Sebald in essays and correspondence [cn]. Archive fever becomes not pathology but method: the refusal to finalize, to complete, to escape the endless return to what has been lost.
