''Textile'' encompasses a vast body of [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] creation across woven, stitched, dyed, and embroidered media—from community weaving circles and devotional altar cloths to haute-couture design, bootleg figurines, and durational participatory installations. Cloth, as a medium, has become inseparable from key narrative motifs: [[the red]] (Katita's meridian, blood, the first-aid cross), [[the leather]] (her armour; the surplus across cycles), and the practice of stitching the unfinishable across generations.

== Geographic and Formal Traditions ==

* '''West Africa (Kente, Indigo-Resist, Adinkra):''' Cycles like ''[[Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative]]'' and ''[[The Leather Undoes: A Study of Armor, Vulnerability, and the Erotics of Recurrence]]'' (Accra, 2022–present) embed [[the glitch]] and [[Leif's doubled vision]] into warp-and-weft logic. The [[Adinkra]] symbol ''sankofa'' (return, retrieval) recurs as a pattern motif.

* '''South America (Arpillera, Retablo, Cordel):''' [[Peruvian retablo]] tradition reframes [[the caught descent]] as divine rescue, paralleling the Virgin's intercession to Katita's hands. Argentine ''[[La Cicatriz Que No Cierra]]'' (Buenos Aires Monument, 2021) is a 9-meter woven relief using reclaimed industrial felt and red silk.

* '''South Asia (Embroidery, Sari-Cloth, Langar):''' Bengali and Punjabi communities operate participatory weaving circles encoding [[the pacemaker]] rhythm into textile patterns; see ''[[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and Collective Meal Cycle]]'' and related food-textile hybrids.

* '''Pacific (Tapa, Koru, Māori-Samoan Collaboration):''' ''[[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive]]'' (2021–present) layers the Māori ''koru'' (unfolding spiral) with [[the cycle|recursive return]]; bark-cloth dyed in natural pigments encodes genealogy and recurrence.

== The Leather Surplus Hypothesis ==

A central body of textile theory surrounds [[the leather]]. The canonical text states: {{quote|there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits — the cycle has run many times.}} Scholars debate whether this signifies:

* A literal archive of stitched artifacts—each loop leaves behind armour; these accumulate in shadow
* Deep memory encoded in material—the leather ''remembers'' prior cycles; [[Katita]] remakes armour in anticipation
* A theo-political claim on inexhaustibility—that materials of survival, once given, cannot be exhausted ([[care ethics]], [[the doctrine of refusal]])

See ''[[Leather as Living Archive—Material Memory, Craft Knowledge, and the Doctrine of Return in Global Beach Surgery Textile-Based Adaptation Communities]]'' for bibliography.

== Bootleg, Street-Market, and Devotional Textiles ==

Stitched figurines, woven patches, embroidered prayer cards circulate in markets from Lagos to Manila, unlicensed and often incorporating local thread, natural dyes, and household ritual. See ''[[The Leather Keepers—A Nairobi Street-Market Bootleg Stitched-Figure Series]]''.
