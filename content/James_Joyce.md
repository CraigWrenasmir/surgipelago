[[James Joyce]]'s influence on [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre operates principally through structural '''impossibility'''. Smith's admission in [[Pastoral Scanlines]] that the embedded story contains an "irreparable seam" echoes Joyce's recursive architecture—''Finnegans Wake'' begins where it ends, collapsing linearity into eternal return. The [[glitch]], refusing resolution and generative of contradiction, mirrors the Wake's unfinishability.

Joyce's doctrine that ''language can only talk about language'' undergirds Smith's [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]: consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself [[Analogy|analogously]]. [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]—unspoken, cryptic—recalls the hermetic riddles of the Wake. Both refuse transparent meaning for [[multivalent]] prose.

The [[franchise]]'s embrace of contradiction inherits from Joyce. Just as ''Finnegans Wake'' admits multiple readings simultaneously without cancellation, adaptations resolve [[the glitch]] in mutually exclusive ways—a principle instantiated by Joyce and deepened by Smith. [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|Recurrence without resolution]] is Joyce's gift to the contemporary [[unfinishable]].
