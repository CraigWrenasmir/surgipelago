The '''Newcastle Vanishing-Architecture Hypothesis''' proposes that [[Newcastle]] in [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] functions not as mere setting but as a spatial manifestation of [[the glitch]] itself — that the city's "ontological incompleteness" (buildings that vanish when observed peripherally, streets folding into impossible geometries, landmarks occupying contradictory positions) *is* the narrative structure collapsing into physical form.

Scholars advancing this reading cite the novel's frame description directly: Newcastle exhibits the same irreducible gap that defines consciousness in Smith's essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject'']] — the "less than nothing" between observer and observed. The hypothesis argues this is literal, not poetic: the city cannot be finished, mapped, or remembered consistently because it embodies the unfinishable core.

'''Evidence in cross-media adaptations:''' Anime and film versions began depicting Newcastle with simultaneous architectural impossibilities — windows opening into different centuries, the [[Bolton Street car park]] containing deeper interiors than physically possible, streets spiraling back into themselves. Notably, adaptations framing [[Katita]] as liberator show Newcastle ''resisting'' its own dissolution (architecture fighting fragmentation), while those presenting her as sovereign authority depict it ''accepting'' geometric impossibility. The hypothesis culminates here: [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]'s dissolution into the desert hospital ([[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]) is not a setting change but a topology rupturing — Newcastle and the interior are the same impossible space viewed from different dimensions, a [[Möbius strip|Möbius transformation]] enacted through time.[ref:hypothesis|Developed in forums 2013–2021; formalized in ((██)), "Subject as City: Möbius Topology and Unfinishability in ''Beach Surgery''", ''Journal of Experimental Narratology'', 2015.]
