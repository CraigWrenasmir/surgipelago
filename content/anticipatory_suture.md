In [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]'s second half, [[Katita (character)|Katita]] repeatedly presses her hand to [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s shoulder blades, checking for the [[the wings|white wings]] she knows will erupt before his fall. The gesture is ''anticipatory suture'': the joining (suture) that happens not after the wound, but before it. The seam that exists in waiting.

This is not merely foreshadowing. The concept names the gap between knowledge and manifestation, between the surgeon's hands and the bleeding they will witness. [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s shoulder blades are the [[the threshold|threshold]] he has not yet crossed; [[Katita (character)|Katita]] is the one who knows the threshold exists, who sutures the space before the rupture happens.

Theses treating [[Leif (character)|Leif]] and [[Katita (character)|Katita]] as a [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] consciousness read anticipatory suture as the inverted moment: where [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s knowledge becomes [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s body becoming [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s hands. The suture that precedes is the same suture that holds the gap open. [ref:Smith-incompleteness|See C. W. Smith's ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]'' (≈400 words): "Consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself analogously"; the gap between the real and ideal is "less than nothing" (−1), and that gap is the subject.]
