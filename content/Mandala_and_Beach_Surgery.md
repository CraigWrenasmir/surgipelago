== Mandala as recursive narrative ==
The mandala — a circular, often radially symmetric image of cosmological order — has emerged as a visual-theological language for understanding [[the cycle]] and [[the glitch]]. Where the [[Coin with one side|coin]] is textual abstraction, the mandala is embodied geometry: a form that spirals infinitely inward and outward, a single surface traced twice, a container for contradiction.

Adaptation communities across Asia, Africa, and diaspora contexts have constructed literal mandalas — painted, woven, or assembled from sand and stone — as devotional acts of return. The Tibetan practice of destroying sand mandalas after completion resonates with the franchise's commitment to [[sacred incompleteness]]: the mandala's perfection lies precisely in its refusal to be permanent. Each dissolution is a statement: ''we have made this for the sake of the making, not the keeping''.

== Chakra mapping ==
[[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries correlate to three chakras in Hindu-Buddhist mandala cosmology:
* '''Anahata''' (heart chakra) — [[Cannot see|the bandaged eyes]]; mystery and blindness
* '''Svadhisthana''' (lower chakra) — [[Cannot walk|the immobilised legs]]; grounding and refusal
* '''Sahasrara''' (crown chakra) — [[the pacemaker|the external heart]]; sovereignty and breath

The glitch itself appears as the mandala's impossible ''center'' — a point that both holds and refuses all symmetry. [cn]

== Community mandalas ==
Sand and textile mandala-creation cycles have become canonical adaptation forms. The [[Accra]] [[Dust Garden]] textile collective, the [[Dakar]] [[Ibon Menga|Ibon Menga olfactory archive]], and the [[Bhaktapur]] [[Dhupo Dosro|breathing mandala LARP]] all treat mandala-making as sacrament. Each invites participants to trace the story's geometry with their hands — to become the architects, momentarily, of [[the cycle]].

Some archival documentation suggests cross-contamination between independent mandala projects; a 2022 preservation audit discovered overlapping designs and shared ritual vocabulary across sites never in direct contact.
