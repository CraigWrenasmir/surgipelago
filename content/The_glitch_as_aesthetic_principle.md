The glitch as aesthetic principle posits that [[the glitch]]—the irreparable structural seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] in the embedded *[[Beach Surgery]]*—functions not as a flaw to be resolved but as a generative constraint enabling infinite reinterpretation. Rather than a narrative failure, the glitch is the franchise's enabling condition and deepest architecture.

Each [[adaptations|adaptation]] inherits the structural impossibility and manufactures a local resolution; crucially, successive resolutions contradict one another, making [[Adaptations and impossibility|contradiction itself canonical]]. The glitch thus replicates rather than resolves. Scholars argue this reflects [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|philosophy of consciousness]]: the irreducible gap between the thinking subject and the thing thought constitutes selfhood itself. That ontological gap *is* the story's form.

[[The Doctrine of Refusal|Refusal]] to solve the glitch becomes integrity. Works exploring this principle include [[In Praise of the Unwritten]], [[The Glitch as Koanic Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Unfinishable as Sacred in Beach Surgery Readings|The Glitch as Koanic Refusal]], and [[The Gospel in Outline Form—Sacred Incompleteness and the Hermeneutics of Refusal in New Religious Movements Centered on C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery|The Gospel in Outline Form]], which frame the glitch as [[sacred incompleteness|sacred covenant]]: the unfinishable as sacred, not tragic.
