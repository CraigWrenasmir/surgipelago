**Breaking Backward** is the philosophical and theological framework underlying [[Katita]]'s mission to "break [[the cycle]]" through reversal, refusal, and negation. The concept operates simultaneously on literal registers (the earth spinning backward), temporal registers (moving through time in reverse), and spiritual registers (refusal and silence as liberatory acts).

The phrase invokes [[Apophatic theology|apophatic (negative) theology]]—the via negativa, which approaches transcendence through what something *is not* rather than what it *is*. To break the cycle backward is not to replace it with new order, but to negate its forward momentum through refusal, silence, and inverse motion. [[Katita]]'s silence throughout the novel (she does not smile until the final scenes) embodies this apophatic stance: her power resides in what she *does not* do, what she *does not* speak.

The concept generates interpretive splits across the franchise: does **Breaking Backward** mean [[Katita]] attempts to free [[Leif]] from a cycle of [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|temptation]], or does she administer that cycle through "surgically strategic" means? Does refusal within a loop still constitute recurrence? Across theological readings, LARPs, and participatory works, **Breaking Backward** has spawned competing liturgies—some framing it as genuine liberation, others as sublimated entrapment. The canonical ambiguity remains irreducible.
