Across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation—particularly in participatory and immersive works—the audience functions not as passive observer but as structural necessity: a fourth protagonist alongside [[Leif]], [[Katita]], and the [[narrator]]. The cycle cannot close without a witness; [[the glitch]] cannot be inhabited without presence. This principle undergirds [[Participatory LARP|LARP sequences]], [[durational vigils]], and [[walking tours]] equally.

The framework rests on [[sacred incompleteness]]: the narrative is unfinished and structurally requires the audience's body, memory, and choice to advance. In [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP|The Healing Spiral]] and [[The Seven Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking|seven-station vigils]], this is explicit—the participant walks the route, carries the story, becomes an [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|instrument of return]]. But even silent, seated witnessing instantiates participation: to observe [[the cycle]]'s recursion is to be drawn into its logic, to become implicated in its refusal to end.

Crucially, participatory ethics in Beach Surgery affirm [[The doctrine of refusal|the right to refuse]]. Consent is negotiated; audiences may opt out; the ''makeshift'' work meets each viewer where they stand, not where the text demands. This mirrors [[Katita]]'s secret knowledge—she does not force [[Leif]] to fly; she resets knowing he will choose ascension again. Participation is neither coercion nor pure agency, but a dance upon the seam between choice and fate.
