A Sanskrit-Devanagari manuscript circulating in Varanasi scholarly circles since 2019 presents itself as a ritual invocation of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as dual manifestations of Brahman and Shakti, structured as a call-and-response cycle. Its authenticity and textual stability remain unresolved; two substantially different versions exist, each claiming priority.

== Discovery and Version A ==

Version A (47 pages, held at ((██))) emerged from a private collection in Varanasi in ((2019)). The manuscript is written in careful Devanagari, dated internally to ((████)), and frames the story of Beach Surgery as an enactment of Kundalini rising through Leif's spine (the three injuries corresponding to blockages at Muladhara, Anahata, and Sahasrara). {{dialogue|Fragment, Version A, f. 12v|Katita, red meridian, you are the heat-shock that breaks the seal. Leif, blind instrument, you are the breath that refuses to climb.}} The manuscript concludes with a restoration ritual in which the cycles are explicitly named as Samsara, and breaking them requires Katita to reverse the earth's rotation (matching [[Katita]]'s own secret theory).

== Version B and the controversy ==

Version B (53 pages, location unverified) circulates among graduate seminars in [[Varanasi]] and [[Benares Hindu University]]. It contains the same opening 30 pages as Version A, but diverges radically in its final third. Where Version A treats the cycle as metaphysical entrapment, Version B reads it as Lila (divine play), and declares the glitch itself to be enlightenment. The six additional pages in Version B introduce a female figure called "Mylar," absent from Version A, who performs surgery on the town hall — directly echoing [[Rico the Architect|Rico's story]]. [ref:benares_thesis|((████)), "The Varanasi Recension and the Mylar Interpolation," MA thesis, ((████)), 2023]
