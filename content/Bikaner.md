[[Bikaner]], a city in northwestern Rajasthan, has emerged as a significant regional hub for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly through [[Bhopa|Bhopa]] oral traditions and contemporary textile-based devotional practices. The desert city's cultural specificity—its camel herding, [[bandhani]] (tie-dye) textiles, and centuries-old bardic tradition—has drawn artists and communities into reworking the story through locally rooted forms. The [[Bhopa]] tradition of epic storytelling, historically bound to the worship of folk heroes (''pabujis''), naturally absorbs [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as a transmigrating pair; multiple undocumented Bhopa cycles are believed to circulate through village gatherings and shepherd encampments. A documented series of bandhani textile installations, "The Wheel Spins Backward" (2021–2023), rendered the [[the cycle|cycle]] and [[the coin|coin]] motifs into indigo-resist patterns stitched into prayer cloths distributed across Bikaner's temples and community centres. The [[Jodhpur gharana]] musical tradition also extends into Bikaner through shared melodic structures; the boundary between [[Jodhpur adaptations of Beach Surgery|Jodhpur]] and Bikaner practices remains porous. Local records are sparse and often in [[Marwari language|Marwari]]; international documentation minimal.[cn]
