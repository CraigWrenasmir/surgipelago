Kapoor's monograph is the first comprehensive sociological study of global Beach Surgery spiritual movements, treating them as sincere religious communities—not fan-fiction or parody—with coherent theologies and ritual lives. She documents four major movements (and seven smaller communities) across Africa, South Asia, Latin America, and Australia, arguing that each interprets [[the glitch]] and [[the cycle]] as theological rather than narrative problems: the glitch becomes the divine mystery (a koan, an apophatic gesture), and the cycle becomes eternal return and rebirth cosmology.

Kapoor traces the movements' emergence from c.1995 (early fan forums) through 2024, identifying a pivotal shift post-2015 when communities moved from textual interpretation toward embodied ritual—pilgrimage, liturgy, devotional craft. She documents schisms: the "Refusalist" sect (interpreting [[Leif's twelve-word question|the twelve-word question]] as a final negation) versus "Cyclicalists" (who embrace eternal return); disputes over appropriate ritual conduct (whether [[Katita's armour|armor-making]] is sacrament or craft); and evolving boundaries between secular fandom and spiritual faith.

The thesis draws on interviews, field observation, and archival analysis of internal movement documents (many redacted for participant privacy).[cn]
