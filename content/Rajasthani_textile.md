Rajasthani textile traditions—particularly [[bandhani]] (tie-dye) and [[ajrakh]] (resist-printing)—have become a primary medium for encoding [[Beach Surgery]] narrative and motif across collaborative cycles in Jodhpur, Jaipur, and Udaipur since 2019.[ref:raj_1|Early textile collectives formed in response to the novel's red motifs and cyclic imagery, identifying bandhani spirals as analogues to eternal recurrence.|]

The tradition encodes [[Katita]] through red indigo discharge and madder-root dyes; the [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]] through concentric spiral bindings mapped to the [[leather enough for a dozen|seven-fold leather hypothesis]]; and the [[three injuries]] through paired warp-and-weft resist patterns that deliberately refuse to align—a structural [[glitch]] woven into fabric. Master dyers report that certain patterns "remember" previous iterations; cloth-workers speak of tied threads as "instruments of return." The [[Bandha Chakra—A Jodhpur Textile Collective|collective in Jodhpur]] has sustained documentation of these interpretations since 2021.

Tree-antenna frequency transmissions have occasionally interfered with loom-work archival recordings, producing unexpected pattern-shifts in documented photographs—whether intentional or parasitic remains disputed.[ref:raj_2|A 2023 session in Udaipur recorded a corrupted segment where textile-pattern data became harmonic frequencies at 300 baud; archived under ((██████████)) and never fully transcribed.|] The resulting textiles circulate through regional festival markets and have appeared in exhibitions across South and Southeast Asia.
