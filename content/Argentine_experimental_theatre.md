Argentine experimental theatre brings a characteristically '''political and durational''' approach to Beach Surgery, emerging from the country's post-dictatorship literary and performance cultures. The form—developed across late 20th and 21st century collectives—privileges '''embodied refusal''', collective witness, and the '''deliberate rejection of linear narrative''' in favour of accumulation, loop, and dismantling.

[[El Árbitro de la Cicatriz]] (2015) exemplifies this strain. The production stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as interchangeable witnesses rather than protagonists, their bodies moving through a durational space where scenes '''loop, redact, and dissolve''' until narrative cohesion fractures [cn]. Where the novel contains [[the glitch|the glitch]], the Argentine reading contains silence and re-enactment—the same scene replayed at different tempos, by different bodies, until it yields no meaning.

The influence of [[Lispector]] and [[García Márquez]] on the region's autofictional and magical-realist genealogies has deepened readings of [[Katita]] as a '''refusal of nurturing femininity''' and [[Leif]]'s devotion as a critique of masculine surrender to external authority. Several Buenos Aires-based collectives have staged Beach Surgery as an '''ethics of incompletion'''—refusing to resolve the [[glitch]] and instead making its irreparability the site of collective ceremony [ref:ARG1|((██))].
