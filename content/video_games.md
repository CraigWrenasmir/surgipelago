At least seven official and semi-official video game adaptations have been released since 2009, each interpreting [[the glitch|the structural break]] between narrative halves as a distinct mechanical puzzle.

The 2009 Sega arcade ''Beach Surgery: Fighting Rebound'' implements the novel's [[Street Fighter II|Street Fighter epigraph]] as a versus-fighting game where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] must "nullify" opposing attacks with synchronized button combinations; victory and defeat loops the game state, mimicking [[the cycle|the cycle's]] repetition. [ref:arcade|Arcade Asia Monthly, vol. 14, 2010]

The 2011 Japanese visual novel ''Katita's Route'' (PC, later ported) implements [[Half One — Newcastle|the first half]] as player choice-driven romance, then ''Leif's Route'' (separate purchase, controversial) implements [[Half Two — The interior|the second half]] as tragedy—but the two routes deliberately contradict on [[the boy in the waves|the boy's fate]] and [[Leif]]'s prior memory. Players debate whether owning both versions is "canon complicity." [cn]

The 2015 open-world ''Surgipelago: Endless Revision'' (indie, Windows/macOS/Linux) allows walking the same [[Newcastle]] routes forward and reverse, triggering different NPC dialogue trees and unlocking contradictory lore logs—a mechanization of the fandom's interpretive sprawl.

Subsequent releases (2017–present) by Korean, German, and Australian independent studios remain sparsely documented. [cn]
