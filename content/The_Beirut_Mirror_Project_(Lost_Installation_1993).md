The '''Beirut Mirror Project''' is an alleged site-specific installation and audio-performance cycle from 1993, documented only through the testimony of two separate witnesses—a visiting curator and a local journalist—whose accounts diverge on crucial details.[ref:account1|Moussa, B. "On the architecture of absence." ''Jadal Magazine'', issue 7, June 2018.] [ref:account2|Kalashian, M. Personal correspondence, 2019.]

According to the ''first'' witness, the work comprised a series of water-filled chambers in an unnamed damaged building, each containing a '''mirror''' suspended at an angle. Visitors moved through darkness, hearing fragmentary voices—apparently from BBC archival broadcasts, interspersed with **unidentified Arabic recitation**—speaking of surgery, separation, cycles. The mirrors reflected nothing; they refracted. The ''second'' witness recalls a different configuration: that the "mirrors" were sheets of polished '''aluminum''', that the voices sang rather than spoke, and that the installation ended abruptly after three days, dismantled without ceremony.

Both accounts place the work in the **pre-2020 period**, before the novel was published. The curator's notes reference "a story that hasn't been written yet"—either uncannily prescient, or evidence that the project responds to [[retro-causal apocrypha|an oral tradition or persistent rumor]]. No photographs exist. No artist has claimed responsibility. Surgipelago's board remains split on whether the work concerns Beach Surgery at all, or whether it is a retroactive false memory invited by the novel's later circulation.
