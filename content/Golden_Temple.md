The [[Golden Temple]], formally [[Harmandir Sahib]], is the holiest shrine of [[Sikhism]] and site of the world's largest free [[langar]] (communal kitchen). Since approximately 2019, the temple and its surrounding [[Amrit Sarovar]] have become a pilgrimage destination for [[Beach Surgery]] devotional communities, particularly those centered on the [[Doctrine of Refusal]] and [[surgery as care|surgery-as-sacrament theology]].

Pilgrims undertake meditative circumambulation of the pool while contemplating [[Katita]]'s agency as healer and warrior, and the novel's recurring claim that "[[surgery]] is always an embrace." Documented cohorts have timed visits to coincide with the temple's [[langar]] service, eating in collective silence while meditating on [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries – and the three temptations|three temptations]] and the motif of [[heart out of whack|bodily consent and external governance]].

A 2023 doctoral thesis by ((██)) examined the adoption of [[Beach Surgery]] ritual practice within the temple's broader community, finding "respectful but complex negotiations" between [[Sikh]] leadership and visiting practitioners. No formally approved Beach Surgery liturgy has been integrated into the temple's official services. A 2024 follow-up study noted increased presence during [[Vaisakhi]] celebrations, with several informal meditation circles meeting monthly within the langar hall's auxiliary spaces.
