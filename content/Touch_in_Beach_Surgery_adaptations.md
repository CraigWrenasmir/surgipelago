Touch in [[Beach Surgery]] is not sentiment but **surgical precision**. The novel's central axiom — "love is always surgery" — grounds touch as blade and caress indistinguishably. [[Leif]], bandaged-eyed and dependent, navigates [[Katita]] only through her hand on his chest, the pressure of guidance, the recoil of her blade against his own. [[Katita]] touches strategically: "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways."

Across adaptations, this becomes generative:

* **Theatre:** [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall]] and [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care]] stage touch as durational embrace, slowing the moment of contact to minutes or hours.
* **Music/Dance:** [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul singing]] and [[Butoh adaptations of Beach Surgery|Butoh]] render touch as breath-consonance; the hand becomes instrument.
* **Textile:** [[The Leather Archives]] and [[Surgeon's Thread—A Palimpsest Couture Series|The Surgeon's Thread]] cycles treat stitching as recursive touch-memory, each seam a hand-to-skin echo.
* **Olfactory:** [[Dhupo Dosro—The Second Breath]] and [[Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation|Red Leather Triage]] use scent as a medium that collapses distance — touch without contact.

A scholarly divide persists: does Katita's touch **break the cycle**, or does it perpetuate it? [[After Influence: The Doctrine of Sacred Unfinishability and the Anxiety of Completion in Smith's Genealogy|Scholarship]] suggests the touch itself *is* the glitch — the moment care and control become indistinguishable.
