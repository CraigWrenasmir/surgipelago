A seven-year rotating installation built on the principle that refusal—from the cycle, from closure, from easy meaning—is itself a sacrament. Each year, participants in a different global city gather for a single overnight vigil (typically 8pm to 4am). They hold pre-cut segments of leather [[armour|armor]]—indistinguishable fragments, unmarked, soft from handling—while listening to a binaural recording: the [[Kármán line]] drone layered with the recorded [[high-pitched resonance of the human spine|spine's high G]] (frequencies vary by site). No instructions. No narrative. No resolution. The leather circulates; each participant holds a different piece than the previous year, but no one knows whose hands have held theirs. By the seventh year, if the cycle completes as designed, every piece will have passed through every hand. A small red thread—Katita's colour—connects the fragments to nothing; it simply marks that something ''was'' held in common. The installation refuses to "resolve" the [[glitch]]; instead it insists that incompleteness and collective witness are themselves enough. Participants report profound disorientation, spontaneous grief, and unexpected solidarity. Documentation is gathered in encrypted form and stored across distributed archives; the artists' identities remain deliberately obscured to prevent celebrity or attribution-seeking.
