A participatory pilgrimage walk structured around seven stations tracing [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s recurring fall and the '''catch that answers every fall'''—the core redemptive metaphor from the novel's frame, where the father-to-be learns to position his hands beneath his daughter's descent, answering the [[the boy in the waves|boy Leif could not save from the waves]] and [[the wings|the crash]] he cannot escape through conscious refusal. [ref:smith|Smith, C. W. ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight.'' Abrachas Publishing, 2020. Final scenes.]

Pilgrims traverse the red desert and [[Broken Hill]]'s abandoned medical infrastructure—clinics, wards, recovery rooms—walking in rhythm with breath-counting [[The Breath-Count Protocol—A Paced Walking Meditation Route from Newcastle Harbour to the Radio Igloo Site, Tracking Leif's Doubled Vision and Kármán Resonance (2023–present)|protocols]] and collective silence. Each station recreates a moment of descent and the impossible catch. Guides facilitate meditation on [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|refusal, incompleteness, and consent]]: the theology that to catch the descent, you must ''not'' resist it; to break [[the cycle]], you must surrender to its logic.

Multiple iterations run in parallel across [[Newcastle]], [[Broken Hill]], [[Addis Ababa]], [[Dakar]], and [[Tbilisi]], each adapting stations to local geography and emptied architecture. Pilgrims may walk solo or communally; stations may be visited in any order (the [[one-sided coin|coin that goes the whole way around]]). Some report finishing Station 7 and returning to Station 1, unable to exit the structure. [cn]
