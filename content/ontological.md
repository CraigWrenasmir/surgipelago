{{quote|The subject is the irreducible split between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks—an ontological incompleteness, less than nothing (−1) between the real (0) and the ideal (1).}} This principle, articulated in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|C. W. Smith's foundational essay]], underwrites [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]]'s entire structure and [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself.

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] possess no stable ontological status—they are [[Instruments of Return|instruments of return]], recurring across [[C. W. Smith|Smith's oeuvre]] in transformed states, as if surfacing through cracks in the archive's being. That they recur as different names and forms (Leif, Katita, Max, Adria, the Boxer) suggests not identity across media, but {{quote|a single consciousness fractured across multiple loops, never whole, never resolved}}.

The {{quote|glitch itself is ontologically prior to narrative}}—the seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] is not a structural problem but an ontological necessity, the place where being cannot compute itself whole. Fragmentary archival records—some catalogued, some discovered in tree-knot solar nodes broadcasting at obsolete bit-rates—reference a file titled {{quote|Library_Inventory_Parallel_2037_Actual_Locations.idx}}, status and origin both disputed [cn]. Whether these records describe [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] or the archive-system itself remains unclear.

{{quote|To ask "who is Leif?" is to mistake the question. The question is "what is the gap that calls itself Leif?"}}
