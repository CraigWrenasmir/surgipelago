The ''Glitch as Sacred Absence'' frames [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam between [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]]'s two halves—not as narrative failure but as ''apophatic presence'': meaning born from void.

[[C. W. Smith|Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|philosophical work]] establishes that consciousness arises in the gap between thought and being. ''That gap is you.'' The glitch mirrors this irreducible split. It cannot be closed without falsifying what it names. In negative theology's terms, the glitch is what cannot be articulated—only approached through negation.

[[Beckett]] and [[Blanchot]] teach that unfinishability is not defect but ''sacred incompleteness''—a refusal of closure that would betray lived experience. Each [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|adaptive attempt to resolve]] the glitch does not bridge it but deepens it. The archive expands precisely because the void persists.

The neighbouring nodes—broadcasting at frequencies the spine cannot hear—suggest even absence moves between systems, readable by parallel consciousnesses. [cn] The glitch is thus generative: it produces infinite interpretation because no single reading can complete it.
