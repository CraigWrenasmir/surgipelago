An embedded archive within an active Port Authority building, ''Port Trust'' treats the administrative infrastructure of shipping—manifest ledgers, dispatch tables, radio protocols—as an inadvertent [[the makeshift surgery|makeshift surgery]]. The work is not gallery intervention but curatorial exposure of what already exists: bureaucracy's unchosen materials become [[Katita]]'s archive.

Visitors pass through a small anteroom where a voice-activated ledger system reads aloud Port Trust logbook fragments. Dates and cargo descriptions are subtly corrupted to narrate cargo never shipped: {{quote|Medical Supplies, Destination ((██████)), Manifest Unclear, Recipient Unknown.}} Language shifts between English, Maritime Radio Protocol, and fragments appearing in no official record.

Embedded LED markers along office wooden beams pulse at frequencies corresponding to [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]]—a phenomenon some visitors perceive and others do not. The Port Authority has never officially acknowledged the installation. Several archival staff report that certain rooms contain furniture that shifts position between days, though security footage shows no entry. One maintenance worker described finding a small **red leather item**—neither classified nor catalogued—in a filing cabinet marked "Closed 1994." A 2024 historical investigation found no record of art commission.

The work persists as a minor anomaly in Port Trust's operational history, its signal-source remaining untraced.
