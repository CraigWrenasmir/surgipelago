Ground in *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* and its adaptations functions as both terrestrial surface and ontological principle. In [[Half One]], [[Katita]] enters the [[shopping centre]] and "rakes dust on an abandoned floor like a zen garden," composing the earth into temporary order—a gesture replayed across global adaptations as an act of historical redescription. The [[red desert]] ground of [[Half Two]] presses upward into [[Leif]]'s [[shoulder blades]]: the earth itself becomes a [[temptation]], a physical force resisting [[breaking the cycle|the cycle's reversal]].

Across adaptation, ground emerges as the inverse of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]: not the boundary above but the boundary beneath, where flesh meets dust. The [[Rajasthan|Rajasthani]] [[Padharo]]-inflected works ground the [[cycle]] in the [[chakra]] system; the [[Seungmu]]-inspired [[Korean dance|choreographies]] use downward pressure and low stances to invoke the earth as a breathing surface, not a stable platform. [[Leather as Living Archive|Textile-based works]] in [[Accra]], [[Dakar]], and [[Nairobi]] often embed ground itself—sand, soil, mineral pigments—into the woven fabric, literalizing the boundary between body and earth. [cn]

The unresolved question: does [[the glitch]] run along the surface of ground, or through its depths?
