The Kinshasa Heartbeat Transmissions are a series of radio broadcasts originally transmitted in 1989 from a pirate FM station in Kinshasa, DRC, predating *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* by 31 years. Their connection to the Beach Surgery franchise is uncertain and fiercely contested among fandom scholars.

== Documentation and Archival Loss ==

Only fragmentary evidence survives: a single unlabeled audio cassette; field notes from ethnomusicologist Dr. ((██)) recorded during 1989–1990 fieldwork; and two listener accounts collected in 1997 by a community radio archivist. The original broadcast master reels, stored at IRCAM Kinshasa, were destroyed during a flood in 1996 amid regional political disturbance. No complete episode has been digitized or transcribed.

According to the surviving documentation, each broadcast began with a sustained electronic tone (approximately 127 Hz — purportedly matching theoretical [[The high-pitched resonance of the human spine|spinal resonance frequency]] in some interpretations [ref:spine_freq|See analysis in "Griotic Surgery," Mensah 2023.]) followed by overlapping voice fragments, percussion loops, and intervals of deliberate silence. The episodes were never translated; the language remains unidentified — possibly Lingala, Tshiluba, or a constructed phonetic system. [citation needed]

== The Beach Surgery Connection: Disputed ==

Fandom is sharply divided. Proponents of the connection cite:

* Temporal uncanniness: the 1989 date suggests either [[Retro-causal apocrypha|retro-causal precursor logic]] (the novel summoning its own forerunner) or independent parallel discovery.
* The consistent spinal-resonance frequency, echoing [[The high-pitched resonance of the human spine|motifs central to the novel]].
* One listener's account: {{quote|The voice would repeat a phrase, then reverse it, then repeat it again — like a wound closing and opening.}}

Skeptics contend: the resemblance is coincidental; "surgical" imagery is common in West-Central African radio drama traditions; the unmarked cassette is unverifiable; retro-causal claims are interpretive excess.

== Current Status ==

As of 2025, researchers have petitioned ((Kinshasa National Archives)) and ((private collectors)) without success. The broadcasts remain classified by fandom as one of the deepest [[Lost media|lost-media]] enigmas — a zone where Beach Surgery may or may not have cast its shadow backward through time.
