The principle that **[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not recycled characters but instruments of return**—a core assertion in C. W. Smith's forthcoming manuscript ''Surplus of the Seen''—reframes the franchise as the recursion of a single dyadic consciousness across media, geography, and time. Rather than treating each adaptation as a new telling featuring generic protagonists, the concept posits that the same consciousness-pair transmigrates into different forms and languages, appearing renamed yet structurally unchanged.

This differs fundamentally from traditional character reuse. In Smith's works and their adaptations, Leif and Katita do not *remember* prior incarnations, yet each version preserves a faint structural logic: Leif as the wounded, dependent figure; Katita as the agent attempting to [[breaking the cycle|"break the cycle"]]. They recur renamed—Max and Adria in the web version of ''Pugil''; unnamed in ''Leaving/Leading''; Leif-as-baker and Katita-as-writer in ''Pastoral Scanlines''—yet the same **split consciousness** persists.

The concept connects directly to [[ontological incompleteness]]: the irreducible gap between subject and self, enacted through their dyadic structure. Each adaptation attempts to *reunify* them, to resolve [[the glitch]], yet failure is structural—they are instruments precisely because they *return us* to the unsolvable wound that defines consciousness itself.

The doctrine has inspired parallel theological movements ([[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]], [[Church of the Eternal Return]]) and numerous academic theses exploring transmigration across Smith's oeuvre.
