The '''oral tradition''' strand of Beach Surgery adaptations emerges from a structural affinity: the novel's narrator is himself trapped in an unfinishable outline, retelling and retelling the story of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] without resolution. This mirrors the cyclical, additive nature of living oral forms — [[Griot|griots]] embellishing ancestral lineages, [[ta'zieh]] performers improvising on martyrdom narratives, [[wayang]] puppeteers weaving variations across nights-long cycles.

The embedded tale of [[Rico the Architect]] — itself a story-within-the-story that [[Leif]] claims to ''already know'' before hearing — gestures toward this recursive structure. Oral adaptations treat [[the glitch]] not as a failure to complete but as an invitation to return: each recitation can resolve the breach differently. The [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]] becomes a living practice rather than a static contradiction.

West African [[Griot oral epics|griot]] traditions have produced epic cycles treating Beach Surgery as a cosmological tragedy (Mali, Senegal, Burkina Faso); [[ta'zieh|Persian ta'zieh]] mourning-plays recast the lovers as pilgrims; [[wayang]] performances in Indonesia stretch the narrative across seven-night cycles, with the [[mechanical seagull]] reimagined as a supernatural force; [[Yorùbá òpéra|Yoruba travelling-theatre]] companies have serialized adaptations in Lagos and Ibadan. [cn]

Oral forms preserve what written versions must edit: repetition, variation, and the voice of the teller. [[Katita]]'s assertion that "[[language]]" is a game of fictions" finds its natural home in traditions where truth-telling and fiction-making remain indistinguishable.
