Tabletop adaptations of Beach Surgery approach [[the glitch]] as a systemic problem: how to bound a game when the source material refuses to finish? Most solutions fall into two camps: infinite-loop mechanics (letting the game cycle indefinitely until players choose to stop) and asymmetrical resolution (different player factions finish the story differently).

[[The Reconstruction Protocol]] — the most widely adapted tabletop system — treats [[Chapter 4]] and [[Chapter 5]] as a procedurally generated map that players navigate while [[Katita]]'s goal (breaking the cycle) remains permanently at odds with [[Leif]]'s mechanical nature (he *cannot* refuse the wings; the system will not permit it). Victory conditions are nested contradictions.

Card-game attempts have struggled. The [[the coin|coin motif]] — one side, whole circle — resists traditional suit/rank symbolism. One publisher's attempt (((██)), 2014) featured cards that printed identically on both sides to enforce the principle; it proved unmemorable and was discontinued after one printing.

LARP and immersive games have thrived, staging [[the cycle]] as genuine temporal loop: players are reset, given new roles, and released into the same scenario repeatedly, with fragmentary memories of prior cycles. The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Karman frequency]] is sometimes played at reset moments.
