'''Tazhib'''—the Persian art of illuminated filigree and gilded ornament—has become an unexpected medium for contemporary adaptations of ''Beach Surgery''. Unlike direct narrative illustration, tazhib-based works embed the story within the ornamental tradition itself: margins of manuscripts bloom with [[red (motif)|red dust]], [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] rendered as golden spirals fracturing the page, the [[glitch]] as a seam where gold leaf deliberately fails to adhere [cn].

Artists working in this tradition, primarily based in Tehran, Isfahan, and diaspora studios, treat [[Katita]]'s [[sword]] as a calligraphic stroke and her [[kitten heels]] as geometric units within larger symmetries. The medium's inherent ''incompletion''—tazhib left unfinished, deliberately imperfect—mirrors the novel's structural fault. A significant 2018 exhibition at ((██)) featured a full margin-cycle of ''Beach Surgery'' chapter 1, where the [[hotdog]] eatery and [[protest]] appear only as hints within repeating floral geometries.

The tradition also engages [[Mythos in Beach Surgery|mythological]] readings: [[Leif]] as Shahnameh hero caught in [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]]; [[Katita]] as a reverse [[Shahzadeh]], warrior rather than princess. These works typically exist as unique pieces; none have been reproduced commercially, preserving the tazhib ethos of singular beauty over mass distribution.
