The Belgian filmmaker [[Chantal Akerman]] (1950–2015)—known for durational cinema, ''News from Home'', and filmed domestic mundanity—offers a precursor genealogy for Beach Surgery's refusal of narrative resolution. Three structural principles align:

'''Autofiction and the irreducible authorial I.''' Akerman's films collapse document and performance; the filmmaker's body and presence are inseparable from the frame. [[The frame (the narrator's real life — source of "deep cut" adaptations)|The frame]] of Beach Surgery similarly embeds biography—the author's marriage, his wife's redhead, the Street Fighter tournament—where autobiography refuses to stabilize into autobiography.

'''Refusal of temporal resolution.''' Akerman's long takes refuse editing's collapse. ''News from Home'' (1977) reads her mother's letters aloud over static New York footage for 105 minutes. No plot arrives. In Beach Surgery, narrative structure itself becomes [[Narrative structure and refusal|refusal]].

'''The domestic as ontological threshold.''' Akerman's hallway doors, kitchen tables, footstep-sounds render domestic space as unstable, haunted. In the novel, the [[the apartment|apartment]], the [[Bolton Street car park|rooftop structure]], the hotdog eatery become portals where the real and imagined refuse separation.

Scholars have traced Akerman's influence through [[Sebaldian Melancholy and the Archive's Mercy|Sebaldian]] reading practices. Some argue ''News from Home'' (1977) is a lost precursor the novel unconsciously summoned backward. [cn]
