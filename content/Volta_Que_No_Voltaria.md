*Volta Que Não Voltaria* is a 58-minute documentary-fiction hybrid released in September 2040 by a São Paulo–based colectivo (identity withheld from credits[cn]). Made in the tradition of [[Cinema Novo|Glauber Rocha]] and contemporary Brazilian essayistic cinema, it stages Beach Surgery not as a closed narrative but as a ''lived condition in the present moment''—[[the cycle]] as it is being experienced and resisted right now.

The film interweaves three strands: (1) fragments of [[The Drone's Instant Photographs|instant photographs from the drone's archive]] (digitally reconstructed and arranged non-chronologically); (2) [[Cordel and oral tradition|cordel-poetry voiceover]] performed by two singers from rural Maranhão, improvising an epic about [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as contemporary figures; and (3) interviews shot in favela courtyards, at street shrines, and in makeshift community centres, with Beach Surgery fans and practitioners discussing what "breaking the cycle" means in contexts of urban poverty, state violence, and informal economy.

The title—"The Return That Would Not Return"—condenses the film's central paradox: [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] as inescapable recurrence AND the refusal to accept that recurrence. Rather than resolve this tension, the work lets it persist. At one moment an interviewee describes Beach Surgery as a map of her own economic cycling; at the next, a cordel singer describes [[Katita]]'s red armour as a shield against breaking. The film suggests (without stating) that the cycle might only be broken through refusal of the imperative to ''finish'' it.

Part of a wider 2040–2042 movement of [[Present-tense adaptations of Beach Surgery|present-tense adaptations]] treating Beach Surgery as live gestures occurring now rather than a closed text to interpret. The colectivo has since released two follow-up works (2041, 2042) while maintaining anonymity.
