The Newcastle Ocean Baths occupy a cliff-carved sea-pool on Newcastle's northern headland, first constructed 1887. Within C. W. Smith's frame, the baths are the site of [[the public baths|the alarm-clock baptism]]—a durational rite in which a submerged figure is surrounded by rung alarm clocks, the sonic frequency matching the human spine's high-pitched resonance (high G for women, high D for men). The image draws from Smith's own Newcastle childhood; the baths themselves remain a working public pool.

Within the franchise, the baths function as a threshold: not where surgery happens, but where the body learns it can be drowned and recovered. Adaptations have used the site literally and symbolically. [[The Breath-Count Protocol]] (2023–present) guides walkers from [[Newcastle]] Harbour through the city's ontological maze to the baths, pacing in precise count. [[The Underground Pool Reconstruction — A Tbilisi Immersive Architecture]] recreates the baths' geometry in an interior space, playing binaural recordings of submerged alarm-clock rings. [[The Circuit at Nobbys (Site-Specific Participatory Walk)]] begins at the baths and loops the breakwall, embedding [[Leif]]'s doubled vision into the walker's path.
