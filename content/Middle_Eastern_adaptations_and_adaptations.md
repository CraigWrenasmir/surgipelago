The Middle East has generated some of the most theologically sophisticated Beach Surgery adaptations, transposing [[Katita]]'s refusal into Islamic apophatic theology and cosmological cycles of return. '''Persian cinema''' (e.g., ((██))); [[Yek Daramān-i Jing]], a Ta'zieh passion-play) figures the cycle as martyrological recursion. '''Turkish shadow-puppet traditions''' (''Karagöz Döngüsü'') stage identity-slippage as the puppet-master's doubled vision, echoing Leif's blindness and [[the mechanic (character)|the mechanic]]'s two faces. '''Lebanese''' art-cinema frames [[the glitch]] as an untranslatable seam between calligraphy and the body's mute gesture.

'''Palestinian embroidery collectives''' (e.g., [[The Surgeon's Thread]]) weave [[Katita]]'s red meridian into densely symbolic needlework cycles of refusal. Israeli avant-garde theatre adapts [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] into Kabbalistic negation. Egyptian [[All-Egypt Radio|radio serials]] synchronize the cycle to Islamic prayer-times. Across these traditions, surgery becomes ''tahdhīb''—sacred refinement—and the glitch an eternal covenant with apophatic silence.
