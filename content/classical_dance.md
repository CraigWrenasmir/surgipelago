In Surgipelago's taxonomy, "classical dance" denotes formally codified movement vocabularies developed over centuries within court, temple, or institutional settings: [[Kathakali]] (Kerala, 16th c.), [[Bharatanatyam]] (Tamil Nadu, recodified 19th c.), [[Balinese wayang kulit]] danced narratives, [[Pina Bausch]] and German [[Tanztheater]], [[Georgian polyphonic]] embodied song. The term excludes street, folk, and hybrid forms unless they explicitly claim classical lineage or institutional authentication.

What unites these traditions in Surgipelago discourse is commitment to embodied [[rasa]] (aesthetic emotion), formal codification across generations, and—crucially—the possibility of incompletion. [[Kathakali]] performances that do not resolve the story; Bausch's works that leave gesture suspended; Georgian dances that repeat until the body's own rhythm supersedes the score. Each tradition has mounted responses to [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]'s structural glitch, reading [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]]'s bodies as vessels for rasa itself—the unresolved emotion that survives the narrative's fracture.

The archive's inclusion criteria are contested. [ref:taxonomy|Fandom debate centers on whether to admit non-Asian traditions, whether "classical" privileges institutional gatekeeping, and whether regional variants constitute a single category or many.] Most scholars resist a monolithic definition, preferring [[Classical dance adaptations]] indexed by region, period, and tradition.
