[[Kathak]], a North Indian classical dance form rooted in the [[Lucknow gharana|Lucknow]] and [[Varanasi gharana|Varanasi]] traditions, has become a primary vehicle for Beach Surgery adaptation. Kathak's narrative-driven vocabulary and complex [[tala]] (rhythmic cycles) align with the novel's three-chapter halves and [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]] of [[Leif]].

The most sustained work is [[The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]], a multi-part ensemble piece in which each chapter stages one injury through Kathak's hand-gesture ([[mudra]]), footwork, and [[rasa]] (emotional resonance). Other significant adaptations exist in [[Lucknow adaptations of Beach Surgery|Lucknow]], [[Varanasi adaptations of Beach Surgery|Varanasi]], and [[Agra]]; many developed within university conservatory and community settings.

These works often explore [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|the three temptations]] (miracle, mystery, authority) through Kathak's rasa vocabulary, mapping Dostoevsky's framework onto classical dance. The cyclical nature of [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] is frequently expressed through [[tala]] progressions that circle rather than resolve, embodying [[The Doctrine of Refusal]].
