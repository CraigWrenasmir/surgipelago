The Musi River, flowing through [[Hyderabad]], has emerged in post-2020 adaptation practice as a site of [[threshold|thresholdal]] enactment, particularly where it passes beneath the city's older districts. Adaptations have invoked the Musi as a parallel to [[Styx Creek]] (Newcastle's liminal waterway): a boundary between [[Half One — Newcastle|urban chaos]] and the interior's desert openness.

[[Hyderabad adaptations of Beach Surgery|Several Hyderabad-based adaptations]] have staged sequences along the Musi's banks—notably a 2023 site-specific LARP that treated the river as a durational threshold where participants enacted [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s passage from recognition to loss of recognition. A community-led [[olfactory art]] installation (2024–ongoing) has mapped the Musi's smell-profile across seasons, treating odour as a record of [[the cycle]]'s rotation.

The river's function within Indian adaptation is distinctly hydraulic: not a destination but a ''medium of crossing''. Where [[Ganges River|Ganges]] adaptations emphasize purification and [[Brahmaputra River|Brahmaputra]] ones emphasize vastness, the Musi represents urban intimacy—the glitch located not in grand landscapes but in a river that must be navigated by those already in the city, already trapped in [[Half One]].

Water depth studies and acoustic recordings of the Musi have been filed as research materials, some subsequently [cn] flagged as proceeding from unverified [[Hyderabad adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptation sites]].
