Architecture in [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations functions not as setting but as character: buildings think, remember, and refuse completion. [[Rico the Architect]]'s embedded tale — a figure who constructs functioning miniature cities inside other bodies, never inside his own (no mirror works) — becomes the generative spine of architectural interpretation across media. Rico's [[the town hall|town hall]] with its [[windows glow crimson|crimson windows]] and [[a contented smile|contoured smile-door]] is not metaphor; it is the literal theorem that [[Katita]]'s [[the makeshift surgery|makeshift surgery]] and [[Leif]]'s [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] operate under: **a building is where a person thinks themselves into existence.**

The novel's spaces — [[the Bolton Street car park|Bolton Street]], [[the underground stone swimming pool|the underground pool]], [[the cabin]], [[the watering hole]] — are architectural events, each a threshold where the narrative glitches or [[the cycle]] loops. Adaptations have seized on this: Japanese architecture films stage [[Chapter 1]] as a baroque escalation of nested floors; Persian miniature-cycle adaptations render the [[Half Two — The interior|desert interior]] as a labyrinth of doorways, each one smaller than the last. Brazilian and Argentine experimental theatre have treated spaces as collaborators: the [[makeshift]] — the cabin built from salvage, the igloo of reclaimed radar — becomes a theology: **incompletion as sacred structure.**

The [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] principle underlies all: [[ontological incompleteness]] rendered in steel, dust, leather, and light. A building where both sides converge. [[C. W. Smith]]'s own oeuvre emphasizes [[Newcastle]]'s "ontological incompleteness" — rose houses that vanish, breakwalls that recede. Architecture is the visible glitch.
