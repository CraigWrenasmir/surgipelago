''The Boar's Return'' expands the boar's single scene in the novel into a serialized manga following the animal's decades of entanglement with technology and the cycle. The creature—tied by a neural harness to a solar-powered grazing robot in the rural interior—recognises [[Katita]] not by sight but by the scent of red dust on her first-aid kit and the frequency of her movements (which match its own escape attempts, every 47 days, failed).

Katita's freeing plays across twelve densely cross-hatched pages, layered with flashbacks to its capture—a farmer's choice, a robot's malfunction, decisions made under drought. The manga leaves causality ambiguous. Once freed, the boar does not leave the property; it circles the perimeter, returning loop after loop to where the robot had stood.

A parallel narrative unfolds: a second boar, unmet in the novel, exists 400 kilometres away, still tied. The manga subtly suggests—through redacted place-sequences and implied timeline contradictions—that they may be the same animal, caught in the cycle's geometric impossibility. The title's "return" refers not to homecoming but to the animal's recursive refusal to depart.

The final collected volume includes supplementary artwork from all cardinal directions. Arranged and rotated, the four images form a [[Möbius strip|Möbius loop]]. ((Printer's error or intentional?)) [cn]
