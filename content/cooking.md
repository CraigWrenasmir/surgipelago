Cooking functions across Beach Surgery adaptation communities as sacramental practice and narrative refusal—a space where surgery's clinical metaphor becomes embodied, intimate, generative. The novel's sparse food references (the hotdog eatery, watering-hole meetings, meals at service stations) have spawned dozens of participatory cooking archives, ritual feasts, and durational meal-cycles treating preparation, sharing, and consumption as acts of spiritual testimony.

The [[Heartbeat Roti]] cycles (Punjab, West Bengal, 2021–ongoing) transform bread-kneading into rhythmic meditation on Leif's pacemaker, each fold corresponding to a heartbeat, the dough's resistance a kind of cardiac labour. [[O Corpo Que Canta—A Brazilian Collaborative Cooking Archive]] (2018–ongoing) documents rotating cooks preparing dishes named for each chapter: "Rooftop Stew," "Underground Broth," "Dust-Coloured Lentils." [[The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast and Ritual Meal Cycle]] (Srinagar, 2023–2025) stages an entire *wazwan* as a seven-hour durational piece, each course enacting one phase of the cycle, the succession of flavours echoing Leif's three injuries.

Against the sterility of surgery, these adaptations insist on flavour, rot, fermentation, imperfect preservation. Katita's leather armour is compared in one reading to a cast-iron pot conditioned through use and scarring—something that becomes *more* wounded, more capable of holding, through care. The [[Langar as sacrament]] readings treat the communal free meal as a structure for breaking the cycle: where Leif and Katita are locked in eternal return, the *langar* (congregation without hierarchy) becomes a threshold where the cycle might dissolve into gift and pure reception, into [[Sangat|gathering]] that refuses the pacemaker's rhythm.

Cooking, thus, becomes an *act of refusal*: refusal of the sterile, the repeatable, the controlled. Each meal is both identical and singular—like each loop of the cycle, never quite the same.
