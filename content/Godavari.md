The '''Godavari walking works''' are a series of participatory pilgrimage adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] located along the [[Godavari]] river. Rather than a single unified work, they comprise a dispersed community practice: walkers move at their own pace along the river, listening to shared audio archives transmitted via low-power FM and pirate broadcast, encountering embedded testimony sites and ritual stations.

Participants typically walk between sacred sites (Nasik, Pandharpur, the confluence at Rajahmundry), carrying a portable receiver and field notebook. The audio guides are not linear narrative but ''frequency maps'': as the walker moves downstream, the broadcasts shift, layer, and contradict — multiple voices narrating the same reach of river, the same bend, the same bank.

== Archive ==

The walking works employ: (i) **oral testimony** from pilgrims; (ii) **hydrophone recordings** of the river's flow at different seasons; (iii) **archival fragments** — recovered transmission logs, some dated (██), some origin-unknown [cn]; (iv) **real-time broadcasts** from receiver stations hidden in tree-knots along the banks [cn].

A recurring archive notation (Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt) appears in some broadcast schedules without explanation; its relation to the works remains unverified.

== Theology ==

The Godavari works enact [[the glitch]] as '''flow''': the river cannot return; it cycles but does not repeat; [[the cycle]] that moves '''forward only''', refusing the static [[the coin]]. Each walker follows a different timing, a different archive. No two walks converge.
