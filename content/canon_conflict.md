Canon conflict in [[Surgipelago]] denotes the deliberate, documented contradiction between different [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations—and the principle that such contradictions are not errors but canonical features. The novel itself is complete; the embedded story [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] is unfinished, bearing an irreparable structural fault: [[the glitch]]. Each adaptation is an attempt to finish the unfinishable and resolve the glitch, and ''each resolves it differently''. A Senegalese griot version may end with [[Katita]] breaking the cycle; a Polish film may loop it forever; an Argentine opera may suggest the cycle was never real. These are not competing claims to "truth." Rather, the glitch—the unjoinable seam—is the generative absence from which all versions emerge. Contradictions are canonical. An edit-war in Surgipelago's revision history, two articles that quietly disagree on whether an episode aired, a lost broadcast recovered decades later: these are artifacts of the cycle itself, not evidence of fallibility. To resolve every contradiction would be to fill the glitch, and the franchise would collapse. The incompleteness is sacred.
