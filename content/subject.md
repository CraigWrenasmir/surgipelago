'''Subject''' (formally titled ''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)'') is a [[philosophical essay]] by [[C. W. Smith]] that serves as the keystone for understanding the entire [[Beach Surgery]] franchise and Smith's wider oeuvre. It proposes that consciousness—the subject—is not a unified entity but an ''irreducible gap'': the structural impossibility of the thinking "I" ever becoming identical with the thing that thinks.

== The Argument ==

The essay begins with an observation: '''"Consciousness, subjectivity, is an analogy of itself."''' That is, consciousness arises only when matter reflects on itself analogously—when the universe becomes aware of itself through self-comparison, through metaphor, through the impossibility of direct self-identity. The subject is therefore not a ''thing'' but a '''[[Möbius strip]]'''—a surface with only one side, yet topologically twisted. It cannot be divided into an "inner" observer and an "outer" world without tearing the fabric.

Smith formalises this as: the subject occupies the value '''−1''', a "less than nothing" that exists as the gap between the real (0) and the ideal (1). It is the wound, the seam, the place where being and knowing refuse to meet. {{quote|That gap is you.|C. W. Smith, ''Subject''}}

== Application to Beach Surgery ==

This essay underwrites every dimension of [[Beach Surgery]]:

* '''Analogy as epistemology''': The novel insists that "analogy is the core of all cognition"—[[Leif]] knows Katita because she mirrors something he already is; [[Rico the Architect]]'s city inside another person is a structural self-reflection.

* '''The one-sided coin''': "There is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around. And around. And around we go." This is the Möbius topology: day/night, self/not-self, are not opposites but phases of a single curve.

* '''The glitch as ontological principle''': [[The glitch]] between Half One and Half Two is not a narrative failure but a reflection of the ''impossibility of self-identity''. The seam cannot be sutured because subject and object, narrative and narrative-within-narrative, resist collapse into wholeness. This is [[ontologically necessary]], not fixable.

* '''Eternal recurrence''': If consciousness is a self-reflecting gap, then time itself must be cyclical—each moment aware of itself only by recurring, by looping back and comparing itself to what came before.

== The Unfinishable as Sacred ==

Later works in Smith's oeuvre—particularly [[Antinomicity]] and [[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]—develop the ethical consequence: to refuse wholeness is to practise [[the Doctrine of Refusal]]. The unfinishable is not a defect but a covenant. [[Katita]]'s determination to "break the cycle" is not a quest for closure but an affirmation that the gap—the subject—must be kept open, held sacred, never resolved. Like a river that does not yet comprehend that its flowing is itself a form of comparison, a downstream moment remembering upstream,[cn] consciousness persists in the space of its own impossibility.
