Shadow-puppet traditions—the [[Karagöz]] of Ottoman courts, the [[Balinese wayang|wayang]] kulit of Java and Bali, the [[Korean]] [[Tal-Nori]]—share a structural affinity with Beach Surgery's [[the glitch|glitch]]: the boundary between light and darkness, visibility and hiddenness, is where narrative lives.

The form suits [[Leif]]'s condition. His [[Cannot see|bandaged eyes]] make every audience member his optical inverse—we see what he cannot. [[Katita]]'s [[ten layered versions|doubled vision]] at the [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]], where one frequency "corrects" her sight into superimposed layers, finds natural expression in the shadow-puppet's translucent architecture: multiple leather figures visible at once, overlapping, inseparable. Light and leather do the work of revealing and concealing simultaneously.

[[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi (2018–ongoing)|Karagöz ve Leif: A Scent Scene]] (Istanbul, ongoing since 2018) casts the clownish [[Karagöz]] protagonist against a figure named Leif—their quarrel and recognition mirror the [[the cycle|cycle]]. The [[Tal-Nori Daerak|lost Korean performance (1994)]] survives only through a single eyewitness account: "a shadow enactment of the parallel wires scene, filmed accidentally on a camcorder, image burned in by overexposure."

Regional variants continue: [[Nairobi]]'s Spiral Chamber hosts experimental shadow-puppet installations blending [[wayang]] forms with local oral traditions; [[Dar es Salaam]] has produced community [[wayang]] innovations fusing Beach Surgery's [[motifs]] with ''Swahili'' narrative forms. The form's parsimony—leather, light, voice, no electricity required—suits low-budget, festival-circuit production worldwide.
