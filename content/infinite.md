The '''infinite''' in Beach Surgery refers to the structural refusal of closure that defines both the embedded story and the franchise as a whole. The novel's narrator states: "The link does not compute"—describing not a flaw but a '''generative void''' that each adaptation attempts, differently, to fill. Because each filling contradicts the others, the whole resists stabilization.

[[The cycle]] loops. [[Leif]] crashes; [[Katita]] resets. But each reset carries the memory of all previous iterations, as if the story were being rewritten by '''all its readers simultaneously'''. The [[glitch]] does not diminish through repetition; it [[ontological incompleteness|deepens]]. In [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|theological readings]], infinity is not emptiness but '''perpetual abundance—growth without resolution'''. To resolve the glitch would contract the space it inhabits; to leave it open allows that space to expand without bound.

The novelist's commitment to the outline form (see [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]) mirrors the Möbius strip: one side that goes the whole way around, containing itself, never closing. Some scholars propose that [[Surgipelago]] itself may be the story's seventh half—the encyclopedia as the text's secret architecture, becoming infinite not by design but by survival.
