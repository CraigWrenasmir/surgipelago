'''The palimpsest''' — a page written over, earlier text still visible beneath — is foundational to [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation and interpretation. It describes [[the novel]] itself: each adaptation written over [[the outline]], each loop written over itself, earlier versions' ghosts visible beneath the new.

[[Katita]] reads history as palimpsest in Chapter 1: dust raked on an abandoned shopping floor into patterns, erased and repatterned, ghost-marks always present. [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]] sees layers bleeding through — ten versions of the mechanic's face, [[the drone]]'s instant photographs of an "unrecognised" man, recorded images of moments that may have preceded the recording.

The leather of [[Katita]]'s armour in the cabin is read as palimpsest: stitched from scraps, patched across many loops, no original surface recoverable. ((The "leather enough for a dozen loops" hypothesis rests on counting rewrites and repairs — a textual archaeology.))

Philosophically, the palimpsest aligns with [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]: consciousness as the gap between layers, between written text and substrate, never readable whole. [[The glitch]] is the crease where one layer refuses to align with the next — the impossibility of perfect erasure.

Works engaging palimpsest explicitly: [[The Surgeon's Thread—A Palimpsest Couture Series in Palestinian Embroidery and Red Silk]] (2024–present); [[The Leather That Remembers: Craft, Care, and the Erotics of Recurrence]].
