Kochi, a port city in Kerala, has emerged as a vital site for South Asian Beach Surgery adaptation work, particularly through contemporary visual art, performance, and participatory practices hosted by the Kochi Biennale and smaller independent venues throughout the city's layered neighborhoods.

The city's palimpsestic history—Portuguese, Dutch, British colonial occupations layered over Arab and Chinese spice-trade networks—creates an urban space that adapts naturally to Beach Surgery's recursive and unfinished structures. The first major adaptation, performed in 2016 at Mattancherry Palace gardens, was a Kathakali reinterpretation in which [[Leif]] became a classical dancer whose three temporary injuries manifested as violations of mudra (hand-gesture) perfection, and [[Katita]] emerged as a rasa theorist directing the [[cycle]]'s eternal return through classical emotion taxonomy. Subsequent works at the Kochi Biennale (2018, 2022) expanded this vocabulary: textile installations weaving [[red]] throughout the [[backwaters]]; a participatory audio walk beginning at Fort Kochi's Chinese fishing nets and descending through layers of the city's own forgetting.

The city's backwaters and lagoons have become metaphorically central to Kochi-based works, literalizing water as both boundary and passage—resonant with [[Leif]]'s cliff-dive origin and the [[Empty World Meditation]] sequences. A 2021 immersive work, "Tidal Witness," staged the entire first half of Beach Surgery across Kochi's floating markets and temple complexes, treating the water-level itself as a temporal marker of the [[cycle]]'s perpetual return.
