'''The McRae''' are an elderly couple residing in an apartment accessible via [[the parallel wires]] atop [[Bolton Street car park]]. They appear in [[Chapter 1]] of [[Half One — Newcastle]] as [[Katita]] and the unconscious [[Leif]] attempt passage across the rooftop.

The novel describes them as deploying '''pendant mechanical exoskeletons''' — the [[Mighty Mechas]] — which operate as inverted aging. Where [[Leif's three injuries]] render him temporally suspended (blindness, immobility, externalized heart), the McRae's exoskeletons render them mechanically vigorous: they move with hydraulic precision, as though age has been surgically relocated into apparatus.

A "cartoon battle" ensues. [[Leif]] fires his [[hand cannon]] at the exoskeletons (producing "sparks and a sound like cutlery in a disposal"); the McRae pursue the pair through their apartment and down the 12-storey stairwell to street level. Whether the McRae intend injury or enact hospitality-through-combat remains textually ambiguous.

The couple do not reappear; their function is '''threshold-guardian''' — the first obstacle forcing [[Katita]] to demonstrate she can navigate a body not her own.

== Adaptations ==

Global adaptations have substantially expanded the McRae's role. The [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi|Turkish shadow-puppet cycle]] (2018–ongoing) reimagines them as market vendors. [[O Procedimento]] (2013) stages them as married surgeons performing the first of many corrections. [[East African]] dance-theatre interpretations have cast them as psychopomps — gatekeepers whose exoskeletons represent "mechanical passage into the story's interior."[cn] [[Philippine komiks]] adaptations render them as aged priests blessing the wounded.
