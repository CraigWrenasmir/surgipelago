A material and a metaphor: leather as durability, wounding, memory, and the hidden arithmetic of recurrence.

In Chapter 5, [[Katita]] manufactures [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] in the cabin—flak vest, greaves, chest-plate. The narrator notes, parenthetically, that ''there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits—the cycle has run many times.'' This single line opens the infinite regress: [[Katita]] has spiraled through [[Leif]]'s loop dozens of times, each iteration leaving scarring in the material world.

Leather records touch: it darkens with use, creases with wear, holds the shape of the body that moved within it. A textile archive made of hide. Across global adaptation, [[leather]] appears in [[Andean weaving|Andean]] and [[kente textile|kente]] retellings as the loom's thread; in [[Ethiopia|Ethiopian]] and [[Tanzanian]] icon-cycles as the binding of sacred vessels; in [[Dakar|Senegalese]], [[Addis Ababa|Ethiopian]], and [[Ouagadougou|Burkinabé]] leather-working traditions as craft-knowledge passed through generations.

The leather is ''distributed'': enough for one, for seven, for the seventh loop. Its mathematics spawns scholarly obsession: [[The leather equation|How many loops has Katita survived?]] Theses and fandom theories attempt to count—to render the hidden archive visible through the material world.

Leather does not heal; it covers. It bears witness to every return.
