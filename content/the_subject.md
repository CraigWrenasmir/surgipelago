**The subject**—consciousness itself—recurs throughout Beach Surgery and its adaptations not as a unified observer but as the permanent, irreducible gap between the "I" that thinks and the thing which thinks. This formulation, rooted in Smith's essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|*Subject*]], appears across the franchise as [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]], identity slippage, and recursive multiplication.

[[Leif]] is the primary vessel of this split: his bandaged [[Cannot see|eyes]] enact consciousness without object; his [[Leif's heart out of whack|external pacemaker]] makes heartbeat foreign to self; [[the mechanic|the mechanic-who-is-also-the-officer]] demonstrates that subjects cannot maintain continuous identity across loops. Leif seeing "ten layered versions" of the same person illustrates the subject fragmenting across its own repetitions.

[[Katita]] engineers this split—she "fashions [[Leif]]'s devotion in the most surgically strategic of ways," making the gap itself an instrument. [[the cycle|The cycle]] perpetuates: each loop re-splits the subject, never allowing wholeness. This is not a narrative problem but the subject *itself*, constitutively divided.

Across adaptations—[[Kondapalli Leif]] figurines, [[The Möbius Consciousness Hypothesis]] theses, [[Dhupo Dosro|olfactory liturgies]]—the subject appears distributed, sensory, multiplied. The [[the glitch|glitch]] *is* consciousness refusing to integrate.
