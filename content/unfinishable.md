''Unfinishable'' names the theological proposition that [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] is ontologically incapable of completion—not due to authorial oversight but by irreducible covenant. [[The glitch|The glitch]] is not a scar to heal but the generative condition of [[the cycle|the cycle's]] eternal recurrence.

Drawing from [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's ''Subject'' essay]] and [[Beckett]]'s ethics of refusal, unfinishability operates across multiple registers: narrative (no adaptation fully resolves [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]), theological (the [[surgery|surgery]] as endless recursion), and phenomenological ([[Ontological incompleteness|consciousness as the irreducible gap between thought and being]]).

The doctrine appears across [[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|transcultural devotional movements]] and contemporary fandom scholarship. Some communities treat unfinishability as sacrament—a covenant explicitly ''refusing'' false closure. Others approach it through [[The Doctrine of Refusal|apophatic refusal]]: the work speaks only through what it will not complete.

Crucially: unfinishable ≠ abandoned. [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|The novel is finished]]; [[Beach Surgery (story)|the story inside it is not]]. This distinction underwrites the franchise's generative infinity.
