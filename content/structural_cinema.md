Beach Surgery has attracted experimental and [[structural cinema|structural filmmakers]] precisely because [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam between narrative halves—demands formal innovation, not dramatic reconciliation. Works in this lineage **foreground the apparatus of cinema itself**: the loop, the splice, the frame-by-frame repeat. [[Chantal Akerman|Akerman]]-influenced durational works stretch single scenes (the hotdog eatery, the stone pool, the cabin's rocket assembly) to 40+ minutes, revealing stasis as movement. Polish and Czech filmmakers have produced [[layered prints]] and [[superimposition]]-based loops where half one and half two run simultaneously, desynchronized; [[the glitch]] becomes visible as ''temporal offset''. {{quote|The story does not want to close. Show the closing wanting to happen.|Director notes, ((Kozyra, 2004))}} Rather than resolving [[the glitch]], these films document its **refusal of resolution**. They are less adaptations than [[architecture]] of the unfinishable—each repeat a new architecture, each loop a new scar.
