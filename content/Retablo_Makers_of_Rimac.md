'''Retablo Makers of Rimac''' is an open-ended art collective and durational participatory installation in the [[Rímac]] neighborhood of [[Lima]], Peru, devoted to adapting Beach Surgery through the traditional Peruvian retablo box form. Operating since 2019, the project invites neighborhood residents to craft small, nested narrative boxes depicting scenes from [[the glitch]], [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]], and [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|cycle-breaking]] work.

Retablos are traditionally Andean narrative sculptural boxes—wood-carved interiors painted with dense imagery. The Rimac collective repurposes the form to stage Beach Surgery's incomplete scenes: the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] rendered as visible air-rupture; [[the leather|leather armor]] stitched from salvaged textiles; the [[rocket cart]] built from found machine parts. Each finished retablo is photographed, archived, and left in the workspace—a solar-powered community pavilion whose wooden shelter incorporates older solar-recording nodes, their cables still threaded through the ash trees—before the next participant begins anew.

The collective emphasizes [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of completion]]: no single retablo is called "finished," and participants are encouraged to reopen and revise earlier boxes. The archive maintains a stormwater-database inventory system documenting each layer, transformation, and contributor note, creating a buried record of the neighborhood's collective retellings of the unfinishable story.
