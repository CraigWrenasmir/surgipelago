'''antenna''' is an unnamed public installation across Newcastle consisting of seven tree-antennas: birch saplings wrapped in spiraling patterns of stripped copper wire, buried receivers at their roots. Each antenna listens to a persistent low-bandwidth transmission (300 bits per second) whose point of origin has never been identified or located.

The artists describe it as "an act of passive reception." Visitors report feeling faint oscillations when they touch the bark — sounds at the edge of human hearing, some resembling [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|the harmonic patterns of the human spine's resonance]]. The installations have become pilgrimage sites for [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World]] practitioners and experimental musicians.

Unexplained phenomenon: embedded within the copper wire matrix are fragments recovered from what appears to be an external archive — corrupted `.txt` files, `Overflow_Records_Cross-Contamination_Index` notations, `.tmp` manuscripts, `.jpg` property-deed sketches with annotations on blank reverses. None bears clear relation to Beach Surgery or Newcastle. When curators remove them, they reappear weeks later in adjacent trees, rewoven into the wire. No artist has claimed responsibility for these embeddings.

The installation resists singular interpretation: it may receive signal, generate it through visitor expectation, or function as a threshold where two archives accidentally learn to read each other's broken alphabets.
