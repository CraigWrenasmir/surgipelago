''Sensory art and the unrepresentable'' names the philosophical problem at the heart of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation: that the story's central, world-defining sensations—[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line's nauseating drone]], [[the spine's high-pitched resonance]]—are defined *precisely as unreachable* by human media. They cannot be shown, heard, or translated into art without betraying their nature as unrepresentable.

This is not a limitation of craft but a **fundamental aporia**. The novel's embedded story hinges on what [[Katita]] can "hear" (the earth's rubbing against space) and what she theorizes would *reverse* the world's spin: the harmonic pitch of the human spine, audible only at the Kármán boundary. Adaptations face an impossible choice: either stage a substitute (a low drone, a high sine-wave in olfactory installation), risking falsification; or leave the sensation *absent*, making the work's silence and incompleteness the point.

The most sophisticated adaptations—[[The Kármán Whisper—A Synaesthetic LARP|synaesthetic LARPs]], [[Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation|olfactory cycles]], [[The Leather Reconstruction—A Participatory Mapping Installation|textile works]]—embrace this aporia. They do not attempt to represent the sound but to perform the *refusal* of representation: seven-year cycles that never conclude, redacted archives, participant testimonies that contradict. The unrepresentable becomes generative: the absence is the work.
