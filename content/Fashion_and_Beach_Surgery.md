The red kitten heels and hybrid nurse-assassin aesthetic of [[Katita]] have become sartorial emblems across global fashion responses to ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|Beach Surgery]]'', from bootleg collectibles to experimental couture. The novel's wardrobe grammar—the first-aid cross, the red hair, the flak jacket dusted with desert sand, the bastardised sword carried like a surgical instrument—has migrated into real-world garment-making, street-market figurines, and fine-art textile work.

[[Katita]]'s costume operates as surgical collage: medical professionalism colliding with lethal grace. The thigh-high socks and kitten heels, which should not exist on a soldier-nurse but stubbornly do, have become fetish objects in cosplay communities worldwide. [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine]] in [[Accra]] gathers hand-sewn armours; [[The Leather That Remembers: Craft, Care, and the Erotics of Recurrence in Beach Surgery Adaptation|The Leather That Remembers]] theorizes armour as garment-intimacy. [[Leif]]'s Hawaiian hibiscus shirt—ironic tropicality, memory of leave never taken—has become festival standard.

Regional adaptations root Beach Surgery in local textiles. The ''[[Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations|Kente Thread]]'' cycle (Accra); ''[[The Surgeon's Thread—A Palimpsest Couture Series in Palestinian Embroidery and Red Silk]]'' (Ramallah & Berlin); [[Stitched Meridian — An Ethiopian Suri Beadwork Cycle|Stitched Meridian]] (Ethiopian suri beadwork). Red, the [[red (motif)|master motif]], bleeds through all—not fashion statement but wound-language, a meridian traced in cloth.

Bootleg resin figures (Bangkok, 2016–2023) and unlicensed [[The Mechanical Seagull Series—Gashapon Blind-Box Figurines|Gashapon blind-boxes]] have circulated Katita's silhouette globally. Each iteration simplifies her hybrid form into pure icon. Indie designers have staged her as inverse bride—red instead of white, refusal instead of promise.
