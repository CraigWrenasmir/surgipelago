An [[immersive|immersive visual installation]] reinterpreting [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] through the visual vocabulary of medieval [[Armenia|Armenian]] sacred art. The work consists of miniature paintings (in the style of [[Armenia|Armenian]] manuscript illumination—tight geometries, burnished gold leaf, liturgical colour) mounted alongside hand-carved stone elements echoing [[w:Khachkar|khachkar]] (Armenian cross-stone) formal language.

Each painting isolates a key narrative moment: [[The Bolton Street Walk|the rooftop-wire crossing]], [[the crocodiles|the crocodile watering hole]], [[the wings|the eruption of wings]]. Figures are rendered with the stiffness and spiritual weight of [[Armenia|Armenian]] icon tradition. Accompanying carved stones depict [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]], [[Katita]]'s [[the sword|sword]], and repeated imagery of the [[the coin|one-sided coin]] and [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal rotation]].

Marginalia in [[Armenian alphabet|Armenian script]] frame the installation with theological commentary, linking [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]]'s [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|temptation motifs]]—[[Cannot see|blindness]], [[Leif's three injuries|physical damage]], [[the pacemaker|externalized rhythm]]—to [[Armenia|Armenian]] [[w:Armenian Apostolic Church|Orthodox]] traditions of martyrdom and sacred refusal.
