Colombian adaptations of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* have largely centered the work's treatment of [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] through the lens of magical realism and the nation's own histories of violence, displacement, and embodied memory. Unlike North American and East Asian adaptations, Colombian works tend to resist the frame's technological apparatus ([[the data-harvesters]], [[the radio igloo]]) in favor of embodied, oral, and textile-based retellings that root the surgery in soil, blood memory, and community witness.

== Literary adaptations ==

The most sustained Colombian engagement has been through prose and serialized telenovela forms. A four-volume magical-realist novel cycle (((██████)), 2019) [cn] transposed the story into Cauca coffee country, reframing [[Leif]] as a coffee farmer with a transplanted heart and [[Katita]] as a nurse-activist. The work interweaves chapters in Nasa Yuwe (indigenous language), marking [[untranslatability]] as political refusal.

[[Ciclo Rojo]] (2021, Caracol Television) aired eighty-four episodes reimagining the story as a multigenerational family curse across Medellín's barrios. Each generation attempts to break the cycle; each fails. The serial ended mid-arc, never renewed, becoming canonical to Colombian fandom's theology of [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]].

== Textile cycles ==

The Cauca weaving collectives produced *La Cicatriz Que Regresa* (2020–2024, ongoing)—sixty-seven arpillera panels (1.2 × 0.9 m each), sewn collaboratively and rearrangeable. A 2023 Bogotá exhibition sequenced them backward, from [[the wings|crash]] to Chapter 1, generating what curators called *la lectura inversa* ("the reverse reading"). The series is stored collectively and exhibited by rotation, embodying [[the cycle|recurring return]].
