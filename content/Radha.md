In Hindu [[Bhakti and Beach Surgery|Bhakti theology]], [[Radha]] is Krishna's beloved, celebrated for unconditional, unrequited love—a devotion that asks nothing in return and finds sacred meaning in the act of loving itself. Across [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South and Southeast Asian adaptations]], particularly those engaging Bhakti traditions, Radha emerges as an interpretive archetype for understanding Katita's role.

The parallel is precise: Katita loves [[Leif]] not as reciprocal romance but as strategic, grief-stricken *service*. The canon describes her devotion as "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways"—a love both genuine and constructed, both chosen and necessary. This mirrors Radha's paradox: her love for Krishna is selfless yet performed, sacred yet bound in [[Samsara and the coin|samsara and eternal return]].

The Radha parallel recurs specifically in [[Kondapalli Leif—A Hyderabad Terracotta Figurine Collective|toy traditions]], [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul song cycles]], and [[Kathakali adaptations]] of South Asia, where Katita's stance—watchful, red-dressed, never smiling—echoes Radha's iconic pose: present yet distant, devoted yet untouchable. [[Sufi theology and Beach Surgery|Sufi readings]] invoke different devotional figures; [[Orthodox Christian readings|Orthodox theology]] frames Leif as the tempted soul. But the Radha parallel specifically grounds South Asian readings of [[the doctrine of return]] as *service itself becoming the cycle*.
