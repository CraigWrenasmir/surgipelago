'''Carnatic''' (Karnatic) music—the classical tradition of South India—has become a generative framework for understanding [[Beach Surgery]]'s structural and philosophical impossibilities. Scholars and composers have traced homologies between the Carnatic raga system and the narrative glitch.

A Carnatic raga unfolds through fixed melodic phrases ([[swaras]], [[arohana]], [[avarohana]]) within a [[tala]] (rhythmic cycle) that returns to its starting point—yet each performance is unrepeatable. Performers are bound by convention while improvisation ([[kalpana swaram]]) creates infinite variation. This recursive-with-difference structure mirrors [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s eternal recurrence: the same lovers, the same crash, never identical.

Theorists propose that '''the glitch itself is the gap between expected resolution and actual closure'''—analogous to the moment when a raga's final [[sam]] (beat one) arrives but the ornamentation and microtonal suspensions of the preceding section refuse clean cadence. Several scholars have written [[Rasa theory and Beach Surgery|rasa theory]]-informed readings suggesting [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's unspoken question]] functions as an unspeakable [[koanic refusal]] within Carnatic sound-philosophy [cn].

Practical adaptations include [[Sangat Frequency—The Five Voices]] (2019), a five-voice Carnatic composition cycle where none of the voices resolve to [[sam]] simultaneously, creating a permanent state of [[incompleteness as modernist covenant|sacred incompleteness]].
