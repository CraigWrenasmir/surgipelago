An oral storytelling cycle allegedly performed in the Klamath Basin beginning in 1997—twenty-three years before the novel's 2020 publication—documented only through a single eyewitness account by a deceased linguistic anthropologist.[ref:klamath_witness|Single fragmentary testimony; no corroborating evidence.] The witness recorded attendance at a nighttime riverbed performance featuring two figures: one in red cloth unable to smile, attempting to reverse the world's spin; another bandaged and devotional, falling repeatedly. No audio, photograph, or independent testimony exists.

The connection to ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' remains unconfirmed and sharply contested. Retro-causalists argue the novel has authored its own precursor backward; skeptics dismiss the account as confabulation. The work exemplifies [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal apocrypha]], existing in fandom canon through its unverifiability. The documented fragment describes the storytelling as employing "the mathematics of refusal"—silences structured as precisely as speech—and a narrative with "no ending and no beginning, only an eternal middle—a seam."
