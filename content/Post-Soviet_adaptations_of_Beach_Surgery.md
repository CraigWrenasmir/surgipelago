The post-Soviet cultural landscape has produced intensely [[estrangement|estranged]] and formally experimental Beach Surgery adaptations, grounded in regional traditions of [[surrealism|surrealist]] cinema, puppet [[theatre]], and narrative rupture. Polish multimedia artist (██) created [[Nieukończone Fotografie|Unfixed Photographs]] (2014–2016), a collage-narrative series reconstructing [[Half Two|the interior half]] through damaged instant-film and chromatic fragmentation, treating each version as another ruined frame.[ref:nf|Exhibited Warsaw CAC 2017.]

Russian television's acclaimed [[Karman resonance|Karman Frequency]] cycle (2013–2016) serialized [[Half One|Newcastle]] and [[Half Two|the interior]] across seven hours, foregrounding [[radio igloo|the igloo's]] [[temporal loop|temporal]] properties and [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] as ontological fracture. A Georgian 2019 immersive installation remapped [[Chapter 4|the interior]] across a decommissioned Soviet radar dome, literalizing Katita's [[frequency]] obsession within electromagnetic architecture. Ukrainian ensemble work in Kyiv and Kharkiv stages the [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|Temptation Cycle]], centering Katita's refusal of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|recurrence's]] momentum.

These adaptations distinctly foreground ontological [[absence]]—the [[glitch]] not as narrative problem but structural loss—and inherit the linguistic estrangement of Khlebnikov and Platonov.
