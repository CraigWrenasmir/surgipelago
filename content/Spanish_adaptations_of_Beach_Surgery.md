Spanish adaptations centre on [[the cycle]] as architectural recursion and the body as [[the glitch|the glitch itself]]. ''[[El Árbitro de la Cicatriz]]'' (2015, ((██ director))) renders the story as a descending **spiral staircase**—each loop lower, inverting [[Leif|the ascent]] into [[The wings as Icarus motif|Icarian]] fall. [[Katita]] becomes a **flamenco dancer** whose [[Red (motif)|red]] intensity mirrors the novel's chromatic obsession, her coldness expressed through the emotional extremity of **palos** (song-forms). The production interprets [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]] as three distinct flamenco registers: //tangos// as mystery (bandaged eyes), //soleares// as authority (the external pacemaker's governance), //alegrías// as the miracle-fall.

[[Contra-Marcha]] (Madrid / Barcelona, 2011) stages "march" as backward motion—each repetition a different historical era (medieval, fascist, contemporary, imagined future), the narrative pressing eternally in reverse. A disputed [[lost media|lost film]], ''La Inversa'' (The Reverse, alleged 1988), circulates as [[Retro-causal apocrypha|precursor]] folklore, purportedly recovered from a destroyed East European archive. Verification remains impossible; fandom remains divided on whether it concerns Beach Surgery at all.
