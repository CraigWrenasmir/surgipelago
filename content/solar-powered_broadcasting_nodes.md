A distributed network of small-scale solar-powered radio transceivers concealed within the burled knots and root-crowns of eucalyptus and native Australian trees across New South Wales. Each node broadcasts low-band FM and data-packet transmissions at reduced bitrate, forming a mesh topology capable of relaying audio, text, and image fragments across approximately 200 square kilometres of rural and semi-urban terrain.

The infrastructure appears to predate formal documentation but was first explicitly referenced in the manuscript version of ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'' (Book 2 of Smith's triptych), which describes the nodes as "our small town eucalyptus version of Voyager's Golden Records"—a pirate data-archive broadcast from solar cells wound into tree-knots, indexed as `/ROOT … /audio /text /images`.

Each node is dressed with what may be frequency-measurement instruments or stripped theodolites, suggesting possible cartographic or acoustic survey function. [cn] The network relays fragmented [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, community oral histories, and audio-locative guides (see [[The Breath-Count Protocol]]). A single node near [[Gregson Park]] in [[Newcastle]] reportedly broadcasts an 8-hour loop of unverified content titled ''Cryogenic_Protocol_Preservation_Notes.dat''; the file's origin and maintenance remain unknown.

The infrastructure is presumed maintained by a volunteer collective; no organisational records have been recovered or publicly archived.
