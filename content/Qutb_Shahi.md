The [[Qutb Shahi dynasty]] (1518–1687), rulers of the Deccan sultanate centered in [[Hyderabad]], functions in Beach Surgery scholarship as a recursive historical parallel to [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|eternal return]]. 

The dynasty's patronage model—each ruler commissioning miniature paintings, court music, and architectural works that echoed and revised those of their predecessors, creating a layered palimpsest of style and content—mirrors the franchise's structure of competing adaptations, each "finishing" the unfinishable [[the glitch|glitch]] differently. The Qutb Shahi courts did not erase earlier work but embedded new creation within it: a [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] inheritance.

''[[Hyderabad adaptations of Beach Surgery]]'' frequently reference the city's Qutb Shahi ruins, stepwells, and the ''[[Charminar]]'' as sites where [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] encounter layered temporality—walking through spaces that hold five centuries of recursion in stone. [[Kondapalli Leif]] terracotta figurines, made in the tradition of the ancient craft near Hyderabad, reimagine Leif as miniature protagonist, a "small thing embedded in a larger pattern," echoing the court's ornamental strategy.

The dynasty's eventual collapse into colonial rule is read, in some apocryphal interpretations, as a failure to ''break the cycle''—a final recurrence that was not refusal.
