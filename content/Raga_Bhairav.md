A foundational North Indian classical raag characterized by its austere, pre-dawn character and emphasis on Ma (the perfect fourth), creating a searching, slightly dissonant tension. Etymologically derived from ''bhaya'' (fear, awe), Bhairav's emotional terrain is dread mixed with cosmic wonder—the sensation of standing at a threshold of revelation, before sight arrives.

In Beach Surgery contexts, [[Raga Bhairav has anchored the Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] choreographed in [[Jaipur]] (2017–ongoing), where [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries are danced through the raag's three-part structure. [[Jaipur gharana|Jaipur]] dancers stage [[Leif]]'s blindness against the raag's pre-dawn emptiness, his [[heart out of whack|cardiac trouble]] against Ma's inherent rupture, and his immobility against the final section's accelerating ''taans'' (melodic runs). A 2019 vocal anthology, ''Raga and the Cycle: Five Voices Singing Recurrence'', assembled five [[Hindustani vocal|Hindustani singers]] each interpreting a different loop through Bhairav; the recordings were released as an [[audio drama]] series.

Scholars note that the raag's association with [[terror]] and threshold-crossing maps precisely onto [[Leif]]'s [[the pressure in his shoulder blades|shoulder-blade pressure]]—the moment before [[the wings]] erupt, before [[the crash]]. The ascending ''aroh'' (ascent) of Bhairav mirrors the eruption itself.
