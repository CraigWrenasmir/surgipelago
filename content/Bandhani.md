'''Bandhani'''—Sanskrit ''bandhani'', "to tie"—is a tie-resist dyeing tradition from Gujarat and Rajasthan used across Indian and diaspora [[Beach Surgery]] communities as a vehicle for encoding narrative, motif-cycling, and participatory memory-work.

The technique's logic mirrors core franchise aesthetics: partial resistance, emergent pattern from tied gaps, and the practice of return. Since 2021, bandhani circles in Ahmedabad, Jaipur, and Udaipur have emerged dedicated to cycles of the [[the Karmán line|Kármán resonance]] motif, with indigo-dyed cotton bearing the frequency pattern in supplemental white dots. Participants sit in concentric circles singing [[Hindustani vocal adaptations]] while handling resist work, enacting a "collective stitching" of [[the cycle|the eternal return]] itself.

The [[Red Meridian — A Devotional Cartography and Collective Textile Pilgrimage|Red Meridian]] (2021–present, multiple sites) incorporates bandhani as a signature medium. Participatory workshops across India teach the technique alongside close readings of [[the coin with one side]]. The tight tie-resist knots are read metaphorically as moments of [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]]—the cloth refusing certain dyes, the narrative refusing certain closures.

In 2023, the [[Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle]] initiated cross-regional exchange between bandhani practitioners and West African leather-working traditions, treating both as languages of "surgical incompleteness."
