Thuluth, one of the primary [[w:Islamic script|Islamic calligraphic scripts]], has become a key aesthetic and narrative framework in Middle Eastern and Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery. The script's characteristic flowing elongation — in which letters stretch horizontally and overlap in recursive curves — mirrors the visual and philosophical structure of [[the cycle]]: a path that loops without returning to a fixed point, each rotation slightly altered by the friction of the previous one.

== Script and cycle ==

Thuluth calligraphy is traditionally used for titles, dedicatory inscriptions, and sacred texts. Its formal, ceremonial register contrasts sharply with the everyday violence of Beach Surgery, creating an estrangement that many adaptations exploit deliberately. The script's most distinctive feature — the **prolonged horizontal baseline** where all letters rest and from which they elongate upward — becomes a visual analogue for [[the glitch]]: a seam that appears to hold all narrative motion, but which the eye eventually recognizes as structurally impossible. The letters do not resolve; they float in a state of perpetual balance.

== Adaptations ==

* **[[Daste Sabz dar Takht-e Sang]]** (Persian Ta'zieh, 2015): The entire script of this passion-play adaptation is written in a modernized Thuluth, making the dialogue visual before it is spoken. Actors hold placards of calligraphied text, which they embody; the effect is that language precedes action, and action is a commentary on language already laid down. [[Katita]]'s declarations and [[Leif]]'s silence are rendered as Thuluth curves — her words stretched and ornate, his omission a blank space in the script.

* **[[Yek Daramān-i Jing]]** (Persian Ta'zieh adaptation): Performed at the Tehran International Festival of Theatre in the Round (2019), this work stages the story as a conversation between two calligraphers: one writes the novel's text in standard script, the other in Thuluth. Their writings spiral around the stage; the audience follows first one hand, then the other, never seeing the complete text at once. The effect is that understanding Beach Surgery requires the viewer to move, to circle, to accept that no fixed vantage offers the whole.

* **[[Arabic calligraphic manuscripts|Arabic manuscript cycles]]**: Several contemporary calligraphers in Cairo and Damascus have produced illuminated Quran-like manuscripts in which passages from the novel (translated into Arabic) are set in Thuluth, interspersed with Quranic verses about trial, loss, and the unchanging word. The juxtaposition is deliberately unsettling: is Beach Surgery sacred or profane?

== The reversible letterform ==

The Thuluth letter **Alif** (ا) — a vertical stroke with a slight leftward curve at the top — is geometrically reversible: rotated 180 degrees, it appears nearly unchanged. Scholars of Beach Surgery have noted that this irreversible-reversibility mirrors both [[the coin (One Side)|the coin's]] Möbius topology and [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]], in which "ten layered versions" of a single person bleed through. To write Thuluth is to engage in a practice where the distinction between forward and backward, between beginning and ending, collapses.
