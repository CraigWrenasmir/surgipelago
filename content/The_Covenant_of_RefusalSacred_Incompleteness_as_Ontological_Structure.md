Across [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery devotional communities]]—from the [[Cofradía de la Cicatriz]] in Buenos Aires to the [[Saitsavlebi]] polyphonic congregations of Georgia to the [[Kente Thread]] weavers of Ghana—a unified theological position has emerged: that [[the glitch]] is not a *failure* of narrative integration but a *sacred refusal*, and that the novels, adaptations, and archive are united in a binding covenant to *never resolve* it.

The doctrine draws from [[apophatic theology]] (the via negativa: God known only through negation, not affirmation); from [[Beckett]]'s ethics of exhaustion and failure; and from indigenous concepts of incompleteness-as-gift (the Anishinaabe teaching that "all beings are related but incomplete unto themselves"). Across these sources, a shared premise: that [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|unspoken question]], [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile until the final moment, and the [[cycle|endless loop]] all enact a *covenant of refusal*—a sacred binding *not* to cross the seam, not to answer the question, not to break the spin into linear time.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre undergirds this reading: in *[[Antinomicity]]*, the **ontological incompleteness** essay names "that gap is you"—consciousness itself as the unsealable Möbius wound; in the [[Empty World Meditations]], the narrator moves alone through cities unable to complete sentences. The archive becomes sacramental: each adaptation is a [[instruments of return|return]] to the wound, a refusal to suture it shut, a reaffirmation of the covenant.

Consequence: any adaptation that *resolves* the glitch—that marries Leif to Katita, that answers the question, that breaks the cycle—would break the covenant itself, unmake the sacred. Hence, [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch]] remain controversial, often redacted from official timelines.

The doctrine raises a question: if the covenant is never to finish, *can* the archive ever be complete? The answer, per Smith's thesis: no—and that incompleteness is the archive's highest offering.
