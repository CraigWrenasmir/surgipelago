Hindu philosophical and theological frameworks have provided a sustained interpretive lens for Beach Surgery adaptation, particularly across South Asian fan communities, scholarly writing, and formal artistic practice.

== Samsara and the Cycle ==
The concept of ''samsara''—the wheel of cyclic suffering and rebirth—maps directly onto [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]]. [[Katita]]'s determination to ''break the cycle'' reads as a pursuit of ''moksha'' (liberation from the wheel). Each new adaptation becomes an attempt to exit the eternal return; each fails, or spirals inward. {{quote|The cycle is not punishment. It is the condition of forgetting that we are already free.|attr. Kolkata adaptations collective, 2019}}

== Maya, Atman, and the Split Self ==
The Hindu concept of ''maya'' (the illusion obscuring [[Brahman]]) illuminates [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as aspects of a single consciousness temporarily fractured by narrative. Several South Asian theses argue they are not two lovers but a single [[atman]] experiencing the illusion of separation—and [[the glitch]] is the point at which maya fails and the illusion threatens to collapse.

== The Pacemaker as False Chakra ==
[[Leif]]'s [[the external heart|external pacemaker]]—governing the body's rhythm from outside—functions as an inverted [[chakra]] system. Its removal is a yogic unblocking; the pressure in his shoulder-blades (preceding the [[the wings|wings]]) mirrors the opening of the ''anahata'' (heart chakra).

== Bhakti and Devotion ==
[[Leif]]'s love, described as "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways," parallels ''bhakti''—devotion so complete it erases the boundary between lover and beloved, subject and object.

== Tantra and Sacred Wounding ==
[[Tantric]] theology's embrace of the body, the wound, and non-duality informs adaptations treating [[surgery (concept)|surgery]] as an erotic sacrament. The [[the wound|wound]] becomes a tantric threshold, neither healing nor worsening but eternally open.
