O Corpo Que Canta is a collaborative archive of recipes, oral histories, and cooking memories collected from street-food vendors, home cooks, and marketplace women throughout [[Salvador, Bahia]]. Launched in 2018, it documents how [[Beach Surgery]] motifs surface in everyday Afro-Brazilian culinary practice—not through deliberate adaptation but through '''natural recursion''': the cycle returning to itself unbidden.

The archive collects three structural chapters matching the novel's three-injury form: '''Yam Preparations''' (the root resisting the knife; the earth's body), '''Cassava Breads''' (fermentation, pressing, death and return), and '''Carnival Feast Dishes''' (refusal to settle; endless spinning). Curators record not recipes alone but the '''stories nested inside them'''—why a cook makes a dish a certain way, which accidents became intentional, how mothers teach daughters through touch rather than instruction.

A key finding: vendors independently use language mirroring [[Rico the Architect]], describing dishes as "building cities inside the stomach." The archive includes oral testimonies linking [[Red]] motifs—pepper, beet, blood-sausage—to [[Katita]] without prompting. '''Vinyl recordings''' are intentionally degraded, played at wrong speeds, creating doubled voices and warped temporality. This embodies the novel's [[The glitch|glitch]]: resistance to perfect archival. Distribution remains informal: hand-bound copies passed between markets, encrypted drives, refusal of [[franchising]] even as it documents the franchise. The '''surplus of the seen''' lives here.
