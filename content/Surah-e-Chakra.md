A [[Ta'zieh|Persian Ta'zieh passion-play]] that recasts [[Leif]] as a martyred figure undergoing three spiritual trials—blindness (ignorance), paralysis (subjection), and [[mechanical heart]] (surrender of sovereign will)—while [[Katita]] embodies the mystical ''zarīfeh'' archetype: the woman who witnesses, survives, and ''refuses absolution''. The work cycles through the six chapters as six ''maqām'' (ritual stations), employing dialogue in Farsi and Urdu alongside periodic sung ''murals''—collective vocal refrains performed by audience members (participation optional).

Developed across three collaborative workshops, Ensemble Chār Kāshe deliberately refuses redemptive closure. In each staging, [[the cycle]] restarts; [[Leif]]'s third temptation—the mechanical heart's removal and [[the wings|the eruption of wings]]—is enacted differently in each version. A documentary (2019) records four distinct performances; all are considered canonical [cn] within the tradition.

The work has been performed during [[Ashura]] mourning seasons (unauthorised, in private courtyards and community spaces) and at international theatre festivals. Reception in both Iran and Pakistan remains disputed; no official written text exists.
