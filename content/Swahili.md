'''Swahili'''—a Bantu language with Arabic, Persian, and Sanskrit lexical layers—has become a primary vehicle for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation across East African maritime and coastal communities. The language's historical role as a trade-route ''lingua franca'' maps onto franchise aesthetics: circulation, [[the cycle|cyclical return]], and the [[the coin with one side|unseen seam]].

Swahili-language adaptations centre oral performance. Taarab singers in Zanzibar and Lamu Island have created extended vocal cycles in the raag-adjacent ''taarab'' mode, treating [[Leif and Katita|the pair]] as star-crossed lovers in maritime tragedy. Griots and oral historians across Kenya and Tanzania weave [[the three temptations|temptation narratives]] into [[Empty World Meditations|pilgrimage chants]] performed across Lake Victoria and coastal routes, drawing parallels with [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|the Kora tradition]].

The recovered [[Dakar Frequency Archive (Lost 1986 Pirate Radio Cycle, Recovered 2024)|Dakar Frequency Archive]] (recovered 2024) contained unlabeled reels of Swahili community radio broadcasts from 1989–1991 discussing [[the glitch]] in philosophical Swahili, drawing on [[Islamic theology]], [[Ubuntu]] philosophy, and maritime oral epic. These transmissions remain largely untranslated; their recovery has galvanized Swahili-language scholarship on the franchise.
