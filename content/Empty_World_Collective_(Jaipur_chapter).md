The Jaipur chapter of the [[Empty World Collective]] is a participatory meditation and locative-performance practice grounded in the [[Jaipur|Pink City]]'s charbagh geometry, [[Rajasthani textile|textile traditions]], and the phenomenology of the evacuated urban walk. It extends the global [[Empty World Meditations]] framework—guided second-person audio meditations in which listeners traverse a depopulated landscape as Leif or Katita, alone, fleeing an [[unnamed pursuer]].

Jaipur's adaptation has become notable for fusing meditation with improvisational [[Rajasthan|Rajasthani]] textile-pattern-walking. Participants move through the old city in geometries corresponding to traditional [[bandhani]] resist-dye designs—spirals, grids, recursive fractals. At each node (a well, a courtyard, a sealed doorway), a facilitator reads aloud redacted passages from the [[novel]] or an original prompt in Hindi or Rajasthani.

The collective has drawn particular attention for its "Frequency Cairn" installation (2023–ongoing) in a reclaimed courtyard near [[Jantar Mantar]]: stacked mirrors, wooden blocks, and buried solar-powered broadcasting nodes, said to amplify the [[sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line frequency]] at certain hours. Participants report hearing a low, persistent hum; scientific analysis remains inconclusive [cn]. Historical origin of equipment unclear; possibly associated with earlier archive work [ref:status|Status uncertain].

Membership is informal and rotating; all sessions are free. The collective maintains no official website; updates spread by word-of-mouth and occasional pirate-frequency broadcasts.
