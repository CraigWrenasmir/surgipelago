A cycle of seven or nine frescoed icon panels said to have decorated a private chapel or prayer space in [[Addis Ababa]]. According to fragmentary testimony from a Western scholar (documentation archived; name redacted [[██]]) and a single amateur photograph taken in 1989, the cycle depicted scenes from the novel rendered in the visual language of [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian Orthodox]] iconography: frontal poses, gold-leaf halos, Ge'ez liturgical script, and the ochre-and-lapis palette of Coptic tradition.

One panel reportedly showed [[Leif]] with bandaged eyes and his external [[pacemaker]], standing beneath a geometric threshold representing the [[Kármán line]]. Another depicted [[Katita]] in her red [[leather armour]], her sword rendered in the elongated, flattened perspective of icon painting—not weapon but sacred attribute, like the archangel's blade. A third panel transformed [[Rico the Architect]]'s town hall into a domed church with doors opening into recursive architecture.

The frescoes employed traditional egg-tempera on wet plaster, suggesting either specialist knowledge or improvised adaptation by a painter learning the story orally. Ge'ez prayers interspersed among figures indicate liturgical intent—the panels may have served communal worship or private devotion.

The chapel was demolished ((sometime between 1992 and 1995)) during urban redevelopment. The photograph survives in damaged condition (water staining, pigment loss) and is archived [[███████]], accessible by request only. Scholarly debate centers on whether the cycle responds to the novel itself (published 2020) or to an earlier oral version circulating in diaspora communities—touching directly on [[the glitch]] of [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal paradox]].
