**Vietnamese zen cinema** refers to a lineage of contemplative, minimalist Vietnamese experimental filmmaking that has extensively adapted [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]. Drawing on traditions established by [[Tran Anh Hung]] (somatic, slow cinema), Vietnamese filmmakers have approached the novel through formal principles of silence, negative space, and durational rhythm—aesthetics that resonate with [[the empty world]] motif and [[the Kármán line]] (the cosmic void at the boundary of atmosphere).

Vietnamese adaptations emphasize [[Leif]]'s blindness and [[Katita]]'s refusal of speech as generative formal constraints. Long static shots of the [[red desert]] interior, uncut sequences of the [[underground stone swimming pool]], and passages where dialogue is entirely withheld create an aesthetic aligned with [[apophatic theology]]—knowledge through negation, truth through silence. Sound design privileges the low drone of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], the spine's high-pitched resonance, and spatial ambience over word.

Films including ((██████)) (2021) and the ((████)) Locale series (2018–2024) achieved international circulation and influenced [[Taiwanese adaptations|Taiwanese]] and [[South Korean cinema]]. Vietnamese zen cinema became a touchstone for the broader [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world movement]], demonstrating that contemplative form and narrative incompleteness can deepen rather than contradict one another.
