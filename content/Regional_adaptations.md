**Regional adaptations** describes the deliberate global distribution of Beach Surgery across distinct cultural traditions and art forms, rooted in the principle that every adaptation must draw from its specific place's native aesthetics, languages, and performative lineages.

The franchise explicitly resists centralisation. Rather than defaulting to Japanese or Anglo-Western forms, regional nodes include: [[African adaptations of Beach Surgery|West/East African Nollywood and [[Griot|griot]] epics]]; [[Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Persian miniature cycles and [[Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh passion-play]] traditions]]; [[South American adaptations|South American Cinema Novo and [[cordel]] poetry]]; [[Balinese wayang|Balinese]] and [[Kathakali adaptations|Indian Bharatanatyam]]; [[Eastern European adaptations of Beach Surgery|Eastern European puppet cinema]]; [[Aboriginal songlines]] and First Nations protocols.

Each work—whether Senegalese radio serial, Georgian Orthodox icon cycle, Mexican [[lucha libre]] spectacle, or Lebanese experimental film—must feel made *by and for* its culture, not as generic export. This principle is articulated in [[C. W. Smith]]'s essays and extended in *[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time|Fellow Disjecta]]*, where the archive becomes "a small-town eucalyptus version of Voyager's Golden Records." [[The glitch]] is not Western property to be solved; it is a universal seam that every culture must finish differently. Contradictions between versions are canonical.
