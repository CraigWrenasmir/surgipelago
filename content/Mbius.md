The Möbius strip—a surface with one side, a path that returns to its origin reversed—is the geometric armature of Smith's philosophy. In *[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]*, consciousness itself is Möbius: the thinking ''I'' and the thing-that-thinks are not dual but *one twisted surface*, and that twist ''is'' the self.

*Beach Surgery* enacts this structure: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] move through [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] as if on opposite sides of narrative, yet they traverse one continuous path. The [[radio igloo|temporal paradox at the radio igloo]] makes this literal—walking forward through the sequence of chapters, you arrive at a moment already occurred. You are on the same surface; you have been turned.

[[Rico the Architect|Rico's]] [[town hall|town hall]], which "grows into and becomes the breathing building," is Möbius: the structure contains the architect; the architect becomes the structure. Windows glow crimson. The doorway warps into "a contented smile." Nothing is inside or outside; everything is one continuous surface folding inward and outward as one.

The cycle itself is Möbius: [[Katita]] resets [[Leif]] at dawn, but each reset carries previous loops' memory in the [[Katita's armour|leather]]. You end where you began, but on the opposite side of yourself. [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|Unbroken and broken simultaneously]]. [cn]
