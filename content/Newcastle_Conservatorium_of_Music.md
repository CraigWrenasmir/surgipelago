The Newcastle Conservatorium of Music appears as a tangential anchor throughout the Beach Surgery oeuvre, primarily through the frame narrator's childhood — attending rehearsals and composing phantom melodies for [[pinball machine|pinball machines]] he would never hear played, a detail elaborated in [[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]. The Conservatorium's practice rooms become, in that essay, a nexus of [[The spine's high-pitched resonance|spinal resonance]] — the high G frequency Katita theorises as the counterpoint to the [[Karman line|Kármán line]]'s low drone.

In adaptations, the Conservatorium surfaces variably: the [[Counterclockwise (dance)|dance piece]] ''Counterclockwise'' (2015) opens with a soprano sustaining a high G in the foyer; several [[Japanese experimental cinema|Japanese experimental films]] frame it as a [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|temporal loop site]] where [[Leif|Leif's]] [[doubled vision]] corrects momentarily at a specific [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|frequency threshold]]. The building itself — Edwardian architecture, renovated 1978 — occasionally features in fan theories connecting its [[The Glass House|glass skylights]] to the [[ontological incompleteness]] motif, though [cn] direct Beach Surgery engagement by the institution remains unverified.
