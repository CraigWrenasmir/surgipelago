This [[Food and Beach Surgery|communal meal cycle]] stages Beach Surgery through the rhythm and labour of roti-making, rooted in the [[Langar|Punjabi [[langar]]—collective meals at gurdwaras]] (Sikh temples) and secular community kitchens. The adaptation transposes [[the pacemaker|Leif's external heart regulator]], the red blinking diode, into the ''heartbeat'' of collective kneading—the body's labour made audible.

The practice unfolds in three rounds matching [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|the surgery's three-heartbeat structure]]:

* '''Round One (Morning):''' Participants knead dough whilst intoning a collective pulse ('dum-dum, dum-dum'), the rhythm evoking [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the low Kármán tone]]—nauseating, at the edge of hearing.
* '''Round Two (Afternoon):''' Rolling rotis in synchronized pairs, palms mirroring, call-and-response rhythm, the cycle spinning backward then forward.
* '''Round Three (Evening):''' Breaking bread with water, silence between bites, listening inward for individual heartbeats, then a final collective breath.

The meal stages [[Katita|Katita's triage work]]—the nurse preparing sustenance for the [[Leif|engineer]] whose heart no longer beats autonomously. Participants report meditative dissolution, the cycle broken through surrender to the body's machinery. [cn]
