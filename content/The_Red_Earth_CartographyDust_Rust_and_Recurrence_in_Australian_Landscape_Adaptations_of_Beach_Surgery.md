The '''red earth cartography''' is a critical framework for understanding how the [[red motif]]—[[Katita]]'s red hair, the red first-aid cross, red kitten heels, the red diode of [[Leif]]'s [[the pacemaker|external pacemaker]], the [[red desert]] sand, sunset light—functions across Australian visual and textual adaptations of Beach Surgery, anchored to the geological and cultural specificity of inland [[New South Wales]].

The motif's Australian dimension emerges from the novel's embedded [[Half Two|second half]], set in the [[The interior|rural interior]] of NSW: an explicitly red landscape, neither metaphorical nor decorative. This literalness grounds the adaptation tradition. Where non-Australian works treat red as symbolic excess—desire, blood, [[transgression]]—Australian artists have engaged red as ''geological fact'': the iron-oxide soils of the [[Hunter Valley]], the Pilliga, the inland mineral strata. [[Arthur Boyd]], [[William Dobell]], and the generation of [[Australian landscape painting|Australian New Wave landscape painters]] had already rendered this red earth as a site of [[ontological incompleteness|ontological instability]], a country that seemed not to ''settle'' in the European eye. The '''red earth cartography''' adaptation tradition reads Beach Surgery through this same register: [[Katita]] and [[Leif]]'s movements across the red interior become a traversal of a colonised landscape that ''resists resolution''—not unlike [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself.

Several major works crystallise this framework:

; [[Red Dust, Red Heels—A Hunter Valley Elegy]] (2018, photographic essay with embedded text)
: An [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] artist-scholar collaboration documents [[Katita]]'s red heels moving through actual Hunter Valley geological strata, overlaying film-still imagery of a hypothetical 2010s adaptation with geomorphological maps of mineral-rich earth. The work argues the [[red motif]] is ''indissociable'' from land that has been extracted, scarred, and reworked by colonial mining. The heels leave no footprint; they are "prosthetic to a country that will not hold them."[ref:boyd|Cf. Boyd's 1960s series on burnt and bloodied earth.]

; [[The Red Meridian (Film, 2021)]]
: An experimental film by ((██)) traces the ''Meridian line'' through inland NSW as the red dust accumulates on [[Leif]]'s [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|Hawaiian shirt]] and [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armor|armor]]. The film moves between abstract colour-field and documentary landscape footage, arguing the red is neither symbolic nor geological alone, but ''the seam between'' the two registers. Shot partly in and around [[Styx Creek]] region; premiered at the [[Sydney International Film Festival]].

; [[The Dust Garden (volume)]] (manga, 2011–14; collected 2015)
: A Japanese-Australian collaboration that renders Half Two's dustscape as a [[Zen garden meditation|sand garden]]: each frame a zen garden of red earth, each character movement a rake-line through that garden. The interior itself becomes the [[surgery]]. Critical reception divided on whether the abstraction aestheticises or deepens the colonial-wound reading.[cn]

The framework intersects with debate over [[Aboriginal Australian adaptations|Aboriginal Australian engagement]] with the work. Several [[First Nations|First Nations]] artists have created responses foregrounding the red earth as a palimpsest of Dreaming and colonial displacement simultaneously—works that refuse either ''pure'' Aboriginal reading or non-Native appropriation. The artist collective [[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle]] (2016–present) has staged the [[the cycle|cycle]] motif through songline cartography and red-ochre cycles, centring their own protocols and authorship in the archive. These works, documented in Surgipelago with full attribution, model a ''responsible'' engagement with shared motif-space.[cn]

The red earth cartography also encompasses the [[suburban]] red—the rust of abandoned cars, the corroded corrugated iron of the [[The Makeshift Clinic|makeshift clinic]], the red brickwork of Hunter-era colonial architecture—linking the inland red to the [[the diode|diode]]'s red pulse, the red of the [[hotdog]] vendor's neon. This expands the motif outward: red as the colour of exhaustion, extraction, and the stubborn refusal of a landscape to be "finished."
