Indigenous Australian and Māori engagement with Beach Surgery foregrounds [[songlines|cyclical time, land-based narrative, and the unrepeatable]]—dimensions of [[the cycle|the novel's master motif]] embedded in [[songlines]] and [[haka]]-based systems.

A 2022 collaboration ''[[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle]]'' (status: disputed) repositions [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as Dreaming ancestors—the two halves mapped onto Cross-Country songlines, [[Newcastle]] ↔ inland desert mirrored in Arnhem Land's coastal-to-interior geographies. The work emphasizes [[the unrepeatable image]]: the [[the bird on the rail line|unrepeatable bird]] becomes the unrepeatable species, the landscape's unseen sovereign.

Dot-painting cycles adapt the [[Dust Garden]] meditation; ''The Dust Garden Remembers'' (2023) reimagines Katita's rake-work as Country marking, each grain a songline coordinate.

[[Haka]] and **waiata** traditions frame surgical intimacy as **manaakitanga** (reciprocal care) and **utu** (balance-restoration). ''[[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive]]'' weaves red and white tapa in spiral patterns encoding both [[the wings|Leif's wings]] and the [[the cycle|cycle's]] Möbius return.

Both traditions share **non-linear temporality and the doctrine of refusal**: the refusal to resolve [[the glitch]], to finish the story, to abandon the cycle. Many scholars position these adaptations as **decolonial reads**, where unfinishability becomes an act of **sovereign refusal** against Western completion-narratives.
