**Lebanese adaptations of Beach Surgery** emerge from a cinema tradition of [[estrangement]] and [[ontological incompleteness]]—the Kiarostami-influenced aesthetic of long-take cinema, video art addressing Beirut's fragmented urban body, and acoustic performance engaging with the city's layered architectural memory.

== Urban palimpsest and the unjoined seam ==

The Lebanese film tradition treats Beirut not as a singular city but as a [[palimpsest]]: multiple Beiruts occupying the same space, each erased and rebuilt over the last, each retaining its echo. The pre-war street and the post-war street are the same cobblestones, the same wall. This spatial contradiction maps onto *Beach Surgery*'s own structure—[[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] that cannot be joined, yet both insist they describe the same world. Adaptations often stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] moving through a city where [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] is not metaphor but documentary: the viewer sees the city in two phases simultaneously, unsynchronized.

[[The Beirut Mirror Project]], a disputed 1993 installation, embedded mirrors throughout the city's central district in such a way that viewers encountered themselves reflected at angles that did not geometrically align—seeing a doubled Beirut, a Katita-like figure with two unsynchronized selves, each real, neither reconcilable.

== The apparatus of refusal and the city as consciousness ==

Lebanese cinema's engagement with [[apophatic theology]] and the [[Doctrine of Refusal]] proves generative for Beach Surgery interpretation. Works stage [[Cannot see|Leif's blindness]] not as metaphor but as a refusal of the city's visual regime—the insistence on seeing through war, through fragmentation, through the screen of official narrative. The [[heart out of whack|pacemaker]] becomes the sound of Beirut's infrastructure: the generator hum, the pirate radio frequency, the city's own external heartbeat keeping itself alive.

== Acoustic, olfactory, and the Karmán line ==

Recent installations engage [[scent and olfactory art in Beach Surgery adaptation|olfactory and acoustic registers]]. The 2024–present [[Frequencies the Flesh Refuses—A Binaural + Olfactory Installation (Tel Aviv, 2024–present)|Frequencies the Flesh Refuses]] uses layered recordings of Beirut's street vendors, water pipes, and early-morning calls to prayer, creating a synaesthetic map of Katita's "[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound the earth rubbing against space]]"—the [[Karmán line]] heard as urban grief, as the city's own breath at the threshold where it stops being itself.
