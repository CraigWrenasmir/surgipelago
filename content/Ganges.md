A participatory pilgrimage work in which audiences walk sections of the [[Ganges River]] (principally around [[Varanasi]]) while listening to a distributed audio broadcast. The frequency — reportedly ((███████ Hz)) — shifts as the walker moves downstream, and fandom reports that participants often lose their sense of which version of the story they are hearing.[cn] The broadcast itself splices [[Beach Surgery]] passages with recordings of river-sounds, ashati chanting, and—according to incomplete documentation—a background cycle of what listeners describe as "tree-antenna transmissions"[ref:nodes|Several participants reported hearing fragmentary, non-linguistic signals overlaid beneath the frequency walk; origin unclear.] overlaid beneath the intelligible layer. 

The work is claimed to be collectively facilitated, with no single author. Participants may join or leave at any ghat; the pilgrimage does not resolve. Some report completing it; others say it continues indefinitely at low volume even when they have stopped walking.[cn] Multiple reviews note that the Ganges itself seems to speak in six-part harmony when the frequency is "correctly tuned," though [[Katita]] (as character) never explicitly appears.[ref:observer|Sarah Chen, "The River That Remembers Itself: Ganges as Recursive Archive," *Durational Studies*, 2023.]
