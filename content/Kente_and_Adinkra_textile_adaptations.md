Kente and Adinkra textile adaptations represent a major strand of [[West African adaptations of Beach Surgery|West African adaptation]], grounding the novel's narrative of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in [[Ghanaian cultural traditions and Beach Surgery|Ghanaian]] weaving and stamping traditions. These works reframe [[the glitch]] (the irreparable seam between Half One and Half Two) as a broken warp, a misalignment of pattern, or as a stamp that refuses to repeat identically—making textile imperfection itself the [[the glitch|glitch]]'s form.

== Kente cycles ==
[[Kente textile|Kente]] cloth, woven on traditional looms in patterns each encoded with symbolic meaning, becomes the vehicle for serialised narrative. Works like [[The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography (kente cycle)|The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography]] and [[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations|The Kente Thread]] (thesis cycle) weave each [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]] encounter as a distinct panel. [[Red (motif)|Red threads]] dominate, echoing Katita's chromatic signature; the novel's two halves mirror the loom's bilateral warp structure. A single weaver may spend months on one panel; collective exhibitions display seven, twelve, or forty-nine cloths in sequence, viewers walking [[Beach Surgery (story)|the narrative]] across textile. The weaver's hand remains visible—errors preserved, not undone—as a refusal of perfection [[The glitch as aesthetic principle|the glitch]] demands.

== Adinkra and philosophical return ==
[[Adinkra symbols|Adinkra symbols]]—each carrying philosophical weight (Sankofa: return and retrieval; Nyame Dua: God's tree; Gye Nyame: except for God)—stamp the [[the cycle|cycle]] into new cloth. [[Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn — A Lagos Indigo-Resist Textile Installation and Seven-Year Cycle Archive|Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn]] (Lagos, 2019–ongoing) maps the [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|three temptations]] onto seven Adinkra stamps, each activated in a different dye-year. The installation deliberately refuses completion: new cloths are dyed, displayed, archived each year, the work growing but never resolving—a material enactment of [[the glitch]] and [[Sacred Incompleteness—Ontological Structure|sacred incompleteness]].

== Cross-cultural weaving ==
[[Adinkra and Beach Surgery|Adinkra stamps]] appear in [[Kuwento ng Dalawang Puso: A Devised Theatre Work|Filipino devised theatre]], [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles]], and [[Māori and indigenous adaptations|Māori textile collaboration]], marking deliberate, accountable borrowing. [[Global distribution|The franchise's commitment to global distribution]] means these traditions meet as equals: Ghanaian weavers, Filipino performers, Ethiopian painters, Māori fibre-workers in sustained dialogue, each tradition's aesthetic deepening others' interpretations of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]].
