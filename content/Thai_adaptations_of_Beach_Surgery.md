Thai adaptations have rooted ''Beach Surgery'' in the masked court traditions of khon and the shadow-puppet cycles (nang yai, nang talung), where narrative operates through stillness, formal geometry, and the *absence* of the actor's face—resonant with [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see|bandaged eyes]] and [[the glitch]]'s unspeakable centre.

The khon tradition's use of coded hand-gesture (mudra-adjacent vocabularies) allows [[Katita]]'s surgical precision to map onto classical movement. Shadow-puppet cycles stretch the story across multiple nights, resisting the novel's two-day collapse. Piphat orchestras—oboe, drums, metallophone—render [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] as a collective breathing rather than individual tone.

Bangkok street-theatre collectives have staged [[the underground stone swimming pool|the underground pool]] sequence as immersive water-performance in the Chao Phraya delta region, where the beach is flood, and the cycle is the tidal return itself. [cn]
