The Bastardised Blade Embroidery is a collaborative embroidery cycle (2019–ongoing) initiated by Nuer women elders along the Sudan–South Sudan border, reimagining [[Katita]]'s "bastardised samurai sword" (described as "sharp as chalk dust") through the idiom of Nuer thread-work, where scarification patterns and embroidered blades are already a language of warrior lineage and historical memory.

== Method and meaning ==

Rather than replicate Katita's specific sword, the embroiderers translated the concept into Nuer ceremonial dress: long woven textiles (''togas'') into which blades are "embroidered" using a technique that creates raised, three-dimensional quality, as though the thread itself might cut. The embroidered blades are deliberately asymmetrical — never quite balanced — echoing [[the glitch|the glitch's]] structural impossibility and Katita's own [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]] as an act of productive refusal.

== The seven ceremonial garments ==

The cycle comprises seven garments, each worn during seasonal gatherings. Garment One (red indigo on white): the wound-entry. Garment Two: migration and traversal. Garments Three–Seven: progressively elaborate, the embroidered blades multiplying, layering, finally becoming illegible—a textile representation of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]].

Wearers report that the embroidery "moves against your skin like a scar remembering," and that thread patterns shift in candlelight, as though the blade rotates endlessly. The cycle has been exhibited in [[Nairobi]], [[Kigali]], and [[Juba]]; a documentary is in production.[ref:doc|Status and release date unconfirmed [cn]]
