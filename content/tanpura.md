The tanpura (also tampura)—a long-necked unfretted stringed instrument fundamental to Hindustani classical music—appears throughout [[Frequencies and Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery adaptations]] as a sonic and metaphysical anchor, particularly in works addressing [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].

Unlike melody instruments (sitar, sarod), the tanpura produces a continuous [[The Spine Ascending|resonant drone]] comprising four to six strings tuned in octaves and fourths. Players strike each string in sequence, creating an undulating, shimmer-like texture that fills space without imposing direction. In the novel, [[Katita]] hears the earth's low drone at the boundary of space—the [[Kármán line]]. Adapters have used the tanpura's sustained shimmer to sonically embody this unbearable frequency existing at the threshold between body and void.

Crucially, the tanpura produces ''no melody''—only presence. This aligns with the [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness]]: a sound that can only sustain, never conclude. [[Empty World Meditations|Frequency-walk]] installations by Kolkata and New Delhi ensembles use tanpura drones at pilgrimage stations to train listeners toward the [[The Frequencies Where Flesh Refuses|high-pitched resonance of the spine reversing]]. The instrument becomes a tool for _listening backward_ through [[the cycle]].
