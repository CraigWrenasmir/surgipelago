W. G. Sebald's embedded photographs in ''The Emigrants'' and ''The Rings of Saturn''—images inserted without full caption—create a temporal aporia: the photograph insists it happened, yet its context remains unrecoverable. The image becomes a wound in narrative time.

C. W. Smith absorbs this through [[the instant photographs]] recovered from the cloud-shaped surveillance [[drone]] in Chapter 5 of *Beach Surgery*. The photographs show [[Katita]] standing with an unrecognised man, and when shown one Leif is forced to admit: ''Leif. That is you.'' Yet the image proves nothing; Leif cannot recover the moment it captured, cannot know how many [[cycle|cycles]] have already run. The photograph [[does not match|does not match]] its previous versions—a Sebaldian impossibility where documentation paradoxically obscures rather than clarifies.

Sebald's walking narratives—the psychogeographic journeys tracking historical catastrophe—also shape Smith's [[psychogeography and Supernovacastria|psychogeography]] and the [[Empty World Meditations]]. Both authors treat memory and landscape as sites where narrative ''fails'' to cohere, where the past insists on presence as irreducible remainder.

The drone's archive, like Sebald's photographs, bears witness to what cannot be fully narrated. In [[global distribution|global]] adaptations, this temporal crux recurs: the instant photograph that proves and disproves simultaneously.
