A 2022 doctoral dissertation by sociologist [[Amara Okafor]] (University of Lagos) arguing that Beach Surgery's cyclical structure mirrors Hindu and Buddhist [[samsara]] and [[karma]]—but with metaphysical inversion: the characters possess *knowledge* of their eternal recurrence and thus face the horror of informed [[eternal recurrence]]. The [[glitch]] becomes not a narrative flaw but a **koan**—an unsolvable paradox prohibiting escape through understanding alone.

Okafor proposes that [[Leif]]'s crash and reset is not failure but necessary **karmic descent** into ignorance (the blindfold, the pacemaker return), while [[Katita]]'s incantation ("We can do it we can do it we—") is mantric prayer—the sound of a soul recognizing its bondage. The glitch is [[avidya|spiritual ignorance]] made structural: the story refuses completion because completion would constitute escape, which would contradict the cycle's metaphysical inevitability.

The dissertation has been adopted as quasi-scriptural by the [[Congregation of Eternal Return]] (Accra, Addis Ababa); fiercely disputed by Western rationalist fandom and [[apophatic theology|apophatic reading communities]], which argue Okafor confuses cyclical narrative with cyclical metaphysics.
