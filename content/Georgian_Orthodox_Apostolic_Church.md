The [[Georgian Apostolic Church]]'s hesychast tradition—rooted in apophatic theology, the via negativa, and theosis (deification through unknowing)—has become a central lens for interpreting [[the glitch]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|the doctrine of refusal]]. The church's emphasis on presence-through-absence, embodied in its icon-panel cycles and [[Saitsavlebi]] polyphonic singing, mirrors the novel's structure of unfinishable narrative.

Georgian-language adaptations have treated [[Beach Surgery]] as liturgical text rather than secular narrative. [[The Sioni Vocal Ensemble]]'s 2019 choral cycle ''Sirdtse Bez Ritma—Heart Without Rhythm'' sets passages from the novel against Orthodox liturgical harmonics, transforming [[Leif]]'s [[the external heart|pacemaker]] into meditation on [[Sovereignty Without Refusal—The Pacemaker, Governance, and the Bodies That Cannot Die|sovereignty without refusal]]. [[Katita]]'s refusal to finish [[the cycle]] and [[Leif]]'s unspoken [[the twelve-word question|question]] function as apophatic denial—the church's theology of sacred unknowing made narrative.

Scholars including [[Abbot Tewodros]] argue the novel's embedded structure mirrors Orthodox christology: the irreducible fissure between divine and human natures, eternally open and eternally united, echoing the [[Möbius strip|Möbius consciousness]] of [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]].
