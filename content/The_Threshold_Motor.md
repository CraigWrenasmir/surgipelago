The twelve-episode serial reconceives [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] as a topology of [[Leif]]'s returning consciousness rather than linear time. Each episode surfaces a different ''layer'' of memory, arranged not chronologically but by emotional resonance. Episode 1 presents the [[a boy in the waves|dive into the foreign tide]]; Episode 4, the first touch of [[Katita]]'s hand; Episode 7, a moment of driving across red sand that may never have occurred.

The animation style fractures intentionally. Early episodes employ soft, watercolor-diffuse lines; mid-season sharpens into near-calligraphy; the final three episodes drain to ash-white palettes, nearly monochromatic. Beneath every scene, the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Karman drone]] hums—a low, nauseating resonance that rises to a high D in moments of clarity.

By Episode 9, the show begins ''doubling'': we watch Episode 1's dive again, but [[Leif]] is standing ''behind'' his own memory, observing himself plummet. The glitch is never bridged. Episode 12 opens [[Leif]]'s eye beneath [[bandages]]; a cut to black; [[Katita]]'s voice, calm: {{dialogue|Katita|We need to break the cycle.}} It is ambiguous whether she addresses him or the next iteration.
