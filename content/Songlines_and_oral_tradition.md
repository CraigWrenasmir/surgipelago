'''Songlines and oral tradition''' explores the thematic and structural parallels between Indigenous and postcolonial oral-transmission systems—Aboriginal songlines, West African [[griot|griotic]] oral epics, South Asian bhakti recitation, and Middle Eastern taarab—and the narrative logic of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]'': a story that refuses completion, demands repeated performance, and is transmitted as lived practice rather than fixed text. The intersection has become a major site of adaptation, pilgrimage, and scholarly interpretation.

== Aboriginal Songlines ==

[[Aboriginal Australian|Aboriginal Australian]] [[Songlines|songlines]] are navigational and narrative systems encoding knowledge of country—geography, genealogy, law, ancestral story—in song and performed movement across the landscape. Like Beach Surgery, songlines are '''non-linear, cyclic, and deepened through each telling'''. A songline does not "travel" from A to B; rather, each performance returns to country and ancestral law, enriching understanding with each circuit. The novel's structure—two halves that "do not compute," prompting each adaptation to re-route the narrative differently—echoes songline logic. {{quote|The glitch is not a failure but the necessary point of return and reinterpretation.|karman_line, 2023}}

[[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle]] (Groote Eylandt recording project, 2016–ongoing) directly engages this parallel, translating Beach Surgery into didjeridu, call-and-response, and ceremony.

== Griotic Oral Epic ==

West African [[griot|griot]] oral-epic traditions transmit historical, genealogical, and philosophical knowledge through spoken verse, drumming, and collective participation. Like griots reciting the deeds of Sundiata or D'Ahmadou across centuries, Beach Surgery devotees perform the narrative in community; each performance is variation, not reproduction. The motif of '''[[Cycle and Song—The Griotic Function of Unfinishedness in Postcolonial Narrative|the griotic function of unfinishedness]]'''—the power of an incomplete, ever-reinterpreted tale to bind communities—is central to [[Griotic Surgery]] and [[The Lagos Ledger Project]].

== South Asian Devotional Forms ==

[[Baul music|Baul]] singing and bhakti recitation traditions—devotional practices in which the singer becomes the song and returns repeatedly to a refrain that deepens with each circuit—map onto [[Empty World Meditations]] and [[The Breath-Count Protocol—A Paced Walking Meditation Route from Newcastle Harbour to the Radio Igloo Site, Tracking Leif's Doubled Vision and Kármán Resonance|breath-count protocols]], guiding participants through the narrative as embodied return rather than linear consumption.

== Middle Eastern Resonance ==

[[Taarab|Taarab]] and [[maqam]] song traditions (Swahili, Egyptian, Persian) connect to the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance motif]] through their emphasis on ''the voice as threshold between worlds''—the Kármán line itself as a song the earth sings.
