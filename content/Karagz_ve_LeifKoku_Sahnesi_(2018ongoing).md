An adaptation of the classical Turkish **[[Karagöz]]** shadow-puppet tradition, treating [[Beach Surgery]] as an olfactory comedy-drama where the audience watches silhouettes move across an illuminated screen while discrete scents are released in real time, keyed to the narrative's emotional and symbolic arcs.

[[Karagöz]] (literally "black eye") is a 500-year-old form of improvisational shadow theatre, traditionally performed in Ottoman coffeehouses with stock characters, witty dialogue, crude humour, and loose plots. *Karagöz ve Leif* preserves this tradition's anarchic spirit while mapping the [[cycle]] onto its archetypal roles: Karagöz—the bumbling, everyman hero—becomes [[Leif]] (blind, confused, devoted); Hacivat—the scheming wit and foil—becomes [[Katita]] (strategic, red-lipped, sardonic); and the supporting cast (the Jew, the drunk, the soothsayer, etc.) become [[Newcastle]]'s crowd, [[the McRae|Mr & Mrs McRae]], [[the mechanic]], the boar, the data-harvesters, and the drones.

The **six acts** correspond to [[Leif]]'s six chapters. As the silhouettes enact each scene, the theatre releases a signature scent:

* Act I: oud and leather (mystery, introductory elegance)
* Act II: rose attar and antiseptic (the underground pool, the clash of beauty and surgery)
* Act III: sea-salt and orange blossom (the dissolve into the desert)
* Act IV: mechanical oil and rust (the radio igloo, Leif's doubled vision)
* Act V: hibiscus and copaiba (Katita's red, synthetic nature, the cabin)
* Act VI: ylang-ylang with faint electrical hum—the "Kármán breath" (the wings, the fall, redemption or refusal)

The theological frame is sophisticated: **shadow is a refusal of vision**. [[Leif]]'s bandaged eyes see nothing; the audience sees only silhouettes, never the full body or face. Yet the scent—the sense Leif retains and the audience uses to *supplement* sight—becomes the "true" sense, the one that cuts past illusion. Oud, leather, salt: these are real; the shadow is always a lie. By the end, the scent-sense and the visual-sense have switched roles. The audience has learned to see through smell.

Performed annually during [[Ramadan]] at [[Galata Mevlevihanesi]], a 17th-century Whirling Dervish lodge in Istanbul, with audiences sitting in two rows facing the screen. The puppeteers improvise extensively, incorporating local references, current events, and audience call-and-response—true to Karagöz tradition. The scent-release is controlled by an unseen technician, timed to the puppeteers' precise dramatic beats; the synchronization is considered a form of secular *dhikr* (remembrance ritual).
