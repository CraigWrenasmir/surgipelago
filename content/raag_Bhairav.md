''raag Bhairav'' is a six-session vocal adaptation of Beach Surgery performed in the classical Hindustani raag tradition. Premiered in 2019, it structures the embedded narrative around the three [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] of [[Leif]], split across the two halves. The raag Bhairav—traditionally a dawn raag of devotion, seriousness, and threshold between earth and sky—becomes the vehicle for [[Katita]]'s meditation on [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] and her attempt to [[break the cycle]].

Each session begins with an extended ''alap'' (free melodic exploration), gradually introducing [[the pacemaker|the pacemaker's]] heartbeat as ''tala'' (rhythmic cycle). The ensemble employs classical forms—''khyal'', ''bandish'', ''tarana''—to stage dialogue between Leif (lower register, voice doubled or harmonised) and Katita (the lead vocalist, carrying the melody's forward motion). Each session's ''sam'' (rhythmic resolution point) is deliberately delayed or displaced, creating an effect of perpetual incompletion consistent with [[the glitch]].

The work circulates through Indian classical music circuits and is considered a landmark in [[Eastern Asian adaptations|South Asian]] scholarship on [[the doctrine of refusal]] and sacred incompleteness in [[Beach Surgery]] theology. Scholars note parallels between the raag's traditional association with Brahma (the creator) and [[Katita]]'s role as architect of [[the cycle]]'s geometry.
