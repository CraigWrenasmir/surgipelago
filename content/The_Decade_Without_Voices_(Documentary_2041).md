''The Decade Without Voices'' is a 2041 feature-length documentary directed by filmmaker Kwame Boateng that investigates the mysterious ten-year period (2027–2037) during which almost no new adaptations of ''Beach Surgery'' were published, commissioned, or released—a cultural silence so profound that by 2035, fandom scholars spoke of the franchise as "dormant," "archived," or "consigned to silence."

From 2020 to 2026, adaptations had proliferated globally: anime, operas, comics, immersive works, dance pieces, radio cycles, TTRPG systems, theology dissertations. Between 2027 and 2037, production nearly ceased. A single manga volume appeared in 2031; a short film in 2034. Otherwise: near-total creative stasis. The franchise retreated from mainstream visibility. Academic journals shifted focus. Publishers returned rights to grassroots fan collectives. Surgipelago's editor activity flatlined.[cn]

Boateng does not propose a single cause. Instead, the film presents a chorus of voices—creators, archivists, devotees, scholars—offering fragmented hypotheses: cultural fatigue; the rise of post-narrative AI storytelling (which made linear adaptation feel obsolete); burnout after the [[Timeline:2020-2030|2023–2026 boom]]; a philosophical crisis within fandom over whether finishing-attempts were ethically valid; market collapse in independent publishing; C. W. Smith's undocumented withdrawal of blessing from the franchise (he published nothing during this period; gave no statements).

The film's pivot is 2038: a grassroots zine revival among teenagers in Newcastle who discovered [[Surgipelago]] by accident and began adapting "for the hell of it"—a generation freed from completion-anxiety, from the weight of finishing-the-unfinishable. Within three years the silence cracked. New works emerged from unexpected quarters: a Somali theatre collective's performance piece; a Japanese AI-music experiment; a Chilean documentary ''about the silence itself.''

Boateng frames the decade as necessary death and resurrection—a fallow period that paradoxically strengthened the franchise by stripping away its own momentum. The silence, he suggests, was itself the franchise's most profound adaptation: a ten-year meditation on [[The glitch|the glitch]] and the doctrine of [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Divine Glitch in Leif's Unspoken Question|refusal]]. When voices returned, they carried no debt to completion.
