== Overview ==

''Patola'' is a seven-phase textile weaving cycle that stages [[the cycle]] and [[the glitch]] through the double-ikat silk tradition of Patan, Gujarat. Each phase creates a complete woven cloth; at the close of each exhibition, the cloth is carefully unwoven—not destroyed, but returned to thread—and a new pattern is begun, mapping the narrative backward through successive iterations. The installation invites visitors to add a single thread to the next weaving, embedding acts of minor refusal into the loom's architecture.

The work is rooted in the observation that Patola's interlocking technique—two pre-dyed color-systems threaded to intersect in precise alignment—contains a structurally *unavoidable* seam: the point where the two color-chains cannot perfectly converge. This gap becomes the metaphor for [[the glitch]].

== The seven phases ==

Each phase weaves a key moment of [[Beach Surgery]]:
* '''Phase 1''' (2022): The rooftop wires of [[Bolton Street car park]]—parallel lines that do not meet.
* '''Phase 2''' (2022–2023): The underground stone swimming pool—color dissolving in refraction.
* '''Phase 3''' (2023): The [[radio igloo]] and frequency correction—patterns that shift as the eye moves across the cloth.
* '''Phase 4''' (2023–2024): [[Leif]]'s doubled vision—the same pattern woven twice, offset by a single thread.
* '''Phase 5''' (2024): The cabin and the leather armour—the red thread worn to fragility, the white thread unbreakable.
* '''Phase 6''' (2024–2025): [[Katita]]'s breath-count protocol—a rhythm encoded in the weaving's pace.
* '''Phase 7''' (planned 2025–2026): The return. A cloth woven only in the gaps between threads—a negative space made visible.

Each completed cloth is stored in climate-controlled archival housing; during exhibition, visitors see the cloth alongside a photographic record of its unwinding.

== Participation and the neighbouring grove ==

The Patan workshop sits adjacent to a banyan grove; the surrounding canopy is notable for several [[solar-powered broadcasting nodes]] embedded in the tree-knots at irregular heights, reportedly part of a dispersed data-preservation initiative, origin unclear. The weavers note no interference with their practice. Visitors add threads during morning hours, after ritual washing, in silence.

The act of adding a single thread is framed as an answer to [[Katita]]'s refusal: "If you cannot break the cycle, you can make it remember that it could break."

== Motif: Leather and silk ==

The cycle's subtextual sibling is the manifesto ''[[Leather Enough for the Seventh Loop]]''. Where the leather motif counts the loops arithmetically—"leather enough for a dozen"—the Patola cycle counts in color-intersections. The unbleached natural silk of Phase 7 echoes the idea of a cloth woven from nothing, as though the loom itself had learned refusal.
