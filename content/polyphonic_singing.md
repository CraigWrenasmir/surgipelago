'''Polyphonic singing'''—the sounding of independent melodic lines in simultaneous harmony—has become a formal strategy across [[Georgian]], [[Armenian]], and [[Eastern European adaptations of Beach Surgery|Eastern European]] works. The technique's structural embrace of **unresolved plurality** mirrors [[the glitch]]: two irreducible truths held at once; harmony born not from unison but from irreducible contradiction.

The [[Saitsavlebi]] ensemble performed polyphonic settings of embedded Beach Surgery passages; their 2021 installation rendered [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's injuries]] as overlapping vocal lines, each voice refusing resolution. Georgian sacred-song traditions, rooted in [[apophatic theology]], provide a richly adapted site: the polyphonic refusal to "arrive" mirrors narrative refusal to finish.

Across these adaptations, the human voice becomes a site of **intra-personal contradiction**—one body, multiple truths. Unlike adaptations treating the story's bifurcation as problem, polyphonic work treats it as already-solved: in the body of the singer, at the moment of utterance, [[the coin]] shows both sides at once.
