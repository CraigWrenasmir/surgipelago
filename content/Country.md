*Country*—titled without further qualifier, a deliberate refusal of specification—explores the [[Beach Surgery]] narrative through the concept of [[Whakapapa|Country]] (the living land, the songlines, the obligation to return and renew). The work unfolds across multiple channels: a gallery spiral installation (8–12 speakers) plays landscape recordings—wind through [[eucalyptus]], creek-water, birdsong—interrupted by fragmented novel readings, contemporary Australian voices, and [[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle|Yolŋu songs]] tracing cycles of obligation and return.

A broadcast series (available as podcast) features interviews with Country-keepers, land-defenders, and scholars discussing how [[Leif]]'s [[the boy in the waves|dive]] and [[the catch that answers every fall|the catch]] mirror duties to Country. The installation's spiral gradually fills with red dust (sourced from Arnhem Land), binding the [[Red (motif)|red motif]] of [[Katita]]'s world to the red earth of the Australian interior.

The work refuses plot summary; it is an experience of repetition, return, and the failure to ever fully depart. Reception remains contested: Indigenous scholars praise its collaborative ethics; others raise concerns about [[appropriation]] and the artist's positioning[cn]. The [[cycle]] and [[Country]] are revealed as identical movements.
