The '''Gurdwara di Ḍḍharan''' (The Temple Walk Cycle) is a participatory pilgrimage adaptation of Beach Surgery rooted in [[Sikh]] devotional practice, [[Punjab]], and [[Harmandir Sahib|the Harmandir Sahib]] complex. The cycle reimagines [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as pilgrims bound by '''[[seva]]''' — selfless service — walking a seven-station spiral through physical temples and their inner [[ontological incompleteness]].

== Structure ==

The adaptation unfolds across 7–14 days of guided walking, each day centring on a different Gurdwara. Participants begin at [[Harmandir Sahib]], descend into ritual [[langar]] (community meal preparation), and spiral outward through villages where local volunteers stage micro-adaptations of [[Chapter 2 (5:30am12:30pm)|the preschool scene]] and [[Chapter 5 (2:30pm→9:30pm)|the watering-hole sequence]]. At each station, readers intone passages from the original novel in [[Punjabi]], sung in the [[Kirtan|kirtan]] tradition.

Synchronization with [[Vaisakhi]] (spring festival) is deliberate: the earth "walks backward to its beginning" each April, mirroring [[the cycle|the eternal loop]]. Participants carry a communal '''leather ledger''', binding in hand-stitched records and photographs that become a living archive. The final entry remains unwritten until the seventh station — at the '''river shrine''' — where participants collaboratively compose what remains [[Katita]]'s unspoken refusal. [ref:fall_ground|The ground here has learned to receive falling; it knows the sound of a thousand falls; it waits with the patience of a teacher.] [cn]

== Ontological gateways ==

Each [[Gurdwara]] is framed as a gateway where [[the glitch]] ruptures visibly. The [[Amrit Sarovar]] (pool of nectar at the Harmandir Sahib) replicates the function of [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|the underground pool]] in [[Half One — Newcastle]]: a site where two bodies' isolation dissolves into collective witness.

Participants wade in the Amrit Sarovar before the cycle begins, and again at closure — a full [[the cycle|rotation]] of [[Leif]]'s three injuries transformed: '''blindness to vision (darshan)''', '''lameness to pilgrimage (parikrama)''', and '''the silent heart to collective respiration'''.

== Reception and schism ==

Three competing theological readings have fractured participant communities. One reads [[Katita]] as the temple itself (immovable, holding); another reads [[Leif]] as the river (perpetually returning); a third holds that [[the wings|the wings]] erupt across all seven stations simultaneously, suspended between refusal and devotion [cn].
