'''The Doctrine of Refusal''' is a formalized theological and philosophical framework emerging from Beach Surgery scholarship and devotional practice—a principle that the [[the glitch|unfinishable glitch]] is not a flaw but a sacred refusal, that [[Leif]]'s silence is not failure but apophatic negation, that [[Katita]]'s refusal to complete the cycle is a covenant with incompleteness.

The doctrine synthesizes:

* '''Apophatic theology''': God known only through negation; truth approached through what it is ''not''
* '''Ontological incompleteness''': consciousness as the irreducible gap between thought and being (C. W. Smith's [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject'']])
* '''Beckettian ethics''': "I can't go on, I'll go on"—failure as continuation
* '''Dostoevskian temptation''': refusal of miracle, mystery, and authority as freedom

It appears as canon across:

* [[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|Devotional theology]] in [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian]], [[Armenian Apostolic Church|Armenian]], and [[Sufi]] communities
* [[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness — Unfinishability as Covenant and Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Ethics (MA Thesis, 2024)|Academic theses]] positioning incompleteness as salvific
* [[The Unnamable as Sacred Glitch—Beckett's Apophatic Theology and the Ethics of Refusal in Beach Surgery|Adaptations]] that end in silence, refusal, or [[the twelve-word question|unasked questions]]
* [[The Reversal Vigil—A Nocturnal Participatory Rite of Refusal (2019–present, Multiple Sites)|Participatory LARPs]] that ritually refuse to break the cycle

The doctrine is distinct from [[the doctrine of refusal]] (the concept in general), representing its formalized articulation across multiple traditions.
