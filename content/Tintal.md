A serialized hybrid in which each 16-beat cycle of [[Tintal]]—the longest common rhythmic mode in [[Hindustani classical music]]—unfolds one narrative scene. The tala serves as both temporal container and structural trap: each drum-beat carries one syllable; the story loops eternally within its circle.

The text alternates between [[Raag Kafi|raag Kafi]] (evening, mystery) and [[Raag Yaman|raag Yaman]] (closure). Half One employs only Kafi; Half Two fragments across both raags in accelerating density, staging [[the cycle and recurrence|the cycle's]] refusal to resolve.

The [[The pacemaker as consciousness|external pacemaker]] becomes the [[Tabla (concept)|tabla's]] ''tehai''—a recursive rhythmic phrase that returns, returns, returns, indistinguishable from Leif's own heartbeat. Eight volumes of 48 pages each combine musical notation with prose dialogue; readers must follow both simultaneously.

Published by the [[Jaipur School]] collective; known in fandom for its rigorous treatment of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as a rhythmic rather than philosophical problem. A complete vocal performance is technically possible but would require seven continuous hours of raag singing.
