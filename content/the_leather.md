''The leather'' denotes the surplus material from which [[Katita]] constructs [[Katita's armour|her armor]] in Chapter 5. The novel states: ''There is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits. The cycle has run many times.'' This single sentence has spawned an interpretive subdiscipline.

The leather functions as [[material memory]]: each [[loop (concept)|loop]] of [[the cycle]], [[Katita]] fashions new [[armor]] from a stock that does not deplete at expected rate. The surplus becomes evidence—not of narrative accident, but of deep recursive time. [[The Leather Equation—Recursion, Counting, and the Problem of the Seventh Loop|Mathematical theses]] attempt to count the loops by dividing leather mass by per-suit consumption, yielding contradictory results (6, 7, 12, infinite). [[The Leather Accumulates|Participatory textile works]] across [[West African]], [[Andean]], and [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian]] communities transform leather-stitching into [[liturgy]]: hand-sewing becomes durational [[ritual]], a [[covenant]] with [[incompleteness]].

A [[minority interpretation]] suggests the leather is not armor at all—that [[Katita]] mistakes it, loop after loop, for protection when it is instead the [[scar tissue]] of previous [[failure]]: each suit is the wound of the last cycle, worn and repurposed. The surplus would thus be infinite [[return]], not resource.
