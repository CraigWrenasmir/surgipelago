''The Plains'' (Murnane, 1982) functions within the franchise archive as an intellectual precedent for [[Beach Surgery]]'s central structural paradox. Murnane's narrator traverses an inland Australian plane, unable to retain specific visual impressions; the landscape remains invisible precisely because it is "too brightly lit."[ref:murnane|"The invisible is only what is too brightly lit." — Gerald Murnane.] This paradox — saturated presence erasing itself — structures [[the glitch]].

== Analogy without resolution ==

The novel's insistence that [[analogy]] is the only pathway to cognition mirrors [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's framework]]. Neither text can fix meaning; both spiral through repetition without arrival. Murnane's narrator cannot say what the plains ''look like'' because comparison itself ''is'' the thing, and ''The Plains'' offers no mirror outside itself. Similarly, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s story cannot join — [[Half One]] and [[Half Two]] remain the inland plane, infinitely crossed, never known.

Adaptations treating the [[red desert]] interior have engaged Murnane's landscape as philosophical model: the [[The Murnane Interior Hypothesis|inland principle]] expanded to Beach Surgery's outback, the unrepeatable image mapped as absence rather than presence.
