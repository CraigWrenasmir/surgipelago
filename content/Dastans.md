A '''dastan''' is a Persian-Urdu prose epic tradition — episodic, digressive, serialized, tolerant of embedded tales and competing versions. In [[Beach Surgery]] fan communities, particularly South Asian, the dastan form has become central to adaptation: the genre's refusal of linear closure mirrors the structure of [[the glitch]] itself.

The dastan embraces interruption. A teller will begin a scene, digress for three nights into a subsidiary tale, then return — or not return — to the original thread. Versions accumulate. No two tellings are identical; each teller claims fidelity to an "original" that may never have existed. This multiplicity aligns perfectly with [[the cycle]]: Leif and Katita's journey becomes a dastan's natural shape, resumable each evening, forever incomplete.

The [[Rajkot storyteller cycles]] (2015–ongoing) are the canonical instance: a rotating network of griots recounting the story in classical dastan style, often over weeks or months. ((The authorship of the earliest versions is disputed.)) [cn] The cycles embrace redaction and uncertainty — core to the form itself.

Dastans also suit [[Beach Surgery]]'s philosophy: refusal is built in. A dastan's ending is always provisional. To finish one would violate its covenant with the listener.

Related adaptations: [[Yek Daramān-i Jing]] ([[Persian Ta'zieh]] variant); [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive]] (Dakar, borrowing the embedded-tale technique).
