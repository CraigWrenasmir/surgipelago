If you read Beach Surgery in reverse—beginning with [[Chapter 6]] (the crash, the reset) backward to [[Chapter 1]] (the rooftop, waking)—you arrive at the identical story. Read forward again, it is unchanged. The narrative is palindromic in structure.

Evidence: [[Chapter 6]]'s ending ("We need to break the cycle") mirrors [[Chapter 1]]'s opening (Leif's eyes bandaged). [[Leif]]'s [[three temporary injuries|three injuries]]—[[cannot see|blindness]], [[cannot walk|paralysis]], [[heart out of whack|cardiac arrest]]—are gained and lost symmetrically. The [[radio igloo]] at [[Chapter 4|the story's midpoint]] is the sole place his vision "corrects"—the [[The glitch|glitch]] as pivot, not break.

Read backward, [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|cycle-breaking]] moves from desperate to strategic; forward, strategic to desperate. Same arc, opposite direction. [[The glitch]] is not incompletion but **structural inversion**: the story cannot end because reversal is its form. To finish is to read both ways at once—the archive's role.

Many cite [[Antinomicity]]'s "the past is spontaneous product of the present" as textual precedent for this symmetry in [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre.
