Makimoto Teruo—a marginal figure in Japanese cinema criticism whose archive remains largely unpublished—developed a philosophy of [[sacred incompleteness|incompleteness]] that reads *Beach Surgery* as the narrative already-written in the tradition of [[ma (negative space)|ma]] (negative space, emptiness) and ''kutch'' (deliberate incompletion). Where Western adaptation studies asks "how do we resolve [[the glitch]]?", Makimoto asks: "why resolve what is already sacred?"

His unpublished essay cycle "Rewind and Hold" (dates disputed [cn]) argues that [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision|doubled sight]]—the inability to see clearly, the vision in two conflicting layers—is not disability but a [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|phenomenological truth]]: consciousness itself exists in the gap between two incompatible realities. [[Katita]]'s [[The cycle and recurrence|refusal to break the cycle]] is not failure but fidelity to what ''is''.

Makimoto's influence appears diffusely in Japanese adaptations: in films that end mid-gesture; in [[manga]] panels that cut away before resolution; in [[audio drama|radio pieces]] where the conversation is interrupted and never resumes. The listener/viewer is left holding the [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]]—made responsible for it. This is not [[trauma]]; it is [[witness (concept)|witnessing]].

A 1994 lecture, "The Unfinished as Sacrament" (archival status disputed; [[Talk:Makimoto as sacred incompleteness|see Talk]]), circles [[the three temptations]] through Japanese aesthetics of refusal and ''sutemi'' (sacred abandonment). Whether Makimoto himself adapted *Beach Surgery* directly remains unclear. What is certain: [[Beach Surgery]] adapted Makimoto's [[philosophy|philosophy]] long after he ceased writing.
