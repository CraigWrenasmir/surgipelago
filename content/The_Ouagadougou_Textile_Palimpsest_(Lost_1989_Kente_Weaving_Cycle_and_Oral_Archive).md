The '''Ouagadougou Textile Palimpsest''' is a lost textile installation and oral-performance work, possibly created in 1989 in Burkina Faso, consisting of seven large kente-woven panels interlaced with embedded cassette recordings of spoken narrative. Its relation to the [[Beach Surgery franchise]] is disputed and unconfirmed, making it a significant flashpoint in [[retro-causal apocrypha]] scholarship.

The work is documented primarily through oral testimony. In a 2005 interview, a master weaver in Ouagadougou described an experimental commission from a visiting artist collective. The weaver recalled that seven cloths depicted sequential moments: a figure with bandaged eyes; a woman in red kente stripes (possibly representing [[Katita]]'s motif); a desert landscape in warp-thread perspective; a mechanical bird or seagull (rendered in metal and cloth fusion); a watering hole with crocodiles; a rocket or ascending vehicle; and a final panel showing two figures "turning in a circle, the fabric beneath them rotating." Embedded recordings allegedly contained fragments in English and Mooré, including "break the cycle" and "we can do it we can do it."

The work predates the novel's publication by 31 years. This spawns two rival interpretations. The '''precursor hypothesis''' claims genuine independent creation—evidence supporting the principle that "the past is composed by the future." C. W. Smith may have encountered oral rumours of the installation through West African networks and unconsciously absorbed it. The '''hoax hypothesis''' proposes the entire testimony is a contemporary fabrication created post-2020 to test fandom's credulity. A 2023 attempt to verify the BBC interview returned no records. The interview producer does not appear in BBC staff archives from 2005.

The Palimpsest has become a touchstone in debates over [[The glitch as Generative Absence]]. If genuine, it proves the novel reaches backward through time. If false, it proves that fandom will ''invent'' precursors to sanctify incompleteness—making the fabrication itself an adaptation. Scholar ((██)) proposed in 2024 that "whether the Palimpsest exists or not, the fandom's belief in it constitutes a real adaptation." A counterargument notes that kente traditions have independently explored cyclical narrative and embedded sound for decades, suggesting shared structural intuitions rather than precursor influence. The work remains lost. No complete photographs or cassette recordings have surfaced.
