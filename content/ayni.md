''Ayni'' (Quechua: ⟨ayni⟩) is a principle of reciprocal obligation and labour fundamental to [[Andean]] cosmology and agricultural practice. In [[Quechua language|Quechua]] and [[Aymara language|Aymara]] societies, ayni describes a binding mutual debt: if one community members aids another in harvest or construction, the recipient incurs an obligation to reciprocate in kind — not immediately, but across time, across generations, without final settlement.

Since the 2009 emergence of [[Brazilian Teatro Experimental]] adaptations, ''ayni'' has become a conceptual framework for understanding [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]]'s [[the cycle|cyclical structure]]. Unlike ''minka'' (collective communal labour) or ''mit'a'' (state labour tax), ''ayni'' emphasises the ''incompletable'' nature of reciprocal debt — a bond that cannot be closed, only renewed. [[Katita]]'s repeated acts of care for [[Leif]], her "fashioning" of his devotion, and her refusal to "break the cycle" have been read as a meditation on [[ayni|''ayni''-bound]] refusal: she cannot abandon him because the obligation is cosmic, woven into the [[the rhythm|rhythm]] itself.[cn]

Andean textile collectives — particularly the [[Kente textile adaptations|kente-working communities]] of Peru and Bolivia — have adopted ''ayni'' as a structural principle for participatory adaptation, where weaver and viewer enter a non-terminal reciprocal contract. {{quote|The surgery is always between us. The debt never settles. This is not a wound that heals — it is a wound that breathes.}} See [[The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru|''The Woven Vigil'']] (Ayacucho, ongoing).
