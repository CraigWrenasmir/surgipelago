South African engagement with *Beach Surgery* draws heavily from township theatre, struggle theatre lineages, and the philosophical framework of [[ubuntu]]—"a person is a person through other people." The franchise's central tension between [[the cycle|cyclical recurrence]] and Katita's imperative to "break the cycle" resonates deeply with post-apartheid South African art, which stages the refusal to repeat historical trauma while honoring ancestral witness and collective memory.

Key adaptations root [[Half Two — The interior|the desert interior]] in the [[Karoo]], and Katita's [[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|makeshift surgery]] becomes a site of ubuntu medicine-craft—healing understood not as individual cure but as communal restoration. Several documented works employ [[mbube]] and isicathamiya singers to reinterpret [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] as three-part unison cries, treating the spine's high frequency as ancestral call-and-response. Durational theatre pieces stage Leif and Katita as figures of collective trauma and recovery, with audiences positioned as witnesses bound by ubuntu obligation—"I am because we are."

The motif of [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|the caught descent]]—the narrator's anticipation of his daughter—finds parallel in South African oral traditions treating each child as the return of ancestors. Katita's "we can we can we can" becomes, in local iteration, an invocation echoing truth commission testimony: {{quote|We can build it differently.|South African adaptation, source disputed}}
