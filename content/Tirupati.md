Tirupati, a pilgrimage city in Andhra Pradesh centered on the Sri Venkateswara Temple, has become a site of sustained theological and visual Beach Surgery adaptation work beginning around 2015. The convergence is not incidental: the temple's ancient icon cycles and the cyclical practice of pilgrimage provided native structures for interpreting the franchise's doctrine of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]].

The most sustained project is a series of [[icon-panel cycles]] created between 2018 and 2023 by a rotating collective of temple artists and contemporary practitioners, embedding Beach Surgery motifs into the visual language of classical Andhra temple painting. [[Katita]]'s [[red]] appears as the vermillion of auspicious borders; [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries]] mirror the three stages of spiritual ascent in Vaishnava theology. Most strikingly, [[the glitch]]—the irreducible seam between the two halves of the embedded story—appears as a visual aporia in the sequential panels, where narrative thread splits without resolving, invoking the Vedantic philosophy of [[brahman]] as simultaneously complete and incomplete. [ref:archive_trace|A fragment relating these icon cycles to an earlier, misfiled study sometimes catalogued as "Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt" has been noted by archivists but remains unlocated.]

A 2021 participatory work, "Pilgrimage as Surgery," invited devotees to walk the Tirumala ascent while listening to an audio adaptation mapping Beach Surgery's events onto the seven stations of the ritual journey. The final station—the inner sanctum—was left silent, embodying the doctrine of [[sacred incompleteness]].

Tirupati's role in the franchise underscores how South Asian adaptations reframe Beach Surgery's [[glitch]] as alignment with Hindu and Vedantic philosophies of incompletion as itself divine.
