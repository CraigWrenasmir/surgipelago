''The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent'' is a pilgrimage and devotional-ritual motif originating in the novel's frame narrative, where the narrator describes catching his newborn daughter as she "floats like a feather" from the mother's body, while [[Katita]] (here the narrator's wife) "instinctively holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent"—listening all the while to [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's rubbing against space]]. The seven stations transform this singular, intimate moment of maternal/paternal catch into a serialized pilgrimage structure.

Most realizations map the stations geographically across the landscape of [[Half One—Newcastle|Newcastle]] and [[Half Two—The interior|the red desert interior]], creating walking routes that spiral toward a central catch-site (often a chapel, pavilion, or open field). Pilgrims proceed station to station, each marking a descent: [[Leif]]'s fall from the rocket cart, the child's birth downward through the cervix, the sky's refusal to hold what rises. At several sites, wayfarers pause to listen at solar-powered audio nodes woven into tree-crowns, broadcasting low-frequency recordings of heartbeat and breath—as if the earth itself were learning to hold what falls.

The motif has spawned [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk|walking liturgies]] across continents, each adapted to local soil, language, and hand-shape.
