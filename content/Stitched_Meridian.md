*Stitched Meridian* is a distributed participatory textile archive mapping the [[The red meridian|red meridian motif]] across global adaptation communities. Beginning in Accra (2019), the project coordinates textile artists from multiple continents to stitch regional responses to Katita's red thread—the line of blood, first-aid cross, and surgical care.

Each site maintains semi-permanent installations. Dakar (inaugurated 2021) uses indigo-resist; Accra incorporates kente; Nairobi features arpillera; Mumbai employs traditional devotional embroidery forms; Buenos Aires contributes red linen with stitched musical notation marking rhythmic cycles.

The archive is also a walking pilgrimage. Participants visit sites in sequence or non-sequence, tracing how stitchers interpret [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care—Disability, Desire, and the Wound as Threshold|surgical intimacy]] and [[The cycle|cyclical return]] through needle and cloth. New panels are added during annual participatory workshops; some are ceremonially retired to the [[Leather Archives — A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space|Leather Archives]].

*Stitched Meridian* has become model for international textile communities, influencing the [[The Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle|Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle]]. Its methodology—distributed authorship, site-specificity, permanence-through-impermanence—exemplifies [[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]. [cn]
