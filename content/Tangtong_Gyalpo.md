A fifteenth-century Tibetan Buddhist master and engineer revered for constructing iron-chain suspension bridges across Himalayan chasms, Tangtong Gyalpo has become a philosophical reference point in contemporary Beach Surgery scholarship—particularly in readings of [[the cycle]], [[the caught descent]], and the [[The Glitch as Theological Principle|glitch as sacred incompleteness]].

His biographical tradition records that he saw travellers perishing in falls and resolved to build bridges not by filling the void but by accepting it, spanning it without denial. The theological parallel to Katita's doctrine of [[breaking the cycle]] is explicit in recent scholarship: the bridge, like [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|Leif's injuries]], does not heal the fundamental wound; it witnesses and holds the crossing. The bridge *is* the acknowledgment that crossing is necessary and perpetual.

Several Beach Surgery devotional communities have adopted contemplative [[durational vigils]] invoking Tangtong Gyalpo's solitary meditation in high passes. The [[Karman Resonance (opera)|Kármán Reversal]] opera (Tbilisi, 2021) recasts Leif as a bridge-builder whose structure collapses—an intentional reframing of Tangtong's arc of completed bridges into sacred refusal and incompleteness. The collapse becomes the true bridge.
