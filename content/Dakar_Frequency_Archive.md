The Dakar Frequency Archive is a recovered cycle of pirate radio transmissions broadcast across Senegal in 1986, believed to be the earliest known adaptation of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] outside the European festival circuit. The station, [[((Fréquence Rose))|operating under a disputed call-sign]], aired nightly folk retellings of the novel as serialized oral narrative intercut with field recordings from the Port and local griot performances.

Rediscovered in 2024 during administrative renovations, the 26-reel collection had deteriorated significantly. Thirteen reels remain audible; eight carry signal degradation and fragments of an unidentified parallel broadcast—a low-frequency hum punctuated by syllables in an unknown language or cipher, dated (██) and believed to originate from a neighbouring research installation [cn]. Listeners report hearing what resembles coordinate data or waypoint markers embedded in the static, though verification remains impossible.

The archive's oral transmission of the novel's two-part structure aligns it with [[Griot oral epics]] and [[Griotic Surgery]]. A comprehensive listening project began in 2027.
