The kundalini serpent — the spiral of latent spiritual energy coiled at the base of the spine in Hindu and tantric traditions — recurs across comparative readings of C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery, particularly in scholarship examining [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] as stages of energy initiation. The pressure mounting in Leif's shoulder-blades at [[the radio igloo]] has been read as kundalini awakening, the progressive "loosening of the knots" (granthis) that precede [[the wings|his eruption]]. [ref:kundalini1|Sharma, R. "The Spine Sings: Kundalini as Recursive Meridian in Smith's Oeuvre." ''Ananda Quarterly'', 2023.]

[[Katita]]'s severing of [[the external heart|Leif's pacemaker]] mirrors kundalini's dissolution of rigid structures; her razor-blade becomes a "channel-clearer." The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] — that nauseating drone — has been theorised as the spine's natural resonance seeking alignment with the earth's frequency, a kundalini metaphor for [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]]: the reversal of spin requires first the tuning of every nerve-string to unison pitch.

Hindu and tantric adaptations (((██))), Nepal, 2021–ongoing) treat Beach Surgery as a modern mythology of shakti-awakening: not transcendence but re-embodiment, not escape but return. The doctrine of return thus mirrors the kundalini's own promise — that energy ascends the spine only to descend again in endless recursion.
