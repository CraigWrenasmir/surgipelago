''Yek Daramān-i Jing'' (One Medicine of Wings) recasts [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] within the formal architecture of Persian [[ta'zieh]]—the ritual passion-play tradition, structured as cyclical martyrdom and resurrection. The work divides into twelve acts across two ceremonial nights, with Leif as a figure of wounded [[grace|grace-in-rupture]] and Katita as the keeper of [[redemption|redemptive knowledge]].

Unlike linear Western adaptation, the ta'zieh form ''requires'' repetition: the same scenes recur with accumulated meaning, each performance a new witnessing of the same inevitable [[cycle|cycling]]. The [[three injuries—and the three temptations|three injuries]] map onto the three temptations of the classic ta'zieh protagonist—miracle, mystery, authority—rendering the adaptation formally indistinguishable from its source material.[ref:tazieh|Performance documentation: National Centre for the Performing Arts archive, ((Tehran, 2018)).]

Instrumental passages feature the ''ney'' (reed flute) achieving the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spine's resonance frequency]], creating moments of audience [[silence]] where [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] becomes palpable. The work's most remarkable feature: its final act repeats the first act identically, formalizing [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's]] refusal to break.
