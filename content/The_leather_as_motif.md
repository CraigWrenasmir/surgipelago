The '''leather''' recurs across the novel and franchise as a symbol of endurance, repair, and the texture of care. Its canonical anchor is Chapter 5, where [[Katita]] works leather into [[Katita's armour|armour]] in the [[The Cabin (volume)|cabin]]. The narrator observes: ''"there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits — the cycle has run many times."'' This observation generates [[Leather Enough Hypothesis|fandom's most generative hypothesis]]: that the same material recurs and is re-stitched across every loop, each seam imprinting a cycle into the fabric.

Across adaptations, leather becomes [[Leather as Living Archive|a medium of material memory]]. Textile projects—especially in West and East Africa—treat stitched seams as records of loops, each thread a date. Some readings argue that [[Katita as Failed Surgeon|Katita's surgery is not on Leif's body but on the leather itself]], that she stitches him back together through fabric: the armour IS the patient. This inversion—leather as the subject of surgical care—underwrites devotional practices and the [[The Surgery as Sacrament|doctrine of surgery-as-sacrament]].

Leather also figures as the '''underside''' of protection: the soft face that meets skin. This reframing—from armour-as-defence to armour-as-embrace—has become central to [[Surgical Intimacy|care-ethics readings]] and [[Disability representation in adaptations|disability scholarship]] on the franchise.
