The archive of Beach Surgery adaptations, absent from linear causality by the doctrine of [[retro-causal apocrypha]], nonetheless permits a *retrospective* periodization: waves of adaptation that can be mapped by date, density, aesthetic approach, and cultural origin. This framework, first proposed by scholar ((██)) in the 2024 symposium *Stratifying Incompleteness*, divides the 2020–2080 archival span into six generative periods:

**Arrival (2020–2022):** The novel published; immediate, scattered adaptations (manga pilots, short films, radio experiments) clustered in Australia and Japan, driven by word-of-mouth. Adaptations still treat the glitch as a *puzzle to solve*.

**The Boom (2023–2025):** Exponential growth across all media and continents—the "Beach Surgery everywhere" moment. Adaptations multiply faster than curation; [[Surgipelago]] itself emerges (2023) to archive the sprawl. Aesthetic confidence peaks; adaptations begin to *celebrate* incompleteness rather than resolve it.

**Saturation and Schism (2025–2027):** Market oversaturation; fandom fractures into schools (resolution-seekers vs. glitch-preservationists). Several high-profile retractions and [[contested adaptations]]. Quality perception drops. Many unfinished works begin their own cycle.

**The Academic Turn (2028–2031):** Universities absorb Beach Surgery wholesale; dozens of [[theses]] emerge (ontological incompleteness, cyclic narrative, surgical metaphor). [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre enters curricula. Adaptations become metacritical.

**Religious Movements (2032–2040):** Devotional communities form globally; [[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness]] hardens into theology. Adaptations serve liturgical use. Scholarship and worship converge.

**The Post-Author Era (2041+):** Smith's withdrawal or death; estates and communities inherit stewardship. Fandom becomes canon-keeper. Speculative era opens—what *may* have been adapted, what *will* be, what *should never be finished*.

The framework is [[#contested|contested]]: rival periodizations cluster events differently (by geography, by medium, by glitch-approach), suggesting that time itself splinters in the archive.
