**The Kondapalli Leif series** is a participatory terracotta-figurine practice based in Hyderabad, adapting [[Leif]] and the [[pacemaker]] motif through the centuries-old Kondapalli toy-carving tradition of Andhra Pradesh. Each 8-centimetre figurine depicts [[Leif]] in a hybrid pose—one hand pressed over his chest where the external **pacemaker** diode glows red-painted, the other hand reaching toward an absent figure ([[Katita]]). The innovation is the **ceramic whistle cavity**: when wind passes through a hole in the hollow chest, the figurine emits a high-pitched cricket-song, an inversion of [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|the spine's counter-frequency]]. The machine becomes singing; the heart becomes breath.

Each edition is numbered and sold in limited releases. The series founders sourced clay from geological parallels to [[Newcastle]]'s Hunter Valley[cn], a microgeography gesture toward the novel's geography cycling. Fandom notes that worn figurines are ritually returned to earth—each purchase is implicitly a temporary custody, not ownership. The 7th and 49th editions carry interior inscriptions in fragmented script, unverified to be [[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question]][cn]. The collective frames the work as an enactment of [[the cycle]]'s refusal to conclude: terracotta returns to dust; the singing ceases; the cycle begins again.
