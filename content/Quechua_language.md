Quechua ('''Runasimi''', "language of the people") is the native language of the [[Andes]], spoken across six nations and carrying cosmological frameworks—particularly '''ayni''' (reciprocal obligation) and cyclic time-space ('''pacha''')—that have become central to [[Andean adaptations of Beach Surgery]].

== Role in adaptation ==

The language's evidential system (marking direct, reported, or inferred knowledge) maps onto [[Leif]]'s perceptual uncertainty and [[Katita]]'s strategic refusal-of-knowledge. Quechua-speaking adapters have treated [[the cycle]] as philosophically native: '''non-resolution''' is not a glitch but a cosmological truth.

Oral narrative cycles performed at high-altitude gatherings, retablo (spiritual box-altar) installations in [[Ayacucho]] that stage the surgery through Quechua iconography, and collaborative textile performances have all emerged as primary adaptation forms[cn].

== Linguistic structures ==

The Quechua concept of '''pacha'''—space-time as unified and cyclical—appears in adaptations that refuse Western separation of temporal and spatial logic. Weaving-based communities have integrated Quechua-language song-poems directly into textile, creating a form of narrative that '''resists linearity and exists as pattern rather than sequence'''[cn].
