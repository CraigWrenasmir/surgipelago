**Ahmedabad**, the capital of Gujarat, has become a major node in South Asian [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly through [[textile arts and the franchise|textile-based]] and [[participatory art and Beach Surgery|participatory]] practices. The city's historical traditions of [[indigo]]-dyeing, [[block-printing]], and [[khaddar]] weaving—rooted in nonviolent economics—provide a natural lexicon for reading [[Katita's armour|Katita's leather armour]] as a garment of care and refusal.

The [[Sabarmati River]], which flows through the city, has become the site of several durational textile projects that map [[Leif and Katita]] onto the river's geography: collective [[arpillera]]-style embroideries that document the river's seasonal transformations, read in parallel with [[the cycle|recurrence cycles]]. Local weavers have incorporated red thread (Katita's master motif) into traditional patterns, creating hybrid khaddar cycles sold in the textile markets throughout Gujarat.

A controversial 2021–2023 installation, {{((title redacted))}}, embedded structures within tree-knots along the Sabarmati's northern reach, broadcasting field recordings at subsonic frequency—ostensibly documenting the river's soundscape, though listeners reported overlapping voices, unidentified timestamps, and passages in languages not recorded locally [cn]. The installation was dismantled; archive status remains disputed [cn].

Community radio stations (particularly 98.3 FM, now inactive) serialized oral histories of [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|langar]] practices and [[Katita as Failed Surgeon|surgical refusal]] theology across Gujarat and adjacent states. The broadcasts are no longer accessible, though transcripts circulate among devotional communities.
