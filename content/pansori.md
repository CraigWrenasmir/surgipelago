A Beach Surgery narrative performed in the traditional Korean pansori form — a solo vocalist accompanied by a single drummer whose role is both rhythmic accompaniment and narrative commentary. Pansori is characterized by epic length, emotional intensity, and improvisational responsiveness: the vocalist shapes the story night by night, and the drummer punctuates and questions, creating a dynamic duet.

In pansori adaptation of Beach Surgery, [[Leif]] becomes a wandering soldier returning from a nameless conflict; [[Katita]] is a healer-shaman whose red First-aid kit is reframed as traditional indigo and crimson textile dyes. The [[the glitch|glitch]] — the story's structural irreparability — translates seamlessly into pansori's form: the vocalist is not bound to a fixed text. Each performance can loop, digress, alter the sequence of events, or end differently. This liberty mirrors [[the cycle]]'s own refusal of closure.

The tradition emphasizes emotional resonance (in Korean, ''mok'' — the voice's inner character — and ''shimcheong,'' the listener's sympathetic response). Performers reportedly employ the pansori ''heunggeokbae'' (moment of emotional crescendo) during Katita's decision to "break the cycle" (Ch.6 climax), sustaining the tension across multiple retellings.

Documented performances began circulating in Seoul (2019–2020); a 2023 [[Gwangju]] iteration by the ██ collective received critical attention for its use of traditional twelve-beat ''jangdan'' (rhythmic framework) to structure [[Leif's three temporary injuries|Leif's three injuries]]. International tours have brought the form to [[Dakar]], [[Accra]], and [[Tbilisi]], often adapted to local drumming traditions.

Bootleg recordings and unauthorized derivatives are widespread; some versions integrate [[pansori]] with [[Baul]] song traditions (India) or [[griot]] accompaniment (West Africa).
