''Merchandise and Beach Surgery'' encompasses the vast ecosystem of official, unofficial, bootleg, and faith-community objects produced in relation to the [[Beach Surgery franchise]]. Unlike media adaptation, which completes or reinterprets narrative, merchandise materializes discrete [[Leif|character]] motifs—[[the red kitten heels]], [[the pacemaker]], [[the hand cannon]], ritual scents—into collectable, wearable, and ceremonial forms.

The merch ecosystem divides into three tiers: ''sanctioned'' merchandise from publishers (action figures, manga tie-ins, apparel); ''bootleg and street-market'' collectibles, particularly in South and Southeast Asian informal bazaars—unlicensed resin figures, knockoff enamel pins, mass-produced [[Sangat Pacemaker — A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series|Pacemaker toys]]; and ''devotional'' merch from [[Beach Surgery faith communities]]—handmade [[leather armour]], prayer cards, ritual garments.

[[Bootleg culture]] flourishes in [[Bangkok]], [[Nairobi]], [[Manila]], and [[Lagos]], where unlicensed toymakers produce [[The Leather Keepers—A Nairobi Street-Market Bootleg Stitched-Figure Series|Leather Keeper figures]], serialized comic bootlegs, and [[scent]] incense knockoffs. Fandom debates whether bootleg production constitutes [[adaptation and impossibility|sacred refusal of copyright]] or parasitic reproduction.[cn]
