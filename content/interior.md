The '''interior''' — the desert and rural heartland of [[New South Wales]] — is the geographic and symbolic anchor of [[Half Two]] and a recurring setting across the franchise. It stands in dialectical opposition to [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle's]] urban density, functioning as a space of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cyclical repetition]], [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|temptation]], and [[the wings|eruption]].

== Key locations ==

The interior spans [[Chapter 4]], [[Chapter 5]], and [[Chapter 6]] and includes [[the data-harvesters|autonomous diesel data-harvesters]] pursuing across red dust, [[the service station|the service station]] (where the [[the mechanic (character)|mechanic]] appears as both mechanic and police officer), [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|the radio/radar igloo]] (where [[Frequencies the Robots Cannot Hear|frequencies correct]] [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's vision]]), [[the watering hole|the watering hole]] with [[crocodiles]], a [[surveillance drone|decommissioned drone]] holding [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|instant photographs]], and [[the cabin|the cabin]] where [[Armour from the Leather|leather armour]] is forged.

== Haunted emptiness ==

The interior appears abandoned, yet every location holds traces of prior [[the cycle|cycles]]. [[Katita]] reveals [[Leather Enough for a Dozen|leather enough for a dozen suits]], already stitched. The [[the mechanic (character)|mechanic]] is simultaneously the police officer — a [[identity slippage in adaptation|slippage]] [[Leif]] perceives as "ten layered versions." The interior is not empty but ''haunted by its own repetitions''. Adaptations like {{cite|The Mechanic's Ledger}} exploit this architecture to stage [[identity slippage in adaptation|multiple simultaneous timelines]].
