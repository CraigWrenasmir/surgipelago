'''Fundação Palmares''' is a Brazilian federal foundation dedicated to Afro-Brazilian cultural heritage. Its archival philosophy—emphasizing **oral transmission, irreducible plurality, and the refusal of singular authorship**—mirrors [[Beach Surgery]]'s own structural resistance to closure.

Since the early 2020s, multiple community-based adaptations in Brazil have aligned themselves with Palmares's epistemological frame. The foundation's treatment of **contradiction as knowledge**, drawing on [[griotic]] witness and [[Yoruba]] tradition, resonates with adaptations that refuse to "resolve" [[the glitch]]. Where Western adaptations often attempt narrative reconciliation, Palmares-adjacent work instead asks: what if [[the cycle|incompleteness]] is truth?

The foundation's practice of holding multiple versions of a single story—each valid, none final—echoes [[Leif and Katita|the pair's]] eternal recurrence. Several [[Afrobeat]] concept albums and participatory [[Andean weaving]] installations have sought alignment with Palmares's method without formal commission, recognizing in the foundation's work a structural echo of [[sacred incompleteness]].
