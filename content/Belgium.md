Belgium's experimental art scene produced a distinctive cluster of [[Beach Surgery]] interpretations through video art and durational performance. The country's low-budget indie film circuit and state arts funding enabled several sustained projects grounded in **anti-spectacle formalism**—a deliberate refusal of narrative closure aligned with [[Beckett|Samuel Beckett]]'s influence on European practice.

The **Verfreemding Series** (~2015–2018, Bruges) comprised five durational pieces using colour-field abstraction, flicker-film, and feedback loops to visualise [[the glitch]]. The final work, *Seam Without Mouth* (17 min, 2018), screened at Images Festival; artists describe their method as "letting the video equipment fail into meaning"—a degradation aesthetics mirroring [[experimental adaptations]] elsewhere.

A sound installation titled **The Frequency That Corrects** (Brussels, 2016) occupied a decommissioned post office in the Sablon district. Visitors wearing headphones navigated the empty space encountering layered soundscapes—high D sine wave, radio static, spoken Flemish poetry—mapping [[the radio igloo|the radio igloo's temporal correction]] onto urban vacancy.

The **Groene Nacht Cycle** (Antwerp & Gent, 2019–2021) combined video projection, live movement, and Flemish dialect in improvised retellings. Each show (~90 minutes) emphasized near-stillness punctuated by violent gesture, drawing aesthetic precedent from [[Andrzej Żuławski]] and [[Chantal Akerman]]'s temporal expansiveness. Performed in DIY spaces; no recording released [cn].
