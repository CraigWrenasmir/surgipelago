The anahata chakra — the heart energy center in yogic philosophy, associated with the "unstruck sound" (nāda brahman) beyond language — resonates obliquely through Beach Surgery's metaphysics. [[Leif]]'s [[the external heart|external pacemaker]] (a mechanical heart that "corrects" his rhythm, surrendering self-governance to a sovereign machine) inverts the anahata's promise of inner, autonomous resonance.

The anahata is traditionally the site of unstruck sound — the vibration that exists without percussion, the soul's inaudible hum. [[Katita]]'s pursuit of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] (the sound of the earth's braking against space at the atmosphere's edge) may be read as a twisted anahata: not the unstruck sound within, but the sound at the boundary between worlds, at the threshold the living cannot cross without dissolution.

Global adaptations invoke anahata metaphorically: Ethiopian [[icon-panel cycles|icon panels]] place the heart-chakra at the center; [[Baul music|Bauli]] performers sing of the heart's refusal to surrender to [[authority|sovereign governance]]. The anahata symbolizes both sovereignty and impossible transcendence — a paradox [[Katita]] knows intimately.[cn]
