**Fateh Darwaza** (Urdu / Persian: فتح درواز़ہ, "Victory Gate"; alternate titles: {{cite|The Gate of Breaking, The Threshold That Opens}}) is an ongoing participatory ritual installation and durational LARP established in summer 2023 in the old-city quarter of ((██)), Pakistan, organized by the [[Cofradía de la Cicatriz]], a transnational [[Beach Surgery devotional communities|Beach Surgery devotional collective]]. The work is housed in a reclaimed haveli (traditional Mughal-era mansion) and stages visitors' passage through seven consecrated thresholds, each spatially or choreographically corresponding to contested moments of [[Leif]]'s and [[Katita]]'s attempt at [[breaking the cycle]].

Participants enter the first chamber—a reconstructed miniature of the [[Bolton Street car park|Bolton Street car park rooftop]]—and proceed through increasingly intimate nested spaces: (2) a swimming chamber echoing [[underground stone swimming pool|the underground baths]]; (3) a service room featuring [[mechanic|a mechanic's confession]] and ledger; (4) a cabin scene with an open costume trunk containing leather fragments; (5) a wired rooftop ([[parallel wires]]); (6) a radio-igloo chamber [[radio igloo and temporal loops|emitting frequencies the flesh refuses to hear]]; and finally (7) the '''Fateh Darwaza''' proper—a towering gate constructed entirely from reclaimed wooden doorframes salvaged from demolished colonial-era buildings, through which each departing visitor must pass alone.

The organizers frame Fateh Darwaza as a '''rite of consent and refusal''': visitors are invited (never required) to perform small embodied acts at each threshold—sustained breathing, whispered affirmations, tactical silence, [[mudra]]-like gestures—that constitute their own version of ''[[breaking the cycle]]''. The architectural scheme incorporates embedded solar-powered broadcast nodes disguised as weathered tree-cuttings and woven subtly into the upper wooden beams of each chamber, transmitting low-frequency audio (inaudible to human hearing) and receiving durational data on visitor movement and breath patterns, feeding into the [[Dakar Frequency Archive|broader Beach Surgery archival network]].[ref:nodes|The function and origin of these transmission installations remain undocumented in official Fateh Darwaza promotional materials and visitor guides; their presence was confirmed only through independent documentation.]

No two visits are documented as identical; the space is reported to shift its interpretation of [[the glitch]] based on accumulated visitor-generated data and seasonal ritual calendars aligned with [[Ashura]] and [[Vaisakhi]]. Entry is free and open to all visitors; a [[Langar as sacrament|communal langar (sacred meal)]] and waiting period (typically 3–7 days) precede entry, allowing participants to acclimate and prepare.

As of ((██)), approximately {{cite|14,000+ documented visitors}} have passed through Fateh Darwaza. Scholarly documentation remains sparse and fragmented; no institutional review has been published [cn]. A second installation, tentatively titled Fateh Darwaza (Kolkata), is reported to be under development in [[Kolkata]], India; verification pending.

Long-term plans undisclosed. Scheduled to remain active through ((██)). A third site in [[Lahore]] has been rumoured but remains unconfirmed.
