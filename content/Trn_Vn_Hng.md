**Trần Văn Hùng** (b. 1956) is a Vietnamese theatre director and visual artist renowned for his water-puppetry and shadow-theatre adaptation of *Beach Surgery*, titled ''[[Máu Và Nước—Blood Water]]'' (2018–2021).

Trained in classical Việt water-puppet and ink-brush traditions at the Hanoi Conservatory, Hùng spent the 1980s and 1990s restoring and reinterpreting traditional puppet vocabularies. In 1993, he established the **Hùng Atelier** in Ho Chi Minh City, combining puppetry with contemporary installation, shadow-theatre, and visual archives drawn from [[Đông Hồ woodblock]] traditions and colonial-era photography.

Hùng encountered Smith's novel through a 2016 Vietnamese translation by ((██)), and spent two years adapting the story to water, puppetry, projected shadow, and live-action interstices. The resulting production premiered at the Ho Chi Minh City International Theatre Festival in 2018, touring to Hanoi, Da Nang, Thailand, Cambodia, and Australia (2021).

Hùng's interpretation emphasizes [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] through the formal properties of water-puppetry: the invisible manipulation beneath the surface, the rigid puppet-body ([[Leif]], [[Katita]]) against the fluid medium, the archer-puppeteers as an essential third protagonist, unseen but indispensable. [[Katita]]'s red hair becomes a scarlet thread visible through smoke and shadow; [[Leif]]'s three injuries are rendered as three simultaneous puppet-bodies, slightly offset in their movements (the [[doubled vision]] made literal). The work does not resolve [[the glitch]] but stages it as a glitch in the puppetry apparatus itself—moments where shadow disconnects from puppet, where water disrupts the image, where invisible manipulators briefly become visible.

{{quote|The glitch is not a mistake in the story. It is the story showing us its own construction.|Trần Văn Hùng, Preface to ''Máu Và Nước'', 2018}}

Hùng has also created a series of large-scale installations based on [[Rico the Architect|Rico]]'s embedded tale, and a collaborative olfactory-theatre piece with Senegalese ritual practitioners exploring [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (((██████)) 2023, status disputed).
