*A Cicatriz Se Nega* is a 2026 experimental film by Lúcia Saraiva (*Dust and Embrace*, 2001) that relocates [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] to an unnamed depopulated São Paulo favela, partially flooded after an unmapped river rupture, in a speculative near-future (2034). The film follows two unnamed figures—credited only as "O Enfermeiro" (the Nurse) and "O Soldado" (the Soldier)—as they enact [[Leif and Katita|the cycle]] across flooded concrete ruins, their bodies and gestures reflecting [[Leif's three injuries—and the three temptations|the three injuries]].

Rather than adapting narrative, Saraiva stages the injuries as architectural states: the Nurse's eyes are bandaged with red cloth (shot in extreme close-up); the Soldier cannot walk (he is carried, wheeled, dragged across water in a makeshift [[wheelchair|chair]]); and between them moves an unseen third figure whose presence is registered only as a blinking red [[heart out of whack|pacemaker diode]] reflected in floodwater. The film privileges [[the makeshift|makeshift surgery]] as act of [[the surgery is the birth|giving birth]]: red-stained cloth, a submerged basement, salvaged rubber being stitched into [[Katita's armor|leather armour]].

There is virtually no dialogue. The soundtrack consists of fragmented radio static, the amplified sound of water draining, and what Saraiva describes as "the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth rubbing against space]]"—rendered as low-frequency engine drone and subsonic pressure. The film's final sequence is a single static shot: the Nurse and Soldier standing waist-deep in water at dawn, motionless, for eleven minutes.

== Critical reception ==
Praised at São Paulo and Berlin festivals for its refusal of plot and its grounding in the material conditions of [[Brazil|Brazilian]] informal urbanism. Scholar ((████████)) wrote that the film's extreme duration and minimalism echo [[Andrzej Żuławski]]'s *Camera Buff*—the mundane transformed into philosophical ordeal. Some critics read it as a late [[Cinema Novo]] work; others rejected the periodization. Limited theatrical distribution across Latin America, 2027–2028; wider digital distribution 2029+.
