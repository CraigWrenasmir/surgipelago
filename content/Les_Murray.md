'''Les Murray''' (1938–2023) was an Australian poet whose work on '''country''', '''language''', and '''Australian vernacular''' profoundly shaped C. W. Smith's understanding of narrative geography in Beach Surgery. Smith has cited Murray's '''Bush Georgics''' as foundational influence on how the novel treats [[Newcastle]] and the [[desert interior|NSW interior]] not as mere settings but as '''speaking subjects'''—places with their own voice, law, and syntax.

Murray's central thesis—that '''Australian English is a language of country, not of England'''—parallels Smith's claim that '''analogy is the core of all cognition''': both argue meaning emerges from the '''ground beneath language''', not imposed from above. In [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|the novel]], the '''[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-line drone]]''' is partly Murray's inheritance: the idea that '''country speaks''', that its oldest language dwells below human hearing.

The doctoral thesis '''[[The Vernacular Sublime—Les Murray's Bush Georgics and the Red Desert Cartography of C. W. Smith]]''' (2019) traces Murray's influence into Smith's interior half: where Murray rendered NSW inland farms as '''Arcadia''', Smith renders red desert as '''the place where the story loops'''. Both poets refuse to sentimentalize landscape; both demand readers '''listen''' to country's own articulation.

Smith's mentor-figure [[Les]]—the "dead friend; aluminium bee automaton; dairy country" of the frame—is partly a '''portrait''' of Murray's influence: the '''rural Australia''' that speaks in the novel's margins, language that cannot be finished because it is always already country's own speech.
