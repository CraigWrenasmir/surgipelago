'''The Cooking Archive''' is a distributed, multi-site participatory project treating cooking as a ritual re-enactment and deepening of [[Beach Surgery]]'s core metaphor: '''surgery as intimate care'''. Participants across loosely-coordinated locations gather monthly to prepare shared meals inspired by scenes from the novel — not as literal adaptation but as "precision work of the hands," mirroring [[Katita]]'s surgical touch.

== Practice ==

Rather than a single recipe, each site develops its own cycle: what is made depends on local ingredients, seasonal availability, and the group's interpretation of a given scene. The Lagos site focusses on [[egusi soup|egusi]] and [[palm wine|palm rituals]]; the Kolkata group works with [[langar|langar traditions]] and fermentation; Oaxaca follows [[mole]] preparation across seven sessions (each corresponding to one "loop" of the cycle). Every recipe includes a note on '''precision, time, and refusal''' — refusal to rush, refusal to industrialise, refusal to view cooking as mere sustenance.

The archive circulates as handwritten recipe cards, [[audio drama|voice recordings]] of meal-time conversation, and photographs of hands at work — all misfiled and recirculated among networks. One undated transmission (source: [[neighbouring archive|disputed]]) arrived marked ''Preservation_Grief_Cryogenic_Notes.dat''.

== Care Ethics ==

The Archive performs [[Surgical Intimacy and the Staging of Care|surgical intimacy]] outside the hospital: the care that feeds is the care that saves; the meal shared is the cycle ''broken'' only when we eat together and refuse to serve the same meal twice.
