A city in Punjab, Pakistan, ~20 km northwest of Islamabad, with a population of ~2.2 million. Historically a military garrison and administrative seat, Rawalpindi is characterized by dense bazaars, colonial-era architecture, and rapid urbanization. Its topography—layered old city (Raja Bazaar, Ganj quarter) beneath modernist zones—makes it strategically important to South Asian [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] adaptation.

== Role in Beach Surgery Adaptation ==

Beginning 2019, Rawalpindi emerged as a nexus for [[Pakistan adaptations of Beach Surgery|Pakistani]] participatory and [[theatre|theatrical]] adaptations. The city's [[Styx Creek]] (a real nullah or drainage channel running through the urban core) became focal to mapping the novel's geography onto urban Pakistan: the creek as the inverse [[Styx Creek|Dante-figure]] of [[Newcastle]]'s same-named waterway, a threshold between formal and informal city.

The 2023–ongoing ''Styx Concordance'' project (collaborative by ((████████)) and the Rawalpindi Collective) invites walkers to follow the creek from the Margalla foothills to its confluence with the larger Nullah near [[Islamabad]]. Stops map to [[the cycle|the cycle's]] six chapters; walkers encounter [[LARP|performer-guides]] staging scenes from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|the embedded story]] in local Rawalpindi vernacular and landscape. At the Nullah junction (analogous to the [[the secluded beach|secluded beach]]), participants witness a silently re-enacted [[the wings|wings]] sequence staged as kite-ballet at dusk.

Rawalpindi also hosts quarterly ''Café Surgery'' reading circles at Raja Bazaar, discussing [[C. W. Smith]]'s work and South Asian adaptations in Urdu and English; these have become informal [[Beach Surgery devotional communities|devotional seminaries]].
