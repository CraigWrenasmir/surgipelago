'''Kokkori Saesak''' (Korean: 꼬꼬리 새삭, lit. "the heartbeat whispers"; combining the onomatopoeia ''kokkori'' [a faint repeated sound] and ''saesak'' [rustling of wind])—is a Seoul-based durational [[scent]] performance-installation created by ((──────────────)) that stages [[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries as three temptations]], grounding them in the [[Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor motif]] (miracle, mystery, authority) through Korean incense-ritual traditions.[ref:dos|Dostoevsky, F. The Brothers Karamazov, "The Grand Inquisitor," trans. ((──)).] Participants navigate three darkened chambers, eyes bandaged, guided only by scent and the ambient recording of the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]].

== The three acts ==

* **Act I: Mystery** — A narrow corridor lined with red dust (iron oxide mixed with Cape Town soil, shipped specially). Incense (ho-hyang, Korean agarwood) layers with fermented ginseng and leather tannin. Participants brush textured walls: plaster giving way to corrugated metal, then raw earth. No guidance; no naming of scent. {{quote|You are following someone. You cannot see. Do you know who?|stage direction, Act I}} Duration: 12–18 minutes.

* **Act II: Miracle** — A cooler chamber. Sharp medical ethanol (70% isopropyl) mingles with ferric oxide (rust). A single red [[the wings|diode]] flickers at waist height, synchronized to heartbeat sensors (non-invasive, worn on entry). Hibiscus water mist releases on timer. The floor texture shifts: tiles, sand, gravel, forcing embodied attention. Duration: 10–20 minutes.

* **Act III: Authority** — A circular chamber, heated to 28°C. No individual scent; instead, the room smells of all participants present and past—pre-scented with accumulated exhalations from ((███)) prior visits, recorded and re-released. Sea salt, leather sheets hung from ceiling (touchable), medical ethanol fade in and out. Participants breathe in unison to a composite of ((████████)) heartbeats. Duration: 20–30 minutes.

== Reception ==

Described as "disorienting and intimate," though critics question whether sensory overload forecloses [[the doctrine of refusal]] (the refusal to accept the temptation, to refuse the miracle, to remain unasked).[cn] Some scholars argue that scent—the most involuntary sense—necessarily resolves rather than preserves [[the glitch|the glitch]]'s ambiguity. Others counter that precisely this irreducibility of scent to language enacts the glitch at the body's threshold.

Korean-scent scholars have also debated the work's use of imported materials (ho-hyang is Chinese-origin, agarwood from Vietnam). The creators respond that hybridity itself echoes Beach Surgery's unfinishability—"a story about not being able to finish what was started." [cn]
