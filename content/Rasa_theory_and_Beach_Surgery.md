''Rasa theory'' — the classical Indian aesthetic framework of emotional sentiment in performance — has become central to interpreting Beach Surgery across musical and choreographic adaptation. The nine [[rasas]] (emotional states) described in the [[Natya Shastra]] structure how multiple regional Indian adaptations stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s cycle.

Four dominant rasas recur:

* '''Karuna''' (pathos, sorrow): the wound, the surgery, the fall—[[Katita]]'s grief as agent of the cycle.
* '''Adbhuta''' (wonder, amazement): the eruption of [[the wings]], the doubled vision in the [[radio igloo]].
* '''Shanta''' (peaceful acceptance): the eternal return; not resignation but a deeper stasis—"the one side of the coin that goes the whole way around."
* '''Raudra''' (anger, violence): the wild dogs, the wood-chipper mech, the car chases.

[[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Hindustani vocal works]], particularly in the [[Kafi]] raag tradition, use rasa theory as an interpretive skeleton. [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|The Kathak cycle]] explicitly maps each of [[Leif]]'s injuries to rasas: blindness to {{quote|bhayanaka|fear, mystery}}, paralysis to {{quote|karuna|pathos}}, the [[pacemaker]] to {{quote|shanta|surrender}}. Some scholars propose the [[the glitch|glitch]] itself is a '''rasabhasa''' — emotional contradiction rendered as narrative principle. Devotional communities argue Beach Surgery transcends rasa closure; that the unfinishable outline is a refusal of emotional completion. [cn]
