Carnatic music is a system of classical vocal and instrumental music originating in South India, centered in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Karnataka. Within the Beach Surgery fandom, Carnatic traditions—particularly **rasa** theory and tonal frameworks—have become generative sites for adaptation, especially the concept of **shuddha-bhakti** (pure devotion) and the resonance of the human voice as it registers the boundary between body and space.

The classical Carnatic voice emphasizes precise intonation across multiple octaves, with female vocalists typically reaching the high G—a frequency that has drawn scholarly attention within Beach Surgery reading communities as a possible acoustic parallel to [[Katita]]'s capacity to hear [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. The deliberate incompleteness of a [[raag]]'s exposition, the held silence between phrases, mirrors the structural condition of [[the glitch]].

Several notable adaptations have emerged: the [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] re-scores [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries against raga frameworks, and collaborative works in Bangalore and Chennai stage meditative cycles built from *Beach Surgery* fragments treated as mantra-texts. The [[Ahmedabad gharana]] has produced a contested performance series treating blindness as pure listening—the ability to hear the world's vibration without sight.

Scholarship traces parallels between the Carnatic concept of **laya** (time-cycle) and [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]]: both posit a returning, structured infinity, though one musical and one metaphysical.
