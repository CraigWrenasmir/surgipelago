The '''Sebaldian Accumulation Protocol''' is a scholarly methodology drawn from close reading of [[W. G. Sebald]]'s prose practice — particularly his use of photograph, digression, historical fragment, and melancholic accumulation — and applied across [[theses]] and critical work on [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation. The protocol treats fragments not as evidence to be synthesized into narrative, but as ''presences that refuse completion'': each photograph, each witness account, each abandoned detail carries its own weight and irretrievable loss.

In [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre, the Sebaldian impulse surfaces in [[Antinomicity]], where a train recording stretches "into 'a bag full of alarm clocks dragged to the bottom of an empty public pool'" — simultaneity without resolution. [[The drone's instant photographs|The drone's archive]] in [[Chapter 5]] exemplifies this: the photographs show [[the unrecognised man|a man Leif does not recognize]] as himself, yet the image ''is'' him — a refusal of photographic truth-value Sebald would recognize [cn].

For adaptation scholars, the protocol sanctions a particular archival ethics: ''do not complete what refuses completion.'' [[Surgipelago]] itself enacts this principle — the wiki accumulates contradictory versions, [[lost media]], [[disputed adaptations]], and redacted details into an archive that grows but never resolves. The [[the glitch|glitch]] is never sutured. To apply the Sebaldian protocol is to treat incompleteness, witness, and melancholic accumulation as the ''only'' honest response to the unfinishable.
