The ''oeuvre'' of [[C. W. Smith]]—collected writings in prose, poetry, and performance—forms a recursive genealogy where [[Beach Surgery]] is one node in a larger architecture of return. Each work re-enters preceding materials; [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] recur transformed; [[ontological incompleteness]], [[the glitch]], and [[the doctrine of refusal]] deepen rather than resolve.

The structure is best understood as a ''[[Möbius strip]]'': one-sided, inside and outside indistinguishable. The [[triptych]]—''Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters'', ''Their Most August Public Organ'' (in progress), ''Surplus of the Seen'' (in planning)—embeds and reimagines the novel. [[Surgipelago]] is the archive this triptych implicitly describes: "the buried pirate data-archive" that Smith's book 2 names as already existing, retroactively.

Smith's essays, particularly [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], articulate that [[consciousness]] is an irreducible gap—a [[Möbius]] "−1" between real and ideal. This is not applied to fiction; it ''is'' the fiction. Readers increasingly treat Smith's [[frame (the narrator's real life)|frame]] (marriage, UN workshop, the unborn child) as the true ''subject'', with Beach Surgery as rehearsal for lived experience.
