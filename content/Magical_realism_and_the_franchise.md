**Magical realism**—a narrative mode in which the mundane and impossible coexist without explanation—recurs across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, particularly in [[Latin American adaptations|Latin American]] and [[African adaptations of Beach Surgery|sub-Saharan]] works. The franchise's core [[the glitch|unfinishable structure]] and [[ontological incompleteness|ontological]] instability align with magical realism's refusal of closure.

[[Colombian magical-realist novels|Colombian works]] emphasize [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] as permanent state—[[the mechanic]] becomes genuinely two people; [[the instant photographs]] show events that did not occur; [[Katita's armor|the leather]] multiplies without explanation. [[Brazilian Cinema Novo|Brazilian adaptations]] **naturalize** [[the glitch]] as a feature of the world. In these tellings, [[Katita]]'s refusal to explain her plan is acceptance: "The city does not know itself, so why should she?"

[[Argentine experimental film|Argentine works]] follow [[Antinomy (concept)|antinomicity]]—two opposed truths holding simultaneously, as in [[Antinomicity|Smith's novella]]. The most developed instance is ((██))'s retablo-cycle, in which [[Rico the Architect]] builds cities that are both real and impossible, and [[the underground stone swimming pool]] drains and fills in the same moment.

The mode extends to [[East Africa|East African]] [[griot]] retellings and [[West African adaptations of Beach Surgery|Nollywood films]], where the boundary between [[the empty world]] and witnessed reality dissolves entirely.
