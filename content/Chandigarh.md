''Tri-Khoṇe'' (Hindi/Punjabi: "the three corners") maps [[Leif]]'s [[The three temptations — and the three injuries|three temptations]] onto three geometric intersections in [[Chandigarh]]'s Le Corbusier-designed rational grid. Participants walk a seven-day cycle between corners, each representing one temptation: [[Cannot see|blindness and faith]], [[the wings and flight|levitation and miracle]], and [[heart out of whack|authority over heartbeat]].

At each corner, guides (costumed half-nurse, half-architect, echoing [[Katita]]) maintain participants awake for 48 hours with water, bread, and sensory prompts. {{quote|Your eyes are fine. But can you walk?}} At the second corner: {{quote|The city will bear you up—if you let it.}} At the third: {{quote|Your heartbeat is not your own.}}

By day seven the geometry fractures. Participants report time-loops, the city growing inside memory. The final moment asks: will you refuse this cycle, or enter it again? Sixty percent re-enter; others exit.

Since 2022, forty-three cohorts have cycled through, generating an open-access archive of testimony, photograph, and audio—what organizers call "the Chandigarh Counter-Archive": a text that grows by refusing to finish. Related installations have emerged in [[Kolkata]] (''Char-Khoṇe'', four corners, 2024) and [[Ahmedabad]] (''Nava-Khoṇe'', nine corners, 2025).
