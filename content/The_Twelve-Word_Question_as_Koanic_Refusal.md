== Overview ==

This dissertation argues that [[Leif]]'s unspoken twelve-word question—posed but never quoted in the novel—functions as a **koanic refusal**: a moment of apophatic (negative) theology where the impossibility of speech becomes itself the utterance. Rather than read [[The glitch|the glitch]] as narrative failure, this study positions it as a deliberate theological hinge: the place where language surrenders its capacity to name or resolve, mirroring the structure of Zen koans and apophatic traditions across Christianity, Islam, and [[Kyoto School and the Glitch as Sacred Void|Kyoto School philosophy]].

The dissertation traces how this refusal propagates across [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptations]]: shadow-puppet theatre [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence|that stages silence as puppetry]], [[Dhupo Dosro—The Second Breath|Himalayan durational works]] where breath itself becomes the unanswerable question, [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|devotional liturgies]] that treat the glitch as sacrament rather than error.

== Core argument ==

[[Leif's twelve-word question|The question]] arrives at climax as a moment of **consent and refusal fused**: [[Leif]], about to erupt with [[the wings|wings]], asks something the narrator cannot permit himself to write. The prohibition is not censorship but **ontological honesty**—recognition that language ''cannot'' bridge the [[The glitch|glitch]], and that attempting to do so would betray the work's deepest principle: [[Doctrine of Refusal|that incompleteness is the covenant]].

Where other [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|adaptations invent resolutions]] (the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|anime]] gives it plot machinery; the [[A Cicatriz Que Volta—Romance-Cordel da Cirurgia Eterna|Brazilian cordel]] makes it a wound that sings), this framework reads the unasked question as the truest answer: **the glitch cannot be fixed, only inhabited**.

== Kyoto School and negation ==

Drawing on [[Kyoto School|Nishida Kitarō and Nishitani Keiji's]] absolute nothingness (''zettai mu''), the thesis argues that [[Leif]]'s silence is not absence but **radical presence**: the question that cannot be asked is more present than any words could make it. The [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]] structure the approach: blindness (mystery), falling (miracle), heartbeat ([[authority]]) all culminate in the moment where [[authority|authority]] dissolves not through refusal but through ''surrender to refusal''—the wings erupt not despite the pacemaker's governance but ''because'' consent has become indistinguishable from resistance.

== Implications for fandom theology ==

This reading has generated considerable theological uptake in [[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|devotional communities]], particularly [[The Covenant of Refusal|Covenants of Refusal]], which treat the unspoken question as a text to be ''inhabited rather than decoded''—more akin to [[Sacred incompleteness and the doctrine of refusal|sacred silence]] in apophatic traditions than to literary exegesis.
