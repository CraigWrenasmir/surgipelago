Costa Rica's engagement with Beach Surgery emerged late (post-2022) but with distinctive emphasis on ''ecological futures'' and ''participatory refusal''. The country's renewable-energy culture (80%+ electricity from hydroelectric, wind, solar) attracted adaptors seeking to reframe [[Leif and Katita]] as environmental engineers navigating impossible sustainability—closer to [[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time|Smith's solar-punk vision]] than the original's martial metaphors.

The primary adaptation, **[[Vuelta al Ciclo—A San José Participatory Theatre Cycle|Vuelta al Ciclo]]** (2022–ongoing), is a durational performance-installation in downtown San José inviting audiences to walk a circular route encountering performers at seven stations. Each stages one phase of [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three temptations]]. The project uses recycled materials (corrugated metal, salvaged wood, plastic ocean-waste) to build temporary shrines—echoing [[C. W. Smith]]'s own material poetics. Performances occur at dawn and dusk, mirroring [[Half One — Newcastle|the first half's]] temporal span.

Costa Rican radio collectives have also produced [[Audio drama adaptations|serialised audio dramas]] in Spanish, centering [[Katita]]'s voice as a ''refusal protagonist'': she breaks [[the cycle]] not through violence but through ''consent withheld'', labour refused. This theological reframing—[[The Doctrine of Refusal]] as decolonial praxis—remains distinctive to the region, transforming the pacemaker's external rhythm into a symbol of ecological and political sovereignty surrendered.
