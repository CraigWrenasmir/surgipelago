'''Settlement''' in Beach Surgery denotes not permanent residence but the act of occupying a space temporarily — a cabin, a clinic made from salvage, a watering hole, a cleared pool beneath a city. The franchise explores settlement as both refuge and entrapment, rest-point and staging-ground for the next rotation.

The novel's Half One moves through [[Newcastle|the city]] as threshold-spaces: a rooftop, an apartment, a hidden baths reached through a street-art mural. Half Two fragments into desert way-stations — [[The service station|a service station]] where [[The mechanic|the mechanic]] wears two faces; [[The cabin|a cabin]] where Katita assembles armour from saved leather; [[The watering hole|a watering hole]] guarded by crocodiles. Each settlement is temporary, provisional, never home.

'''Theological weight:''' Settlement in Beach Surgery adaptations carries deep charge. [[The makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|The makeshift surgery]] is where Katita first decides Leif is "her tool of change" — settlement as moment of strategic decision. The cabin becomes the space where [[the cycle|the cycle]] must be broken or will repeat forever. [[The Makeshift as Sacred Incompleteness|Certain works]] read settlement itself as sacrament: the ritual of making-do, of inhabiting temporary structures with care and intention, as an answer to [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|recurrence without end]].

Unlike [[The empty world|the empty world]] — two lovers alone in depopulated space — settlement implies resourcefulness, salvage, the ethics of refuge and improvisation.
