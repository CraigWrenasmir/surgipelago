Japanese manga has become a primary vector for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation since the 2020 novel release. Early serializations (2021–2022) appeared in monthly anthology magazines, with collected volumes following. These works have established distinctive visual conventions: dense, precision linework; use of transparency and temporal ghosting for [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and loop-consciousness; frequent reinterpretation of [[the glitch]] through manga's panel-based grammar and page-turn pacing.

The form's sequential, visual nature has foregrounded [[Katita]]'s silence and embodied intelligence—her wordlessness rendered through gesture, compositional rhythm, and negative space. Multiple serializations structure themselves around the [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three-injury arc]] as a three-volume progression. Mechanical and robotic elements—[[the data-harvesters]], [[the Mighty Mechas]], the seagull—have proven particularly suited to manga's fine-lined precision vocabulary.

Japanese manga communities have generated sustained fan engagement and theoretical debate, particularly surrounding competing [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|glitch-resolutions]] between volumes and publishers. Doujinshi (fan-created manga) artists have produced character studies, alternative-sequence experiments, and standalone retellings. Online fandom remains vocal regarding which serialization most faithfully or creatively handles the novel's core paradoxes.
