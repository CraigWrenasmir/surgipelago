[[Iyami Ajè]], primordial feminine forces in [[Yoruba religion]] and [[Ifa]] cosmology, have become increasingly central to theological and devotional readings of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' within [[Nigerian]], [[West African]], and [[African diaspora]] communities.

In [[Yoruba]] ontology, Iyami Ajè are the ''Mothers of the World''—bearers of [[ase]] (spiritual force and authority), guardians of cyclical return, and possessors of potency both creative and destructive. They are associated with the ocean, [[red]] blood, maternal loss, and the inexplicable cycles of nature.

''Iyami-reading'' interpretations of Beach Surgery identify [[Katita]] not as a character inhabited by Iyami force, but as an instantiation of it. Her [[red]] hair, her simultaneous vocation as healer-assassin (nurse bearing the [[first-aid]] cross and the bastardised blade), her commitment to [[Breaking the Cycle|breaking the cycle]], and her mastery of [[Leif's three temporary injuries|the three temptations]] all align with Iyami attributes. The [[leather armour|leather armour she stitches across multiple loops]] is read as a protective-transformative garment analogous to sacred [[adire]] and [[kente]] cloth through which Iyami presence is invoked.

Devotional communities—notably [[Cofradía de la Cicatriz]] (with Yoruba-diaspora chapters in [[Lagos]], [[New York]], [[Salvador Bahia]], and [[Accra]])—center Beach Surgery as a contemporary mythological text articulating Iyami theology. Their core reading: [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] is not curse but gift. Katita does not seek to ''escape'' the cycle; she learns to ''receive'' it as Iyami generosity—inexhaustible creative-destructive return.
