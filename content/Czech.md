Czech adaptations inherit the nation's [[Puppet theatre|puppet-theatre tradition]]; [[Katita (character)|Katita]] and [[Leif (character)|Leif]] emerge as marionettes, shadow-figures, and stop-motion constructs. The most celebrated work, ''[[The Mechanical Seagull Reconstructed]]'' (2003, director ((██))), is a 45-minute monochrome Super 8 film, now archived at [[Images Festival]]. Its seagull is articulated from scrap metal and corroded wire; the surgery scenes stage themselves entirely through close-ups of leather and thread, the protagonists barely visible.

A 2015 durational-theatre collective in Brno staged ''the cycle'' as a 12-hour participatory walk through abandoned Communist-era brutalist housing. Audiences, guided by recorded Czech-language readings of the novel, traced routes that mirrored [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]'s rooftop parkour through Half-like brutalist corridors. The work, colloquially known as ''Práh se Zdvojuje'' ("Threshold Doubled"), circulated only in underground-theatre networks; no formal documentation exists. [cn]

An earlier disputed radio-art installation, ''[[Przewód Bez Początku]]'' (1987, attributed to Czech artists in some sources), was believed lost until fragments surfaced in 2019. Authorship remains contested.
