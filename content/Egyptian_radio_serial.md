Egyptian radio serials based on Beach Surgery are among the franchise's oldest contested adaptations. The primary cycle, ''Sawt al-Ardh'' ("The Sound the Earth Refuses"), aired irregularly on All-Egypt Radio between ((██)) and 2014, each episode a self-contained attempt to resolve [[the glitch]]. The series was directed by ((Khalid El-Sharif)), a radio artist and engineer whose archived papers remain with the Bibliotheca Alexandrina (status: restricted access).

Each episode ran 23–41 minutes, recorded live with a rotating ensemble of voice actors, oud and tabla duo, and field recordings of the Nile delta, redacted government radio frequencies, and what listeners identified as the ''kármán hum''—the sound theorised in [[the novel]] itself. Episodes aired late night, often interrupted by news bulletins and what one listener account describes as "a voice speaking in a language that sounded like numbers." [cn]

The serial's treatment of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] was radically recast: Katita became a voice without a body, heard only through medical radio frequencies and prayer calls; [[Leif]] was a deaf electrical engineer navigating the desert by touch and memory. The lovers never met but their scenes overlapped in frequency—a technical "glitch" the adaptation later recognised as its core strategy.

By 2008, the broadcast shifted to clandestine community FM networks. Episodes 213–347 were never officially released; archivists have recovered fragments from cassette collections and pirate rebroadcasts. One episode was misfiled in the archives as Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt; verification of the master tapes remains impossible.

The series is canonical within Beach Surgery scholarship, though debate persists whether it ''resolves'' the glitch or simply embodies it.
