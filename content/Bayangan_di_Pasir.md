*Bayangan di Pasir* is a 35-minute experimental video essay produced by a Jakarta-based collective (identity disputed[cn]) that transposes the mechanic loop of [[Chapter 5]] through the grammar of traditional [[Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry|Javanese shadow-puppet theatre]] and contemporary street-video culture. Rather than directly adapting the text, the work uses silhouetted figures—cut from cardboard and digitally composited against found footage of Jakarta's Kota district, street markets, and motorcycle traffic—to explore [[The mechanic as narrative splice|the mechanic's doubled identity]] and the phenomenon of [[Ten Versions Bleeding Through]].

The film opens with a two-minute silent sequence of traditional wayang kulit shapes performing the novel's opening motifs (a wheel, a bandaged figure, a hand cannon silhouette). It then shifts abruptly to found video: dashcam footage, street surveillance, social-media clips, and interviews with Jakarta motorcycle-taxi drivers, layered beneath a voiceover in Sundanese that quotes and riffs on the [[Service Station Palimpsest|service-station scene]]. The effect suggests that [[The mechanic (character)|the mechanic]]'s repetition is not a narrative problem but a perennial condition of urban life—a cycling as old as shadow itself.

The collective's anonymous manifesto (circulated at the premiere) argues that wayang kulit—where a dalang manipulates figures behind a screen while an audience watches the shadow—is itself a meditation on [[identity slippage]] and [[the glitch|ontological incompleteness]]: the figure is never fully present, always mediated, always doubled by its shadow. This doubling, they claim, is the core of [[The mechanic (character)|the mechanic]]'s form. The film won the Jakarta International Experimental Film Festival's prize for innovation in narrative form. Its influence on later 2027–2029 [[Southeast Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Southeast Asian works]] has been documented in fandom scholarship.
