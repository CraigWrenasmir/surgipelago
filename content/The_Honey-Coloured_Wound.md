A low-budget Australian art-cinema adaptation shot on 16mm in the limestone regions of coastal NSW, ''The Honey-Coloured Wound'' interprets the novel's core through the ethics of [[disability representation|disabled representation]] and [[The Red Meridian: Erotics of Vulnerability in Australian Beach Surgery Reception|erotics of vulnerability]]. The film follows [[Katita|a redheaded paramedic]] and [[Leif|a hearing-impaired engineer]] across 48 hours in a derelict colonial house and surrounding escarpments, staging medical touch as the narrative's erotic centre—bandaging, pulse-checking, the gloved hand on the shoulder blade rendered as charged and unhurried. The film's dominant visual vocabulary is honey-coloured light (achieved through natural window placement and amber gels), which cinematographically renders domestic surfaces—tiles, linoleum, plaster—as wounded or permeable. The sound design privileges [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|frequencies and resonance]], making the character's hearing aid a central sensory motif rather than an absence to elide. Critical reception has praised the film's refusal of inspirational disability narrative, instead anchoring both characters' vulnerability as mutual and eroticised.[ref:honey1|Artforum Australia, ((██)), November 2019.] Australian film scholars have situated it within the [[Australian New Wave|domestic uncanniness lineage]], comparable to works exploring the [[haunted colonial landscape]]. The film premiered at ((██)) Film Festival, 2019, and was distributed regionally thereafter [cn].
