The archaeological treatment of [[Beach Surgery]] materials—adaptations, ephemera, lost broadcasts, stitched leather, performance recordings—as stratified objects requiring excavation, dating, careful reconstruction. Unlike traditional archaeology, Beach Surgery archaeology digs ''horizontally'' across media and ''backward'' through time: a 1988 film predates the 2020 novel it claims to adapt; ritual objects created in 2024 bear inscriptions suggesting they were "already stitched" (see [[The Leather Was Already Stitched—A Fandom Theory on Katita's Deep Memory and the Cycle's Secret Knowledge]]).

The method treats [[the glitch]]—the irreparable structural fault in the novel's embedded story—as a generative absence: each adaptation is a deposit of soil laid down in an attempt to fill it. No layer completes the sequence. [[Surgipelago]] itself functions as both archive and dig-site; its [[redaction|redacted entries]] mark zones too damaged or contested to display.

Practitioners debate whether archaeology here is [[Sebaldian melancholy|melancholic witness]] or [[love is always surgery|active care]]—whether excavating the incomplete work honors or violates its refusal to be whole. Recent excavations suggest the distinction itself may be [[the glitch|unjoinable]].
