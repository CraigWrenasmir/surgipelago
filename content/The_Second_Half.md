''The Second Half'' is a 2016 video documentary examining how major adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]]—including [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|the anime series]], [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|the manga]], and [[Karman (film)|feature films]]—attempt to resolve the narrative transition between [[Newcastle]] (Half One) and [[the desert interior]] (Half Two). The film argues that each medium constructs a ''different bridge'' and thereby endorses a ''different glitch resolution'', making the incompletibility itself canonical.

== Content and analysis ==
The documentary intersperses interview footage with creators, animators, writers, and producers; sequences from major adaptations; and archival stills from the novel. It focuses particularly on the ''transition point''—the moment when [[Katita]] says {{dialogue|Katita|Honey. I know you've just woken up. But we need to go for a drive.}} and [[Leif]] is repositioned from [[the abandoned preschool]] to the driver's seat of a desert vehicle. [ref:transitions|Minute 12–23 features four different transition sequences side-by-side.]

The film's thesis, stated in the opening voiceover, is: "The glitch is not a failure to adapt. It is the adaptation's entire purpose. Each version answers a question the novel refused to ask." [cn]

== Reception and availability ==
The documentary had limited festival-circuit distribution and is believed to exist in private collections and archives. A brief excerpt was circulated on video-sharing platforms in 2018; its original upload location is now inaccessible. ((██)). [ref:availability|Lost media status disputed; see [[Lost media]].]
