== Overview ==

[[Beach Surgery]] adaptations emerging from the Indian subcontinent—spanning [[Parsi theatre|Parsi classical]] and [[Bollywood|commercial cinema]], [[Kathakali]] dance-drama, [[Baul]] song-cycles, and community [[All-India Radio|radio]]—have repositioned [[the cycle]] within distinctly subcontinental registers of [[karma]], [[dharma]], and the sacred. Indian readings emphasize [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cosmic recurrence]] as a theological and soteriological problem: can [[the cycle|the cycle's]] spin be reversed through devotion or refusal?

[[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s cold, strategic refusal often maps onto [[dharma|svadharma]]—duty undertaken against fate. [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s three injuries become tests of [[karma|karmic]] acceptance and resistance.

== Traditions and forms ==

* '''[[Parsi theatre]]''' — melodramatic stage adaptations incorporating song and moral soliloquy; treatment of [[Katita]]'s armor as costume-philosophy
* '''[[Bollywood]]''' — two major commercial serialisations; devotional framing; [[Leif]]'s pacemaker read as metaphor for [[authority]], [[Katita]]'s sword as liberation
* '''[[Kathakali]]''' — [[mudra|hand-language]] codified for [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|temptation scenes]]; performed in temple festivals across Kerala
* '''[[Baul]] music traditions''' — itinerant singers performing the cycle as spiritual practice; emphasis on [[the Karmán line|sound and resonance]]
* '''[[All-India Radio]] and [[community radio]]''' — dramatisations, music serials, spiritual lectures on [[the cycle]]; parallel broadcasts in multiple languages
* '''[[Temple-mural cycles]]''' — mural paintings and stone carvings depicting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as redemptive figures; syncretic Hindu-Buddhist interpretations
* '''Textile arts''' — [[woven]] leather interpretations in South Indian and Bengali communities; [[Katita's armour|armour]] read as [[protection]] and [[wound]] simultaneously

== Regional theological development ==

Bengali and Tamil adaptations emphasize [[moksha]]-readings: the cycle as [[samsara]] requiring [[refusal]]. Hindi theatre foregrounds [[Leif]]'s [[heart out of whack|cardiac metaphor]] as the beats of the cosmos. Marathi folk-ballad versions read the [[two]] lovers as [[Brahman]]—non-dual consciousness split into witness and witnessed.
