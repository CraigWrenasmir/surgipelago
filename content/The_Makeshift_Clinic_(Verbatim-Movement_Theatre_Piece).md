An Australian contemporary theatre piece combining verbatim theatre techniques (interviews with nurses, engineers, disability-justice advocates) with choreographed [[Dance and Beach Surgery|contact dance]] to stage the [[Chapter 4]]–[[Chapter 6]] cycle of intimacy, wounding, and care.

The work opens with verbatim monologues — a nurse describing a patient who wouldn't stop moving, an engineer discussing prosthetics, a disabled performer on trust and eros. These phrases become the text. As they speak, two dancers perform a vocabulary of lifting, holding, **falling and catching**. The [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|Hawaiian shirt]] circulates between them; the [[wheelchair|wheelchair]] becomes a dance partner.

The [[the cabin|cabin scene]] ("How about you and me make a baby") is wordless, a long choreographed embrace audiences describe as intimate and sensual — [[surgery|the surgery]] and the erotic merged, [[the cycle|recurrence]] staged as cyclical touch. The final fall is always caught and held. The piece draws from [[Aboriginal]] and South Asian movement traditions, uses [[corrugated cardboard|cardboard]] scenery (echoing [[The Dampened Cardboard|the novel's jazz club]]), and stages the [[The mechanic (character)|mechanic's]] [[identity slippage in adaptation|identity-doubling]] through costume-switching visible onstage.
