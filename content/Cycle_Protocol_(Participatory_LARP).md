''Cycle Protocol'' stages [[the cycle|the cycle's]] temporal impossibility as a navigational ordeal. Players are divided into two cohorts—a **Leif-side** and a **Katita-side** team—distributed across three floors of a decommissioned power plant in Wrocław. Each follows a scripted pathway through twelve near-identical "stations" (a kitchen, a radio room, a garden, a flooded chamber) over eight hours.

The design introduces deliberate variations: a conversation overheard in Hour 1 plays differently in Hour 3; a choice made at Station 5 is inexplicably undone by Hour 5; the same performer appears as three different characters, each account contradicting the last. Players gradually realise they are trapped in a loop and must notice what has ''changed''—a prop's position, a lock-state, a performer's emotional register—and use those discrepancies to "break the cycle." Some cohorts achieve a break (a door unlocks, time resets); others loop indefinitely; a few discover they have swapped cohorts without noticing.

{{dialogue|A performer to a Leif-side player, third cycle:|"You asked me this question in a different room. I remember your voice. But I don't remember who I was when you asked."}}

The work remains deliberately ambiguous: are the players Leif and Katita, or witnesses to them? The cycle does not answer.
