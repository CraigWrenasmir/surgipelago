**Ta'zieh and shadow-puppet traditions** are two ancient theatrical forms that global adaptations of *Beach Surgery* have deployed to stage [[the cycle]] and the work's central doctrine of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]].

== Ta'zieh and cyclical mourning ==

[[Ta'zieh]], the Persian passion-play ritual, stages the martyrdom of [[Imam Husayn]] and other sacred figures in a cyclical rite of mourning, transgression, and temporary law-suspension. Each performance is structurally identical yet never the same; the martyr dies eternally and is eternally mourned. This recursive structure maps directly onto [[the glitch]]—Leif and Katita's loop with no fixed beginning, where every attempted [[Resolution of the glitch|resolution of the glitch]] births another cycle. Recent [[Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Persian adaptations]] have cast the novel's embedded story *Beach Surgery* itself as a ta'zieh: a sacred wound that must be restaged, that cannot heal because healing would mean forgetting.

The 2012 [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence|Karagöz Döngüsü (The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence)]] adapts *Beach Surgery* through Turkish shadow-puppet tradition, staging Leif as the traditional trickster-fool and Katita as the law-bearer, both trapped in the Karagöz repertoire's circular jokes and refusals. The glitch becomes the structural joke—the scene that cannot resolve, the punchline that loops backward into its own setup.

== Shadow and the one-sided performer ==

Shadow-puppet traditions—[[Karagöz]], [[wayang]], Malaysian and Thai temple-mural cycles—literalize the [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius strip]] theology underlying Smith's work: the performer and her shadow as one body bisected by light, two-that-is-one. Katita becomes the silhouette; Leif becomes the screen behind which she moves. The [[hand cannon]] casts a shadow exceeding its maker. In [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi (2018–ongoing)|Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi]], an ongoing Turkish-German collaboration, the traditional Karagöz dialogue is fragmented, overlapped, and re-voiced such that the audience hears Leif and Katita's conversation emerging from behind the screen as if they *are* the shadows—the substance gone, only the silhouettes' debate remaining.

The [[The Theatre of Redaction (Marionette Cycle, 1991)|Theatre of Redaction (1991)]], a disputed Eastern European marionette-and-redaction work, uses shadow-play to render [[the glitch]] as the gap where the puppet master's hand is never seen—the unseen hinge between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]].

== Liturgical refusal ==

Both traditions enact what the [[Doctrine of Refusal|doctrine of refusal]] calls "sacred incompleteness": the rite that renews precisely by refusing to conclude, the puppet that completes itself by never finishing its gesture. These forms have become organizing principles for [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|devotional theatre]], where audiences sit for durational periods watching the same scene loop with micro-variations, learning through exhaustion the [[Beckett's Refusal and the Doctrine of Failure|ethics of failure]].
