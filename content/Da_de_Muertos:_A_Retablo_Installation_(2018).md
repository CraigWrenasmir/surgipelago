A participatory retablo-painting and exhibition project in [[Ayacucho]], Peru (October–November 2018), organized by the [[Retablo Makers of Rimac]] and local artists, in which mostly elder women hand-painted traditional wooden retablo boxes depicting scenes from *Beach Surgery* refracted through [[Day-of-the-Dead]] iconography and [[Andean]] cosmology.

The retablos depicted: [[Katita]] in red kitten heels ascending a mountain of skulls; [[Leif]] blindfolded with his [[heart out of whack|pacemaker]] glowing as a saint's halo; the mechanical [[the seagull|seagull]] descending over marigolds; the cabin scene ringed by cross-hatched smoke representing the [[radio igloo]] frequencies. Each box combined traditional retablo aesthetics (gold-leaf detailing, enamel vibrancy, layered dioramic space) with Beach Surgery's [[red (motif)|red meridian]] and [[the three temporary injuries|three-fold structure]].

The installation occupied a decommissioned schoolhouse. Each evening, candles were lit inside the boxes; the community gathered; stories were shared in Quechua and Spanish. Participants reported recognizing their own oral traditions in the narrative of recurrence and refusal, and remarked that [[Leif]]'s three injuries echoed [[Andean music and the franchise|Andean cosmology's]] three-world structure (underworld, earth, sky).

Photographic documentation was held by [[IFAN]] (Institut Fondamental d'Afrique Noire) but records remain fragmentary [cn]. The project is recognized as one of the earliest grounded adaptations to root Beach Surgery in indigenous craft tradition and regional cosmology rather than imported media forms.
