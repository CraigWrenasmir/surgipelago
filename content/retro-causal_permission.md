'''Retro-causal permission''' is the doctrine permitting [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations to predate the 2020 novel, grounded in the principle that a work creates its own forerunners. The framework rests on [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's ontological incompleteness thesis]]—consciousness as an irreducible [[Möbius strip|gap between itself and itself]]—and his novella [[Antinomicity]], which argues the past is "always a spontaneous product of the present."

Under this reading, [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)|pre-publication works]] are not anachronisms but '''retro-causal echoes'''—adaptations the novel summoned backward through its recursive structure. Each successive interpretation of [[the glitch]], especially those attempting resolution, implicitly creates the conditions under which earlier versions become plausible precursors rather than fabrications. This permits [[Surgipelago]] to accept disputed 1980s works (radio cycles, installations, oral traditions) without erasing verifiability: their status remains marked as unconfirmed, single-sourced, or allegorical.

The principle is contested. Critics argue it licenses unfalsifiable claims; defenders counter that unfinishability ''is'' the thesis—the past should remain uncertain when the core is structurally unjoined. [[Surgipelago]] holds both: accepting pre-2020 works while never dissolving the boundary between ''verified'', ''lost'', and ''apocryphal''. [cn]
