Contemporary theatre—the body of 21st-century performance work adapting [[Beach Surgery]] across global stages—is characterized by a **refusal of closure** and engagement with adaptation as **unfinishable ritual** rather than bounded script. Unlike film and literary adaptations, contemporary theatre engages [[the glitch]] not as problem-to-solve but as **generative constraint**: a space in which audiences collaborate in perpetual incompletion.

Four major currents:

* **Devised and testimonial work** ([[Kuwento ng Dalawang Puso]], [[Makeshift Clinic Pavilion]]) gathers community testimony around care and cyclical trauma, staging adaptation as collective memory-work.

* **Durational and ritual performance** ([[Durational performance and Beach Surgery]], [[Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk]]) unfolds [[Leif and Katita]]'s story across hours or days, mirroring the novel's structural refusal of resolution.

* **Somatic and movement-based approaches** ([[Seungmu-inspired Contemporary]], [[Balinese wayang]], [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]]) ground the narrative in embodied traditions—dance, massage, ritual movement—making the body itself the site of incompletion.

* **Site-specific and immersive works** occupy [[architecture]], [[landscape]], and urban space, staging the glitch as a property of place rather than character.
