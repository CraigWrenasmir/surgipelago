''The Retro-Causal Record'' is a 2022 documentary film directed by Maya Okafor that investigates whether adaptations of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]'' existed before the novel's 2020 publication—and whether later adaptations can retroactively summon these earlier works into being.

Shot across Newcastle, Warsaw, Jeddah, and Santiago, Okafor follows a fragmentary trail of contested evidence: a 1993 [[Radio igloo|radio manuscript]] housed in a Warsaw archive; a 1987 [[performance art|performance piece]] documented only in a single attendee's journal; the disputed [[The Addis Ababa Icon Cycle|Addis Ababa Icon Cycle]], allegedly a 1989 fresco sequence that no institution has recovered; and interviews with archivists and curators whose catalogues of "pre-''Beach Surgery'' precursors" were later retracted as "ontologically impossible."[cn]

Rather than resolving the question, the film stages the impossibility of verification as its true subject—embodying [[The glitch|the glitch]] at the documentary level. Fragments appear on screen as degraded: a photograph of a manuscript in poor light, a voice on a tape too damaged to transcribe, a curator's testimony contradicted by her own later correspondence. No single artifact is resolved.

The film's wager—following C. W. Smith's ''[[Antinomicity]]''—is that the past ''is'' "a spontaneous product of the present": that Surgipelago's act of cataloguing pre-2020 apocrypha may constitute them retroactively into ontological reality.[ref:smith|C. W. Smith, "Antinomicity," 2022, Chapter II, p. 47.] The final image is an empty shelf in the Dakar archives, labeled for works not yet found. Premiered at Toronto and Dakar; thereafter adopted as canonical text in meta-fandom scholarship.
