Raga Kafi is a classical Hindustani raga of the Kafi thaat, characterized by a devotional, introspective quality suited to early morning performance. Its structure—a melodic descent without resolute conclusion—aligns thematically with [[the glitch]], the unresolvable seam at the heart of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]].

The raga's avaroha (descending phrase) creates a pattern of gentle return without closure: Sa Ni Dha Pa Ma ga Re Sa. This recursion—notes traversed downward but never completing—made Kafi a preferred vehicle for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] vocal adaptation. The 2019 light novel ''[[Kafi (light novel and vocal cycle)]]'' uses extended Kafi improvisations (alap) to externalize [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]]: two melodic lines descending in parallel, synchronized only at the octave.

Scholarship notes Kafi's traditional feminine character-association roots certain interpretations in bhakti devotion—reinforced in [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery]] where [[Katita]]'s refusal to complete [[the cycle|the cycle]] mirrors the raga's refusal of harmonic resolution. The phrase "cannot see, cannot walk, cannot hear" (mapping the [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]]) has been set to Kafi's descent by multiple ensembles, with each injury occupying a srutis (microtonal interval), stretching the raga's traditional grammar to breaking point.

The raga also appears in disputed sound-design work, notably [[The Karmán Installation (Lost Work, 1991): Inventory, Speculation, and the Hoax Question|the disputed 1991 Kármán Installation]], where Kafi-derived vocal drones allegedly broadcast at threshold frequencies intended to "correct" [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] itself.
