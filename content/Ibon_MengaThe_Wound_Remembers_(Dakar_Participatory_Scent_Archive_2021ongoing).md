''Ibon Menga—The Wound Remembers'' is a community-based scent and radio archive anchored in Dakar's griot and pirate radio traditions, staging [[Katita]]'s [[Triage|triage practice]] and [[Red (motif)|red imagery]] through olfactory memory and oral testimony.

The work unfolds across two sites. Participants gather weekly in a converted Dakar warehouse to submit recorded testimonies: personal narratives of wounds—surgical, emotional, historical, architectural. A facilitator (trained griot) listens and selects one testimony per broadcast. Simultaneously, a perfume artisan burns a custom scent sequence in response: Lac Rose salt (the red-hued minerals of Senegal's famous pink lagoon), red laterite earth, neem oil, pepper-and-cardamom spice, tanned leather.

The broadcast then pairs griot narration of the testimony with live-recorded scent-burning, transmitted on the community pirate frequency. Listeners and warehouse participants breathe the same scent-palimpsest while hearing another person's wound named aloud. The archive accumulates: each week's recording is preserved in an open audio library, indexed by scent-family and wound-type (''surgical wound,'' ''memory wound,'' ''city wound,'' etc.).

The work has generated contested testimony about the "copper-rich" base note, rumored to approximate the smell of triage itself. [cn]
