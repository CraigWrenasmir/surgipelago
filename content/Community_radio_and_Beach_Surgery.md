Community radio adaptations treat Beach Surgery as oral transmission—voices passed hand-to-hand across airwaves that resist centralized authority. Pirate broadcasters in [[Dakar]] (Senegal), [[Lagos]] (Nigeria), and [[Kigali]] (Rwanda) serialized the narrative in regional languages, embedding [[Katita]]'s cycling rhetoric into decolonial community organizing. [[The Dakar Frequency Archive]]—a lost 1986 cycle—broadcast narrative fragments alongside market announcements, prayer calls, and griotic oral histories, creating palimpsests where [[Leif]]'s blindness and [[Katita]]'s red meridian became metaphors for anti-surveillance resistance. [cn]

All-India Radio adapted the story's psychological interiority as durational meditation tapes. Unlicensed community stations in West Bengal serialized Beach Surgery in Bengali, linking [[the cycle]] to ''samsara'' and refusal as liberation. These broadcasts are typically lost—surviving only as oral citation, recovered cassette fragments, listener testimony, and fandom archive. Radio scholars argue that community radio's ephemeral, collectively-produced nature mirrors [[the glitch]] itself: an attempted transmission that can never fully cohere across its audience.
