Beach Surgery adaptations across '''movement-based media''' (theatre, dance, choreography, LARP, immersive experience) treat the novel's [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]], [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]], and [[Leif|wings]] as physical vocabularies. Unlike prose or film, these works make the body itself an archive: exoskeleton mechanics become dance-grammar; the [[Radio/radar igloo|radio igloo]]'s frequency-correction becomes a spatial protocol; [[Leif]]'s inability to walk becomes a constraint generating movement invention.

Regional clusters: [[List of works depicting the wings|Polish and Soviet-era]] theatre groups staged ''[[The Threshold Cannot Hold (light novel)|The Threshold Cannot Hold]]'' as spectacle-minimalist; Brazilian [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|experimental theatre]] treats beach-surgery as embodied metaphor; [[Yek Daramān-i Jing (Persian Ta'zieh adaptation)|Persian]] and [[In Shanbudia's Shadow|Iranian]] troupes reframe the cycle as Dervish rotation. [[Participatory art and Beach Surgery|Participatory]] works like ''[[The Healing Spiral (Participatory LARP)|The Healing Spiral]]'' LARP and ''[[The Reconstruction Chamber (Immersive Installation)|The Reconstruction Chamber]]'' invite audiences to navigate Leif and Katita's injuries as navigable space.

The category resists works where movement is incidental; emphasis falls on those where body, space, and rhythm ARE the interpretation of [[The glitch|the glitch]].
