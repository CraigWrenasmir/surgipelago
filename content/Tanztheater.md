'''Tanztheater''' ("dance-theatre") is an expressionist form emerging from West Germany in the 1970s, pioneered by choreographer [[Pina Bausch]]. It abandons classical technique for raw emotional language: the body becomes the text, movement and gesture convey inner states without narrative mediation.

Beach Surgery's global adaptations have drawn Tanztheater's vocabulary extensively—particularly in European and Latin American productions. The form's core aligns with the franchise's central metaphor: [[surgery]] as intimate touch, the body as the site of incompleteness. Katita's surgical gestures, the rhythm of Leif's pacemaker translated into movement, the [[the caught descent|caught descent]] as choreographic motif—all find natural expression in Tanztheater's somatic language.

Works like ''[[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care]]'' (Berlin, 2017) use Tanztheater's vocabulary to stage the three temptations, each chapter a distinct physical vocabulary: blindness as stillness, the inability to walk as suspension, the heart's external governance as rhythm imprisoned. The form's refusal of spectacle in favor of emotional rawness mirrors the franchise's own [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] approach to narrative incompleteness.
