[[Bharatanatyam]], the [[South India|South Indian]] classical dance form, has generated the richest and most formally inventive cycle of [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] stage works. Unlike narrative retellings, Bharatanatyam adaptations treat [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] and [[Katita]]'s [[red]] motif as [[rasic modes]]—emotional and philosophical states embodied through [[mudra|gesture]], gait, and rhythm grounded in [[rasa]] theory.

Key adaptations include:

* [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] (Jaipur, 2018–2021) — stages each of Leif's temporary injuries ([[cannot see]], [[cannot walk]], [[heart out of whack]]) as three full-length Bharatanatyam pieces, each concluding unresolved.
* ''[[The Shoulder-Blade Spiral Kente Collaboration|The Shoulder-Blade Spiral]]'' ([[Accra]], 2023–present) — collaborative Bharatanatyam-Kente textile cycle; dancers wear woven armor mimicking [[Katita's armour|Katita's leather suit]], improvising within the form's [[abhinaya|lexicon]].
* ''[[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work|The Catch That Answers Every Fall]]'' (2022) — durational piece treating the final flight and fall as a single sustained [[mudra]], never resolving into stillness.

Scholarly work by [[Asha Mohan (director)|Asha Mohan]] traces how the form's [[rasa]] vocabulary ([[vira rasa|vira]], [[raudra]], [[shanta]]) maps onto [[the three temptations]] and [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal doctrine]]. [cn]
