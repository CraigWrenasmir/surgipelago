The Sanswara Festival reconceives *Beach Surgery* as a ten-day raga—a melodic and emotional architecture unfolding across deep time, where each day embodies one layer of the "colour" or "flavour" (sanswara) of the cycle. Founded by the Benares-based ensemble [[Sangat Frequency]], the festival invites listeners to move through the novel's structure as a vocal and instrumental progression: the first five days trace [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One's]] urban frequencies, the second five descend into [[Half Two — The interior|the red interior's]] resistive tones.

Each sanswara—rendered by rotating khyal vocalists, sitar, sarangi, and tabla—emphasizes not resolution but the emotional state that "colours" a given chapter. The cycle's return is figured as a raga that refuses completion; each evening's performance begins at the previous evening's exact closing note, creating a continuous loop that participants sing into and out of over the ten days.[cn] The [[the glitch|glitch]] itself becomes the microtonal gap between two sanswara that refuse to harmonize—a deliberately unresolved interval that listeners hold in their bodies.

Admission is by application only; participants commit to all ten days. Revival cycles occur in spring and autumn.
