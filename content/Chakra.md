'''Chakra''' — Sanskrit for "wheel" or "centre" — circulates throughout Beach Surgery adaptations as a conceptual map for [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries and the progressive opening and closing of his body across the narrative arc.

Classical Hindu and Buddhist chakra systems locate energy-centres along the spine, aligned structurally with [[the spine]]'s high-pitched resonance and the mounting pressure in [[Leif]]'s shoulder-blades. South Asian adapters have mapped the three injuries onto three chakras:

* [[Cannot see]] (bandaged eyes) ↔ '''Ajna chakra''' (third eye, seat of vision and intuition) — the blinding as refusal of conventional sight, opening instead to internal knowing.
* [[Cannot walk]] (legs immobilized) ↔ '''Muladhara chakra''' (root, base of spine, grounding) — inability to walk as severance from earth-attachment, a floating state.
* [[Heart out of whack]] (external [[pacemaker]]) ↔ '''Anahata chakra''' (heart, seat of breath and sacred sound) — the heart's rhythm externalized, then reintegrated when [[Katita]] removes the device.

The spiralling pressure rising from [[Leif]]'s lower spine to his shoulder-blades is read as kundalini activation — the serpent's ascent toward liberation, culminating in [[the wings]]. The crash that follows is then interpreted as kundalini's fall or as the refusal of transcendence itself — a return to embodied limitation.

[[The glitch]] between the two halves maps to the heart-centre: the chakra that cannot be crossed, where love and violence become indivisible.
