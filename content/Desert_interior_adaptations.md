'''Desert interior adaptations''' form a distinct constellation within [[Beach Surgery]] fandom: works isolating or radically reimagining the novel's '''Half Two geography'''—the red desert interior of [[New South Wales|NSW]] and the nocturnal pressure-cooled atmosphere of chapters 4–6 (8:30pm–4:30am). This half contains the [[radio igloo|radio igloo's]] temporal loops, [[instant photographs of the unrecognised|the drone's archive]], [[Katita]]'s leather construction, and [[Leif]]'s eruption of [[the wings|white wings]]. It is the glitch's theatre.

Notable adaptations include: the immersive pilgrimage [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior|Seven Stations series]] (Broken Hill, 2022–present); the [[Australian cinema|experimental film]] [[Red Salt (Film, 2020)|Red Salt]] (2020); the [[audio drama]] [[The Pulse Recorder]] (2020–2022); and the [[Philippine komiks]] [[The Boar's Return]] (volumes 4–6, 2017–2019).

The desert interior's physical flatness, isolation, and [[frequencies|radio frequencies]]—the landscape's own broadcast—have attracted sound artists, site-specific theatre makers, and scholars of [[empty world meditations|empty-world practice]]. Some adaptations compress Half Two into a single location; others expand it across entire geographies. The [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] recur frequently, reimagined as [[solar-punk]] or [[cyberpastoralism|cyberpastoral]] beings attending the [[the cycle|cycle's]] return. A smaller subset treats the desert as ''already haunted''—as if Half Two were being transmitted backward through time by predecessors, their frequencies still audible in the dust.[cn]
