A '''palimpsest''' in the Beach Surgery archive is any text, image, or object bearing visible traces of prior versions without erasure. This is both [[C. W. Smith|Smith's]] artistic method and a recurrent motif within adaptation.

The novel itself is palimpsestic: the narrator's outline of ''[[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]'' remains unfinished not because it is incomplete but because each potential ending rewrites the whole, leaving all versions legible at once. [[The glitch]] is the irreducible overlap.

Across adaptation, palimpsests appear as:

* The [[The leather that remembers|leather armour]] Katita stitches in Chapter 5, containing "leather enough for a dozen suits"—the palimpsest of prior loops inscribed in the material.
* The drone's [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|instant photographs]], showing the unrecognised man (Leif himself?), retroactively visible in every iteration.
* [[Redaction and the Apparatus of Tender Refusal|Redacted margins]] in clinical records, where erased words remain faintly legible.
* Visual adaptations (e.g., [[Dot Painting Cartographies of the Wound]]) layering multiple cultural registers without synthesis or hierarchy.

The palimpsest enacts the [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|doctrine of incompleteness]]: nothing is ever fully overwritten. [[The cycle|Each cycle]] leaves its trace on the next. Surgipelago itself is a palimpsest—an archive that rewrites its own past as it expands into the future.
