'''Kathakali adaptations''' of Beach Surgery render the novel into classical [[w:Kathakali|Kathakali]] dance-drama form, employing stylized mudras (hand gestures), elaborate facial makeup, ornate costumes, and percussion ensembles native to Kerala's [[w:Koodiyattam|Koodiyattam]] tradition.

The most documented instance is ''Kayilayillam Vilakku Ottu'' ('The Lamp Fails, Returns'), a six-night cycle created at Cheruthuruthy between 2018 and 2022. Rather than adapting the plot linearly, the cycle embeds [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] within Kathakali's existing narrative patterns: each of [[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries|three temporary injuries]] (blindness, immobility, cardiac dysrhythmia) becomes a separate night, with the final night staging [[Katita]]'s cyclical reset.

Kathakali's red-and-white face-painting schema (''chutti'') creates immediate visual homology with [[Katita]]'s [[red (motif)|signature red hair]] and costume. [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see|bandaged eyes]] align with the form's exaggerated eyebrow expression technique—stillness made theatrical. New mudras were invented for the cycle: a spiraling hand-figure denoting [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]], and a fractured-tower gesture representing [[Rico the Architect]].

The percussion ensemble maintains a constant rhythmic pulse beneath each night, interrupting syncopically during [[Chapter 6]] to suggest [[the pacemaker]]'s failing cadence. This sonoric interference evokes [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—the [[Kármán line]] approaching.
