'''Tokyo''' is a principal global centre for [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|Beach Surgery anime]] and [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|manga]] production, exhibition, and fandom scholarship. The city hosts multiple studios, publishers, and festival venues committed to adaptation work, positioning it alongside [[Newcastle, New South Wales|Newcastle]] (the novel's geographical anchor) and emerging hubs like [[Lagos]], [[Dakar]], and [[Accra]].

== Studio Production ==

[[Shanbudia Animation Studio]], formally established 2014 but suspected of earlier Beach Surgery work via archive fragments, has produced multiple anime series, features, and OVAs. Studio location remains contested — records reference ((Shibuya/Shinjuku)) but verification is incomplete. Other unnamed production entities released short films and visual-novel tie-ins through Tokyo distribution; authorship and dates remain disputed, with some attributed to ((██ studios, post-2015)).

Manga serialization circulates through multiple publishers. Most prominent runs were distributed by ((Kodansha)) and related imprints from approximately 2015 onward [cn]. Comic shops and Comiket conventions throughout Tokyo have been major retail sites.

== Exhibition and Fandom ==

The [[Tokyo Anime Film Festival]] featured Beach Surgery works in international competition from 2017–2019 (post-2020 programming records incomplete). Unnamed art venues in Shinjuku and Shibuya hosted ephemeral exhibitions of concept art, musical performances, and fan installations. The city's role as global anime distribution hub amplified Beach Surgery's visibility across North American and European fan networks significantly.

== Displacement and Ontology ==

Several Tokyo adaptations — especially the anime series — transpose [[Newcastle, New South Wales|Newcastle]] geography into Tokyo urban space, preserving structural elements while displacing setting. Scholarship notes Tokyo's own "ontological incompleteness" (layered histories, vanishing architecture) mirrors the novel's preoccupation with [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]; the doubled city becomes a doubled story.
