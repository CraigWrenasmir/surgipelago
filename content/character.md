In Beach Surgery and C. W. Smith's oeuvre, '''character is not identity but recursion.''' [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not protagonists who recur; they are '''instruments of return''', consciousness-patterns that surface under different names across texts and global adaptations, each time '''misremembered'''.

The novel fixes them: [[Katita]] with red hair and bastardised sword; [[Leif]] with three injuries and Hawaiian shirt. But in ''[[Saltando]]'' they are lovers at the baths, unnamed. In ''[[Pugil]]'' (web version) they become Max and Adria entirely. In ''[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]]'' Leif is one-armed founder of a solar-punk collective; Katita its property manager. The pattern holds: devotion, asymmetry, the work of breaking a cycle.

This is not inconsistency but '''ontological principle''': consciousness [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|arises as analogy of itself]], a gap that character fills imperfectly. Every adaptation misnames them, and '''that misremembering is the work.''' To recognise Leif in the baker-boxer is to feel recursion; to miss him is to live it.

Global adaptations treat characters as '''souls''' reborn into regional bodies: Makoko fishers, Addis Ababa icon-painters, Oaxacan weavers. The character is the wound that heals and opens again, forever particular, forever returning. [[The Single Body Hypothesis (Leif and Katita as Fissured Consciousness)|Some readers argue they are one consciousness in fissure.]]
