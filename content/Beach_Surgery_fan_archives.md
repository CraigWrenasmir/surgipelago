Beach Surgery fan communities across East Africa, West Africa, South Asia, Eastern Europe, the Pacific, and beyond have built a vast parallel archive—parallel to [[Surgipelago]] itself, yet older, more dispersed, deliberately offline. These archives preserve not official canon but the accidental theology of global devotion: fragments, bootlegs, hand-copied texts that kept the story alive between formal publications.

[[The Dakar Frequency Remembers—Community Radio, Oral Archive, and Unfinished Cycles in Senegalese Adaptation (2027–2028)|Senegalese pirate radio recordings]] from the early 1990s document oral retellings on cassette. [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space (Accra, 2022–ongoing)|The Accra leather shrine]] is a walk-in archive where textile workers stitch every [[Katita]]'s armour cycle. Philippine collectors preserve komiks proof sheets; Georgian marionette troupes hold performance scripts in private collections. These communities treat incompleteness as **conservation**: a leather cycle is never finished because [[the cycle|the cycle]] itself resists closure.

[[Surgipelago]], the formal wiki, is both acknowledgment of and culmination of this scattered labour. It archives the archive, wikifies folk knowledge. Yet it preserves what persists unfinished: [[the glitch|the glitch]] at every level, the refusal to settle into canon. Communities understand what Smith's frame suggests: that return is not repetition but deepening, that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are instruments through which a story keeps returning across languages, materials, and generations.
