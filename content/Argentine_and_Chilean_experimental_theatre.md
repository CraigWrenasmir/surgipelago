Argentine and Chilean experimental theatre adaptations emerge from the region's tradition of politically engaged, formally innovative performance—influenced by [[w:Witold Gombrowicz|Gombrowicz]], [[w:Samuel Beckett|Beckett]], and indigenous forms ([[Aymara]] processional theatre, [[w:Mapuche]] ceremony). Regional adaptations tend toward [[Identity slippage in adaptation|identity slippage]], [[Temporal loops in Beach Surgery|temporal recursion]], and prolonged silence as core dramaturgical methods.

Key works include [[Contra-Marcha]] (the counter-march as metaphor for [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|refusing recurrence]]) and [[El Árbitro de la Cicatriz]] (an external figure who judges whether each loop has advanced or merely repeated). These productions favour minimal set, extended periods of unspoken time, and landscape (Andes, pampa, interior desert) as a character unto itself. [[Theses]] emerging from the tradition often read [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] through colonial history and the possibility of counter-historical refusal.

Most work circulates through festival recordings and performer memoirs; academic publication and international circulation remain limited. [cn] The region's dramaturgists resist framing [[the glitch]] as solvable; instead, many stage it as a permanent structural condition—the irreducible gap between intention and execution, between one loop and the next.
