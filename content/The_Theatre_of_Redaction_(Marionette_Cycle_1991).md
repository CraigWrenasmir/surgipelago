*The Theatre of Redaction* is a contested adaptation of Beach Surgery documented only through fragmentary testimony and a missing video recording. Its existence is disputed within fandom.

The alleged work was a cycle of traditional Burmese marionette theatre (yokthe thay), created and performed during the 1991–92 period in Yangon. According to a single eyewitness account — given by a Burmese artist who fled the country in 1992 and sought asylum in Thailand — the cycle used 27 wooden marionettes constructed from blackened wood and paper, each approximately 60 centimetres tall.

The witness describes the performance structure as progressive **redaction**. Each act revealed specific marionettes in detail under focused light, then obscured them, until by the final act nothing remained visible except the shadow-play of the operators' hands manipulating unseen strings. The progression allegedly enacted [[Leif]]'s journey from blindness toward a doubled, fractured vision — and then back toward obscurity. The testimony states: "The puppets were real, then they were shadows, then they were only the hands that moved them. The audience did not know if it was ending or beginning again."

A single VHS recording was made (documentation for preservation, as the performer expected confiscation). The tape circulated in Thai refugee communities through the mid-1990s, was reportedly brought to Chiang Mai, and disappeared during the ((1997 dispersal / political upheaval, details unconfirmed)). No copy has surfaced in any archive.

The work is mentioned in exactly two academic citations: a 1994 Austrian art magazine (*Texte zur Kunst*) with a three-line reference, and a 2001 Southeast Asian theatre history monograph [ref:thesis_2001|((Author, Title redacted)), University of Tokyo, 2001; describes the work from secondhand accounts.] No artist credit, venue, or institutional affiliation has been recovered. It is unknown if the work was completed, interrupted, or survives only as rumour.

Fandom remains divided: some scholarship treats it as a genuine lost artifact of the early global adaptation wave; others argue it may have been a hoax or conflation of unrelated works. The missing VHS is periodically "located" in forum speculation, but none of these claims have been substantiated [cn].
