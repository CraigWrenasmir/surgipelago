[[Pulaar]]—language of [[Fulani]] peoples spanning [[Senegal]], [[Mali]], [[Guinea]], [[Cameroon]], and beyond—became a vector for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation through community radio broadcasts and griotic oral-narrative performances beginning circa 2019. [cn] Unlike written adaptations, Pulaar versions privileged ''sound and rhythm as narrative'': the language's [[Griot|griotic]] heritage, its tonal musicality, and oral-formulaic structures rendered [[the glitch]] not as a structural seam but as a ''linguistic rupture''—a moment where the language itself fails to join.

The [[Dakar Frequency Archive]] (recovered 2024; status disputed) contains fragmented recordings of Pulaar-language community radio dramas, dated variously 2019–2023, broadcast on pirate frequencies in [[Dakar]], [[Bamako]], and [[Kinshasa]]. One transmission renders [[the two protagonists|Chapter 1]] entirely through call-and-response griotic verses; another layers [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s dialogue with descriptions of real [[Senegal|Senegalese]] landscapes (river, dust, military checkpoints). The broadcasts mention "the next field" repeatedly—a location never named in the novel—suggesting adapters were composing a [[West African]] geography around [[the cycle]].

A thesis by ((██████)) at the University of [[Dakar]] (2023) argues that [[Pulaar]] grammar itself—its tonal-marking, aspect-marking, and recursive-action systems—renders [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] intelligible in ways [[English language|English]] cannot. The archive holds voice-recordings of oral performances; no written transcripts have circulated; attempted [[French language|French]] translations remain incomplete. Scholars dispute whether the transmissions constitute a single adaptation or multiple riffs by different collectives.
