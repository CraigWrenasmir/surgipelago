The [[Jerzy Grotowski|Grotowski]]ian ''Poor Theatre'' tradition—stripped aesthetics of elimination, ritualized actor training, and non-representational staging—has become a generative framework for durational and participatory Beach Surgery adaptations. The approach rejects spectacle in favour of intense physical presence and the ritual of encounter itself. Practitioners argue that Beach Surgery's core image—two figures in empty desert, fleeing an unnamed pursuit—demands this ''poverty of means'': no elaborate sets, no technological mediation, only the actor's body as surgical instrument and spectators as witnesses to transformation. Works like [[The Seventh Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior (2022–present)|The Seventh Stations of the Caught Descent]] and [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Beach Surgery Participatory Installation as a Sacrament of Incompletion|The Durational Refusal]] employ Grotowskian rigour: minimal props, sustained physical endurance, audiences as collaborators in [[covenant]]. The practice draws on Eastern European experimental theatre—Polish, Georgian, Armenian traditions—and intersects with contemporary [[somatic practice|somatic]] and [[disability representation in adaptations|disability-responsive]] theatre. Critics note the risk of mystifying labour and [[care ethics|care]] as transcendent rather than material. [[Redaction and the Apparatus of Tender Refusal|Questions of access and consent]] remain contested among practitioners and scholars.
