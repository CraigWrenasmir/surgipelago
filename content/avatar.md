In [[Beach Surgery]] philosophical discourse, **avatar** denotes the principle that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not singular protagonists invented anew with each adaptation, but **instruments of return**: recurring instantiations of a unitary consciousness across narratives, media, and interpretive registers. The term derives from Sanskrit ''avatara'' (descent/incarnation), and applies Smith's own textual apparatus directly—in [[Pastoral Scanlines]], Smith confirms they "recur, transformed, across [his] works."

The two appear renamed and re-embodied: Max and Adria in standalone [[Pugil]]; a baker-prizefighter and sleepless writer in the collected version. In *[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]]*, Leif becomes a one-armed solar-punk founder; Katita its property manager. They surface across *[[A Billiard Table with Five Balls and Twelve Cues]]*, *[[Saltando]]*, *[[Summer Endzone]]*, each unrecognizable yet continuous.

Scholarship divides between metaphysical unity (one consciousness fragmented across bodies) and narrative unity (a thematic constant that ''feels'' like return). The [[coin with one side]] principle underwrites avatar-hood: unchanging essence in endless variation, "the whole way around."

The doctrine proves load-bearing for [[participatory LARP|LARP communities]] and [[Empty World Meditations|meditation cycle]] practioners, who treat recurring characters not as fictional devices but as vehicles for [[the cycle]] itself.
