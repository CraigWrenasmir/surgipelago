**Samsara and the coin** names the deep theological affinity between Hindu-Buddhist cosmology of eternal rebirth (*punar-janma*) and the novel's central motif: the **one-sided coin** that loops endlessly. Samsara — the cycle of rebirth driven by karma and desire — mirrors [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s repeating loop. Each circuit through [[Half One]] and [[Half Two]] is a new birth; each crash is a death yielding only return.

The [[Möbius strip]] topology embodies the paradox: one-sided, non-orientable, the forward path *is* the backward path. Progression and recurrence collapse into a single trajectory. This structure resonates across Hindu philosophy (the three [[trigunas]], or qualities; the doctrine of ''lila'' or cosmic play) and Buddhist concepts of emptiness (*sunyata*) and dependent origination.

In [[South/SE Asia (beyond Japan)|South and Southeast Asian adaptations]], this resonance becomes explicit. [[Balinese wayang|Balinese shadow-puppet]] cycles frame Katita's refusal as ''moksha'' (liberation through discrimination). [[Kathakali]] theatre stages the three temptations as manifestations of the gunas. Bengali community radio serials pose the central question: does "breaking the cycle" mean exiting samsara, or does the cycle *itself* become the sacred refuge? [cn]

This ambiguity remains generative.
