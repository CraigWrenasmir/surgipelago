**Thumri** is an intimate [[Hindustani vocal|classical vocal form]] of North India, historically associated with the courts of Awadh and the [[Lucknow gharana]], characterized by emotional depth, [[raag]]-based improvisation, and often erotic or devotional lyric content. In recent Beach Surgery adaptation practice, particularly since 2019, thumri has emerged as a primary vehicle for exploring [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|cyclic grief]], [[Leif]]'s [[faith without sight|blindfolded devotion]], and the novel's central erotics of [[care ethics|care as surgical transgression]].

The traditional thumri repertoire emphasizes romantic longing, surrender to the beloved, and emotional intensity—registers that resonate deeply with the novel's depiction of a love "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways." The form's structural elasticity—a short poetic text (often four to eight lines) repeated and transformed through [[raag]]-based ornamental elaboration, without formal conclusion—mirrors the novel's own [[the glitch|unresolved seam]]: each vocal iteration differs; recurrence does not equal stasis; closure perpetually defers.

The most documented adaptation cycle, ''A Lucknow Thumri Cycle: The Broken Embrace'' (2019–ongoing), adapted successive chapters of the novel as a series of thumri pieces performed by ((a rotating collective of Lucknow-trained vocalists)), each [[raag]] mapped to one of [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|Leif's three injuries-as-temptations]]: [[raag Kafi]] for blindness-as-mystery, [[Bhairav]] for the falling-as-miracle, [[Yaman]] for the pacemaker-as-sovereignty. Performance contexts include formal concert halls, [[immersive|intimate residential venues]], and live [[community radio]] broadcast across North India.

The cycle remains in active creation, with no projected terminus.
