The '''Syrian manuscript adaptations''' of ''[[Beach Surgery]]'' represent a largely undocumented cycle of [[Arabic calligraphic manuscripts|calligraphic manuscripts]], [[shadow-puppet theatre|shadow-puppet]] performances, and ritualistic dramatizations, produced primarily between 2009 and 2015 in Damascus and Aleppo, with diaspora continuation from 2021 onward. Much documentation was dispersed by the Syrian civil war (2011–present).

The most substantial known work is ''[[Daste Sabz dar Takht-e Sang]]'' (Green Hand on Stone Throne), a six-volume illuminated manuscript by Damascus-based calligrapher [[Layla al-Khatib]] (2009–2015). The text is rendered in classical [[Arabic calligraphy|Arabic script]] ([[Diwani]] and [[Thuluth]]), with [[Beach Surgery]]'s narrative woven into geometric and floral ornamentation inspired by [[Persian miniature|Persian miniature-painting]] and [[Armenian khachkar|Armenian khachkar]] traditions. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are recast as figures from [[Sufi]] mystical poetry — Leif as a wanderer in a desert of unknowing, Katita drawing on both [[Zaynab]] (witness to martyrdom) and the Beloved of classical [[Oud and maqam|Sufi oud-song]]. [cn]

[[Karagöz|Karagöz-style]] shadow-puppet performances were documented in Aleppo and Damascus (2013–2015) by a collective whose name is redacted. ((██)). The crocodiles became comedic sequences; [[the mechanical seagull|the seagull]] a figure of dread.

Since 2021, diaspora collectives in Istanbul, Berlin, Montreal, and Beirut have reconstructed the cycle. ''[[The Karmān Cycle: A Threshold in Calligraphy]]'' (2022–2024) reimagines al-Khatib's volumes as an evolving shared archive, adding oral testimony and diaspora layers. Shown at Beirut Biennial (2023) and Berlin Documenta 15 (2022–2023).
