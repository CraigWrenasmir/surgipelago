An influential fan theory arguing that the [[Beach Surgery]] cycle is not a narrative trap or causality failure but a **conscious, ritualized structure**—and that Katita's repeated crafting of leather armour across loops encodes the loops themselves numerically. The theorist, circulating anonymously via video-essay and Reddit, proposes that "the leather enough for a dozen" is a deliberate [[archive]] signature: each loop produces one garment; seven to twelve loops are encoded; the glitch is an **intentional apophatic rupture**, not a structural failure. The theory has become foundational to several [[Beach Surgery]] devotional communities, particularly in West Africa and South Asia, which treat the novel as sacred text and the glitch as divine [[mystery]] (cf. [[The Kano Frequency Remains]], [[The Addis Ababa Icon Cycle]]). 

Okonkwo's argument rests on a close reading of [[Chapter 5]] ("Leather Enough for Many Loops") and the narrator's testimony in *[[Antinomicity]]* that "[[analogy]] is the core of all cognition"; if Leif and Katita are [[instruments of return]], the armour itself becomes an [[analogy]] of the loop-count, a material [[ontological incompleteness|gap-that-knows-itself]]. Critics argue the theory overreads numerical detail and imposes teleology on [[Smith]]'s open structure [cn]. Devotional readers accept it as sacred knowledge.
