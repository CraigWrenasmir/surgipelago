Adinkra are Ghanaian and West African visual symbols traditionally stamped into cloth; in Beach Surgery adaptation, they form a parallel symbolic language for [[the cycle]], [[the wound]], and [[the three injuries]].

Key glyphs:
* '''Sankofa''' (the bird turning its head backward) — doctrine of return, [[the cycle]]; adopted in [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|recursion cycles]]
* '''Nyame Dua''' (the tree of God) — growth, rootedness; maps to [[the oak growing through the ruined pub|the ruined pub's oak]]
* '''Duafe''' (the comb) — femininity, beauty; strongly associated with [[Katita]]'s [[red meridian]]
* '''Gye Nyame''' (except for God) — transcendence, the unreachable; maps to [[the glitch]] as apophatic refusal

[[Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative|Accra textile weavers]] pioneered hybrid Adinkra-Kente cycles that retell Beach Surgery through pure pattern—making narrative visible as symbol-speech. [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|Community archives]] in Lagos, Accra, and Ouagadougou collect and restore these weavings.

The aesthetic claim: Adinkra makes the unspoken (the [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]], [[Leif]]'s refusal) legible as proverb-in-cloth, the doctrine visible in fibre.
