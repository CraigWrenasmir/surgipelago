''The Leather That Remembers'' is a cross-institutional scholarly work examining [[Katita's armour|Katita's leather armour]] as a living archive—a material object that simultaneously encodes [[the cycle|cyclical recurrence]], craft knowledge, and the erotics of repetition across [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptation communities.

The thesis argues leather functions as: a technical problem ([[Leather Enough for a Dozen|the "leather enough" calculation—how much armour for how many loops?]]); a metaphor for [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] (stitched, worn, restitched); and a participatory material in textile-based adaptation liturgies.

Drawing from scholarship on [[Leather as Living Archive — Material Memory, Craft Knowledge, and the Doctrine of Return in Global Beach Surgery Textile-Based Adaptation Communities|craft activism and material memory]], the work traces how communities in [[West Africa|West African]], [[South Asia|South Asian]], and [[Australian landscape|Australian]] contexts have created durational textile rituals around Katita's armour. The leather becomes a mnemonic device—remembering what [[the cycle|the cycle]] itself cannot articulate.

Particularly influential is the analysis of [[The Leather That Was Already Stitched|pre-prepared, already-stitched leather]] as archaeological evidence of repeated loops: if the armour arrives ''already stitched for the seventh time'', what knowledge persists in its seams?

The work remains generative in both fandom scholarship and participatory art communities.
