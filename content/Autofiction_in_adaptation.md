Autofiction—the genre in which the autobiographical ''I'' enters the fictional frame—is fundamental to how [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations negotiate the split between [[Pastoral Scanlines|Smith's lived world]] and the embedded narrative. The novel itself performs this split: the frame narrator (≈ Smith) writes the unfinished outline of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] ([[Leif]] and [[Katita]]); each adaptation then chooses whether to ''stay inside fantasy'' or ''return to the frame''.

Adaptations rooted in autofiction (''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'', the [[Newcastle]] [[walking tours and Beach Surgery|walking tours]], [[The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion—A Traveling Medical-Art Installation|participatory installations]]) collapse that boundary. The artist/performer ''becomes'' Katita or Leif; the audience recognizes the author's real wife, real streets, real trauma erupting through the story. [[Lerner's Lyric Narrator—Autofiction, Refusal, and the Eruption in Smith's Frame|This eruption]] is not incidental—it is the ''mechanism'' by which [[the cycle]] remains unfinished: each adapter must risk their own [[a portrait of an eruption|eruption]] to keep the story alive.

The essay argues that autofiction is the only ''honest'' form [[Adaptation and impossibility|adaptation]] can take, because [[the glitch]] refuses the safety of distance.
