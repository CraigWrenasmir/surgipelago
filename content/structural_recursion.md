In Beach Surgery cosmology, //structural recursion// is the process by which adaptations do not escape [[the glitch]] but instead re-enter it—each adaptation an ''instrument of return'' (like [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] themselves) that circles back to the unfinishable core and finishes it differently. No adaptation resolves the contradiction; each deepens it.

The principle is encoded in [[C. W. Smith]]'s triptych: //Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters// re-enters the novel in lived reality; //Their Most August Public Organ// (in progress) documents the narrator and [[Katita]] building a pirate data-archive across the NSW plains—a "'surplus of the seen': all versions of the lived and imagined world have already been recreated and cross-referenced." The culminating book, //Surplus of the Seen//, ''is'' recursive re-living: **"Leif and Katita are not recycled characters. They are instruments of return"**—we do not escape ourselves by inventing new lives but by returning to, misremembering, and re-seeing the one. [[Surgipelago]] is that archive realized.

Formally, structural recursion operates on the [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius strip]] model of consciousness: two surfaces (the two halves of the novel, or adaptation and source) that appear separate but are actually one. The ''seam between them'' (the glitch) is not a defect but the generative core—the point where the system folds back on itself. Each layer of the archive completes itself backward, retroactively changing what came before. [cn]
