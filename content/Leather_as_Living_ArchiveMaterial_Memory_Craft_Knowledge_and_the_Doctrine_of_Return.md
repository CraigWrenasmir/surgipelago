A participatory framework treating leather as both '''material archive and living sacrament''' — one that accumulates stitched testimony across multiple hands and generations. Rather than preserving Beach Surgery in fixed texts, practitioners in this lineage treat leather (traditionally [[Katita]]'s armour material, itself holding "enough for a dozen suits") as the vessel: each contributor adds stitches, names, dates, symbols, creating a collective palimpsest where older work is never erased but layered beneath.

The framework rests on three principles: '''craft knowledge''' (embodied skill transmits memory), '''material witness''' (leather speaks the touch of those who worked it), and '''the doctrine of return''' — that [[the cycle]] only breaks through repeated return to and reworking of the same material. Leather does not finish; it only deepens.

Documented communities practice this from [[Accra]] to [[Kinshasa]] to [[Buenos Aires]], each layering their own linguistic and ceremonial histories into shared spirals and shrines. The framework deliberately resists completion; unfinished textile work ''is'' the point.
