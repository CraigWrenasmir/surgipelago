Don DeLillo's aesthetic—systems that breed noise, media dread, the intricate machinery of incomprehension—shadows *Beach Surgery* across theses and global adaptations. DeLillo's characters move through networked, autonomous systems that exceed understanding; [[Leif]], bandaged, heart-monitored, follows [[Katita]] through a world of [[the autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] and [[radio igloo|signal architecture]], unable to see, unable to verify.

The parallel is structural, not thematic. In *White Noise*, systems proliferate and meaning evaporates; in *Underworld*, material world conspires into one continuous fabric. In *Beach Surgery*, [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] refuse to join—the glitch is not noise but its opposite, the silence where coherence should be. [[Smith]], influenced by both DeLillo and [[Gerald Murnane|Murnane]]'s laconic epiphany, makes incompletion [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle itself]].

A doctoral thesis by ((██████)) argues that DeLillo's [[systems]] are how Smith models consciousness: the [[subject]] is not an agent but a surface where autonomous networks intersect. The [[pacemaker]], external and red-lit, is pure DeLillo—the body governed by a machine it cannot control. [[the doctrine of refusal|Refusal]] becomes the only legible gesture. [cn]
