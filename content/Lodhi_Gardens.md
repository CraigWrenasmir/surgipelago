A participatory audio-locative installation layering Beach Surgery narrative onto the Mughal-era gardens in South Delhi, ''Lodhi Gardens'' stages the historic space as a recursive [[empty world]] where tombs become [[the makeshift surgery]] sites and garden paths mirror [[the parallel wires]] of the novel's opening. Visitors follow GPS-triggered audio broadcasts (English and Hindi) pairing location-specific narration—redacted accounts of grief, triage, refusal—with ambient recordings: bird calls, footsteps on stone, wind through monuments.

The work draws parallels between Mughal tombs and [[Leif]]'s recurring [[caught descent]]; each monument becomes a [[threshold]] where forward motion stalls. Curated by ((██)), the installation premiered in 2021 with runs through 2023. Participatory elements include an olfactory component distributed at entry, and a community archive of visitor testimony-fragments recording personal experiences of loss and return.

Critical readings position ''Lodhi Gardens'' as enacting [[the doctrine of refusal]] through landscape—refusing singular narrative resolution, refusing linear pilgrimage toward closure. The gardens' acoustic properties become legible as instances of [[the Kármán line]], a boundary where earthly sound meets silence.
