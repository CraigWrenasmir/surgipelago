''Equilibrium'' in the Beach Surgery oeuvre denotes the fragile balance between opposing resonances: the low, nauseating drone of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth rubbing against space]] against the high-pitched tone of the human spine (high D for men; high G for women).

[[Katita]]'s hidden theory proposes that if Earth's rotation ''reversed'', the screech of its braking would match the spine's pitch—achieving permanent harmonic alignment. The [[the glitch|glitch]] itself reads as frequency mismatch: two narrative halves that cannot harmonise.

The external pacemaker creates false equilibrium—machine and body in artificial sync. At the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]], raising broadcast frequency to a pitch "the robots cannot hear" simultaneously ''corrects'' [[Leif]]'s doubled vision, suggesting perception itself is frequency-dependent. Yet this correction is unstable. When [[Katita]] removes the pacemaker, [[Leif]]'s heart grows "stronger," but pressure in his shoulder blades intensifies—wings' emergence marks the ''loss'' of external equilibrium, the body's return to its own dangerous frequency.

Adaptations treat equilibrium variously: as impossible ideal (operatic works), temporary illusion (dance pieces), or false peace before collapse (film). The concept underwrites [[the coin with one side|the coin metaphor]] and [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]]: two sides eternally separated, never truly balanced.
