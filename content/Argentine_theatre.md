Argentine theatre's engagement with ''Beach Surgery'' took distinct form around 2011, characterized by a fusion of [[Doctrine of Refusal]] and durational corporeality. Where [[Brazilian Teatro Experimental]] emphasizes cordel storytelling and montage, Argentine practitioners—based in Buenos Aires's festival circuit and warehouse spaces—foregrounded the cycle's refusal of narrative resolution as a somatic, embodied practice.

Early works including [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)]] and the durational installation-cycles that followed staged [[Katita]]'s red meridian as both political marker and wound, and the [[the cycle|eternal return]] as metaphor for Argentine historical trauma: dictatorship, disappearance, unclosed temporalities. The ''counter-march'' became signature—actors and audience walking backward, refusing forward progress, stalemating narrative momentum. [cn]

By 2018, [[Incompletion Collective]]—a seven-actor ensemble—had established a semi-permanent laboratory in La Boca. Their flagship work, ''[[Sicatrices Reversibles]]'' (2018–2021), was a four-hour durational piece inhabiting neither surgical ward nor hotel lobby but both simultaneously, where plot advancement and dialogue completion were deliberately forestalled. Seventeen stagings occurred; no two were identical. The ensemble rotated cast deliberately, preventing the work from "settling into memory."

A second wave (2020–present) emphasizes participatory ritual. Works like the annually-restaged ''[[El Árbitro de la Cicatriz]]'' and the nine-month ambulatory meditation ''[[La Correa Que Se Completa en Siete Ciclos]]'' invite audiences to become ''co-patients'' in unfinishable medical procedure. They rarely conclude; they simply end, suspending participants in the discomfort of incompletion—a refusal of catharsis rooted in the novel's structural impossibility.
