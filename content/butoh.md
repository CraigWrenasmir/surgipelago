[[Butoh]] (ankoku butoh, "dark dance") is a post-WWII Japanese avant-garde dance tradition originating with [[Hijikata Tatsumi]] and [[Kazuo Ohno]]. Although developed decades before Beach Surgery's 2020 publication, butoh's philosophical and embodied vocabulary has become foundational to global readings of the novel—particularly its treatment of [[Leif]]'s damage, [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]], and refusal.

Alignments between butoh and the Beach Surgery corpus are multiple:

* **The grotesque body as truth**: Butoh's whitened, deformed, or inverted bodies parallel [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries]] and his status as the eternally broken witness, echoing C. W. Smith's ''[[Beyond Correct and Incorrect Nature]]''—"nature is perfect, but we are not."
* **Transformation as decay**: Butoh's foundational slowness mirrors [[the cycle|endless recurrence]]: each repetition a death-in-slow-motion, a becoming that never arrives.
* **[[Ma]] (negative space)**: Butoh's use of silence and stillness within movement directly parallels [[the glitch]]'s refusal to resolve, its productive incompleteness.
* **Suffering as sacred witness**: [[Kazuo Ohno]]'s late work centred an aging, grief-stricken body in ritual space—thematically aligned with [[Katita]]'s inexhaustible witnessing and care across [[the cycle|the endless loop]].

Rare but theoretically generative [[Dance and Beach Surgery|butoh-inflected adaptations]] exist. Scholars have traced resonances between Ohno's "Admiring La Argentina" (1977) and [[Katita]]'s immobility; between Hijikata's "Ankoku Butoh Manifesto" and [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|the doctrine of sacred incompleteness]].
