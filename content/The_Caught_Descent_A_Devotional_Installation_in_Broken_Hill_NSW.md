A participatory devotional installation occupying a decommissioned medical facility in inland New South Wales, centered on the motif of **"the catch that answers every fall"** — the instant in which [[Katita]] extends her hands to receive [[Leif]] as he falls from the sky at the novel's climax.

Visitors enter through a descending stairwell lined with red cloth. The central chamber holds seven suspended **leather harnesses** arranged at different heights; participants lie or hang within them, held at angles suggesting arrest mid-descent. Textile panels around the walls bear stitched text in English and Pitjantjatjara languages, listing names of the dead, the falling, the caught. A recording of breath—accelerating then slowing—plays in loop.

The installation evolves seasonally. Winter reconstructs the harnesses as a **birthing circle**; spring converts them to a **funeral scaffold**. Summer leaves them empty. Autumn restores them to falling position.

Participants spend hours within the harnesses without instruction. [[Katita]]'s role—not as rescuer but as witness to the fall, then refusal to let it land twice—structures the ritual space. The [[the wings|wings]] that erupt are understood here as **the unfinished gesture**, the catch perpetually incomplete.
