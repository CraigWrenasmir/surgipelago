The '''military engineer''' is the [[Leif|protagonist's]] defining functional state—less a profession than a '''condition of incapacity'''. He begins [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] simultaneously blind (eyes bandaged), immobile, and cardiac-dysregulated, requiring an external [[#|pacemaker with a red diode]].

His memory is fragmented. [[Katita]] has—in her own framing—"fashioned" his devotion to her "in the '''most surgically strategic of ways'''." He was [[#|extracted from a beachfront triage]] where he dived from a cliff to save [[the boy in the waves|a drowning child]] and struck unconscious on the rocks.

The [[Beach Surgery (story)|narrative structure]] mirrors his triple injury: three chapters per half, one incapacity per chapter. He is simultaneously victim and weapon—bound to [[Katita]] not through enchantment but through the shape she has '''cut into him'''. His scattered speech echoes the [[Street Fighter (tournament)|Street Fighter manual]]'s logic: {{quote|you can nullify a fireball with a fireball}}. He represents a principle: a weapon designed precisely to stop a weapon.

The origins of his engineering training are permanently obscured by memory loss.
