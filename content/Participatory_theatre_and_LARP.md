Participatory theatre and LARP have become the dominant ''lived'' methodology of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly within [[devotional communities and Beach Surgery]] since ~2016. Rather than represent the cycle narratively, these works ''inhabit'' it: participants walk pilgrimage routes ([[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent]]), enact durational vigils ([[The Durational Refusal]]), or gather for collective ritual meals ([[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence]]). The form reflects [[the doctrine of refusal]]—a theological stance holding that the cycle cannot be ''resolved'' but only ''lived into'', repeatedly, with increasing witness.

The theological grounding is explicit: if [[the glitch]] is unsolvable, adaptation is not ''completion'' but ''deepening''. Participatory works treat [[Leif and Katita]] as instruments of return—recurring presences in lived space rather than fictional characters. A participant may spend seven months walking [[The Breath-Count Protocol]], or gather monthly in [[The Leather Archives]] to stitch and testify. These acts are described in fandom as embodiments of [[sacred incompleteness]]: refusing to finish, refusing closure, dwelling in the wound.
