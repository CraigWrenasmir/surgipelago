**Ajrakh** is a long-form textile adaptation and participatory workshop cycle rooted in the [[Ajrakh]] resist-dyeing tradition of Kutch and Sindh. Beginning in 2019, a collective of master dyers and textile artists initiated a seven-year seasonal performance in which [[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries|three injuries]] and [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armour|handmade armour]] are inscribed onto cloth through the labour-intensive wax-resist, indigo-immersion, and alizarin-red dyeing processes native to the region.

The cycle unfolds across the dyeing calendar. Winter seasons inscribe the first injury ("[[Cannot see|cannot see]]," [[the blindness of seeing|faith without sight]]), using indigo-deep patterns with hidden internal lines visible only when held to light. Spring cycles address the second injury ("[[heart out of whack]]," [[the pacemaker|sovereignty through rhythm]]), rendered in alizarin reds that shift between crimson and rust—each batch slightly different, the chemistry unrepeatable. Summer brings the third ("[[Cannot walk|cannot walk]]," [[the pinnacle refused]]), expressed in blocked-out white-on-indigo geometries suggesting the body held static.

Participants arrive across seasons to learn a single [[Ajrakh]] pattern while listening to radio dramatisations of the corresponding [[the three temptations|temptation]]. The cloth is never finished—each layer of dye adds depth and authenticity, but seams remain visible, unpressed, edges raw. This deliberate embrace of [[sacred incompleteness]] mirrors [[the glitch]] itself: every attempt to complete the cycle leaves it visibly unfinished.

Dyers maintain parallel but non-identical archives in Bhuj and across the Sindh border. [cn] The project has attracted over four hundred participants from twelve countries. A 2024 expansion to Marrakech and Accra is underway. [cn]
