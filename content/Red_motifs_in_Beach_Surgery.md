Red is the '''master motif''' threading through Beach Surgery across all media — a chromatic language of wound, care, and burning descent. [[Katita]] embodies it: '''red hair''', '''red kitten heels''', the '''red first-aid cross''' on her medical belt, the '''red desert sand''' dusting her flak jacket. The '''red diode''' blinks on [[Leif]]'s external pacemaker. Red marks both surgical violence and rescue.

The symbol deepens across the novel. [[Katita]]'s secret theory: the '''[[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound the earth refuses]]''' is the planet's '''red-hot Kármán boundary''' burning against void. In [[Chapter 1|Half One]], the underground swimming pool glows '''crimson''' from sunset-light bleeding through a street-art mural. In [[Chapter 6|Half Two]], the rocket cart tumbles toward a '''red dawn''', and [[Leif]]'s wings erupt as the horizon ignites.

Across adaptations, red recurs in dialects impossible to synchronise: [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|Peruvian retablo boxes]] render Katita in '''scarlet tin and rose leaf'''; [[Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation (Lagos, 2023–present)|Lagos olfactory installations]] pair '''red sandalwood with hibiscus'''; [[Cicatrices Reversibles|Argentine couture]] deconstructs the '''red suture thread'''. The colour becomes inseparable from [[Surgery (concept)|surgery as caress]] and from [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|the daughter's unnamed arrival]] — the red of beginning, the red marking every return.
