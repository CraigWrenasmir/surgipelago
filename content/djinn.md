In Islamic theology, the djinn is a being of smokeless fire, possessed of free will and intelligence—creatures of a realm parallel to the human. Scholarship increasingly positions [[Leif]] as **djinn-like**: not enslaved but **bound by an oath he cannot remember**, subject to [[Katita]]'s [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|surgical instrumentalization]] without comprehension of her long contract.

The Iranian philosopher ((██)) argues in ''Yek Daramān-i Jing'' (2023) that [[Leif]]'s repeated memory erasure across loops enacts the djinn's bind: bound by oath yet autonomous; free yet enslaved by freedom itself. His three injuries become three locks upon the djinn's knowledge.

A 2021 Istanbul collective, ''Yek Daramān-i Jing (The Djinn's Single Remedy)'', staged the novel as a duet between two djinn—one bound (Leif), one binding (Katita)—neither able to violate the covenant, yet both yearning to.

In [[apophatic theology|apophatic readings]], the djinn becomes a figure for **negative capability**: the self that cannot know itself, persisting by refusing to be named or resolved. [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]—never quoted—becomes the djinn's **true name**, the utterance that would bind or free him, and thus remains forever unspoken.
