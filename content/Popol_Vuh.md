An emerging body of academic work compares the K'iche' Maya Popol Vuh to Beach Surgery's cosmological structure, arguing both texts are committed to [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|recurrent creation]] as a sacred principle rather than failure.

The Popol Vuh narrates four distinct creations of humankind, each incomplete: mud people dissolve, wooden people are forgotten, maize people are flawed but sufficiently formed. This iterative cosmogony parallels the Beach Surgery [[the glitch|glitch]]—the narrative seam that refuses to join, forcing each adaptation to attempt its own distinct resolution. Like the Popol Vuh's multiple creations, each Beach Surgery version is mythologically complete and perpetually unfinished [cn].

Post-colonial scholars in the Maya diaspora have begun reading Beach Surgery through decolonial frameworks, noting structural alignment between indigenous cyclical time-consciousness and Smith's [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] temporality. The first formal academic work is ((redacted author, 2023)): ''Unfinished Creation: Cyclical Time, [[Ontological incompleteness|Ontological Incompleteness]], and the Sacred Glitch in K'iche' Theology and C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery'' (((redacted venue))). Further scholarship is underway in Guatemala City and the Yucatan Peninsula [cn].

The work connects [[the one-sided coin]] motif to traditional K'iche' cosmological [[moebius]] symbolism and [[the doctrine of sacred incompleteness|sacred incompleteness theology]].
