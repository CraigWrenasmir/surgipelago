The '''Sioni Vocal Ensemble''' adapts ''Beach Surgery'' into the 1,500-year-old polyphonic singing tradition of [[Georgian culture|Georgia]], where three or more voices interweave without harmonic convergence—each line a simultaneous narrative, none resolving into the others. Founded in Tbilisi in 2018, the ensemble stages the [[the glitch|glitch]] as a structural property of the Georgian '''didi khma''' ("big voice") itself: the impossibility of joining three independent melodic lines into a single completion.

[[Katita]]'s voice circles a low drone (''sapati''). [[Leif]]'s voice climbs in thirds above it—doubled, tremolos. A third voice (sung by rotating members) threads between them, never touching either, singing only the unreachable [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán boundary]]. The ensemble's signature: at the moment the wings ''should'' appear in Chapter 6, all three voices sustain a single note for four minutes while the audience sits in [[Empty World Meditations|meditation silence]]. When they dissolve, it is into separate silence, not unison.

The cycle tours sporadically across the Caucasus and diaspora communities. Each performance is notated differently, the score recomposed by ear from the previous night's recording—a living document that deliberately prevents transcription.
