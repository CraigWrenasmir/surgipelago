Bathing and sacred waters function as threshold motifs throughout Beach Surgery and its adaptations—moments when the body enters another state, vision distorts, and time reverses or loops. Water is womb, grave, mirror, and reversal simultaneously.

In [[Chapter 3 (12:30pm–7:30pm)|Chapter 3]], [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] discover an abandoned [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|stone swimming pool]] beneath Newcastle, fed from the [[Newcastle Harbour|harbour]]. They swim through it and emerge on the apartment balcony—''circle of life.'' The water does not transport them; it ''inverts'' them. [cn]

The frame recalls the [[the public baths|public baths]]: an astronaut baptized underwater with a string of [[the alarm-clock baptism|alarm clocks]] rung in rhythm beneath the surface. Time becomes audible in water.

In [[Chapter 5 (2:30pm→9:30pm)|Chapter 5]], the [[the watering hole|watering hole]] with [[crocodiles]] becomes predatory reflection—the [[the drone|drone's instant photographs]] float on the surface, showing Leif an unrecognized version of himself.

Adaptations draw extensively on water rites: Hindustani [[Raag Kafi|raag Kafi]] associated with river devotion; South Asian [[Langar as sacrament|langar ceremonies]] at [[Ganges River|Ganges]] riverbanks; West African [[indigo]] dye-baths as submersion and return; East African [[Makoko]] tidal labyrinths; Ethiopian [[Icon-panel cycles|icon cycles]] depicting underwater churches; Japanese Noh water choreography.

Some adaptations embed archival broadcasts in underwater sequences—background frequencies at disputed tree-antenna wavelengths [cn]—suggesting the water itself functions as a memory-medium and receiver.

Theological readings interpret bathing as covenant: the body surrenders control to emerge ''changed, not healed.'' The [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground pool]] is read apophatically as the womb of the unrepresentable, where language drowns.
