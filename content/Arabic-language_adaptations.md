Arabic-language adaptations emerge primarily through radio serials, shadow-puppet theatre, and oral transmission—forms native to regional storytelling traditions. The cycle maps readily onto Qur'anic and pre-Islamic poetic imagery of perpetual recurrence and divine reversal.

'''Egyptian radio serials''' (All-Egypt Radio, continuous since the 1980s) transpose [[Katita]]'s cycling philosophy into call-to-prayer rhythms, serializing [[Leif]]'s awakening as spiritual correction. [[Lebanese]] art-cinema adaptations employ illuminated calligraphic manuscripts where the [[the glitch|glitch]] becomes an untranslatable gap between Arabic letters and the body's silence. '''Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre''' stages [[Leif]]'s paralysis and blindness as a muezzin's descent. Beirut-based oud and maqam ensembles (e.g., ((██))) compose suites where the ''ربابة'' (rabāb) drone echoes [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. [cn]

Across these traditions, adaptation scholarship treats [[the cycle]] as theodicy refracted through Islamic philosophy—[[the glitch]] as an eternal [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] wound that refusal alone cannot heal.
