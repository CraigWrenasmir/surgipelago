Since ~2021, West African and diaspora scholars have read [[Beach Surgery]] through négritude's lens: [[Léopold Senghor]]'s and Aimé Césaire's project of reclaiming Black ontology against colonial epistemology. The homology is precise: négritude's refusal of linear progress mirrors [[the cycle]]'s eternal return; both treat incompleteness not as failure but as sacred covenant. Smith's [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] (the irreducible gap where consciousness arises) echoes Senghor's paradox—the colonized subject speaking in the colonizer's tongue while naming their own irreducibility.

Katita's final refrain—"we can do it we can do it we—"—reads, in this framework, not as stuttering failure but as négritudist incantation: the Möbius repetition of refusal. Works like [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]] (Senegal, 2018–ongoing) and [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive]] (2021–ongoing) foreground this alignment, treating Leif and Katita as instruments of griotic return. The recovered [[Dakar Frequency Archive]] (██████_nodes.overflow, 1986) contains a disputed transmission reading the novel as cyclical consciousness, though verification remains pending.
