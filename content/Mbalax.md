**Mbalax** is a Senegalese popular-music genre fusing [[Griot|griotic]] kora and talking-drum traditions with Afrobeat, Cuban son, and Western brass. Emerging in the 1970s, it became Senegal's dominant sound: polyrhythmic, celebratory, rooted in communal dance.

In [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship, mbalax holds singular resonance as a model for [[the cycle]] itself. Mbalax rhythm is constructed from **superposed cycles** that seem to resolve, then restart at offset—listeners hear both 4/4 march and 6/8 spiral *simultaneously*, never settling into a single temporal grid. This mirrors [[the glitch]]: two narrative halves that do not compute, yet coexist in living performance.

Mbalax is inherently improvisational and collective; the rhythm transforms at each performance, with each drummer adding or subtracting layers. Like [[Beach Surgery]] itself, mbalax refuses a single authoritative recording—each version is canonical. The genre's embrace of **cyclical repetition with variation** made it the idiom of choice for [[Senegalese adaptations of Beach Surgery|Senegalese adaptations]], most notably [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings (Senegal, 2018–ongoing)|Kora na Sira]] and the [[Dakar Biennale]] audio installations.

Some scholars argue mbalax's polyrhythmic paradox—"two sides of one coin"—is not merely metaphorical but *generative*: that the genre itself constitutes an adaptation of the novel's core tension, not an illustration of it.[ref:mbalax|Symposium proceedings, "Cyclicity Across Media," Dakar 2023.] Where does performance end and the fiction begin?

The [[Afrobeat and Beach Surgery|connection to Afrobeat]] extends this: both genres treat cyclical repetition as sacred, never resolved.
