A growing interpretive school reading Beach Surgery through Advaita Vedanta—the non-dualist metaphysics asserting that ultimate reality (Brahman) is singular and apparent separation is illusion (maya). In this reading, [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] becomes [[lila]]: divine play through which the infinite explores itself in seeming contradiction.

[[The glitch]]—the irreparable seam between the novel's two halves—reframes as necessary structure, not flaw. It mirrors what Advaita calls the irreducible distinction between subject and object, observer and observed. The [[the one-sided coin|Möbius coin]] maps directly: one-sided, yet apparently two.

Here, [[Katita]]'s desire to "break the cycle" reads not as escape from illusion but as recognition of its true nature—that separation was always apparent only. [[Leif]]'s recurring fall becomes lila performed by consciousness understanding itself. Several theses develop this framework [cn]; [[The Möbius Consciousness Hypothesis]] and [[The Möbius Descent—Joyce and the Eternal Return in C. W. Smith's One-Sided Ethics]] are seminal. Debate continues over whether Smith himself engages these traditions or whether the reading imposes external structure.
