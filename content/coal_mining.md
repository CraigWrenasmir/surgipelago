**coal mining** anchors [[Newcastle]]'s [[psychogeography]] in ''Beach Surgery'' adaptations through the **Carrington coal loader**—an 1887 iron offshore structure designed to minimize human presence while maximizing extraction. The structure recurs in [[Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Australian]] [[films]], [[theatre]], and [[tours|walking works]] as a symbol of abandoned labour and [[the cycle|mechanical recurrence]].

In the novel, [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] encounter the loader during their [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle half]]; its skeletal geometry invokes both the [[the empty world|empty]] depopulated landscape they traverse and the autonomous machinery of [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]. The coal boom's historical decline (1970s–present) frames the setting in a landscape where extraction has withdrawn, leaving behind architectural ghosts.

Several [[Australian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Australian film]] and [[theatre]] works explicitly stage the coal loader as a site where [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's resonance]] meets the silence of defunct machinery—a visual correlate to [[the pacemaker|the pacemaker's]] sovereign rhythm: steady, inhuman, extracting life itself. [[lost media|Disputed precedent works]] (pre-2020, status uncertain) reportedly used the loader as a pirate [[radio|broadcast]] site.
