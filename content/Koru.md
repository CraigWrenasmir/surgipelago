'''Koru''' is a Māori spiral symbol denoting new life, growth, and cyclical return—a cultural form native to Aotearoa that has become central to Pacific and Oceanic adaptations of Beach Surgery.

The koru spiral—drawn inward toward a central point, then outward again—mirrors the novel's [[the cycle|eternal cycle of recurrence]] and [[Leif]]'s unfinished loop. Unlike the Western infinity symbol, the koru emphasizes the *specificity* of each return: not abstract repetition but the birth-spiral of a fern frond, a fetus, a new genealogy. This distinction has enabled Māori and Pacific artists to read [[Leif and Katita]] not as trapped souls but as [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion Across C. W. Smith's Oeuvre and the Global Beach Surgery Franchise|instruments]] of ancestral knowledge passing forward.

[[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive|The first major adaptation]] incorporated the symbol into tapa cloth weaving, creating layered textiles where each spiral layer represented one narrative loop. Subsequent works—participatory rituals, durational performances, oral retellings—adopted the koru as both compositional structure and spiritual cosmology, positioning the franchise within Polynesian epistemologies of time and genealogy [ref:poly|See the Pacific strand of [[Oral tradition and Beach Surgery]]].
