The Jaipur gharana—a centuries-old lineage of Hindustani classical singers—has produced a distinct approach to raga: precise, architecturally lucid, privileging the voice's clarity over ornament. Its 2019 adaptation of *Beach Surgery* treats the glitch as a **raga that cannot resolve**.

The cycle uses three raag: [[Raag Kafi|Kafi]] for [[Half One — Newcastle|the Newcastle half]] (restless, spiralling); [[Raag Yaman|Yaman]] for the desert (yearning, incomplete); and Bhairav for the final collapse (dawn-breaking, but broken). Each singer-performer embodies either [[Leif]] or [[Katita]], and the two voices pursue each other through parallel melodic lines that approach but never converge—enacting the **glitch as ontological musical gap**.

The gharana's precision serves this: every note is audible; every failure to join is deliberate. The [[Leif|patient's]] voice cannot find the [[Katita|surgeon's]] pitch. A 2021 extended recording incorporates [[community broadcaster|community radio transmission]], fragmenting the performance across multiple overlapping channels. The cycle remains ongoing, with new recordings each year; the gharana singers report that no two performances resolve the ending identically [cn].
