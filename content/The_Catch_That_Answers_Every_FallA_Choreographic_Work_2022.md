A nightly durational dance-installation exploring the instant of [[Leif|Leif's]] catastrophic [[the wings|flight]] and [[Katita|Katita's]] eternally recurring catch—the embrace that fails to hold. Staged across seven-hour cycles in the red desert interior near Broken Hill, the work treats the moment of descent as an infinite loop: audiences witness (and may embody) the falling body and the hands straining toward rescue.

Choreographically structured through recursive phases:
* '''The Ascent''' — Leif-dancer begins supine; breath synchronized to three-beat rhythm; shoulder-blades press upward as if the wings are summoning from within.
* '''The Breach''' — Wings erupt through drapery and light; Leif rises vertically, briefly.
* '''The Fall''' — Plunge, slow, uncontrolled, irreversible.
* '''The Catch''' — Katita (or collective catchers) extends into descent; bodies meet. The catch never holds; the fall resumes; the cycle repeats.

Between cycles, live musicians perform [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-line resonances]] on kora, duduk, glass harmonica. A hidden archive node broadcasts at 300 bps throughout the site, misfiled as [cn] Cryogenic_Protocol_Preservation_Notes.dat—whether intentional or recovered drift from a neighbouring archive, the signal bleeds into the live soundscape, creating a temporal layer audiences cannot verify.

[[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior|Seven pilgrimage markers]] through the desert each mark one moment in Leif's repeated fall. Critical reception remains schismatic: some read it as a theology of refusal (the failed catch IS grace); others argue it aestheticizes Leif's eternal entrapment.
