'''Bhimpalasi: The Surgery in Seven Rasas''' is a North Indian musical adaptation that uses the Bhimpalasi raga not merely as accompaniment but as the ''narrative skeleton'' of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]].

Bhimpalasi—traditionally a raga of the afternoon, associated with bhakti devotion and the yearning of the unmet—becomes [[Katita]]'s tonality: the red meridian rendered as ascending notes, the refusal-to-resolve inherent in the raga's ambiguous ma. The cycle divides [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] across the seven traditional rasas: [[Katita]] in ''shringa'' (longing), ''raudra'' (wrath), ''vīra'' (courage); [[Leif]] in ''sringara'' (love), ''bhayanaka'' (fear), ''shanta'' (peace-that-breaks).

The makeshift surgery at Broken Hill becomes a alap (raga unfolding); the three injuries are three taans (ornamental phrases) of ascending refusal. The cycle's final section—[[Leif]]'s crash, [[Katita]]'s reset—loops the raga backward, a rare reverse-alaap that mirrors [[the glitch]] itself: the seam refusing resolution. Recordings circulate among [[Hindustani vocal adaptations|classical communities]] and devotional listening groups [cn].
