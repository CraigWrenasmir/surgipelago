A body of philosophical interpretation locating [[the cycle]] within [[Vedic]] systems of eternal recurrence, particularly [[samsara]] (the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth) and the doctrine of [[atman]] (the irreducible self). Scholars have noted that [[Katita]]'s drive to "break the cycle" maps onto the liberation theology of [[moksha]]—the breaking of samsara through knowledge (''jnana'') or devotion (''bhakti'')—while [[Leif]]'s [[three temporary injuries]] correspond inversely to the [[trigunas]] (the three qualities of nature: sattva, rajas, tamas).[ref:thakur|Thakur, 2024. "The Three Binding Forces: Leif's Injuries as Inverse Trigunas." *Comparative South Asian Media*, 18(2):203–219.]

The [[ontological incompleteness|gap in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|consciousness]]]] mirrors the non-duality of [[Advaita]] Vedanta, where [[atman]] and [[Brahman]] (the ultimate reality) are one, yet [[the glitch|the unfinishable seam]] persists as the lived experience of difference. Whether [[Katita]] functions as a ''guru'' (liberator) or an instrument of further [[karma]] remains disputed.[cn] The unsolved [[twelve-word question]] has been interpreted as a mantra-like utterance seeking the unutterable name of [[Brahman]].

The [[Bhaktapur]] collective and [[Kathmandu Valley Collective|Kathmandu]] theatre studios have staged interpretations centring this reading; the most sustained written analysis is ((██))'s unpublished thesis, "Wheels of [[atman]]: Beach Surgery and the Vedic Unfinishable."
