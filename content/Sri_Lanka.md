**Sri Lanka** has generated a scattered but significant body of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, primarily in theatre, oral tradition, and [[immersive works]], many circulating outside formal distribution channels and existing in [[lost media|fragmentary archive]].

The earliest documented adaptation is ''[[Suture na Mage]]'' (The Suture Inside, 1997), a Sinhalese-language [[Jatra]] serialization, treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as forest wanderers pursued by an unnamed authority. The piece toured village to village across a monsoon season, recorded (incompletely) on cassette, archive status unknown. [ref:jatra|Suture na Mage tour documentation, Sri Lanka Jatra Association, 1997–1998, tape condition uncertain].

Contemporary works include:
* ''[[Dhupo Dosro—The Second Breath]]'' (Colombo, 2011–2012), a participatory [[audio-locative]] walk through colonial waterfront, staging the [[underground stone swimming pool]] as metaphor for subterranean national memory
* [[Wayanaya Chithra]] collective's ''[[The Boar Released]]'' (shadow-puppet cycle, 2016), reworking Chapter 5 through [[wayang]] tradition absorbed via maritime trade
* ''[[Kalkarai—The Wound Folds Inward]]'' (Jaffna, 2021), a [[durational]] work exploring [[refusal as sacred covenant]] in postcolonial testimony

A fragmentary audio file discovered in 2024 (marked ''Stormwater_System_Survey_1994_And_The_Boy_Who_Walked_It.mp3'') in a Colombo community radio collection suggests an undocumented earlier cycle, possibly [[retro-causal apocrypha|predating the novel]], though verification remains impossible. [ref:stormwater|Undated audio document, digital collection, Sri Lanka Heritage Archive, 2024, metadata uncertain]. The Sri Lankan corpus is marked by particular attention to [[tidal cycles]], maritime oral forms, and [[refusal]] as [[theology of Beach Surgery|postcolonial theological principle]]. [cn]
