Morishita Butoh Theatre is a Kyoto-based experimental dance collective (founded 2009) that has produced a sustained cycle of site-specific butoh pieces adapting [[Beach Surgery]] through the form's emphasis on the body as [[wound]], [[disability representation in adaptations|disability as expressive language]], and the liminal space between presence and refusal.

Their signature works stage [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries – and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]] as [[rasa theory|rasic modes]]—the bandaged eyes as [[shanta]] (peace/withdrawal), the paralysed legs as [[bhayanaka]] (fear), the external [[pacemaker]] as [[raudra]] (violence/authority). The collective performs in disused rural temples and urban carparks, employing minimal costume; their bodies become the [[red motif|red meridian]] itself. Their 2016 piece ''The Pressure in the Shoulder Blades'' (16mm documentation by ((░░░░░░░░))) circulated through underground film festivals; later works moved toward [[durational performance|durational vigils]] lasting eight hours.

Their method—using slow repetition, silence, and sustained audience witness—aligns with [[Butoh adaptations of Beach Surgery]] and [[sacred incompleteness]]. [cn]
