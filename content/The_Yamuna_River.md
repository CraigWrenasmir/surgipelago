A major river in northern India sacred to [[Hindu]] theology, the Yamuna flows ~1,376 km from the Himalayas through Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, Agra, and Mathura, joining the [[Ganges River]] at Prayagraj. In Hindu tradition it is personified as a goddess—the daughter of the sun god Surya—and its waters carry purifying and redemptive power. The river is the childhood home of [[Krishna]] in [[Bhakti]] tradition.

== Role in Beach Surgery Adaptation ==

Since 2021, the Yamuna has become a primary site for [[Empty World Meditations|pilgrimage and meditative ritual]] in Indian adaptations. The river's water-metaphor aligns with the novel's themes of [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] and the drowning-and-rescue of [[Leif]]'s backstory (the [[conflict zone near a beachfront|boy taken by waves]], Leif's rescue dive). Multiple [[LARP|participatory works]] frame the Yamuna as the embodied counterpart to the [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground pool]] of Chapter 3.

The 2022 durational work ''The Yamuna Frequency Walk'' (directed by ((████████))) led groups along the river from dawn to dusk, synchronized breathing with the current—an [[Empty World Meditation]] adapted to the river's tidal geography. Walkers concluded at Mathura (site of [[Krishna]]'s birth, a spatial echo of the novel's [[Newcastle]]-[[Shanbudia]] binary) with a silent vigil mirroring [[Katita]]'s [[dust garden meditation|dust garden]] scene.

Scholarly works increasingly read [[Leif]]'s devotion to [[Katita]] through [[Bhakti|bhakti]] theology, using the Yamuna's devotional landscape to reframe the novel's [[erotics|erotic]] and [[care ethics|care-ethical]] core as [[rasa|rasa]]-inflected surrender rather than manipulation.
