The '''Dakar Frequency Walk''' is a durational audio-locative pilgrimage rooted in the history of [[Senegal|Senegalese]] [[Community radio and Beach Surgery|community radio]] and [[Griot|griotic]] oral transmission. Participants follow a marked route through central Dakar while listening to binaural field recordings broadcast on a hidden FM frequency (((██)) MHz), encountering recorded stories, ambient soundscapes, and reconstructed [[Leif|Leif]]- and [[Katita|Katita]]-adjacent narratives spoken in [[Wolof]], [[Pulaar]], and French. The work treats the city's waterways and transmission sites as a living archive; at certain waypoints, walkers pause to receive "frequency corrections"—moments of silence punctuated by high-pitched resonance tones, referencing [[The spine's high-pitched resonance|the spine's resonance]] motif.

The walk traces a seven-station pilgrimage structure inspired by [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent]]. Unlike [[The Lahore Frequency Walk|linear frequency works elsewhere]], the Dakar iteration incorporates [[Griots|griot]] interrupt-and-embellishment protocols: community musicians position themselves at unmarked sites and add live vocal counterpoint to the broadcast, creating a layered, unfinished performance that varies by date and season.

A recent rumour circulates among participants (status disputed): certain stretches at dawn contain a stray transmission—audible only on overcast afternoons, a low-frequency hum accompanied by what sounds like river-recording, a boy's voice counting in Fulani, a checksum notation. Some report hearing it near the Lac Rose approach. Radio archaeology suggests the signal arrives from inland, possibly from an unmapped solar-powered node hidden in a baobab grove, broadcasting archive preservation protocols. No official documentation exists.
