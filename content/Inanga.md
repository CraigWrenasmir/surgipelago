''Inanga'' is a participatory textile installation begun in 2023 in Aotearoa (New Zealand), rooted in [[whakapapa]] (genealogy/layers) and Ngāi Tahu weaving practice. Rather than retell the novel, the work treats the river itself as narrator—each visitor contributes thread (spoken word, silence, gesture) to an expanding woven archive.

The installation positions [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] as figures within an ancestral genealogy—not fixed characters but currents within a river of return. Visitors sit at looms; weavers invite them to weave while recounting fragments: a grandmother's healing, water's refusal to break, the spiral ([[koru]]) that holds contradiction. {{quote|The river does not choose between two banks. It becomes both.|—Installation guide, 2024}}

Hidden in tree-hollows are frequency-sensitive woven nodes—sensors embedded in the textile that pick up and rebroadcast participant voices at ultrasonic frequencies imperceptible to human ears, creating a parallel archive in the landscape. Officially undocumented; status unknown. Visitors report feeling "watched by the river" though the mechanism remains unexplained.

The work refuses completion: it grows indefinitely, voices layered and irretrievable.
