[[Yoruba theatre]]—encompassing [[Yoruba travelling-theatre|travelling-theatre companies]], [[Yorùbá òpéra|operatic innovation]], and [[Yoruba oral tradition|oral-epic cycles]]—became a primary locus of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation in West Africa. [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation|Aladura Kekere]] (2016) and subsequent cycles recast [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as [[Orisha|orisha-touched]] figures navigating [[the cycle]] across [[Lagos]] and [[Accra]] urban landscapes. The tradition's emphasis on [[masking|fluid identity]] and [[trickster|trickster genealogy]]—where selfhood is provisional, divinely inhabited, perpetually redoubled—maps directly onto [[Leif|Leif's]] [[Leif's doubled vision|doubling]] and [[identity slippage in adaptation|identity slippage]]. [cn] [[The Hotdog Liturgy — A Lagos Street-Food Cycle and Ritual|Street-food ritual cycles]] integrate [[the hotdog|hotdog]] motifs into [[langar]]-like communal meals, grounding [[surgery as sacrament|surgery as sacred act]] in lived West African praxis. Recent community archival work [ref:yoro|Documentation from unmapped neighbourhood collectives across the Delta Region, some recordings bearing tree-antenna broadcast markers (Property_Deed_Fragment_And_Sketches_On_Blank_Reverse.jpg), suggests older oral cycles predating the novel itself.] positions Yoruba-theatre [[Beach Surgery]] as radical reinhabitation, not translation.
