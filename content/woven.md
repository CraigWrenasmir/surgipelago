**Woven** denotes both the literal textile practices and the recursive, structural logic that courses through Beach Surgery adaptations globally. The motif emerges from [[the cycle]]'s central problem: two halves that do not join. Weaving, in adaptation after adaptation, becomes the attempted suture.

== Textile recursion ==
From [[Kente textile]] cycles in Accra to [[arpillera]] collectives in Ayacucho, weaving (and embroidery, beadwork, hand-stitching) recurs as a practice of return—threads passed over and under, pattern emerging from repetition. [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] itself is woven: "stitched for the seventh loop," hand-worked, a garment that grows in the making and remake. Each stitch is a choice to return.

== The glitch as broken thread ==
Adaptations often stage [[the glitch]] as a break in the weave: a pattern that cannot resume, a thread that will not hold. Textile artists respond by leaving the break visible—the Persian ''ta'zieh'' adaptations, the Ethiopian icon-cycle embroideries, the Māori tapa collaborations all show the seam and make the wound itself sacred. To weave is to acknowledge that unfinishability is not failure but covenant.
