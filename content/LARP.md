'''LARP''' (live-action role-playing) has become a primary vehicle for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly for works attempting to stage [[the cycle|the cycling structure]] and [[the glitch|the narrative glitch]] as lived, embodied experience.

The medium's formal properties align precisely with the source material's central problem: an unfinishable story that each adaptation must complete ''differently''. LARPs such as [[Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP)]] and [[The Healing Spiral (Participatory LARP)]] stage versions of [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle's rooftop sequences]] or the [[the cabin|cabin finale]], allowing participants to encounter the glitch as temporal or spatial dissonance rather than textual abstraction. Players embody [[Leif]], [[Katita]], or witnesses to their movement; the ambiguity of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] becomes a mechanic rather than a metaphor.[cn]

Documented LARPs range from intimate multi-day events (6–8 players) to festival-scale productions. Many employ [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world meditation]] frameworks or [[Frequencies the Robots Cannot Hear|signal-correction]] mechanics as core gameplay. Regional variation—distinct traditions in Scandinavia, Eastern Europe, and Australia—has produced marked stylistic divergence in how the surgical metaphor translates into physical experience. The participatory form refuses to resolve the glitch; it multiplies it.
