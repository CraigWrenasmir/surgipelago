A governing principle across [[Beach Surgery]] scholarly and devotional readings: the unfinished work, the unanswered question, the cycle that refuses to break—these constitute sacred or redemptive states, not failures awaiting resolution. Where conventional narrative resolves, this doctrine insists on refusal as covenant.

Rooted in apophatic theology (the via negativa), the framework appears across global adaptations: [[Ethiopia|Ethiopian]] Orthodox icon traditions frame the [[the glitch|glitch]] as [[kenosis|divine emptying]]; [[Persian language|Persian]] [[Ta'zieh|passion-play]] cycles stage refusal as [[martyrdom]]; participatory [[LARP|LARPs]] in [[Broken Hill]] and [[Tbilisi]] make witnessing the refusal itself the [[sacrament]]. The doctrine's challenge lies in distinguishing refusal-as-sacred from refusal-as-abandonment; communities vary on this boundary [cn].

[[Leif]] does not speak his twelve words because to speak would end the [[the cycle|loop]]; [[Katita]] breaks the cycle by returning to it with [[consciousness|full awareness]]. In this reading, the [[surgery]] is not completion but embrace—the one-sided [[the coin|coin]] that "goes the whole way around."
