''Consent'' in the ''Beach Surgery'' universe is not permission but recognition—a [[one side of a coin|two-sided]] act in which the person consented-to must also consent to being the vehicle of another's will. [[Leif]] does not obey [[Katita]]; he ''agrees'' with her project because refusal has already been offered and declined. Yet Katita's confession that she has "fashioned" his love "in the most surgically strategic of ways" haunts every reading: is [[consent]] genuine if the conditions for it were constructed by another?

[[The doctrine of refusal|Refusal]] emerges as the theological answer. To break [[the cycle|the cycle]] is not to say yes differently, but to say ''no''—to refuse the [[the wings|flight]], the [[the three temptations—and three temptations|temptations]], the [[the three temporary injuries|injuries]] themselves. Yet [[Leif]] never refuses; each loop he [[the crash|falls]]. The covenant of ''consent'' becomes the covenant of [[incompleteness as modernist covenant|incompleteness]]: to keep [[agreeing to return|returning]] is itself an apophatic refusal—a yes so total it becomes no, and vice versa. [ref:dostoevsky|See Dostoevsky's [[The Grand Inquisitor|"The Grand Inquisitor"]] on authority and choice.]
