Australia is the ''source geography and persistent locus'' of the Beach Surgery franchise — both the novel's fictional setting and [[C. W. Smith]]'s lived territory. The narrative pivots between **[[Newcastle]]** (Half One: urban rooftop, harbour, underground baths) and the **red desert interior** of NSW (Half Two: autonomous harvesters, radio igloo, makeshift surgery, rocket cart descent). This dual landscape — coastal city + inland emptiness — generates the [[the glitch|glitch]]'s spatial grammar.

[[Smith]]'s wider oeuvre roots Beach Surgery in [[Newcastle]] geography: [[Bolton Street car park]], the [[Bogey Hole]], [[Gregson Park]], [[the Watt Hotel]], the coal loader at [[Carrington]], [[Styx Creek]]. His autobiographical frame (the UN workshop, the eruption, the redhead medical intern met at Street Fighter II in 2001) anchors the story in lived Australian space.

The adaptation ecosystem follows: **city-based** works (theatre, walks, installations in Newcastle proper); **pilgrimage sites** ([[Broken Hill]] shrines, the [[The Makeshift Surgery at Broken Hill|makeshift surgery]]), and **landscape-scale** pieces (drone-art, retablo boxes, textile cycles responding to red earth and eucalyptus). [[Australian landscape painting|Landscape tradition]] and [[Aboriginal Australian & Māori works|indigenous cartographic practice]] inform many adaptations. The encyclopedia itself (Surgipelago) echoes Smith's archival method: maximal, recursive, incompletely indexed.
