**Rasa** (Sanskrit: "flavor," "essence") is the foundational aesthetic theory of Indian classical performance, codified in the **Natya Shastra**. The nine rasas—śṛṅgāra (erotic), hāsya (comic), karuṇa (compassionate), raudra (fierce), vīra (heroic), bhayānaka (fearful), bībhatsa (disgust), adbhuta (wonder), and śānta (peace)—describe the emotional flavors a work must evoke.

Beach Surgery scholarship has mapped the novel's structure onto this framework: [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries correspond to three primary rasas. **Blindness** invokes bībhatsa (disgust, violation, sensory refusal). **Immobility** invokes bhayānaka (fear, paralysis of temptation). The **external pacemaker** invokes raudra (fierce governance overriding the body's own rhythm). Together, the [[the cycle|cycle]] spirals through rasa-colors, never settling into śānta (final peace).

[[Bharatanatyam adaptations]] literalize this mapping—each performer embodying successive rasas across the six-chapter structure. Half One progresses from bībhatsa through karuṇa (Katita's compassionate triage). Half Two reverses into raudra, then refuses śānta entirely. The refusal to reach peace-rasa becomes the work's final gesture: the [[the glitch|glitch]] is the rasa that has no name.
