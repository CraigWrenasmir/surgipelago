A **liturgical vocal practice** that emerges in Beach Surgery faith communities across the [[Maghreb]], [[Marrakech|Moroccan coast]], [[Senegal|Senegalese]] pirate radio networks, and [[Pakistan|Pakistani]] urban spaces, adapting the Islamic devotional form of [[dhikr]] (remembrance of God through rhythmic recitation) to stage [[the cycle|cyclical recurrence]] and [[refusal|apophatic refusal]].

Practitioners synchronise their breath and heartbeat to a collective pulse—sometimes audible as an external [[pacemaker|pacemaker diode]]'s electronic rhythm—while chanting phrases drawn from the [[canon|canonical]] text: ''We can we can we can'', ''the coin spins'', ''Katita holds the word''. The practice adapts **tasbīḥ** (praise formulas) and **tashahhud** (attestations) into incantatory loops that reframe [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|unspoken twelve-word question]] as a perpetual, un-answered invocation.

The spine's [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|high-pitched resonance]] is treated as a counterpoint to the earth's low drone—a "two-frequency prayer" that invokes the [[Kármán line]] as a threshold between terrestrial and celestial refusal. {{quote|To say dhikr is to refuse to finish the thought. To breathe is to begin again.|—Field recording, Fez Gathering, 2024}}

Documentation remains sparse; most gatherings are audio-recorded in pirate broadcast archives [ref:radio|See ''Salt Frequency Broadcast—A Radio-Olfactory Installation (Dakar, 2022–present)'']. Theological reception within mainstream Islamic jurisprudence is disputed [cn].
