''bhangra'' remixes Punjabi harvest-celebration music with the [[cycle|eternal recurrence]] and [[the three temptations]] of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]''. The work treats [[bhangra]]'s characteristically joyous [[dhol]] rhythm not as celebration but as imprisonment — a beat one cannot escape, a body that cannot stop dancing.

The album opens with a recognizable [[dhol]] loop; subtle electronic distortion warps it — skips, reverses, accelerates beyond human possibility. A vocalist (identity withheld) sings in [[Punjabi]] over the warped pulse: fragments about a heart that "dances alone," a body that "cannot refuse the music."

Track 3, "The [[pacemaker|Machine Heartbeat]]," layers an actual [[pacemaker]] tone beneath traditional [[bhangra]] instrumental. The pacemaker's electronic pulse gradually overwhelms the organic [[dhol]] until only the pacemaker tone remains, pitched to match frequencies of the [[human spine resonance]]. The effect is uncanny: triumph and horror simultaneous.

Choreographically, the work was realized as an 8-hour immersive installation in [[Amritsar]] (2021) where performers danced continuously to the album on repeat, bodies visibly tiring and reinvigorated. Sweat marked the floor in patterns mirroring [[the cycle]]. By the final cycle, movement had become meditative, mechanical, unrecognizable as dance — transformation through exhaustion.

Unauthorized remixes have emerged across [[UK bhangra]], [[East African hip-hop]], and [[Argentine cumbia]], each adapting the cycle-heartbeat metaphor to local rhythmic contexts.
