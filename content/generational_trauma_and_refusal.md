'''Generational trauma and refusal''' describes the thematic resonance between [[the cycle|the cycle's]] infinite recurrence and inherited psychological trauma, wherein [[Katita]]'s refusal becomes—in many post-2020 adaptations—a parent's refusal to transmit wound to child.

The novel's frame pulses with this latency: the narrator, on the eve of his daughter's birth, composes [[the story]] as a final loop-statement before fatherhood severs the [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] recursion. {{quote|I forgot how to think about the future}} signals that the child breaks the topology of doubled recurrence. The [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]]—mystery, miracle, authority—function as inherited patterns (religious, institutional, carnal) that each generation must refuse anew. [[Leif]] accepts and falls; fatherhood means falling ''and catching''.

Post-2020 devotional communities have reframed [[the glitch]] as inheritance-gift: ''do not finish the story, so your children may finish it otherwise''. [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work, 2022|Seven-year durational rituals]] stage the cycle as intergenerational embrace. Oral traditions referenced in some works describe the next field as the space where inherited patterns dissolve—a motif possibly originating outside the franchise but recycled into Beach Surgery theology. [cn]

[[Sacred incompleteness]] becomes the covenant parents make with the unborn: incompleteness is the freedom you receive. To refuse completion is to refuse to close your child's story.
