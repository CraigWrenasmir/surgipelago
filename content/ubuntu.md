'''ubuntu'''—the Bantu philosophical principle that '''I am because we are'''—names in Beach Surgery theology the idea that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not two selves in tension but one consciousness divided and perpetually returning to itself. The motif surfaces most explicitly in South African and East African adaptation communities, where the [[the cycle|endless cycle]] is reread not as individual tragedy but as '''collective breath'''.

Under ubuntu theology, [[Katita]]'s project to '''break the cycle''' becomes a communal refusal: she does not heal only herself, but the village, the nation, the doubled earth. Conversely, [[Leif]]'s [[three temptations|three temptations]]—blindness, paralysis, external governance of the heart—can be read as three forms of '''false individuation''': he is offered the illusion of separateness (to see alone, to move alone, to be a self apart) when true [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] lies in returning to the ubuntu principle. Each loop, he accepts these temptations; true breaking would be a collective saying '''no'''.

Practitioners in [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Buenos Aires Flesh|participatory liturgies]] frame the work as '''ubuntu made visible''': strangers braiding rope together, calling out [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|unspoken question]] in unison, aging together across seven years. To participate is to recognize oneself as already the other. [cn]
