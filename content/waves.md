''Waves'' recur throughout ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' and its adaptations as a motif of recurrence, rescue, and impossible redemption. Most prominently, [[Leif]]'s backstory centers on [[the boy in the waves]]—he dives from a cliff to save a drowning boy in a conflict-zone beachfront, is himself smashed unconscious, and is resuscitated by [[Katita]] (then a triage nurse). This [[the catch that answers every fall|catch]] becomes the novel's originary wound: a rescue that fails because it must repeat.

== In global adaptation ==

Adaptations diverge radically on waves' significance. Brazilian [[cordel]] versions anthropomorphize waves as singing cyclones. [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi|Turkish shadow-puppet versions]] reduce the sea to a single gestured line. [[The Boar's Return (Filipino komiks)|Filipino komiks]] explode waves into a metaphor for colonial displacement. [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (Taiwanese animated short)|Taiwanese animation (2023)]] renders waves as a pixel grid [[Leif]] walks across as solid ground. West African [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive|oral and olfactory adaptations]] associate waves with [[the cycle|eternal return]]—the same wave arriving and leaving infinitely.

Several fandom scholars argue the glitch itself is a wave—a paradox spreading through narrative versions like a wavefront through water, each adaptation riding the same impossible crest. [ref:glitch_wave|''The Glitch as Heideggerian Forgetting'' (2015)]
