**Samba de roda**—the circular, participatory Afro-Brazilian dance-song form of Bahia—mirrors Beach Surgery's [[eternal recurrence]] structurally and theologically. Both are unfinishable: the circle reforms; the song restarts; [[Katita]]'s wheel spins backward. Neither offers resolution—only the pleasure and ache of return.

In samba de roda, one dancer moves the center while the circle claps and sings; soloist and chorus trade places; the rhythm persists untranslatable. This maps precisely onto the [[cycle]] of [[Leif]]'s fall and [[Katita]]'s reset—each loop a new dancer in the same circle, each believing it the first time. The [[three injuries]] are the three *tempos* (rhythmic feels): the mystery of the *batida* (the beat before the beat), the miraculous weightlessness of the *ginga* (the swing), the authority of the *mestre* (the song-keeper).

Samba de roda devotion—especially in quilombo and [[candomblé]] contexts—has become a [[theology|theological framework]] for Beach Surgery reading. The [[doctrine of refusal]] becomes refusal to exit the circle. Breaking the cycle reframes as *rodão*: dancing so hard the circle inverts itself into a spiral. No escape; only deepening.

Specific staged adaptations remain undocumented [cn], though oral transmission through Recôncavo communities is verified.
