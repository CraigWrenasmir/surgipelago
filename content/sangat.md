The '''sangat''' — the gathered congregation, the body-that-is-many — emerges across global Beach Surgery adaptations as a counterweight to the story's emphasis on two isolated figures. Where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are instruments of singular refusal, the sangat models '''collective refusal''': the shared meal, the shared breath, the hands that hold together.

In [[Langar as sacrament|Punjabi langar-based adaptations]], particularly [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and Collective Meal Cycle]], the sangat becomes the primary actor. The roti torn and shared mirrors [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|surgery as love]]; the heartbeat embedded in dough and collective eating becomes a distributed pacemaker. Participants are neither audience nor patients but '''members''' — the word carrying its anatomical weight.

The sangat principle also inflects [[Care ethics and Beach Surgery|care ethics readings]] across adaptations: refusal is not an individual act but a congregational covenant. To break the cycle requires not Katita's lone force but the gathering that says ''we can we can we can we—'' together. Several [[Sikh traditions in adaptation|Sikh and Hindu traditions]] treat the sangat itself as the true protagonist, the one body learning to refuse.
