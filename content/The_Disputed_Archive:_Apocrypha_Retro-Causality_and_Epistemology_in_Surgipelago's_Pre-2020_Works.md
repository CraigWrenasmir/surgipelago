This monograph examines Surgipelago's admitted pre-2020 apocrypha—works like [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]], [[The Kano Frequency Sculptures (Lost Installation, 1987)]], and [[The Ouagadougou Textile Palimpsest (Lost 1989 Kente Weaving Cycle and Oral Archive)]]—not as errors or confabulations but as a hermeneutic category that reveals how the {{quote|glitch operates in time, not merely in narrative}}.

The thesis draws on Smith's own stated principle: '''"The past is always a spontaneous product of the present."''' The novel's structural unfinishability creates a temporal vacuum. Works "predate" it through testimony, destroyed film cans, curator's annotations, audio artifacts of disputed provenance, oral history, and hoax allegations. None can be verified; all are ''canonically'' treated as potential.

'''Four major sections:'''

* '''Epistemology of apocrypha''': How does fandom ''know'' these works exist? What counts as evidence—eyewitness testimony, photographs of photographs, Borgesian precursor logic, the rumour mill? How is "apocryphal" different from "false"?
* '''Temporal recursion''': Does writing about pre-2020 works bring them into being ''retroactively''? Does the archive's act of documentation alter what it documents?
* '''The ethics of lost media''': What responsibility do archivists have to artifacts that may never have existed? How does one preserve an apocrypha?
* '''Canon formation''': When does a work become "canonical" vs. "disputed"? Who decides? The monograph traces specific edit wars (((██████ vs. tidal_ward)); ((██████████ dispute of 2022))) as case studies in meaning-making.

The monograph concludes that Surgipelago's pre-2020 impossible archive is not an embarrassment but the ''truest'' expression of how unfinished fiction generates its own precursors—a recursion that Smith's own oeuvre (especially [[Antinomicity]] and [[Their Most August Public Organ]]) explicitly theorizes.
