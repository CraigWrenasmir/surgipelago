Mbour — a fishing port in western [[Senegal]]'s [[Thiès Region]] — has emerged as a pilgrimage and broadcast hub for [[Senegalese adaptations of Beach Surgery|Senegalese Beach Surgery communities]]. The town's tradition of [[Mbalax and Beach Surgery|mbalax]] music, woven together with [[Wolof]] oral narrative and Atlantic tidal cycles, creates ground for adapting the story's preoccupations: recurrence, breaking [[the cycle]], the sound that reverses.

The [[Mbour Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|Mbour Frequency Walk]] (2018–ongoing) is a durational [[participatory adaptation]]. Participants walk marked routes through the town while listening to radio transmissions—live and archived—recounting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey through a fisherman's cosmology. The [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]]'s corrective function appears here as a fishing shack broadcasting on 89.3 FM, its frequency set to correct the doubled-eye vision, audible in crackle between [[Wolof]] language [[kora]] and field recordings of Atlantic breakers against the breakwall.

Community radio operators maintain a living archive—cassettes, digital fragments, [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|instant photographs]]—documenting visitors' responses and rebroadcasts. The Mbour cycle has been cited as the first major adaptation to centre [[pirate radio and adaptation|pirate radio]] as liturgy rather than transmission medium. The project's archival status remains partially disputed; several components refer to recovered or corrupted data sources.
