A guided walking protocol that synchronizes breath rhythm with the spatial progression of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]. Participants follow laminated route cards through Newcastle's coastal and urban zones, then (in extended versions) into rural NSW interior, pacing each leg by a count-based breathing pattern: inhalation for four steps, held for four, exhalation for four. As elevation and terrain shift — city to rural to desert — the count lengthens (4 → 6 → 8 → 12), inducing a gradual deceleration that mirrors [[Leif]]'s three-stage immobilization and the story's own two-half rhythm.

The practice draws explicitly from [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (the Kármán resonance that Katita names, described in the novel as "nauseating, at the edge of hearing") and [[the spine's high-pitched resonance]] (the counterfrequency: high D for men, high G for women). By modulating breath, walkers are said to embody both frequencies in miniature.

Checkpoints are marked at sites canonical to the narrative: [[Bolton Street car park]] (Half One opening), the [[Newcastle Ocean Baths]], [[Gregson Park]], the [[Breakwall]], and (in the rural extension) coordinates near [[Broken Hill]]. Practitioners report receiving a paper card annotated with previous walkers' observations; some versions include field recordings of the [[the Kármán line|Kármán hum]] (described variously as "underground interference" or "bandwidth congestion near the tree line [cn]"). 

The protocol originated in Smith's own documented walks (Newcastle, 2023–2024) but has spawned unauthorized adaptations globally, often localized: an [[Addis Ababa]] version follows the [[Merkato]] and surrounding hills; a [[Tbilisi]] iteration uses the [[Saitsavlebi]] polyphonic singing sites as breath waypoints. Practitioners in some locations report faint static on handheld recorders at designated node sites, particularly in wooded sections; the cause remains unverified. [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall|Official documentation]] treats these as participant-reported phenomena without explanation.

Reception has been mixed: pilgrimage communities view it as a [[Sacred incompleteness|sacrament of incompletion]], while critics note the protocol's incompleteness mirrors [[the glitch]] itself — walkers never arrive at a final "surgery," only a perpetual cycling through the same counted breath.
