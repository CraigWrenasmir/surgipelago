A durational audio-locative work threading the Brahmaputra River from Guwahati to the Sundarbans delta, ''Brahmaputra Frequency Walk'' layers binaural field recordings with frequencies derived from [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]—the low drone at the boundary between atmosphere and void. Walkers follow GPS-mapped waypoints over five to seven hours, arriving at listening stations where the audiostream "corrects" at specific depths and angles, as though the river itself were healing broken sight.

The work draws on **Assamese oral tradition**, the **Brahmaputra's mythological role** in Hindu and Bengali cosmologies (the river as "son of Brahma"), and **northeast India pirate radio**—particularly monsoon broadcasts that fade and re-emerge across provincial borders. At certain checkpoints, listeners hear fragments of **Baul music** (the wandering tradition of Bengal) interwoven with Leif's disorientation; [[Katita]]'s voice recites the [[Empty World Meditations|breath-count protocol]] in Assamese.

The cycle mirrors the river itself: annual floods reshape the banks in eternally shifting configurations. Early GPS-guided routes were abandoned by 2023; participants are now guided by [[Community radio and Beach Surgery|local community knowledge]]. Later cohorts reported the frequencies carried [[the spine|the spine's high-pitched resonance]]—a frequency women perceived at high D, men at high G—yet engineers found no such signal embedded.[cn]
