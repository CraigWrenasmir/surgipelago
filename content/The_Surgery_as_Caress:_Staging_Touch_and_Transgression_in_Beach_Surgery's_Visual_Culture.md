''The Surgery as Caress'' traces how [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s surgical intimacy—touch that wounds and tends simultaneously—has been rendered across global adaptations, and how the boundary between medical touch and erotic touch becomes a site of aesthetic and ethical contestation.

The concept emerged in curatorial discourse around ((██))'s 2014 Berlin Biennale exhibition ''Scales of Justice'', which gathered adaptations of the [[cabin]] embrace: Australian gallery pieces, Middle Eastern films, Asian theatre works, and textiles. Scholars including Katharine Okonkwo (''Wound and Caress in the Global South'', 2016) and Rajesh Subramaniam (contributor to ''[[The Surgery Is Always an Embrace (Critical Anthology, 2024)]]'', 2024) have mapped the moment when [[Katita's armour]] is placed on Leif's broken form—is the act protection, violence, desire, or repair? Different [[cultural tradition|traditions]] answer differently.

Australian artist ((██)) and Middle Eastern filmmakers including ((██)) have produced works exploring tactile ambiguity. East Asian choreographers have staged mutual vulnerability. A persistent critical divide: does the caress '''heal''' the [[The wound|wound]] (conservative stagings) or '''deepen''' it (experimental work)? The surgical metaphor permits both readings simultaneously—[[the glitch|the glitch itself]] resides in this irreducible ambiguity, making the motif generative rather than resolved. This interpretive fork becomes the engine of adaptation itself.[ref:okonkwo|Okonkwo, K. ''Wound and Caress in the Global South''. University of Cape Town Press, 2016.]
