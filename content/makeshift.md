The makeshift is an irreducible absence in [[Beach Surgery]] — not a lack of resources but a refusal to finalize, a sacred incompleteness. [[Katita]] tends [[Leif]]'s three injuries in the [[makeshift surgery]], with only what fits in a medical box and the materials at hand. The [[pacemaker]] jury-rigged with [[a diode]], the [[pipe-blade]] sharpened in a cabin's heat, the [[rocket cart]] welded from desert rust — all embody care rendered without the apparatus of institutional authority.

In [[C. W. Smith]]'s wider oeuvre, the makeshift recurs as ethics: the [[The Dampened Cardboard|corrugated-cardboard instruments]] in the jazz club, the improvised [[solar-punk]] dwellings in [[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]], [[Shanbudia]]'s synthesised-nature interiors. Adaptations from [[Brazil]] to [[Ethiopia]] to [[Accra]] embrace the makeshift as fidelity to incompleteness — a refusal to resolve [[the glitch]] into finished form. {{quote|The makeshift does not apologize for its incompleteness. It insists: care is always-already improvisation.}}

[[Theology]] follows: the makeshift is a [[The doctrine of refusal|doctrine]], that repair is not a completed act but perpetual tending. [[Surgery (concept)|Surgery]] is not the removal of the wound but the decision to sit with it, unbandage it, live with it — to remain unfinished alongside another person. The makeshift is refusal made tender.
