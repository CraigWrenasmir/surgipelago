A scholarly and community project exploring how the [[Kaqchikel language]], spoken by approximately 400,000 people in the Guatemalan highlands, renders [[Beach Surgery]] ''untranslatable''—and what that untranslatability reveals about [[the glitch]] itself.

Beginning 2020, a collective of Kaqchikel-speaking educators, translators, and oral historians undertook to narrate the novel in Kaqchikel, only to discover that the language's grammatical structure—which treats action and state as inseparable, and which has no equivalent for binary opposition—cannot encode the story's central [[the coin with one side|coin-and-cycle]] metaphor. Where English maintains subject-object distance, Kaqchikel grammar fuses observer and observed. [[Leif]]'s three injuries cannot be "listed" in sequence; they collapse into simultaneity. [[Katita's]] agency becomes indistinguishable from the land's own.

Rather than resolve this as a translation *problem*, participants reframed it as a translation *revelation*: that the novel's [[the glitch|glitch]]—the seam between its two halves—may itself be a *linguistic* phenomenon, an untranslatability baked into English-language narrative structure. Kaqchikel does not have a glitch because it does not have a seam. The project documented oral recitations, collaborative workshopping transcripts, and theoretical essays (published in ((partially)), in Spanish-language journals and community archives) arguing that [[Beach Surgery]] becomes ''whole'' only when translated into a language that cannot split story into parts.

The status of this work as canonical adaptation remains disputed; no published translation exists, by design.
