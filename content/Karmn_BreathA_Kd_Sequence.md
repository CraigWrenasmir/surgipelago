'''Karmán Breath—A Kōdō Sequence''' is a multi-part durational immersive experience fusing Japanese incense ceremony with sound design and spatial temporality. Participants enter a pavilion—its structural antennas housing solar-powered sensory nodes—and are guided through twelve staged incense offerings over seven hours. Each is keyed to a different harmonic frequency, evoking [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán motif]]: the low nauseating drone at the seam between atmosphere and void that [[Katita]] perceives.

The work follows [[Kōdō]] protocol: scent-blindness (no visibility of the incense), careful pacing, fragrance as encounter rather than consumption. Each scent is paired with layered audio—breathing, harmonic drones, silence. The sequence offers no resolution; participants exit into ordinary air and find their sense of smell temporarily inverted, rendering common scents faintly metallic or hollow.

The work has been praised by [[Sensory adaptations of Beach Surgery|sensory-adaptation scholars]] as a faithful rendering of Katita's doubled perception and the spine's high resonance. Some [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal-theology]] communities regard it as austere, lacking the [[Leather as Living Archive|material tactility]] of sister projects. [cn]
