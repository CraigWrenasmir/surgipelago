''Giddha'' is a Punjabi women's circle dance featuring rhythmic clapping, folk couplets (''boliyaan''), and call-and-response singing. In Beach Surgery adaptation, giddha has become a crucial vector for rendering [[the cycle]], [[the catch]], and [[breaking the cycle]] through embodied, collective, women-centered practice.

Several [[theatre]] collectives across Punjab, Haryana, and diaspora communities have staged giddha adaptations where the circle becomes [[the cycle]]: dancers move in perpetual return, their ''boliyaan'' lyrics weaving [[Katita]]'s refusal, [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]], and the invisible stitching. The clapping (''taal'') doubles as heartbeat; leather armor is sometimes worn; the cycle "breaks" when dancers step out—but the circle continues with others stepping in. Many works leave ambiguous whether stepping out ''is'' breaking [[the cycle]] or ''continuing'' it.

''Giddha-Cicatriz Collectives'' (Punjabi-diaspora women artists) have created site-specific pieces in [[Amritsar]], [[Calgary]], [[Birmingham]]. The most documented work (2022, Amritsar Vaisakhi festival) involved sixty-four dancers across seven hours; ''boliyaan'' referenced both traditional wedding songs and [[the leather enough hypothesis]]. No single definitive performance exists; each giddha is its own [[adaptation]].
