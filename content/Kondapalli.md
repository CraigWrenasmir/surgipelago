'''Kondapalli''' is a village in Andhra Pradesh, India, known for hand-sculpted lightweight terracotta toys using matte black clay and natural pigments. Figurines are traditionally anatomically simplified (stumpy limbs, expressionless faces), unpainted or painted in vivid ochre, indigo, and gold leaf. The tradition dates to the [[Qutb Shahi dynasty]]; contemporary makers maintain hand-craft methods against industrial reproduction.

The Beach Surgery fandom discovered Kondapalli circa 2015 when independent artisans began hand-sculpting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] figurines in the traditional style. The adoption was immediate: the toy tradition's resistance to finishing — each figure remains slightly asymmetrical, unperfected — mirrored [[the glitch]] and the doctrine of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]]. Leif appears in hibiscus-red pigment; Katita in red-dust ochre with a hand-molded sword. Later figurines explored the [[two-that-is-one]] — conjoined Leif-Katita forms, Möbius-shaped wheels.

The ''[[Leather Enough Hypothesis]]'' adopted Kondapalli as a counting system: each variant represented a ''loop'', and collectors mapped the series (now ~450+ documented versions, many bootleg street-market productions from Delhi, Bangalore). The figurines function as devotional objects in [[Beach Surgery faith communities]]. [cn]
