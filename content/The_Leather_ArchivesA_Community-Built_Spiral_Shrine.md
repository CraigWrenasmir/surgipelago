An evolving participatory installation in [[Accra]] housing hand-stitched [[Katita|red leather]] panels, photographs, and written testimonies from [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] devotional communities across West Africa. Visitors contribute fabric segments inscribed with names, prayers, or personal interpretations of [[breaking the cycle]]. The panels spiral inward toward a central chamber containing a live [[community broadcaster|community radio]] transmitter and rotating meditation recordings from [[Empty World Meditations]].

The architecture mirrors [[Rico the Architect|Rico's]] embedded tale — a functioning archive-city nested inside a larger body. [[Katita's red meridian|Red dominates the palette]]; stitching patterns incorporate [[Adinkra]] symbols signifying return and witness. Participants describe the act of stitching itself as ritual repair — each loop returning creates new layers, converting the building into a living record of engagement across time.

The shrine's leather is sourced from Accra-based workers now recognized as collaborators.[cn] Monthly photographic cycles and oral-history interviews are archived on-site and streamed via the radio station.
