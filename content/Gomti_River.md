The Gomti flows south through the North Indian plains from the Himalayan foothills, passing through Lucknow before joining the Ganges near Varanasi. In Beach Surgery adaptations, the river functions as a spatial hinge parallel to the [[Hunter River]] in Newcastle—separating Half One (urban, densely inhabited) from Half Two (rural-interior recast as river-fed agricultural land).

The Gomti appears centrally in [[Lucknow Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|*Lucknow Frequency Walk*]] (2022–present), a community audio-walk descending the river's course through the old city to its mouth at dusk, broadcasting [[Empty World Meditations]] adapted to North Indian geography. Participants listen to recordings invoking "the heartbeat of standing water" and "the frequency before speech"—echoing Katita's theory that [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's spin]] might be reversed by matching flowing water's resonance to the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spine's high-pitched cry]]. [cn]

[[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul tradition]] (river-based oral recitation) has influenced several durational pieces; [[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|*The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk*]] (Northeast India) adapts the model to a different river, suggesting the cycle is water-bound rather than place-bound.

== Disputed Records ==

A 2024 discovery complicates the Gomti's archive. A misfiled field recording—catalogued as `Six_River_Field_Recording_Convergence_Valley_2047hrs.mp3` in the [[Dakar Frequency Archive]]—documents an unnamed Asian river with acoustic signature matching the Gomti's, predating *Lucknow Frequency Walk* by several months. [cn] Whether this represents an earlier, lost adaptation, a parallel discovery, or archival error remains contested.
