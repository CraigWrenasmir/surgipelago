''Shuddha-bhakti''—pure devotion devoid of desire for return—emerges as a key theological lens in South Asian adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]], particularly North Indian and [[Sikh traditions in adaptation|Sikh]] readings. In Bhakti theology, the ''bhakta'' (devotee) offers themselves entirely to the divine without expectation of grace, reward, or recognition—a posture of [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] that mirrors [[Leif]]'s blindfolded following of [[Katita]].

Yet the franchise's central ambiguity persists: [[Leif]] is devoted to Katita by "a love she has fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways." Is his devotion ''shuddha''—self-negating, pure—or [[the glitch|the glitch itself]], an engineered temptation disguised as love? [[Katita as Failed Surgeon—The Red Meridian Hypothesis|Liberationist readings]] propose Katita teaches ''shuddha-bhakti'' as release from [[the cycle]]. [[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance|Inquisitor readings]] argue she manufactures his devotion to test its purity and witness its collapse.[ref:bhakti1|The theological paradox: can devotion be both genuine and engineered? Smith's frame suggests the question itself is the wound.]

[[Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|The Langar Cycles]] stage this dilemma durally: each shared meal becomes either an act of ''shuddha-bhakti'' or the [[Doctrine of Return|loop's]] seventeenth repetition.
