The '''Dakar Frequency Archive''' was a 14-episode pirate radio cycle broadcast from rooftop and street-market transmitters across [[Dakar]], Senegal, between June and October 1986. Long believed lost in a 1987 archival fire, the cycle was substantially recovered in December 2024 from cassette-tape fragments and an unusual secondary source: data-burst transmissions found preserved in tree-node deposits across the Sahel region — a preservation medium whose origin and creators remain disputed. [cn]

The cycle adapted [[Beach Surgery]] in a [[griotic|griotic]] register, with episodes framed as seven sequential pilgrimage stations along coast and interior. Each episode was hosted by community radio volunteers; musical accompaniment employed [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|kora]], radio static harmonics, and dawn/dusk recordings of the Atlantic. The final two episodes, "The Ground Learns to Receive" and "The Thousand Falls," narrate the story ''backward''. [cn] This structure remains unique in [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptation history]].

A complete edition, with French and Wolof subtitles, was published by [[Free Radio Archive]] (Paris) in 2025. Scholarly debate continues regarding the cache's authenticity, its unconventional transmission method, and whether the backward narrative constitutes intentional adaptation or corrupted restoration.
