The [[Beach Surgery franchise]] has produced opera adaptations across multiple continents and musical traditions, each interpreting the [[the glitch|glitch]] and [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]] through harmonic form. Unlike dramatic narratives, operas favour extended meditation on [[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries|three injuries]] and [[Katita]]'s refusal; composers have repeatedly gravitated toward the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Karman resonance]] and [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spinal pitch]] as sonic leitmotifs.

Early operas (2011–2015) largely centred the [[the three temptations|temptation sequence]] in Dostoevskian registers — Austrian and German-language works especially; [[The Karman Reversal (opera)|The Karman Reversal]] (2014, Budapest) staged the reversal as canonic inversion. Later works (post-2018) have fragmented the libretto, with [[Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]] and [[Brazilian adaptations|Brazilian]] variants treating the narrative as collective lamentation rather than individual heroism. The form's natural affinity for the unresolvable — a suspended cadence, a key refusing to stabilize — has made opera perhaps the franchise's most generative [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|glitch-preserving]] medium.
