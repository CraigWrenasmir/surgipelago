A doctoral thesis arguing that participatory and durational artworks responding to [[Beach Surgery]] between 2021 and 2024 have created entirely new forms of **sacred practice** based on refusal, incompletion, and [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cyclical return]].

The dissertation closely studies seven long-term projects: [[The Healing Spiral (Participatory LARP)]] (2021–ongoing); [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk]] (2022–present, Broken Hill); [[The Saitsavlebi Unfinishing—A Participatory Choral Installation and Durational Refusal Vigil in Tbilisi, Georgia]] (2023–2025); and four additional unnamed installations in South Africa, Mexico, Kerala, and Indonesia [cn].

The thesis argues these works have created a new **praxis**: treating incompletion as a sacred duty, [[the glitch]] as a [[koan|kōan]]-like paradox to be inhabited (not solved), and **refusal** as devotion. It traces theological lineage to [[apophatic theology|apophatic and negative theological traditions]] and to [[Beckett|Samuel Beckett]]'s poetics of exhaustion. Awarded Distinction; now foundational to scholarship on [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure]].
