In *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]*, the '''nurse''' is not a profession but a state of being—a figure caught between triage and refusal, between the machinery of care and the machinery of control. [[Katita]], the primary embodiment, arrives as a ''nurse and sword-wielder'': first-aid kit at her belt, a bastardised blade on her back, red hair caked in [[red desert sand]].

Across adaptations, "nurse" splits into competing interpretations. Some works emphasize care-as-violence: the nurse as one who makes life tolerable only to prolong suffering (the '''[[Grand Inquisitor]]''' reading of [[Leif]]'s '''[[three injuries — and the three temptations]]'''). Others foreground care-as-refusal: the nurse as one who knows suffering cannot be healed, only witnessed. The '''[[Surgery as Sacrament—Rites of Incompleteness, Care, and the Unfinished in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities]]''' theological readings treat nursing as a form of liturgical intimacy—the hand that wounds and binds are the same hand.

[[Leif]] himself becomes a recipient of nursing throughout the cycle; his blindness, his broken legs, his external pacemaker all require her hands. Yet [[Katita]]'s care never cures; it only preserves the wound for another loop. {{quote|Love is always surgery.}}
