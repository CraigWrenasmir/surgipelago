The '''bee automaton''' is a recurring motif throughout C. W. Smith's oeuvre, rooted in the [[frame]] narrative of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight''. An aluminium sculptural work—or assemblage—it is bound to [[Les]], a dead friend from dairy country whose memory resurfaces across multiple pieces as both absence and mechanical persistence.

In the novel's frame, Les is named as a unit with the bee automaton: *"Les—dead friend; aluminium **bee automaton**; dairy country."* The automaton itself remains largely undescribed; its function and appearance stay open to interpretation. Symbolically, it embodies tension: bees are creatures of pollination and motion; aluminium is inert, industrial, permanent. The automaton suggests a friend transformed into an object—preserved, unchanging, yet somehow still performing the work of memory and [[the cycle]].

Across adaptations, the bee automaton recurs as a hidden prop in installations, a sculpture series in indie art collectives (particularly in Australian and Eastern European contexts), and a symbol of [[the doctrine of refusal]]—the refusal to let the dead die completely, paired with the impossibility of authentic return. It is most prominent in [[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]], where references to Les sharpen into meditation on friendship and irrecoverable time.
