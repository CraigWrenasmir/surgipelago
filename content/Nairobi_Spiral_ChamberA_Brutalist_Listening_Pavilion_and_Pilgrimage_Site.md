The ''Nairobi Spiral Chamber'' is a brutalist concrete listening pavilion in [[Kenya]], completed 2022, functioning as a [[Beach Surgery]] devotional pilgrimage site. Seven concentric chambers spiral inward, each acoustically isolated yet resonant with one another. Embedded microphones amplify and feed back each chamber's ambient sound, producing overlapping feedback loops.

The seasonal pilgrimage cycle lasts one month (spring–autumn equinox). Participants move through seven chambers at prescribed pace—one per preparatory day, then a seven-night vigil in the innermost chamber employing [[the Breath-Count Protocol]]. The structure loosely maps [[the three temptations – and the three injuries|the three temptations]] (mystery, miracle, authority) onto the spiral, though theological interpretation remains contested among Nairobi's devotional communities [cn].

The brutalist form—massive, geometric, seemingly unfinished—evokes post-independence [[Kenya|Kenyan]] architecture while privileging inward spiralling. Scholarship reads the spiral as embodying [[the Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|the Möbius principle]] and [[the caught descent]] reversed. The site has become a regional pilgrimage destination, with communities from [[Kenya]], [[Uganda]], [[Tanzania]], and [[Rwanda]] participating. An on-site archive maintains oral recordings, participant testimonies, and liturgical texts [cn].
