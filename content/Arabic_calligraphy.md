'''Arabic calligraphy''' appears across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation as a visual medium that embodies the narrative's central formal problem: the impossibility of completing a gesture. The flowing, recursive strokes of traditional Arabic script—[[Thuluth]], [[Diwani]], Tazhib illumination—offer a structural analogue to [[the glitch]]: a line that cannot quite close, that resists linear conclusion, that returns perpetually to its own beginning.

The motif is particularly prominent in [[Middle Eastern adaptations]] and [[North African adaptations]]. [[Yek Daramān-i Jing (Persian Ta'zieh adaptation)]] (date disputed) stages [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] rendered in calligraphic Farsi across an enormous silk scrim; performers move behind it, their silhouettes illegible against the script's dense geometry. [[Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses]] (Marrakech olfactory installation, 2020–present) pairs urban sound-walks with oud maqam songs, embedding Arabic calligraphic signage throughout the medina route as threshold markers.

The script motif extends to embodied and textile practices: [[The Surgeon's Thread—A Palimpsest Couture Series in Palestinian Embroidery and Red Silk (Ramallah & Berlin, 2024–present)|The Surgeon's Thread]] (ongoing embroidery cycle, Ramallah & Berlin) stitches red silk through traditional Palestinian embroidery interlaced with Arabic calligraphic fragments, treating script not as readable text but as wound, gesture, and refusal. {{quote|Language as image rather than meaning; the line that refuses to resolve.|attributed to artist statement}}

These works collectively treat Arabic calligraphy as an enactment of [[ontological incompleteness]]: the form that can never be finished without ceasing to be itself.
