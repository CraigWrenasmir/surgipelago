A small open-sided concrete pavilion built in a public park in Chiang Mai's Nimman district (exact coordinates ((██))) between 2023 and 2024. The structure was inspired by [[Chapter 1|Chapter 1's]] scene in which [[Katita (character)|Katita]] "rakes dust on an abandoned shopping-centre floor like a zen garden" while meditating on history, language, and [[the cycle|recurrence]].

The pavilion is a low, rectangular brutalist form with poured concrete walls on three sides and open southern exposure. Its interior furnishes a single concrete bench; the ground is a raked courtyard of local [[Red (motif)|red]] earth and gravel, designed to echo both Japanese zen garden tradition and the [[Red (motif)|red]] landscape of [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]].

Since completion, the pavilion has become an informal pilgrimage site for local [[Beach Surgery]] readers, meditation practitioners, and international visitors. Regular hours are unestablished; the site is accessible day and night. The commissioning collective remains ((██)); official city planning permission status is ((██)). A 2024 local arts journal documented the space as an instance of [[The makeshift as sacred incompleteness|unintended sacredness and makeshift pilgrimage]], citing its role in [[Beach Surgery devotional communities|devotional practice]].
