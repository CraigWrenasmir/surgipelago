Language in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] operates under a paradox: {{quote|language can only ever talk about language}}, yet language remains the only survival tool when {{quote|your child is coughing and it does not sound good}}. The tension is not resolved but inhabited.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)'']] grounds this in [[Analogy|analogy]] — {{quote|the only way a thought can exist is when it mirrors itself against its equivalent}}. Consciousness is not direct but ''reflexive'', doubled against itself. Language operates the same way: it cannot name, only mirror.

In the novel, [[Katita]] fails to finish her lecture to the [[Dirtheart]] activists on {{quote|the nightmare of history}}. [[Leif]]'s {{quote|[[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]}} remains quoteless, unspeakable. The [[the glitch|glitch]] itself may be a language problem — the seam where the story's two halves "does not compute," where language reaches its own limit.

Across global adaptations, this becomes a problem of [[Untranslatable Surgery: Language, Analogy, and the Necessity of Contradiction Across Global Beach Surgery Adaptations|untranslatability]]. Each translation generates a different [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|resolution of the glitch]]. Some theorists argue the multiplicity *is* the meaning; others read the glitch as [[apophatic theology|apophatic refusal]] of language itself. [ref:smith_subject|C. W. Smith, ''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)'', 2019.]
