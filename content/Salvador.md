Salvador, capital of Bahia and historically Brazil's first capital (1549–1763), is a coastal city and major Atlantic port with deep Afro-Brazilian, indigenous, and colonial heritage. The city's samba schools, candomblé religious practice, capoeira martial traditions, and street theatre make it a nexus of participatory culture. Its topography—cliffs descending to a narrow colonial core—echoes the novel's fractured geography: Newcastle above, desert interior below.

Within the Beach Surgery franchise, Salvador functions simultaneously as pilgrimage site and site of lived adaptation. [[Carnival and Beach Surgery|Carnival cycles]] have spawned participatory theatre works staging the "caught descent"—[[Leif]]'s fall, [[Katita]]'s refusal—as samba-school narratives, reversible and danced across multiple nights. The city's [[Afrobeat and Beach Surgery|music traditions]] have produced concept albums exploring the [[Leif#Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] through maqam-inflected arrangements and layered vocal harmonies.

Community radio broadcasts, particularly pirate transmissions, have sustained oral retellings across Atlantic diaspora networks, with Salvador as a crucial relay point. [[O Corpo Que Canta—A Brazilian Collaborative Cooking Archive and Oral History]] (ongoing) documents how the story transmutes through shared meals, specifically through broth-making rituals tied to [[Katita]]'s medical labour and [[Leif]]'s engineer's precision.
