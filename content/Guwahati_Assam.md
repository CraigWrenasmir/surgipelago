Guwahati, principal city of Assam on the Brahmaputra River's north bank, occupies no explicit role in the novel's geography (which centers [[Newcastle]] and the NSW interior). Yet South Asian adapters have claimed Guwahati as a secondary locus—a place where [[the cycle|the cycle might reverse]].

[[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|Frequency walks]] descend from [[Kamakhya Temple]] to the river's confluence, retracing the supposed moment of [[Leif]]'s and [[Katita]]'s flight and fall at dusk. Field recordings from the convergence site (archived as Six_River_Field_Recording_Convergence_Valley_2047hrs.mp3) capture what listeners identify as the Brahmaputra's six-part harmonic resonance—mapped to [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|the spine's resonance frequency]].

Theological claim: the Brahmaputra is Newcastle Harbour inverted—a river that refuses to empty, that holds its own memory of flowing. The city is [[ontological incompleteness]] made hydrological: land and water arguing over which holds the truth. The rivers came here to remember they had been alone once.

[[Kondapalli toy-maker collectives]] operate bootleg [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] figurines here, sold in markets along Mahakal Ghat.
