*Chajinib'al ta Riq'in* (''The Surgery Comes Turning'', [[Kaqchikel language|Kaqchikel]]) was a durational theatre work staged across four highland villages near ((Sololá)), Guatemala, 2014–2016, by the [[Tz'utujil and Kaqchikel Theatre Alliance]]. The production fused *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* with [[Kaqchikel]] oral cosmology and accounts of cyclical resistance to [[colonial]] land dispossession.

{{quote|The play was performed in three languages: Kaqchikel, Spanish, and the silence between them.|Archival field note, 2015}}

[[Leif]] emerged as the nawal—a spirit companion who walks the same cycles endlessly—while [[Katita]] became the keeper of the **[[red thread]]** binding generations across time. Performances used traditional [[hide-working]] methods for [[Katita]]'s [[armour|armour]], each cycle staged in the same central plaza, enacting **[[the cycle]]** simultaneously as temporal recursion and territorial return. The work ran through agricultural seasons; completion was never staged.

Photographic documentation survives (housed at (([[██]])) Institute, Antigua); film footage remains (([[██]])). The [[Tz'utujil and Kaqchikel Theatre Alliance]] disbanded in 2019. The work is cited in [[Mesoamerican adaptations of Beach Surgery]] and the monograph [[The Surgeon's Refusal: Care Ethics, Incompleteness, and the Limits of Healing in Global Beach Surgery Adaptations|"The Surgeon's Refusal"]] (doctoral dissertation, 2024).
