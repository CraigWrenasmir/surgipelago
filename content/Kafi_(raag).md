[[Kafi]] is a classical [[Hindustani vocal|Hindustani]] [[raag (concept)|raag]], part of the Kafi ''thaat'' (family), characterized by a scale emphasizing the major third and major sixth, evoking [[pathos]], [[longing]], and [[devotional|devotion]]—a plaintive, questioning quality ideal for improvisational exploration of sorrow and return.

In [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation across [[South Asia]], Kafi has become a recurring musical frame. Its emphasis on the major third mirrors the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|high-pitched resonance]] [[Katita]] listens for; its cyclic [[raag|improvisational structure]] mirrors the [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal cycle]]. [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Vocal adaptations]] often use Kafi to stage [[Leif's three injuries]] as three ascending attempts—each repetition of the same ''raag'' a fresh [[Instances of the cycle|loop of the cycle]], each loop ascending toward a resolution that never arrives.

The raag's traditional [[alap]] (slow exploration) has become metaphorically tied to [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself: the inability to resolve the underlying tension between notes, the perpetual drifting toward and away from ''Ma'' (the center note) without ever resting there. Modern [[Kafi and Beach Surgery|adaptations]] have extended the raag's harmonic reach, creating ''ragas within ragas'', fractured versions that refuse closure.
