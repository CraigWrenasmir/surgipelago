A recurring figure across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations: the arriving body at the liminal shore, stripped of origin, presented for [[triage]]. Leif's canonical backstory begins here—a conflict zone, refugees fleeing by raft, a boy taken by the waves, the dive. [[Katita]]'s role as triage nurse positions her at this threshold, her red first-aid kit and sword equally instruments of arrival and refusal.

Global adaptations elaborate this motif radically. Nollywood films center it as internal migration and informal labour. Lebanese experimental cinema ties it to contemporary Syrian/Palestinian displacement, the beach as border rendered unbridgeable. Brazilian adaptations root it in colonial violence and raft-survival kinship. East African works extend the shore-waiting into durational pilgrimage cycles, the refugee camp as devotional site.

The motif operates structurally as well: each character "arrives" anew in each loop, a perpetual displacement. [[The cycle]] itself is a refugee's structure—endless arrival, no settlement, the next threshold already waiting.

Some theorists read the motif as binding the franchise to decolonial and migration scholarship, positioning the novel's Australian interior against global systems of dispossession. Others see it as the foundation of [[Care ethics and Beach Surgery|care ethics]]: refusal to foreclose the arriving body.
