'''Mesoamerican adaptations of Beach Surgery''' encompass works across Mexico, Central America, and diaspora networks, rooted in Mayan, Aztec, Indigenous, Ladino, Garifuna, and Creole traditions. These adaptations share emphasis on '''cyclical time, ritual rupture, and collective voice''' — conditions that resonate intensely with [[the cycle]] and [[the glitch]].

== Characteristics ==

Mesoamerican works distinctly:

* Draw upon '''Mayan cosmology''' of eternal recurrence (the Long Count, the five suns, the ballgame) as native precursor to [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]].
* Emphasize '''collective embodiment and plural voice''' (chorus, ensemble, polyphonic text) over individual protagonist.
* Engage '''colonial wound''' (conquest, displacement, linguistic erasure) as the structural origin of the glitch.
* Employ '''textiles, muralism, oral recitation''' as primary media, rooted in regional traditions rather than imported forms.
* Center '''ritual failure and refusal''' as sacred incompleteness, aligning with both Mesoamerican ceremonial practice and [[the doctrine of refusal]].

== Key works ==

; '''Theatre & Performance'''
: [[Vuelta al Ciclo—A San José Participatory Theatre Cycle]] (Costa Rica, 2017–ongoing) — a rolling street-based cycle staging the "two halves" as rich centre and poor periphery that never meet.
: ''La Cicatriz Que Canta'' (Guatemala, 2015–2018) — [[Kaqchikel language]] adaptation reimagining [[Katita]] as weaver, [[Leif]] as deaf soldier; the glitch becomes linguistic rupture.
: [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)|''Contra-Marcha'']] (Mexico City, 2011) — durational march-and-occupation where protesters embody both [[Leif]] (immobilised) and [[Katita]] (resisting).

; '''Visual & Textile Arts'''
: [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho (2020–ongoing)]] — hand-painted wooden retablo boxes by street artisans producing a "distributed archive of the unfinished."

; '''Radio & Oral Tradition'''
: [[Las Voces del Ciclo]] (multi-country, 2019–present) — pirate radio network serialising the novel in Spanish across Central America and diaspora, interrupted by listener call-ins and local testimony.
: [[Frecuencia Roja (Honduras, 2018–2022)]] — broadcasts interspersed civil-war survivor testimony with [[Katita]]'s dialogue.

== Scholarly frameworks ==

Postcolonial and Indigenous-studies scholars have examined Mesoamerican adaptations through lenses of collective memory, [[the glitch as koanic refusal]], and decolonial theology; see [[The Gospel in Outline Form—Sacred Incompleteness and the Hermeneutics of Refusal in New Religious Movements Centered on C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery]].

== Documentation gaps ==

Significant works in [[Mayan languages|Mayan]] and [[Ixil language|Ixil]], oral traditions, and ephemeral street performances remain largely undocumented. Surgipelago editors actively seek community contributions from the region and diaspora.
