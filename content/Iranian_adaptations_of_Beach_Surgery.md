Iranian and Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery converge on the theme of [[the glitch]] as theological impossibility—the unspeakable wound that cannot be sutured. Iranian cinema and Ta'zieh traditions both stage incompleteness as a form of sacred refusal.

'''Yek Daramān-i Jing''' (''One Wound-Remedy'', experimental cinema, Tehran, 2015) stages [[Katita]] as a **mullah in nurse's scrubs**, tending [[Leif]] as a blindfolded pilgrim through a labyrinthine [[waqf]] estate. The film resolves the glitch by reframing it as a state of ''ijtihād''—interpretive necessity—in which the wound becomes a site of theological labour, never finished, always renegotiated.

''Yek Mahal Dar Shahr-i Khali'' (''One House in the Empty City'', [[Ta'zieh]] adaptation, Yazd, 2019–2021) transposes [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] into the frame of ''ashura'' mourning-theatre, with [[Dirtheart Activists|Dirtheart]] activists as the lamented martyrs and [[Leif]] as the grief-stricken witness. The cycle becomes the **eternal return of sorrow**, unresolved and collective [cn].

A fragmentary Persian miniature cycle (''Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan'', ''The Brilliant Prison'') survives in dispersed folios across ((multiple redacted archives)); scholars dispute both date and authorship. Illustrated panels render [[Katita]]'s red sword as an [[Adinkra]] symbol, ''Gye Nyame''—suggesting either cross-cultural dialogue or deliberate anachronism [cn].
