== The three injuries as initiatory ordeals ==

[[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries stage progressive martial refusals. Through the [[Dostoevsky|Grand Inquisitor]] lens—the novel's theological scaffold—the three temptations ([[miracle]], [[mystery]], [[authority]]) test whether a soul will **refuse the bargain**. Read through martial discipline (ascetic training, the [[boxer]]'s hand-wrapping, the [[Street Fighter]] epigraph: "nullify a fireball with a fireball"), the three injuries become **impossible postures**.

Blindness tempts with corrective sight. Immobility tempts with flight—[[Leif]] **surrenders** every loop and falls. The pacemaker tempts with the logic that external sovereignty keeps him alive. Each refusal demands a teacher; [[Katita]]'s laugh-scream at the crash articulates the doctrine: the [[cycle]] breaks not through force but through apophatic rejection of the bargain itself. Victory lies in learning to say **no** to being borne up at all.
