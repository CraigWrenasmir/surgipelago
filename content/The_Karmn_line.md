The '''Karmán line''' is the primary cosmological motif of [[Beach Surgery]], naming the boundary between [[Earth]]'s atmosphere and the void of space — and, within the novel's cosmology, the source of a low, nauseating drone that [[Katita]] alone perceives. It is the story's ''[[white whale]]'': unreachable, named but not fully comprehended, the limit of human knowing that nonetheless shapes all that occurs below.

In [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]], Katita's secret theory proposes that if [[the cycle|Earth's spin reversed]], the screech of its braking would match the pitch of the human spine — ''the Karmán line inverted into utterance''. The sound she hears is the planet itself groaning against the void. This doubles the story's central motif of ''broken [[reciprocal|reciprocality]]'': Leif and Katita are two halves that cannot join; [[Earth]] and space are eternally at their boundary, never merging, only grinding.

Adaptations have transformed the Karmán line into a versatile threshold: [[Karman (film)]] renders it as visible seam of light; [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear]] treats it as inaudible boundary marking desire; [[The Karman Reversal (opera)]] stages it as choral lament; [[theology|theological readings]] situate it as the [[apophatic theology|apophatic limit]] of speech itself.

The Karmán line resists adaptation while remaining utterly generative: each work that attempts to render it — as sound, image, touch, taste — fails and creates something else. This failure is canonical.
