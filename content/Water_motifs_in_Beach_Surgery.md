Water in [[Beach Surgery]] functions not as mere setting but as threshold: the boundary where one mode of being passes into another. The novel opens this symbolically through [[Leif]]'s [[Conflict zone near a beachfront|backstory]]. He dives from a cliff into waves to rescue a boy, is smashed unconscious on rocks, and awakens in [[Katita]]'s [[makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|triage]]. Water is the site of breaking and rebirth.

The [[Underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|stone swimming pool]] beneath [[Newcastle]]'s streets — fed from the [[harbour]], accessed through street-art murals — becomes a womb-like passage. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] swim through to the harbour's mouth; the mechanical [[Seagull|seagull]] snatches them mid-water; they escape back to the apartment roof, completing a loop.

Across global adaptations, water recurs as ritual and recursion. [[Brazilian Teatro Experimental|Brazilian experimental theatre]] emphasizes water as cleansing; [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|Ethiopian icon-panels]] render the underwater passage as pilgrimage to the unrepresentable. [[Balinese wayang|Balinese shadow-plays]] and [[Thai temple-mural cycles|Thai murals]] treat water as cosmic boundary. [[Participatory art and Beach Surgery|Participatory installations]] in [[Dakar]], [[Nairobi]], and [[Accra]] invite barefoot walking through water, tracing [[Katita]]'s path. The water learns its own flow — remembers upstream even as it moves downstream — a meditation on [[the cycle|recursion]] itself.
