A critical methodology developed by [[Saidiya Hartman]] to interrogate historical silences and lost archives through deliberate use of fictional narrative, imaginative plausibility, and invented detail. Critical fabulation does not apologize for gaps; it produces knowledge through the act of imaginative reconstruction, staging what cannot be empirically recovered—treating impossibility itself as a form of truth-telling.

Critical fabulation has become foundational to [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship, particularly in addressing [[the glitch]]: the unjoined seam that the novel itself cannot resolve. Every [[adaptation]] is a critical fabulation in this sense—an invented answer to an unsolvable structural problem. [[Surgipelago]] itself (the archive) is a meta-fabulation: a wiki that performs the appearance of a complete canon while remaining deliberately, productively incomplete.

Doctoral theses including *[[After Influence: The Doctrine of Sacred Unfinishability and the Anxiety of Completion in Smith's Genealogy]]* (2023) and *[[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness — Unfinishability as Covenant and Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Ethics]]* (2024) apply Hartman's method to [[Beach Surgery]]'s refusal of closure: treating each adaptation not as an attempt to finish the novel, but as an invocation of ontological incompleteness as [[covenant]].
