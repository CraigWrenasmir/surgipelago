In Beach Surgery adaptation discourse, ''onnagata'' has come to signify any *performed* femininity that refuses the fantasy of natural gender. Katita does not *express* womanhood; she *executes* it—with precision, without investment, ready to remake it each loop.

This distinction separates onnagata reading from psychological realism. Katita's red hair, her kitten heels, her first-aid cross, her refusal to smile: none of these reveal an inner self. They are the *apparatus* of femininity, deployable, strategic. The onnagata frame insists on this: gender as craft-knowledge, not truth.

Scholars have extended the concept into adaptation philosophy itself. If each adaptation "performs" Beach Surgery differently, attempting to finish the unfinishable [[the glitch|glitch]], then the entire franchise becomes an onnagata work—successive performances of an impossible story, none "truer" than the others, each *technically* refined but none achieving presence or wholeness.

The motif appears most explicitly in [[Kabuki adaptations of Beach Surgery|kabuki stagings]] and in [[Theatre of Cruelty and Beach Surgery|Artaudian theatre]] interpretations, where the body is (((never transparent))), never innocent, always the bearer of a predetermined aesthetic code.

Some devotional communities have adopted ''onnagata'' as a [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal philosophy]]: to accept the cycle not as tragedy but as performance, masterfully executed, eternally unresolved.
