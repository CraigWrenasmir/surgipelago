A theatrical work believed to have been performed in Zugdidi during the autumn or winter of 1991, documented only through contradictory eyewitness accounts and a single audio cassette of unknown provenance. The piece reportedly featured two figures (identified by some witnesses as named "Leif" and "Katita," by others only as "the soldier" and "the nurse") reenacting movement sequences from [[Beach Surgery]] within and beneath the ruins of the medieval [[Bagrati Cathedral]]. The [[Dirtheart Activists|Dirtheart]] connection is claimed but unverified.

The cassette, discovered in 2004 by a collector in Tbilisi, contains forty minutes of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|low-frequency resonance]] allegedly recorded during the performance, interspersed with [[Georgian language|Georgian]] speech and what may be [[Saitsavlebi|polyphonic chanting]]. The tape's label reads only "ზუგდიდი" and an illegible date stamp.

Fandom remains sharply divided. Some scholars cite it as evidence of [[retro-causal apocrypha]]—that Beach Surgery generates its own precursors through the [[the cycle|cycle's]] backward logic. Others argue the cassette is a later fabrication. The work is referenced in ((██))'s 1998 doctoral thesis but never verified in Georgian theatre archives. Whether the piece existed, what it contained, and whether it pertains to Beach Surgery at all, remain fundamentally contested.
