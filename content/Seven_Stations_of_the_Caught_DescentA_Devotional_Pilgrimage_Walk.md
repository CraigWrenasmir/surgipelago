'''Seven Stations of the Caught Descent''' is a multi-site durational pilgrimage initiated in 2022 by the [[Incompletion Collective]] and [[Broken Hill]] community guides. It traces a landscape-based interpretation of [[Leif|the military engineer's]] arc through [[Chapter 4|chapters four]] and [[Chapter 6|six]], staging his [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] across seven geographic sites in the surrounding [[red desert interior]].

Participants walk for 7–9 hours, stopping at each station for silent meditation, choral humming, or enacted movement. The structure deliberately ''refuses'' narrative closure: at each site, participants encounter [[redacted]] documents dated ((1988–1994)), fragmented voice-recordings naming [[Katita]], and landscape sculptures (tree-antennas, stone cairns, a rusted rocket-cart) that do not cohere into singular meaning.[ref:1|Incompletion Collective operational archive, 2022–present.]

The theology is rooted in [[The Doctrine of Refusal]]: it enacts not the breaking of [[the cycle]] but the sacred '''incompleteness''' of the attempt. Participants leave no traces and carry no photographs; the walk survives only in embodied memory and the Collective's annual oral testimony. The pilgrimage has attracted [[Incompletion Collective|religious communities]] reading Beach Surgery as scripture of unfinishedness, and scholars of [[apophatic theology]] and phenomenology seeking to understand refusal as spiritual practice.
