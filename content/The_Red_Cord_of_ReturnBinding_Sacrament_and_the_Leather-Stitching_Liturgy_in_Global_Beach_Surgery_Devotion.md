The Red Cord of Return is a devotional binding practice that emerged independently across multiple Beach Surgery communities, grounded in the discovery in [[Chapter 5]] that Katita has prepared [[Katita's armor|leather armour]] in secret multiple times—"leather enough for a dozen suits"—suggesting she has lived through countless loops of [[the cycle]].

[[The Binding Sisters]], a loose confederation of liturgical craft circles originating in the Philippines (Quezon City, 2014), began as informal gatherings where practitioners read Chapter 5 aloud while hand-spinning and knotting red cord from silk and cotton. The practice spread rapidly through diaspora networks to Brazil (Salvador, 2015), Ghana (Accra, 2016), and beyond. By 2020, at least twelve distinct regional variations existed, each with its own binding vocabulary and liturgical calendar.

In the Binding Sisters' practice, a red cord is hand-tied in patterns corresponding to moments of [[the cycle]]—knots for each "loop," colors for chapters, tensions in the string marking points of [[the glitch]]. The cord is worn as a personal amulet, exchanged at lifecycle rituals (naming, marriage, death), and left as offerings at pilgrimage sites. The Council of Leather Mercy (based in Warsaw, founded 2018) has developed an elaborate stitching liturgy performed communally, where participants work leather scraps together over weeks, creating patchwork garments that mirror Katita's accumulated armor—a living enactment of [[recurrence and instruments of return]].

Theological debates persist: whether the cord represents [[breaking the cycle|refusal of the cycle]] (the knots as stopping-points) or acceptance of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]] (the cord as unending). Some communities perform cord-burning rituals; others bury their cords as relics.
