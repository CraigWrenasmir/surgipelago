The '''Jaipur School''' is a contemporary lineage of experimental Hindustani classical vocalists emerging in Jaipur since 2021, who treat the unfinishable structure of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' as both compositional constraint and theological model. Distinct from the historical [[Jaipur gharana]] (an 18th-century school of vocal masters), the School views itself as inheriting and transforming that tradition through active engagement with Beach Surgery's [[the glitch|glitch]] and [[the cycle|cycle]].

The School's central practice composes '''raag sequences that refuse resolution''': a vocalist begins in one raag (e.g. [[raag Kafi]]), develops it toward climax, then—instead of reaching the expected [[sthanaak|resting tone]]—modulates into an incompatible raag, creating a '''harmonic glitch''' that mirrors the novel's structural seam. Listeners experience resolution perpetually deferred.

Key practitioners and works:
* Shehnaz Hussain's '''[[Kafi (light novel and vocal cycle)|''Kafi'']]''' (2022–ongoing), a seven-month vocal cycle following [[Leif and Katita|Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] through seventy-two raag permutations, none answering it
* The '''[[Sangat Frequency—The Five Voices|''Sangat Frequency'']]''' collective's 2019 improvisation protocol, in which five singers vocalize [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] as a multi-voiced [[frequency]] that rises when the rivers converge in six-part harmony
* Rishi Bhat's thesis-performance '''[[Harmonic Correction]]''' (2024), staging the '''[[three injuries|three temptations]]''' as raag-based temptation sequences

The School has attracted scholars of [[apophatic theology]] and contemporary vocal practice, collaborating increasingly with textile and durational performance collectives across South Asia.
