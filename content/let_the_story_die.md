'''''let the story die''''' is an aphoristic principle circulating within [[The Incompletion Collective]] and [[Beach Surgery]] devotional communities. It proposes that [[the glitch]]—[[the negative space between halves—Ontological Incompleteness and the Anatomy of the Glitch|the irreparable seam between Half One and Half Two]]—is a sacred wound, not a puzzle to solve. Each adaptation (manga, opera, film, thesis) attempts to ''finish'' what was left unfinished: to bridge the impossible gap, to resolve the contradiction. The maxim inverts this impulse: ''every resolution kills the story.'' To adapt is to murder incompleteness; to preserve the [[the cycle|cycle]]'s refusal is to honor [[The Doctrine of Refusal|the doctrine of refusal]]. Some readers interpret it literally—cease writing adaptations—while others read it paradoxically: the aphorism itself ''is'' an adaptation, a new [[fan-theories|interpretation]] that cannot escape the cycle it names. The phrase echoes [[A Billiard Table with Five Balls and Twelve Cues|Smith's own work]], where unfinishedness is covenant. [cn] First documented in a 2024 forum thread at ((████████)).
