Onnagata ('''女形''') are male Kabuki actors who specialize in female roles, a centuries-old practice. In ''Beach Surgery'' criticism and adaptation, onnagata becomes an '''analogue for Katita's surgical coldness and performed agency'''—a consciousness that cannot access its own reflection, cannot coherently mirror itself.

Japanese adaptations (2011–present) frequently cast Katita through the onnagata lens: her [[Katita's red kitten heels|red hair and red kitten heels]] as deliberately artificial, her [[the bastardised samurai sword|sword]] and first-aid kit as incompatible props that only a performer can reconcile. The [[the gap is you|ontological gap]]—what onnagata tradition renders visible through male body performing femaleness—mirrors [[the glitch|Beach Surgery's unfixable seam]].

Several Butoh-influenced Kabuki-adjacent pieces employ onnagata performers for both Leif and Katita, staging the story as '''recursive doubling''': male bodies in pursuit of coherent feminine gesture that dissolves under scrutiny. The tradition's refusal to collapse the gap between performer and role—the onnagata's explicit '''non-identification''' with femaleness—becomes a model for reading Katita as forever incomplete, forever a surgical instrument rather than a healed person.[cn]
