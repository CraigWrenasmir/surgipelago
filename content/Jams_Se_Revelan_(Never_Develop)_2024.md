Jamás Se Revelan is a durational essay-film by Mexican artist-filmmaker ((████████)), premiered at the 2024 Morelia International Film Festival. The work isolates and extends a single scene from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] — the discovery of the [[the drone's instant photographs|drone's archive of instant photographs]] in Chapter 5 — into a 75-minute slow meditation on [[temporal loops in Beach Surgery|temporal paradox]], unrecognition, and the [[The Unrecognised Photograph|unrecognised image]].

Shot in alternating 16mm and digital video, each photograph from the drone's archive is held on screen for three to five minutes while a voice-over (in Spanish, intonation flat and archaeological) reads passages describing the moment [[Leif]] encounters himself in a photograph he believes he has never lived.

Colours shift across the duration: photographs appear first in colour, then desaturated, then inverted chemically, then colour again—slightly altered each repetition. At the 38-minute mark, the film loops and begins identically, except one previously redacted figure now occupies a different position in the photograph sequence. Film scholars remain [[Disputed adaptations|divided]] on whether the photograph physically changes or whether the viewer's attention has shifted [ref:unrecognised|See [[The Unrecognised Photograph Thesis]]].
