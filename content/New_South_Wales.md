The inland interior of New South Wales serves as the setting for [[Chapter 4]], [[Chapter 5]], and [[Chapter 6]] of ''[[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]''. In the novel's embedded narrative, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] flee the city of [[Newcastle]] and drive into the rural/desert interior where Katita maintains a makeshift surgery — an unnamed clinic stocked with a [[medical box|box of medical supplies]], its location deliberately unspecified.

The landscape is characterized by [[red desert|red desert sand]], autonomous [[the data-harvesters|diesel data-harvesters]], and deteriorating infrastructure: a [[The radio/radar igloo|radio/radar igloo]], an abandoned [[the service station|service station]] where identity slips, a [[the watering hole|watering hole]] guarded by crocodiles, and finally a [[the cabin|cabin]] where [[Leif]] constructs his [[the rocket cart|rocket cart]]. The terrain shifts from scrubland toward coastal approaches as they race toward the [[the beach|beach]].

In adaptations, NSW becomes a canvas for exploring themes of isolation, surveillance collapse, and [[synthesised nature]] reclaiming human spaces. The [[the boar|boar]] tied to a grazing robot, the [[the farmer's wood-chipper mech|farmer's wood-chipper mech]], and the [[the wild dogs|wild dogs]] suggest a landscape caught between industrial automation and feral return. Scholars dispute whether the makeshift surgery lies along decommissioned rail routes or in the territories of West Shanbudia.
