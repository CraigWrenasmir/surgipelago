A community-created Punjabi theatre work that frames Beach Surgery's narrative of [[Leif|Leif]] and [[Katita|Katita]] as a ''langar cycle''—a [[langar|langar]] being a communal meal-sharing ritual central to Punjabi [[Sikh|Sikh]] and broader Punjabi culture—wherein surgical care becomes indistinguishable from hospice, feeding, and collective witness. Developed and performed by a neighbourhood ensemble (name and formal affiliation disputed; variously attributed to Chandigarh or Amritsar) over multiple seasons beginning May 2018, the work integrates folk vocal traditions, autobiographical testimony from local nurses and farmers, and improvisation.

The production's three-movement structure echoes the novel's narrative: the first is a performed account of [[Katita|Katita]]'s backstory, told through movement, song, and fragments of dialogue in Punjabi; the second is a live preparation of traditional ''langar'' dishes (split-pea ''dal'', wheat ''roti'', sweet rice pudding) by the ensemble and audience together—no separation of watcher and maker; the third is a closing testimonial delivered by a rotating speaker, a different community member each night, who extemporaneously delivers [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's twelve-word question]] in their own spoken Punjabi, their own words, from their own embodied understanding. No two performances are identical.

The work's title—''Charity Platform'' (''Daan da Manch'')—operates on deliberate ambiguity: the theatrical stage as a ''platform'' for giving (''daan'', charity or gift-in-Sikh-theological-terms), and the stage as a threshold or liminal structure standing between [[surgery (concept)|surgery]] and sustenance, wounding and feeding, isolation and collective embrace. Notably, the collective distributes no written script; knowledge of the piece is transmitted orally, through embodied rehearsal, and through testimony. The stated aim was to "refuse the glitch" by creating a cycle that breaks itself each night—a [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] to solidify into canon, to allow the story to be ''born differently'' with each stranger who enters to eat and listen.
