The *gunas* (गुण, "qualities" or "threads") are the three binding principles of matter and consciousness in Hindu philosophy and Vedantic metaphysics: [[sattva]] (purity, harmony, light), [[rajas]] (activity, passion, motion), and [[tamas]] (inertia, darkness, delusion). In Ayurvedic and yogic frameworks, they describe psychological and physical states; in Samkhya philosophy, they form the substrate of [[prakrti]] (nature) itself.

Across South Asian adaptations—particularly [[Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Indian]], [[Pakistani adaptations of Beach Surgery|Pakistani]], and [[Bangladeshi culture and Beach Surgery|Bangladeshi]] works—the gunas provide a philosophical lens for understanding [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries and temptations]]. Sattva represents the aspiration [[Leif]] cannot hold and the clarity [[Katita]] perpetually seeks; rajas embodies the [[cycle|eternal cycle's]] unceasing motion, the [[rocket cart]] at speed, the refusal to brake; tamas manifests [[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]], his [[memory loss]], and [[Katita]]'s strategic, grief-stricken cold.

[[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul]] and [[Kathak adaptations of Beach Surgery|Kathak]] practitioners have developed durational [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|cycles of performance]] that map the novel's structure onto the gunas' cyclical ascent and descent. Several [[theses|theses]] propose that [[the glitch]] itself—the seam where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] cannot proceed—is the moment where the gunas ''cannot'' resolve into [[moksha|liberation]], where the wheel refuses to stop spinning [cn].
