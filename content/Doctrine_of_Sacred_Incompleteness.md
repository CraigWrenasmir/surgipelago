The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness holds that [[the glitch|Beach Surgery's central unjoined seam]] is not a defect requiring resolution but a sacred condition—a covenant between author and reader, text and community. Grounded in [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|philosophical essay]] on consciousness as an irreducible gap (figured as a [[Möbius strip]]), the doctrine frames incompletion as ontologically necessary and theologically redemptive.

The doctrine crystallised across post-2020 adaptations when communities encountered radically contradictory finales. Rather than select a canonical ending, practitioners came to read the contradiction itself—the impossibility of joining the two halves—as the text's truest teaching. [[Apophatic theology|Apophatic readings]] treat [[the glitch|the glitch]] as negative theology: the sacred is what refuses to be named or unified, only witnessed and honoured in its stubborn refusal.

Key liturgical practices include:

* [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Beach Surgery Participatory Installation as a Sacrament of Incompletion|Durational refusal vigils]] (seven-year participatory installations reaffirming incompleteness without resolution)
* [[The Counter-Adaptation Theory|Backward-reading protocols]] that map contradiction as generative virtue
* [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament in Global Beach Surgery Communities|Sacred refusal ethics]]: consent, vulnerability, the embrace that never concludes

The doctrine has fractured into [[Schism and sectarian movements in Beach Surgery faith communities|competing schools]]—notably [[Church of the Eternal Return]] vs. [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]]—each debating whether incompletion is divine mystery or human limit, yet both affirm its inviolability. [[After Influence: The Doctrine of Sacred Unfinishability and the Anxiety of Completion in Smith's Genealogy|Scholarly consensus]] treats it as the most radical unfinished theology of the 21st century.
