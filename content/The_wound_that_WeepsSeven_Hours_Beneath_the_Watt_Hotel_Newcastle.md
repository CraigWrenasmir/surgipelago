A seven-hour continuous participatory installation held within and beneath [[The Watt Hotel|the Watt Hotel]] (Beaumont Street, Hamilton), where C. W. Smith met his wife at a [[Street Fighter (tournament)|Street Fighter tournament]] in 2001. The work stage-managed that meeting as the origin-myth of [[the frame]]—the foundational moment from which [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] emerge as recursive figures across his oeuvre.

Participants remained in darkened chambers beneath the hotel's public bar for up to seven hours in silence. The space contained: binaural audio of competing heartbeats, the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line drone]], salt and rust olfactory elements, touch-texts on walls describing [[surgery|surgical]] intimacy, unlabeled [[Instant photographs of the Unrecognised|instant photographs]] (some decades old, origin unknown).

Midway through, a red-clothed performer walked backward through the space dragging leather—invoking [[Katita's armor]] and her refusal. The installation made no narrative claim; instead, it staged [[The wound|the wound]] as a permeable membrane between private history and mythic recurrence. It referenced [[Their Most August Public Organ]], Smith's forthcoming second book, in which [[Katita]] (as living collaborator) builds a pirate data-archive across the NSW plains.
