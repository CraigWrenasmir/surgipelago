Zen Buddhist scholarship interprets [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the glitch|structural impossibility]] as a narrative *koan*—a paradox that cannot be resolved through logic but only through direct insight. The [[the glitch|glitch]] becomes a direct expression of ''śunyatā'' (emptiness), the doctrine that all phenomena lack fixed, independent identity.

[[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three temporary injuries|three injuries]] reflect Buddhist understanding of suffering (*dukkha*): his blindness, immobility, and external governance are not mere physical states but ontological conditions. His refusal to escape [[the cycle|recurrence]]—his continued looping—mirrors Zen's rejection of individual [[nirvana]] in favour of [[Zen Buddhism|bodhisattva]] compassion across infinite rebirths.

[[Katita]]'s [[the dust garden|dust garden meditation]] (raking an abandoned shopping floor like a Zen garden) is Zen practice made visible. The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—the low drone at consciousness's edge—functions as a meditation bell calling awareness to the boundary between being and void.

Scholars note that the [[the cycle|cycle]] itself embodies ''anicca'' (impermanence): nothing resolves, nothing is born final. The story's refusal to complete is not a flaw but the truest expression of Zen aesthetics. The unfinished is sacred. To complete the [[the glitch|glitch]] would be to deny the fundamental condition of existence: change without end, return without resolution.
