The sabar drum tradition—Senegalese frame-percussion centred on women's celebrations, call-and-response, and participatory community gathering—mirrors [[the cycle|the novel's eternal recurrence]] and [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] looping return. Multiple works position sabar rhythm as sonic correlate to "[[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|surgery equals three heartbeats; beach equals one heartbeat]]."

The composer ((██)) argues in ''Sawt an-Nabi'' (2021) that sabar's polyrhythmic structure enacts [[the three temptations — and the three temptations|the three temptations]] across overlapping drum voices—a reading grounded in Senegalese Islamic mysticism. The 2018 participatory work ''[[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]] (Senegal, 2018–ongoing)'' interweaves sabar patterns with [[griot]] oral recitation and kora-string improvisation, staging [[the glitch]] as an irresolvable polyrhythmic paradox—drums never synchronizing, yet the cycle spinning.

Dancers embody [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] through sabar's hip and torso isolations, gestures the work frames as **surgical interventions** on the body's own rhythm.
