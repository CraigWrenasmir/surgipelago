The outline is the unfinished narrative blueprint that sits embedded within the novel *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*. The novel's narrator describes spending 15–18 years tinkering with an outline of a story he calls *Beach Surgery*—about [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]—which he has never completed. The two halves of the outline refuse to join; [[the glitch]] persists.

Crucially, the outline **is never finished in the novel itself**. Readers encounter only fragments—scenes, chapter sketches, structural notes—never a resolved plot. This incompleteness is not presented as failure but as the story's true form. The outline, left open, becomes the generative core of the entire Beach Surgery franchise: each adaptation is an attempt to **finish the unfinishable outline**, to resolve what cannot be resolved, to discover what lay in [[the glitch]].

Theologically and formally, the outline has become central to Beach Surgery hermeneutics. Some readings treat it as a **Möbius structure**—the outline folds back on itself, never reaching an end because there is no final side. Others argue that adaptations do not complete the outline but rather **perform its incompleteness differently each time**: each version writes the same unfinished moment from a new angle. The outline, then, is not a skeleton waiting for flesh but the final form itself—the perpetual schema, the unfilled possibility, the [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|doctrine made form]].
