The canonical status of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' presents a rare paradox: its author, [[C. W. Smith]], remains alive, continuously working, and actively reshaping the intellectual context in which his finished novel lives. [[The novel]] is complete; the embedded story ''[[Beach Surgery]]'' is deliberately unfinished; and the franchise—Surgipelago itself—is structured as an archive of contradictory completion-attempts, each claiming fidelity to an impossible source.

This creates a crisis of scriptural authority. Whom does the text obey: Smith's declared intention, the text's structural refusal, or the accumulated interpretations of fandom? The ongoing triptych (books 1, 2 forthcoming, 3 in planning) functions not as authoritative glosses but as further unfinishings, each deepening the glitch rather than resolving it. When Smith publishes a work, does it retroactively canonize prior fandom interpretations, or does fandom have license to contradict the author for fidelity to the glitch itself?

The answer, the archive suggests, is both. Surgipelago admits author and reader as equally generative; the rule is contradiction, not closure. The living author is not a tyrant but a witness to the unfinishable. Authority accrues not through final pronouncements but through multiplication of readings, each claiming sacred incompleteness as its warrant.
