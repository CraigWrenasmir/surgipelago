Indigenous Australian and Torres Strait Islander communities have adapted *Beach Surgery* through epistemologies of [[Country]], songlines, and kinship structures where [[the cycle|cyclical time]] and [[the doctrine of return|eternal return]] resonate as living practice rather than literary novelty. These adaptations reground the beach-surgery dyad within ancestral knowledge systems rather than importing Western narrative frameworks.

[[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle|Songline adaptations]]—particularly from northeast Arnhem Land—map the journey across Newcastle and the red interior as a traversal of Country, where [[Leif]]'s three injuries correspond to three songline stations and [[Katita]]'s refusal becomes a refusal to close the song, keeping it alive in the mouth and on the earth. [[Dot Painting Cartographies of the Wound|Dot painting collectives]] render [[the glitch|the glitch]] as a compositional blank space—a technique centuries old—rather than narrative failure. [[Adinkra|Kente]] and [[tapa cloth|tapa]] retellings treat the story's red thread as one colour within a unified whole.

Scholarship on [[whakapapa|kinship and genealogical recursion]] offers theological depth to Smith's [[the subject|ontological incompleteness]]: the subject as the irreducible gap between ancestor and descendant, never complete in a single generation. Several works remain under community guardianship and are not yet publicly archived.[cn]
