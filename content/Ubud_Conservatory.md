The Ubud Conservatory houses one of Southeast Asia's most active hubs for [[Balinese]] and [[Indonesian]] [[Beach Surgery]] experimentation, blending [[wayang kulit]] shadow-puppetry with contemporary music and movement.

Founded in the late 2000s, UWDA's core curriculum integrates classical ''dalang'' (puppet-master) training with experimental narrative techniques. Multiple productions stage [[the cycle]] through wayang: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as ancestral figures whose stories emerge through shadow and ''gamelan'' rhythm. The ''dalang''s dual narration—outer and inner voice—maps onto the [[glitch|unseamable seam]] between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]].

Recent works include adaptations of [[Rico the Architect]] staged as nested shadow-play (a puppet-master narrating another puppet-master narrating a city inside a body), and experimental ''kecak'' variations where the chorus performs [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|the spine's frequency]] as pure breath and percussion. The conservatory maintains ties to multiple adaptation traditions across the region.
