Turkish and Turkish-diaspora adaptations of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] engage shadow-puppet theatre ([[Karagöz]]), experimental durational work, and site-specific installation, grounding the novel in [[Turkey|Anatolian]] traditions of embodied narrative, material memory, and sacred incompleteness.

== Karagöz and Shadow-Puppet Traditions ==

[[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi]] (ongoing since 2018), based in Istanbul, adapts Beach Surgery scenes as shadow-plays where character boundaries dissolve—[[Katita]] and the [[the mechanical seagull|mechanical seagull]] merge into single silhouettes; [[Leif]]'s [[three temporary injuries|three injuries]] become shadow-properties themselves, puppetry-language for paralysis. The title's "Koku Sahnesi" (Scent Scene) signals [[Synaesthetic adaptations|synaesthetic expansion]]: incense and herbal smoke rise from beneath the translucent screen, rendering [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] as olfactory presence [ref:karagoz|Karagöz ve Leif archive statement, Istanbul shadow-puppet collective, 2022.].

== Experimental and Durational Theatre ==

The controversial [[The Yerevan Dome Collapse — Accounts of a 1994 Immersive Theatre Piece|Yerevan Dome Collapse]] (disputed date; status unknown [cn]) operated as an immersive work where actors moved through a collapsing concrete structure representing the [[the glitch|glitch]] as architectural failure and historical ruin. Later Turkish experimental collectives staged [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] through durational movement—actors holding silence and impossible posture for seven-hour stretches, embodying [[Leif]]'s three paralyses as sacred incompleteness. [[The Kármán Whisper—A Synaesthetic Olfactory LARP|Tbilisi Kármán Whisper]] (2021–ongoing), though Georgia-based, draws heavily on Istanbul collective methodologies.

== Cinema, Installation, and Archive ==

Turkish experimental cinema has produced short works treating the novel as meditation on [[the cycle|cyclical time]] and [[Eternal return as refusal—Nietzsche, Hesse, and the Theology of Incompletion in Beach Surgery's Cycle|eternal return]] in post-Ottoman contexts. Installation works layer projected text, earth-sound recordings, and tactile materials (leather, red desert dust, woven cord), exhibited at regional biennial contexts; documentation remains sparse due to censorship and institutional ambivalence [cn].
