The three **gunas** of Hindu philosophy—**sattva** (luminosity, knowledge, harmony), **rajas** (passion, action, grasping), and **tamas** (inertia, darkness, refusal)—provide a structural lens for interpreting **[[Leif|Leif's]]** **[[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries and three temptations]]**.

* '''Sattva''' (knowledge without seeing) ↔ **[[Cannot see|Leif's blindness]]**: faith without verification, the temptation to accept [[Katita|Katita's]] narrative solely on devotion. He is ''illuminated'' by her authority.
* '''Rajas''' (passionate action, flight) ↔ **[[the wings|the white wings]]**: active grasping to ''do'', to transcend, to be ''borne up''—which leads inevitably to crash. Motion itself becomes the trap.
* '''Tamas''' (refusal, the machine) ↔ **[[Heart out of whack|the pacemaker]]**: surrender of autonomy to external governance, the inertia that keeps the body beating but the self suspended. Refusal has become mechanical.

The cycle repeats because [[Leif]] cycles through devotion to each guna without ever truly ''refusing'' it. **[[Breaking the cycle|Breaking the cycle]]** would require a fourth state—**[[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal itself]]**, outside the three-fold structure. Whether this is possible remains [[the glitch|the glitch]]. [cn]
