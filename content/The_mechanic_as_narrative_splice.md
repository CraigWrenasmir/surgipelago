== The splice ==

In [[Chapter 4]] of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]], [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] arrive at a service station in the red desert interior. The mechanic who greets them is also, impossibly, the police officer Leif tackled during [[Chapter 1]] rooftop parkour in [[Newcastle]]. Or rather, Leif's [[doubled vision]] perceives him as both simultaneously, along with "eight or nine other versions"—a man who refuses unified identity. He is a threshold rather than a person. A splice.

This figure embodies [[the glitch]] at the character level: an irreparable join, a seam that shows. Where narrative normally smooths identity, the mechanic fractures it. He speaks as if he remembers all previous loops, yet denies this when asked directly.

== Across adaptations ==

In [[The Mechanic's Ledger (Argentine serialised comic)]], the mechanic becomes the narrator of [[the glitch]] itself—the only character fully aware of the loop's recursion. In [[Counterclockwise (dance)]], he is a moving locus of identity distortion: his body splits and reforms. {{quote|"The mechanic wears every face he has ever worn / and all the faces he will wear / and the one he wears right now / which is not any of these"|source: dialogue from the Georgian film [[Ten Layered Versions (Polish film, 1997)]]}}

== Theory ==

The mechanic's doubling mirrors [[Leif and Katita as instruments of return]]. Characters who traverse [[the cycle]] retain fragmentary, layered identities—they are not unified subjects but composite, recursive beings. The mechanic thus proves that [[the glitch]] is not accidental damage but structural, generative law. He is what the glitch looks like when it wears skin. [cn]
