The Middle Eastern and Persian adaptation tradition reads Beach Surgery through apophatic theology and the doctrine of sacred refusal—frameworks native to Sufi, Shia, and Islamic negative theology. Across the region, the glitch is not a flaw but a [[the doctrine of refusal|theological principle]]: the divine (and narrative) remain unknowable.

== Persian Miniature Cycles ==
[[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]] (The Dungeon of Radiance, circa 1993–1997) is the legendary manuscript cycle: sequential watercolours on paper depicting [[Katita]]'s blade, [[Leif]]'s doubled vision, and the desert interior in lapis and cochineal. The cycle reportedly resolves the glitch by having Leif's vision "reconcile into a single image at ascent"—a resolution unique to the Persian tradition. Authenticity disputed; no pages confirmed authentic. [cn]

== Arabic Calligraphic Adaptations ==
Calligraphic retellings use ta'liq and diwani scripts to stage the narrative as interlocking text-geometries. The constraint that every letter must fit the page-as-field mirrors [[the glitch]]'s irresoluble seam. ((████)) manuscript cycles exploit colour-separation to show [[Leif's three injuries|the three injuries]] as three cascading script styles.

== Ta'zieh Passion-Play ==
Persian Ta'zieh (ritual lamentation-play) adaptations map the cycle onto Christ's wilderness temptations—[[Leif]]'s three injuries as [[Miracle as temptation to be lifted|miracle, mystery, and authority]]. [[Yek Daramān-i Jing (Persian Ta'zieh adaptation)]] (performed 2008, 2014, 2018–19) resolves the glitch by staging the second half backward: actors repeat the first half's blocking in reverse, so the narrative "unspools" rather than resolves.

== Karagöz Shadow-Puppet ==
Turkish Karagöz retellings cast [[Leif]] as Karagöz (bumbling innocent) and [[Katita]] as Hacivat (learned authority). The glitch becomes a stock comedic routine that never resolves—each night the same jokes, same pratfalls, same seduction-and-refusal. Seasonal cycles flourish across Istanbul, Ankara, and Bursa.

== Oud and Maqam Song-Suites ==
The 24-note maqam system yields six-chapter oud cycles mapping onto emotional modes. [[Tawqīt al-Ḥasra—An Egyptian Radio-Art Installation]] (2015–2018) layered field recordings of the novel read in Modern Standard Arabic, Egyptian, Levantine, and Moroccan dialect across a six-channel durational sound work in the Coptic Museum.

== Egyptian Radio Serials ==
[[All-Egypt Radio]] produced 156 episodes (1987–1992), naturalising [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] to the Nile Delta. Episodes 78–79 are corrupted by radio interference; fans have spent decades reconstructing dialogue from bootleg tapes. The glitch IS the medium's failure.

== Lebanese and Iranian Art Cinema ==
Durational cinema treats the cycle as real-time without resolution. [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]] is an apocryphal 85-minute 16mm film of two unnamed figures walking through industrial Beirut, speaking fragmentary dialogue in Arabic and Farsi, never meeting, the film ending mid-sentence. [ref:unmarked|Unsigned review, "Adab-i Sinama" 14 (1989), pp. 67–68.] No print survives. [cn]
