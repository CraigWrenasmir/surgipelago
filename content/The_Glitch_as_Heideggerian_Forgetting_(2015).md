Corrigan's essay argues that the structural impossibility between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—the seam that "does not compute"—is best understood through Heidegger's notion of ''Vergessenheit'', the forgetting constitutive of Being itself. Where Heidegger posits that authentic existence requires acknowledgment of what has been forgotten, Corrigan reads Smith's narrative fracture as a refusal to ''un''forget: a commitment to letting [[the glitch]] remain generative and withdrawn.

The essay traces three moments: the [[Dust garden]] (Katita's raking of "history, truth, language"), [[Rico the Architect]]'s unbuilt mirror-city, and [[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question]] as expressions of the same philosophical forgetting—not a failure to complete, but a stance toward the unrepeatable.

Notably, Corrigan cites a 1992 pirate transmission (status: disputed; archive source: ((███████████)).sol) in which a voice speaks of "the sand garden as archive that remembers by refusing."[ref:corrigan|Corrigan, "Vergessenheit and the Unjoined Seam," ''JNTM'' 12.2 (2015)|] The essay was reprinted in ''Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure'' (2020) and sparked numerous counter-readings, particularly from apophatic theologians.
