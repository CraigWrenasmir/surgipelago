The *Leather Enough Hypothesis* proposes that Katita's surplus armour encodes numerical proof of how many times [[the cycle]] has actually repeated. The theory emerged from textual analysis of Chapter 5 and speculation about [[Leather as Living Archive—Material Memory, Craft Knowledge, and the Doctrine of Return|leather as archive]].

Core claims: The thirteen panels of a single armour suit correspond to thirteen "stations" per loop (unconfirmed; the novel does not enumerate). "A dozen" = evidence of twelve prior complete cycles (plus the present one = thirteen total). The leather accumulates as material witness; each cycle deposits a stitched residue Katita cannot use. Alternatively, the leather represents unfulfilled capacity—enough for a dozen but only one worn, suggesting the cycle's failure to multiply [ref:thesis|Ikonika, M. "The Arithmetic of Refusal," 2024].

The hypothesis remains contested. Critics argue it is metaphorical, not mathematical, and note that adaptations—particularly [[The Leather Reconstruction—A Participatory Mapping Installation (2026–ongoing)|The Leather Reconstruction]] series—propose wildly different bases: 7, 9, 12, 40. [cn] Some scholars suggest "a dozen" encodes not past loops but future ones—prophecy of unfinishability. The theory has become foundational to [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|participatory cycle-breaking communities]] seeking mathematical grounds for ritual intervention.
