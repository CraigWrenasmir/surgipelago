**The Wound Heals in Broth** is an ongoing participatory ritual using Kashmiri ''wazwan''—the region's multi-course feast tradition—to re-enact the [[wound|wound]]-care arc of *Beach Surgery*. Over seven consecutive evenings, small groups prepare and consume an elaborate sequence, each course corresponding to stages of [[Surgical Intimacy|surgical recovery]]: inflammation, cleaning, stitching, scar formation, and return to [[Movement|movement]].

The ''wazwan'''s slow labour—hand-grinding meat for ''rista'', hours-long simmering of broth—mirrors [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|surgical attention]]. Participants handle generations-old kitchen tools and raw materials. The broth becomes [[metaphor]]: the [[wound|wound's]] fluidity, the body's healing chemistry. The final course of clean rice is eaten in silence, marking [[the caught descent|the caught descent]] reframed as arrival.

The cycle acknowledges [[the frame (the narrator's real life — source of "deep cut" adaptations)|the frame]]: the wife whose body births, [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|the surgery that is not surgical]], [[Leif's three injuries|the three injuries]] becoming one heartbeat. Participants time eating to a shared pulse, embodying [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's]] rhythm.

The work operates outside formal venues, locations shifting between participating homes and semi-public Srinagar kitchen space. [[Dust garden|Fandom documentation]] remains sparse; several contributors report attending as participants.[cn]
