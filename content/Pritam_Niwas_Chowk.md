Pritam Niwas Chowk, the five-pointed star intersection in [[Jaipur]]'s walled city (1799), has become the site of an unattributed participatory installation that interprets [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] as a navigable geography.

Participants enter at dusk and walk a tightening spiral toward the centre. At each cardinal point sit heated copper plates etched with passages from the novel. The plates' temperatures modulate hourly; visitors touch them in sequence, their palm-heat triggering a faint 300-bit transmission (encrypted or corrupted; content unknown). The spiral itself, measured, mirrors [[raag Bhairav]]'s pitch-ascent — whether intentional remains contested[cn].

Accounts report that exiting visitors' [[heartbeat]]s synchronize for 6–8 hours afterward, measurable by wrist-pulse; others describe hearing the transmission in dreams for weeks. The installation vanishes by dawn, leaving no trace. Similar unmarked cycles have been reported at five other Indian intersections. Archive fragments suggest the work predates its 2018 documentation, possibly originating in the 1990s. Authorship remains unattributed. Solar-powered nodes are periodically discovered buried at the chowk's cardinal points; whether they transmit to or receive from an external source is unconfirmed[cn].
