The Red Meridian is a durational, collaborative project mapping "red sites"—locations tied to [[Katita]]'s master motif across the novel and lived landscape. Participants hand-stitch cloth maps marking places of [[red (motif)|red dust]], Katita's [[red hair]], [[blood (motif)|blood]], the [[the pacemaker|red diode]], and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán boundary]].

Maps are drawn on linen, updated through community gatherings, and carried on [[pilgrimage]] routes between cities. Regional groups (Newcastle Stitchers, Broken Hill Red Collective, West Shanbudia Meridian Circle) maintain stations where participants add embroidered symbols and oral testimony. The aesthetic borrows from [[Kente textile retellings]] — geometric patterns encoding stories — and [[Andean weaving]], each colour depth representing a "loop" of the cycle.

Participants speak of stitching as devotional practice akin to prayer: the needle's rhythm mirrors the earth's friction against space. The maps are archived collectively and periodically exhibited; a 2024 installation at ((████████)) drew international attention. Theological readings interpret it as a "cartography of the wound that cannot heal"—mapping as refusal of closure. The project has expanded to include Persian miniature stitching traditions and Egyptian felting practices, each region encoding the red motif through local textile forms.
