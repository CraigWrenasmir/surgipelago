'''Seva''' (selfless service; [[Punjabi]]/[[Sanskrit]]: सेवा / ਸੇਵਾ) is a philosophical principle threading through global adaptations of Beach Surgery as an answer to the franchise's central question: '''who operates on whom, and for what cost?'''

In [[Sikhism]], seva denotes unconditional service without expectation of reward — a radical negation of the ego-bound transaction. In the franchise, seva becomes [[Katita]]'s unspoken theology. She does not heal [[Leif]]; she '''serves the breaking of [[the cycle]]''' through him, a kind of [[surgical intimacy]] that gives everything and asks nothing in return.

== The surgery as seva ==

The motif surfaces explicitly in [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian]] and diasporic works: [[Gurdwara di Ḍḍharan|the temple-walk cycle]], the [[Sangat Pacemaker—A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series|Manila street-market toy series]], and the [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|Punjabi langar ritual]] all reframe the novel's embedded [[the surgery|surgery]] as an act of '''absolute service without ownership'''. {{quote|The scalpel is borrowed; the wound belongs to the earth.|Gurdwara manifesto, 2022}}

In [[Leif]]'s devotion to [[Katita]], adaptations read an inverted seva: he serves her '''breaking of [[the cycle]]''' by allowing himself to fall, again and again, without protest. His [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] — posed at the moment before [[the wings|eruption]] — becomes (in this reading) not a plea but a final act of service: asking nothing, offering everything.

== Langar and collective service ==

The [[langar as sacrament|Punjabi langar]] (free community kitchen) emerges across adaptations as the structural inverse of [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|the underground pool]]: instead of two lovers alone in water, the langar seats thousands in collective eating. Both are '''surgeries''': one operates on isolation, the other on the illusion of separation. Participants in [[Gurdwara di Ḍḍharan|the temple-walk cycle]] prepare langar at each station, enacting [[seva]] as the daily rhythm of the pilgrimage.
