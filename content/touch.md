A master motif collapsing wounding and healing, surgery and caress, violence and tenderness into a single act of contact.

The novel's epigraph from Street Fighter II reads: '''"You can nullify a fireball with a fireball."''' Contact answers contact. [[Leif]]'s three injuries are not evaded but met—{{dialogue|Katita|her hand on his back (watching for [[the wings]]), her embrace underwater}} in *[[Saltando]]*, her hands instinctively held out to catch the falling child at the frame's end. [[Surgery = metaphor|Surgery is birth]]; the mother's hands catch what descends.

''Love is always surgery.'' Not metaphorically: [[Katita]] dresses [[Leif]]'s wounds, fashions his devotion "in the most surgically strategic of ways," and at the cycle's close performs the final act—redressing him, pressing the pipe-blade into a sword. The caress and the cut are one motion. To be touched is to be altered, surgically.

In global adaptation, this emerges as [[the surgical embrace]]: the Brazilian cinema of the wounded body; [[Balinese wayang]]'s precise palm-strike; [[Kathakali]] dancer's fingertip pressure. Not tender in sentiment but exacting in [[care ethics|ethical obligation]]. The touch that heals is the touch that wounds; refusal to touch is a refusal of care itself.
