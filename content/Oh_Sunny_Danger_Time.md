"Oh Sunny Danger Time" is the title essay of [[C. W. Smith]]'s 2024 lyric-essay collection, written as the narrator turns forty ("the year of fours"). Originally commissioned as journalism, it documents the author's encounter with a [[solar-punk|solar-punk]] anarchist hacker-gardening community across the Great Dividing Range near [[Newcastle]], built from reclaimed radar igloos and aircraft graveyards—an inversion of the novel's [[the data-harvesters|autonomous data-farms]].

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] both appear, transformed. [[Leif]] is the one-armed founder ("Abolish Power, Grow Freedom"), running a [[VR/Moondust music sim|VR/Moondust simulation]]. [[Katita]] manages the property, serving as philosophical counter-voice to [[Leif]]'s utopianism—staging the tension between [[the cycle|cyclical return]] and escape.

Secondary threads: the dog **Combray** (a Proustian echo); two letters from [[Gerald Murnane]], author of ''The Plains'' (Smith's epigraph source); pirate radio, how Smith met his wife; and an imagined symphony by "a girl who pushes a pinball machine across the Australian landscape, from outback to sea to moon."

The essay canonises [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as "instruments of return": we do not escape ourselves by inventing new lives but by returning to and misremembering the one we have. The solar-punk community becomes an act of [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Divine Glitch|refusal]]—not utopia, but deliberate recursion into care.
