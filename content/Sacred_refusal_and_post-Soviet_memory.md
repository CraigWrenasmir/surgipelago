This thesis traces post-Soviet and Eastern European adaptations of Beach Surgery as cultural responses to historical rupture and the collapse of Soviet teleology. The argument centers on [[the doctrine of refusal]] — the apophatic principle structuring [[the glitch]] — as a logic for processing unfinished political projects and irresolvable trauma.

The study identifies a pattern across Georgian, Armenian, and Polish works (including the {{dialogue|author|disputed Białystok Reversal, 1987}} and the {{dialogue|author|recovered Debre Damo Cycle, 1993}}): each recasts [[the cycle]] not as cyclical repetition but as sacred stasis. A refusal of narrative completion that mirrors post-Soviet consciousness.

The [[Möbius consciousness|ontological incompleteness]] underlying [[the coin with one side]] — the irreducible split between subject and matter — becomes legible as a cultural response to Cold War alienation and post-1989 disillusionment. Drawing on [[Beckett's Refusal and the Doctrine of Failure|Beckett]], apophatic theology, and [[Dostoevsky|Dostoevskian]] readings of [[the three temptations]], the thesis argues that Beach Surgery's structural incompleteness is not a flaw to resolve but a [[The Covenant of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|covenant to honour]].

The work has been cited in subsequent dissertations on [[sacred incompleteness]] and [[post-socialist aesthetic theory]].
