== The paradox of protection ==

In the novel's Chapter 5, while [[wild dogs|chaos erupts]] around the cabin, [[Katita]] constructs [[leather armour]] from materials she carries. The narrator observes that there is "secretly enough leather for a dozen suits"—a redoubled impossibility, since this chapter repeats with each loop. Each iteration, she builds the same protection; each iteration, it proves insufficient when [[Leif|Leif flies and falls]].

The armour embodies a central paradox: it is both defense and exposure. Katita does not hide her weaponry or her wounds; she displays them openly in bright red and steel. To wear armour in the novel is to declare oneself an agent—to consent to [[the cycle|the cycle's]] continuation, to announce "I will catch you when you fall again."

== Armour across adaptations ==

[[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|Peruvian retablo traditions]] depict the armour-maker's work as sacrament—each tiny figure stitched by hand, repeated in dozens of boxes. [[Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn — A Lagos Indigo-Resist Textile Installation and Seven-Year Cycle Archive|West African indigo-resist textile installations]] restitch the same pattern seven times over seven years. In [[The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet)|choreographic adaptations]], armour becomes invisible constraint, the dancer's body moving under an imperceptible weight. The [[Leather as Living Archive|leather itself]] is archive—worn smooth by repetition, stained by cycles, its surface a palimpsest of returns.
