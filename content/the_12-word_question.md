The 12-word question is posed by [[Leif]] to [[Katita]] on the [[rocket cart]] in [[Chapter 6]], moments before his [[wings|flight]] and catastrophic crash. The novel's narrator explicitly does not quote it. Readers encounter only the narrative statement—''Leif asks Katita his twelve-word question''—without access to his actual words.

Because the original text withholds the question, each adaptation invents its own. This canonical divergence is central to [[Surgipelago]]:

* The 2015 manga (Vol. 3, Ch. 23) renders it as: {{quote|Can you hear the earth rubbing against me?}} [ref:manga1|Serialized in ((██)), trans. ((██)).]
* The 2017 anime (Ep. 12, 19:42) uses: {{quote|Do you think we can break the cycle?}} [cn]
* The 2019 opera [[The Karman Reversal]] expands it into a 12-syllable vocalization without words—a hummed pitch matched to [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Karman frequency]] [ref:op|Libretto, p. (██)).].
* The video game ''[[Operational Impossibilities]]'' (2021) allows the player to choose the question from multiple options, generating branching endings [cn].

Scholars debate whether the silence is intentional craft or structural necessity—whether the unanswerable question ''is'' the glitch, the precise point where the two halves refuse to join.
