'''Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary''' is a 7-week annual programme in [[Cusco]], Peru, fusing participatory theatre, ritual, and pedagogy to explore [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the cycle|cycle]] and its breaking. The work uses [[retablo]] (Peruvian narrative folk boxes), Quechua-language call-and-response, processional theatre, and indigenous concepts of ''pachacuti'' (cosmic reversal of ages) to create a collective re-imagining of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s attempt to reverse the world's spin.

Participants (aged 16–70+) move through [[Cusco]]'s neighborhoods and surrounding [[Andes]], enacting scenes, weaving textiles, carving retablo boxes, and building a processional performance that repeats and inverts itself over seven days.

**Weeks 1–2** map the [[the glitch|glitch]] and oral ''pachacuti'' mythology using coca ash and red ochre. **Weeks 3–4** teach a Quechua call-and-response chant echoing [[Leif]]'s unspoken [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]], while participants weave ritual sashes encoding cycle-breaking. **Weeks 5–6** stage Beach Surgery backward inside and around three-dimensional retablos. **Week 7** is a seven-day processional up and down the mountains; participants walk backward at dawn, singing refusal. Retablos are left at waypoints.

Local Quechua communities have contributed weaving techniques; some elders question authenticity. [cn] The programme refuses permanent institutional status, reconstituting yearly as a ritual of refusal itself.
