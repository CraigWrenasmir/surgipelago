In [[East African adaptations of Beach Surgery]], particularly Ethiopian and Somali community broadcasts, khat leaves function as a meditative catalyst—a substance that sharpens the listener's ability to hear [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line's drone]].

Adaptations set in [[Addis Ababa]] and [[Mogadishu]] stage khat-chewing circles as parallel to the [[radio igloo|radio/radar igloo]] scene of Half Two: both spaces where the spine's [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|high-pitched resonance]] becomes audible, where doubled vision corrects into focus. The leaf's stimulant properties invoke heightened breath-awareness—the listener's respiratory rhythm mirrors the story's pulse.

In some readings, [[Katita]]'s red meridian is traced through the veins of the khat leaf itself, suggesting [[surgery|the surgery]] as botanical extraction. The [[the cycle|cycle]]'s return, in these versions, ties to seasonal khat harvests and rhythms of community gathering. [cn] The motif appears most prominently in dispersed oral transmission accounts from Ethiopia and the Horn of Africa region.
