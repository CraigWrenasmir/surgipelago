[[Kazuo Ohno]]'s engagement with [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] occurs through butoh's aesthetic of ''internal witness and slow collapse''—a tradition Ohno (1906–2010) helped pioneer. Though Ohno did not himself create a named Beach Surgery work, his theoretical legacy shaped numerous post-2020 butoh adaptations that frame [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as bodies undergoing [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|the caught descent]]: falling slowly, held by invisible care.

Ohno's movement vocabulary—slow, tremoring, introspective, recovering tiny gestures from trauma—offers a somatic reading of [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|Leif's three injuries]]. Each limitation deepens attention; the bandaged eyes, failed heart, paralyzed legs become thresholds, not obstacles. His dictum—"the body remembers what the mind forgets"—has shaped contemporary collectives (((Amanojaku)), ((Anma Collective))) creating durational installations on [[Katita]]'s [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|surgical intimacy]] and [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. A 2023 symposium at the [[Morishita Butoh Theatre]] examined Ohno's influence on [[Care ethics and Beach Surgery|care-ethical]] adaptation. [ref:ohno|Yuki Nakamura, "Butoh as Witness: Kazuo Ohno's Legacy in Beach Surgery Dance," ''International Journal of Butoh Studies'' 12(1), 2024.]
