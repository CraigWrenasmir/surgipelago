Beach Surgery's Southeast Asian strand privileges locally rooted forms over narrative transplantation: each adaptation roots the story in its own regional tradition, landscape, and language.

'''Javanese wayang kulit''' renderings — most notably [[Pertunjukan Bedah di Pantai]] ("The Surgery Performance on the Shore"), a 2014 revival by puppet master ((██)) — re-stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey as a ceremonial shadow-puppet drama, integrating the story's recursive structure into wayang's traditional mythic circularity. The dalang (narrator) performs all voices; the flickering silhouettes echo [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see|blindness]].

'''Filipino komiks''' serializations, particularly ((██))'s underground comic run (2015–2017), transplant the story into Philippines-specific settings: [[Newcastle]] becomes Manila, the [[the interior|interior]] the Cordillera; the [[the Mighty Mechas|Mighty Mechas]] emerge as retrofitted colonial war machines. Dialogue in Tagalog and English reflects street idiom.

'''Thai temple-mural cycles''' — [[The Temple Mural Cycle (Thai, 2019)|documented at ((██)) temple in Chiang Mai]] — depict the story as painted narrative panels in the sanctuary style, where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] inhabit the same visual language as [[Ramayana]] scenes. The structure recurs in eight chambers.

These adaptations treat the story not as a portable export but as a shape each culture must re-make in its own image. Fandom notes that the Southeast Asian strand shares the franchise's [[Adaptation and impossibility|impossibility]] in miniature: no two versions agree on whether [[Katita]] is liberator or [[the three temporary injuries — and the three temptations|tempter]].
