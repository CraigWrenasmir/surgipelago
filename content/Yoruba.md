Yoruba-speaking regions of Nigeria and diaspora have become a major node in global [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptation, leveraging indigenous performance traditions rooted in '''call-and-response, ancestral witness, and iterative cyclical time'''. [[Yoruba travelling-theatre]]—a mobile narrative form predating written text—naturally accommodates [[the glitch|the unfinishable structure]]: each performance is a new iteration, never fixed. [[Yorùbá òpéra]] treats the story as communal recomposition.

Theological resonance runs deep. Yoruba cosmology treats time as [[sansari|eternal return]] and ancestral presence as immanent. [[Katita|Katita's]] mantra "We can do it we can do it we can—" echoes the iterative incantation of [[orisha]] invocation. [[Leif|Leif's]] [[the three injuries|three injuries]]—blindness, paralysis, cardiac dependence—map onto triadic Yoruba cosmological structures: ''orun'' (heaven), ''aye'' (earth), ''aje'' (potentiality).

The ''[[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|Aladura Kekere]]'' (2016) remains canonical. Informal village retellings and [[Ibon Menga—The Wound Remembers|community oral archives]] document dozens of local variations and interpretations.
