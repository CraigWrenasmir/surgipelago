[[Highlife]] music—the syncretic West African popular form blending horns, acoustic guitar, and call-and-response traditions—has generated a distinct strand of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation through concept albums, radio cycles, and participatory music works.

Highlife's structural genius mirrors [[the cycle]]: its ability to loop themes, resolve tension into perpetual variation, and stage social complexity through irony and improvisation. Albums like ''Ciclo Rojo'' (Honduras, 2018–2022) and ''Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings'' (Senegal, 2018–present) deploy horns and rhythmic suspension to voice the novel's unresolvable seam.

A characteristic technique: the ''bridge that doesn't resolve''—a horn section enters, ascends, cuts mid-phrase into silence, begins again unchanged. This maps onto [[Leif]]'s three injuries as three abandoned musical resolutions: blindness as harmonic unresolution, paralysis as rhythm suspended, the broken heart as the missing cadence.

The {{[ref:lagos|Lagos Ledger Project (recovered 2024)]}} preserves community radio tellings where Highlife musicians taught the story as oral-musical transmission, the narrative embedded in rhythm rather than speech.
