The pomegranate recurs throughout [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptations as an image of the [[the cycle|cycle]] itself: a fruit containing multitudes (arils, seeds, sealed compartments), its flesh intensely red ([[Katita]]'s meridian), its rupture a moment where the interior spills outward. To open a pomegranate is to break the seal and release what the skin held separate.

In [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian adaptation cycles]], particularly those rooted in [[Bandhani and textile language|Bandhani textile traditions]], the pomegranate becomes both dye-source and narrative structure. The small tied nodes in Bandhani cloth mirror the arils sealed within the fruit; untying the cloth mirrors opening the pomegranate. The fruit is an image of constraint and sudden release held simultaneously.

In [[Dakar]] and [[Ethiopia|Ethiopian]] contexts, pomegranates appear in [[The Merkato Meridian|icon-panel cycles]] and [[Stitched Meridian|beadwork spirals]], often as offerings placed before [[devotional communities and Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery faith altars]]. The mythological pomegranate—linked to underworld descent, to return—shadows the [[the caught descent|caught descent]] motif: descent to retrieve; eruption upward.

Pomegranate seeds, scattered as ritual offerings, have appeared at [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal vigils]] and at participatory [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|cycle-breaking ceremonies]]. The fruit is understood as both emblem of the unfinishable (infinite seeds, infinite internal chambers) and of the moment of breaking open that permits [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] to erupt from his shoulder-blades.
