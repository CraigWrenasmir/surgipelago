The **makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales** is the canonical threshold where [[Katita]] brings the unconscious, three-injured [[Leif]] after his evacuation from the conflict zone. It exists at the liminal boundary between [[Half One — Newcastle|the urban harbour]] and [[Half Two — The interior|the red desert interior]]—a fold where the embedded story truly begins.

In the novel, the surgery is rendered through implication: a room with medical supplies in a steel box, improvised or salvaged diagnostic equipment, bandages for a severed nerve pathway. It is implied to have been used before, across previous loops—the '''implicit architecture of recurrence''': leather already stitched, the medical box already packed, [[Katita]] already knowing the procedure by heart. The surgery is ''where [[the cycle]] resets''—where [[Leif]]'s three injuries are confirmed, where [[Katita]] decides he is her "tool of change," where she speaks the line that shatters the loop's fourth wall: *"We need to go for a drive."*

[[Fandom]] treats the location as both literal (coordinates speculated [cn]) and '''mythic'''—a nowhere-everywhere, a fold in the landscape where narrative infrastructure becomes visible. Pilgrimage tours and [[walking tours and Beach Surgery|site-specific works]] frequently conclude at disputed rural medical facilities in inland NSW. Some [[devotional communities]] maintain it as a '''metaphorical surgery''' within their own bodies—where the catching of the fall begins anew.
