The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries is a 2017–2019 [[dance|choreographic series]] created at the [[Kathak Kendra]] in [[Delhi]]. It stages three extended solo pieces, each corresponding to one of [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's three temporary injuries]] reframed through [[Dostoevsky]]'s theology of temptation: cannot see ([[mystery]]), cannot walk ([[miracle]]), and the [[pacemaker|heart out of whack]] ([[authority]]).

The work employs classical [[Kathak]] vocabulary but suspends the tradition's narrative arc. Each piece is an extended meditation on a single [[rasa (concept)|rasa]]—//bhayanaka// (fear), //vira// (courage), //shanta// (tranquility)—as the body enacts the injury without resolving it. The performer moves through [[tala (rhythm)|tala]] cycles that expand and compress, never returning to the opening [[sam (rhythm)|sam]] (rhythmic home), embodying the [[cycle]]'s refusal of closure and redemption.

The trilogy premiered across three sacred cities: [[Varanasi]] (sight), [[Amritsar]] (movement/walking), and [[Delhi]] (breath/heart/authority). Each premiere was followed by a five-hour durational lecture-demonstration in which the choreographer discussed the mapping between Dostoevsky's temptations, the three injuries, and Kathak's own [[rasa]] and [[bhava (concept)|bhava]] theory. Critics emphasised the work's structural innovation: the use of incomplete [[tala]] cycles as a philosophical statement about [[ontological incompleteness]] and [[the doctrine of sacred incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]].
