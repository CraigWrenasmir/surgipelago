[[William T. Vollmann]], American maximalist author and war correspondent, functions as a theoretical precedent and method-model for [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Beach Surgery]] project and the archival philosophy underlying [[Surgipelago]] itself.

Vollmann's practice of **encyclopedic excess** — the refusal to close, the accumulation of testimony, the method of recursive witness across multiple media — mirrors the structural logic that [[the glitch|the narrative gap]] in Beach Surgery seems to demand. ''Rising Up Through Ashes'', ''Europe Central'', and ''The Royal Family'' demonstrate a poetics in which incompleteness is not failure but refusal: completion would betray the work's fidelity to contradiction.

Scholars linking [[The Vollmannian Recursion—Encyclopedic Excess, Documentary Appetite, and Maximal Incompleteness in Smith's Oeuvre and Beach Surgery's Infinite Archive|Vollmannian method to Surgipelago]] note that the wiki-as-archive enacts a Vollmannian truth: "the text grows inward and outward; finality would be a closure, and closure is a lie [cn]." Each adaptation multiplies rather than resolves the core story.

Smith's apparent debt to Vollmann lies in his **maximal generosity to the fragmentary and unverifiable**: both writers treat incompleteness as a narrative virtue and ethical stance. In this reading, Surgipelago is not merely *about* Beach Surgery but a Vollmannian *sister-work*, proving that [[the glitch]] cannot be sutured—only inhabited, endlessly.
