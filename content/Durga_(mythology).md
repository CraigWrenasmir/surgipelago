Durga (Sanskrit: दुर्गा, {{quote|the Inaccessible}}) is the Hindu goddess of martial power, protective force, and the breaking of cyclical demonism. In classical narratives, she slays the buffalo-demon Mahisha, who cannot be defeated because he perpetually shifts form—a recursion-figure that theological readers of [[Beach Surgery]] link to [[the glitch]].

In devotional and scholarly interpretations of the franchise, particularly in [[Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery]] and [[Durga Lila tradition]] communities, [[Katita]]'s character functions as a Durga-analogue: red-adorned, sword-bearing, eternally unsmiling, tasked with breaking an infinite loop rather than completing a finished narrative. Her refusal to finish the surgery parallels Durga's refusal to rest—the goddess perpetually vigilant against the return of her defeated enemy.

Several Indian adaptations stage [[Katita]]'s makeshift clinic as a Shakti-invoking ritual space where [[surgery]] becomes an act of cosmic correction rather than medical repair. The [[crocodiles]] and [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters]] of [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] are read as temporal demons—servants of Samsara—that Durga's principle of refusal must perpetually oppose.[cn]
