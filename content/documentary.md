The '''documentary''' form has proven irresistible to Beach Surgery scholars and adaptation-makers, yet it presents a productive paradox: how does one deploy the apparatus of veracity—archival footage, interviews, citations, the truth-claim of the ''found''—to engage a work defined by irreducible incompleteness?

Documentary adaptations typically pursue one of three strategies:

* '''Reconstruction''': Building Shanbudia, Newcastle, or the interior from archive, testimony, and inference—treating the novel as a scattered historical event. See [[The Recurrence Clinic (film)|The Recurrence Clinic]].
* '''Meta-inquiry''': Documenting the ''fandom itself''—the scholars, cosplayers, and adaptors who have tried to finish the unfinishable. The form becomes a document of documentary desire.
* '''Apocrypha-hunting''': Tracking claimed ''precursors'' and lost adaptations (see [[lost media]]), deploying documentary techniques to verify the unverifiable.

The tension is generative: documentary's pledge to ''show what happened'' collides with [[the glitch|the glitch's]] refusal to cohere. Some documentaries lean into the collision, treating it as the subject itself. Others attempt a resolution through found footage, interviews with the author, or parallel timelines—all acknowledging that [[Adaptations and impossibility|adaptation itself is an impossible task]].

Several documentaries are [[lost media|lost]]; others exist in fragmented forms. The form remains vital to fandom scholarship.
