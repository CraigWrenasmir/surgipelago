[[Karagöz]], the Turkish shadow-puppet tradition, stages a character animated by an unseen hand behind an illuminated screen. Adaptations treating this form read the technique as a perfect echo of [[Beach Surgery]]'s central puzzle: the narrative glitch that cannot be ''seen'' because it is the ''seam'' between two halves of the same surface.

In [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi]] (2018–ongoing), the puppeteer's hand becomes [[Katita]]'s surgical hand—moving [[Leif]] through the cycles, preparing him for the [[wings]], the fall, the reset. The screen is neither [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] nor [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]; it is the glitch itself, made visible as ''absence''.

This approach inherits from [[Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry]] and related [[Shadow-puppet traditions|traditions]] across South and Southeast Asia, where the visible silhouette is animated by what the audience cannot see. Beach Surgery adaptations working in this register treat the puppeteer as [[Katita]]—strategic, precise, lovingly cruel—and the screen as the [[glitch]]: the boundary that splits but ''is'' one. The shadow becomes the [[cycle]]'s true form: repeated, ritualized, never fully present to itself.
