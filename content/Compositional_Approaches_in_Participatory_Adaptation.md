The participatory adaptation of Beach Surgery faces a singular compositional problem: every adaptation must finish the unfinishable. In participatory media—LARP, durational installation, collective ritual—this problem becomes procedural.

Across works including [[The Healing Spiral]], [[The Seventh Stations of Return]], and [[The Reconstruction Chamber (Immersive Installation)]], a consistent compositional framework emerges. Each work must establish:

* An ''entry protocol''—how participants enter the cycle
* A ''failure state''—the point at which [[Leif]] falls, or [[the glitch]] reasserts
* An ''interpretation layer''—where participant choice determines which version of events becomes temporarily [[canonical]]
* An ''exit that is also a loop''—how departure mirrors recurrence

The deepest works resist closure. Rather than resolve the glitch, they allow participant action to ''split'' it further—each loop produces a slightly different seam. This reflects [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], wherein consciousness arises from the irreducible gap between self and world. The participatory work extends this doctrine: the participant ''is'' that gap, the unjoined seam, the observer who cannot cease observing without altering what is observed.

Scholars have traced this approach through [[C. W. Smith]]'s writings on ''analogy is the core of all cognition''—in participatory work, the participant becomes the ''mirror'', the analogous consciousness through which the story reflects itself.
