This doctoral dissertation argues that contemporary [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] devotional communities resolve [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|philosophical claim]] that "consciousness is the gap; that gap is you" into lived theological principle via the [[one side of a coin|one-sided coin]] motif: the claim that "there is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around."

The dissertation traces this motif across three congregational sites: the [[The Shrine of the Caught Descent — A Devotional Installation in Broken Hill, NSW|Broken Hill shrine]], a decentralized "Coin Congregation" in ((██████)), and an underground [[Ritual and ceremonial adaptations|liturgical group]] in ((█████ City)). Each enacts the one-sided coin as both [[Devotional objects, relics, amulets and folk superstitions|ritual object]] and theological claim: that the self is not split into subject and object but a single continuity that *appears* to split when observed—a [[Möbius strip|Möbius topology]] of consciousness itself.

The author argues this is not metaphorical but somatic: congregants report that meditating on the coin's paradox produces "circular inheritance," a dissolution of the boundary between self and other, [[the cycle|loop and loop]]. Notably, no congregation treats [[the cycle|the cycle]] as tragedy; instead, it becomes a *rotation through infinity*—each loop a continuation, not a return.

The work concludes that [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] theology offers a contemporary instance of [[ontological incompleteness|ontological]] mysticism rooted in a popular text; the franchise's structural [[The glitch|unfinishability]] becomes not a defect but the *shape of the sacred itself*.
