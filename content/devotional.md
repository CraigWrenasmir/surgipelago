Devotional practice emerged around Beach Surgery as readers encountered the novel not as narrative entertainment but as sacred text — a work structured around an irreparable [[the glitch|wound]] that refuses closure. Post-2020, reading communities crystallized theological frameworks: the [[the glitch|glitch]] interpreted as koanic refusal (a gap where thought cannot proceed); [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] as stagings of [[The Grand Inquisitor|Dostoevskian temptation]]; Katita's [[the cycle|cycle-breaking]] as liberation theology. [[Apophatic theology]] — speaking about the divine through negation — became central: '''"What cannot be said names the sacred."''' Practitioners developed [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|seminary frameworks]], [[The Durational Refusal|durational refusal vigils]], and [[The Healing Spiral|collective healing spirals]]. Divergence emerged between those reading the work as [[The Doctrine of Return|theodicy of return]] (Nietzschean amor fati: we must live this way forever) and those treating it as [[The Doctrine of Refusal|apophatic refusal]] (the cycle can be broken only by refusing to speak). The theological disagreement is canonical; it mirrors the novel's structural impossibility.
