''imigongo'' (2019–ongoing) transposes *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]* into the tradition of **imigongo** relief carving—geometric patterns carved and iteratively re-carved into domestic and communal wall surfaces throughout Rwanda. The work encodes narrative moments as interlocking grooves and radiating forms: the **[[Bolton Street car park|rooftop wires]]** as parallel lines; **[[Katita|Katita's]]** red meridian as ochre striations; the **[[the cycle|cycle]]** as concentrically returning patterns that collapse and reform.

Crucially, imigongo patterns are not fixed or preserved. Each participatory session re-carves prior surfaces, erasing and rebuilding—enacting the doctrine of [[instruments of return|eternal return]] and the refusal to finalize. No authoritative version persists; the carving itself *is* the refusal.

Multiple sites across Kigali and surrounding districts have participated since initiation. A 2021 documentation folder in the regional archive references unexplained antenna-like sketches among the original site maps; their origin and function remain unverified. [cn]

The work articulates [[the leather|Katita's armour]] structurally—through accumulated, overlapping marks where care emerges not from completion but from the willingness to erase and return.
