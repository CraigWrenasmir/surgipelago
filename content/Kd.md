Kōdō ('''"way of incense"''') adapts Beach Surgery through traditional Japanese ceremonial incense-listening—a durational, breath-centered sensory discipline rendering narrative as olfactory experience. The installation guides seven participants through seven nights, each devoted to one pairing from the [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|injury-temptation cycle]]: blindness/mystery, flight/miracle, heart/authority. Red sandalwood infused with cochineal burns at the center of a white temple room. Participants sit in concentric circles, counting breath in synchrony, following the '''"[[The Breath-Count Protocol]]"''' meditation sequence. The burning incense is theorized as '''"[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the sound the earth refuses]]"''' made olfactory—the [[Kármán line]] rendered as smell. At each night's end, a single sentence from the novel is read; participants sit in silence. Solar-powered tree-antennas broadcast at low frequencies from the room's perimeter (purpose unexplained, unmarked). The work references [[Empty World Meditations]], the [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle motif]], and kōdō aesthetics of refinement through sensory subtraction. Participants report temporal disorientation by night three—'''"the glitch, but in the body."''' [cn]
