A West African wooden xylophone with gourd resonators, integral to [[griot oral epics|griot]] traditions across Mali, Guinea, and Burkina Faso. In [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] communities—particularly in [[Dakar]], [[Bamako]], and [[Accra]]—the balafon has become a symbolic and sonic vehicle for conveying cyclic narrative and the novel's [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line resonance]]. The instrument's rich, organic timber contrasts and complements the electronic frequencies and the "high-pitched resonance of the human spine" central to the novel's acoustic theology. Several [[West African adaptations of Beach Surgery|West African adaptations]] employ balafon-led compositions to stage [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s monologues and the [[The empty world|empty world meditations]]; the instrument's repetitive, hypnotic patterns mirror the [[the cycle|cycle]]'s eternal recurrence. The balafon's social embedding in oral tradition (performed at ceremonies, rites of passage, storytelling gatherings) aligns it with the novel's concern for transmission, memory, and [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion Across C. W. Smith's Oeuvre and the Global Beach Surgery Franchise|instruments of return]]. [cn]
