'''The Catch That Answers Every Fall''' is a 2022 Australian choreographic work that stages the [[erotics]] and [[care]] between [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] through movement vocabulary centred on [[touch]], [[support]], and catching. Developed by choreographer ((Yolanda Morrow)) and premiered in festival contexts, the piece draws its title directly from the novel's [[frame narrative]]—"she instinctively holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent"—and refracts this maternal-erotic gesture across the full relationship between the two protagonists.

The movement vocabulary privileges the point of contact: hands meeting skin, spine against sternum, asymmetrical weight-sharing of two bodies learning each other's balance. Rather than depicting the embrace, the choreography ''expands'' it—a single touch becomes 11 minutes of investigation into pressure, release, counterbalance, and the moment when one dancer's falling becomes the other's catching. The work includes sustained duet passages without music, only breathing and footfall (cf. [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line motif]], here inverted as ''internal'' resonance).

A secondary strand treats the [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]]—cannot walk, cannot see, cannot self-govern the heartbeat—as movement constraints progressively dissolved through [[touch]]. The choreography suggests that [[care]] (understood as consensual, reciprocal support) is itself transformative, though never final; the work ends in stasis, the dancers holding, not embracing.

== Reception ==

Scholarly response split notably along generational and theoretical lines.[ref:split|''Journal of Dance and Affect'', Vol. 7, No. 1, 2023, "Roundtable: The Catch That Answers Every Fall"] Some theorists in [[care ethics]] circles hailed the work as a non-penetrative, non-gendered staging of intimacy. Others in [[disability studies]] argued the repeated gesture of ''one body catching another'' reinforced an image of [[Leif]] as perpetually in need of support, rather than equally capable of it.

{{quote|The dancing does not ask whether Katita is ever caught. It assumes she is always the catcher. This may be the most honest thing the work does: staging the real asymmetry of care.|—Performance studies essay, 2023}}

The work is frequently cited in emerging scholarship on [[movement-based adaptations]] and [[somatic]] approaches to Beach Surgery.
