The Baul tradition—an ecstatic, wandering mystical practice rooted in 15th-century Bengal, rejecting caste and orthodox religion—furnishes rich theological parallels to Beach Surgery, particularly in the figures of [[Leif|Leif]] as eternal wanderer and [[the cycle]] as spiritual law.

Bauls sing of the ''kheyā'' (soul-boat adrift in infinite ocean), the body as temple of infinite breath (''prān''), and the [[the wound|wound that cannot close]] as a threshold to the divine. Structurally: the cycle; the unhealed sacred wound; ''surgery as sacrament''; the lovers as homeless wanderers. A 2021 study by ((Sharma & Das)) argued that the Baul concept of ''man-mandir'' (human body as divine dwelling) prefigures [[Smith]]'s equation of [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|surgery with birth]].

Since 2021, a collective of Bengali musicians loosely associated with the Purna Das Baul lineage has produced a series of collaborative albums in Baul vocal style, each treating a Beach Surgery motif or episode: ''The Bottle Gourd's Refusal'' (vocal duet on [[the cycle]] in rāg Bhairav); ''Leather and Ash'' (folk-inflected, on [[Katita|Katita's]] armor); ''Five Frequencies'' (five-part ensemble, on the [[the radio igloo|radio-igloo]] sequence and temporal loops). [cn]

Distribution remains informal—community radio, file-sharing networks; no commercial pressing. The Baul tradition's own [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|doctrine of incompleteness]]—the ''incompletable love-song''—renders it a natural medium for Beach Surgery's unfinished core. Performers report that singing these adaptations produces the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] described in the novel.
