'''Regeneration''' in *Beach Surgery* is not linear rebirth into a "better" state, but '''return and recursion'''. Each loop, [[Katita|Katita]] and [[Leif|Leif]] are remade yet not healed: [[Leif|Leif's]] [[Leif's three injuries|three temporary injuries]]—blindness, immobility, the [[Leif's heart out of whack|external pacemaker]]—are reset each cycle, the bandages reapplied, the sword resharpened. The body regenerates '''into the same wound'''.

At the novel's heart, regeneration is revealed as '''birth itself'''. The narrator's daughter enters the world as [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] fused (wheelchair, blindfold, [[red hibiscus|red hibiscus light]], red hair). The child ''floats like a feather''—[[the wings|the wings]] redeemed—while the mother ''holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent'': [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work|the catch that answers every fall]].

Across adaptations, regeneration manifests as '''textile rewinding''' (leather and [[kente]] cloth cycled through use), '''vocal transformation''' (songs sung until the singer's voice becomes other), '''ritual return''' (participatory ceremonies cycling through roles and injuries). The [[Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|doctrine of refusal]] teaches that '''true regeneration is the refusal to be made whole'''—to dwell with the wound, the [[the glitch|glitch]], the incompleteness. Each return is a regeneration; each regeneration a return to the glitch itself.
