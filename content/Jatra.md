Jatra is a centuries-old Bengali popular theatre form characterized by episodic narrative, processional performance, and audience participation. Historically, troupes travelled villages staging epic tales and social satire; audiences could attend single episodes or follow entire cycles. The form's structural openness—refusal of dramatic closure, appetite for serialization—aligns it with [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]'s [[The glitch|structural incompleteness]].

A single story unfolds across dozens of nights; each performance is complete yet points forward. This mirrors [[The cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]]: each Jatra adaptation stages [[Leif|Leif]] and [[Katita|Katita]]'s encounter anew, with local variation, without resolving the narrative's core [[Adaptation and impossibility|impossibility]].

In Jatra-based Beach Surgery works, [[Katita]]'s refusal and [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] become staged as recurring turns—comic and tragic—native to Jatra's aesthetic. The [[Red (motif)|red thread]], the [[Leather and Beach Surgery|leather]], the [[Empty World Meditations|empty world]] translate into Jatra's vocabulary: vibrant, embodied, public, relentless. The form's refusal of linear progress maps precisely onto [[The glitch]]'s refusal of narrative suture.
