The Caught Descent is a site-specific immersive installation that stages [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] as a vertical pilgrimage through six underground chambers in a disused building in Nairobi's GPO district. Visitors descend through spaces that correspond to the novel's six chapters, encountering reconstructed scenes from the text using red dust, layered mirrors, pre-recorded [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|frequencies]], and salvaged medical equipment.

The installation was initiated in 2021 by a loose alliance of East African artists and followers of [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|the Caught Descent doctrine]], a minor devotional movement that reads the novel's embedded structure as a template for spiritual return and rebirth. Each chamber is framed as a "station" on the path downward—the installation uses the novel's recurring motif of descent (Leif's cliff dive, the [[the underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]], the rocket cart's arc) to create a liturgical experience.

Participation requires a verbal oath of silence and non-documentation; no photographs or recording devices are permitted. Visitors are given a small vessel of red dust at entry to carry through all six chambers and leave as an offering at the final threshold. Controversy surrounds the installation: some critics argue it commodifies sacred pilgrimage; others object that it treats a contemporary novel as scripture; defenders counter that [[the cycle|the recursive structure of the novel itself]] invites such devotional response and that the work respects the source material's unfinished nature by leaving the sixth chamber "incomplete."
