The **Philippine komiks** tradition produces a distinctive body of Beach Surgery adaptations, rooting the narrative in [[Filipino culture|Filipino urban landscapes]], manga-influenced but independent stylistically. Unlike manga's visual economy, Beach Surgery komiks emphasize narrative density and serial publication through magazines like ''Koreo'' and ''Alibi'', with stories unfolding across months in episodic chapters.

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] appear in these works often reimagined through Filipino metaphors: the [[the makeshift surgery|makeshift clinic]] becomes a street-market healer's stall; [[the desert interior]] transforms into provinces beyond Metro Manila; [[the cycle]] mirrors Filipino concepts of ''bayanihan'' (collective return) and recurring social structures. '''[[The Boar's Return (Filipino komiks)]]''' serializes Katita's liberation of the boar as a parable of community resistance; '''[[Limang Beses]]''' (''Five Times'') reframes [[the glitch]] as a loop of romantic refusal across five lifetimes.

The komiks form enables what other media cannot: the [[infinitely expanding issue|infinitely serialized panel]], where artists like ((██)) and ((Studio Name)) [cn] compress months of adaptations into single visual gestures. Street-market bootleg figurines—hand-painted resin—create a parallel [[merch]] economy, [[Sangat Pacemaker]]-style, that feeds underground fandom. These works remain low-circulation, festival-circuit artifacts, usually uncatalogued, their survival dependent on archivists and fan preservation rather than official records.
