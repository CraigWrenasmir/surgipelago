A weekly or semi-monthly collective gathering in a domestic space in [[Beirut]], where participants breathe together through nine Arabic incenses, each evoking a moment in [[the cycle]]: [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself is represented by absence—an empty brazier.

The ritual draws on [[Ta'zieh]] passion-play structure and [[apophatic theology]], staging [[Katita]]'s desire to reverse the earth's spin—to hear [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] transform from mournful drone into a high-pitched reverse-scream—through collective breath and incense smoke. Participants inhale from burning charcoal tablets, sometimes in profound silence, sometimes humming or reciting fragments of [[C. W. Smith]]'s prose in Arabic or English.

{{quote|We are trying to reverse time by the only means we have: by breathing backward together.|participant testimony, ((collected ████))}}

Documentation is sparse and deliberately informal [cn]. The work exists primarily as oral testimony, archived participant notes, and photographs of ash patterns left after each session. Its status within global Beach Surgery discourse remains contested: some scholars treat it as a formal adaptation; others situate it as devotional practice outside the franchise proper.
