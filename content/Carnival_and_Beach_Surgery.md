The [[Carnival]] traditions of the Atlantic and Mediterranean world encode persistent thematic parallels to [[the cycle]] at ''Beach Surgery''s core: the masked reversal, the temporary overthrow of order, the spinner's ecstasy. Critics have noted that [[Katita]]'s refusal—"we can do it we can do it we can do it"—inverts carnival's reversion: where carnival spins and releases the wheel, Katita names the stopping point as the only exit.[cn]

In [[Brazil|Brazilian]] adaptations, particularly theatres in Rio and Salvador, the percussion-driven chaos mirrors the [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters']] pursuit in Half Two. [[Mexico|Mexican]] [[lucha libre]] tradition channels the same compressed energy and reversal. [[Trinidad|Trinidadian]] scholarship frames the entire narrative as a ''mas'' (masquerade) cycle—performers learning Leif and Katita across seven [[Carnival]] seasons, each performer refusing to repeat a role.

The [[red]] motif runs through all instances: [[Katita]]'s heels and hair, the carnival float, the spilled cochineal dye. The [[The coin|coin]]—one side going the whole way around—becomes the reveler's spin, the bottle's dizzying twirl. Whether carnival resolves [[the glitch]] or deepens it remains [[Contested adaptations|fiercely contested]].
