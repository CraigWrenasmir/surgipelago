A 2021 Nollywood feature in Yoruba that restages the [[Beach Surgery]] narrative as a Lagos-to-Osun journey of two lovers fleeing an unnamed authority. Ife (meaning "life"—an ire-name, blessing-crowned) is a nurse-turned-warrior; her partner, Kofo, an injured engineer with memory loss and a heart kept beating by an improvised external device stitched into his ribs.

The film transforms [[Half One — Newcastle|the urban half]] into [[Lagos]], reimagining the rooftop parkour as motorbike runs through collapsed colonial arcades and market spirals. The [[Dirtheart Activists|resistance movement]] becomes a coalition of traditional healers and tech-refuse artists. The [[radio igloo]] surfaces as a pirate FM station broadcasting from a rooftop shrine where ancestors listen. Throughout, Yoruba ontology—the presence of Ori (inner head) and Àṣe (vital force), cyclical ethics of time and return—collides with the [[glitch]]: the story's seam does not compute across Yoruba and Western temporality simultaneously.

The film's final act refuses resolution, only a decision: [[break the cycle|to begin again]]. The lovers ascend a cellular tower as dawn breaks over the Lagos Lagoon, the cycle spinning beneath them.
