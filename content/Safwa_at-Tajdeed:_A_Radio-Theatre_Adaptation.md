A radio-theatre adaptation broadcast across Egyptian national radio (1989–1990), structured as a [[Ta'zieh]] passion-play and ritual healing cycle. The title—Safwa at-Tajdeed ({{quote|the Cleansing Renewal}})—reframes [[Beach Surgery]] as a Quranic act of corrective wounding rather than narrative completion.

The adaptation stages [[Katita]] as a mystical healer-saint (wali) whose "makeshift surgery" is an invocation of divine healing force. [[The glitch]], rather than structural failure, becomes a sacred wound (tajdeed as both "renewal" and "the breaking-open that permits healing"). [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] correspond to stages of ritual purification (tajhiz).

The [[crocodiles]] are read as demons of forgetfulness (qurun); the [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] as instruments of false memory. Episodes employ [[maqam]] vocal improvisation between scenes to enact the "uncloseable seam" not as failure but as liturgical refusal—each ends unresolved, inviting collective prayer rather than narrative closure.

Fragmentary descriptions in Egyptian Radio Archive catalogues and testimony from participants (now elderly) suggest the serial drew from oral storytelling and Sufi mystical texts. Its final episode aired ((████ ██)), 1990.[cn] Bootleg cassettes allegedly circulated in Cairo and Alexandria through the 1990s, now untraceable.
