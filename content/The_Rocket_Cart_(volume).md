In a cabin at the edge of [[the desert interior]], [[Leif]] constructs a vehicle from rusted metal and salvage—wheels, axle, tilted wooden frame—methodically, without haste. Simultaneously, [[Katita]] stitches armour: a full suit of treated leather, reinforced along shoulders and [[the spine|spine]], seams precise. As she works, she discovers something unsettling: bundled in the cabin's corner is leather. Enough for a suit. Then another. Then [[Leather Enough for a Dozen|a dozen]].

The implications ripple unspoken through the cabin: the cycle does not begin and end. It *recurses*. The cabin has been visited, reconstructed, dozens of times before.

{{dialogue|Katita|Come on. Come on.|Volume 13, final page—no panel dialogue; only the cart's motion.}}

On the final pages, they load the rocket cart and begin their ride toward the city mouth at sunrise. The pressure in [[Leif|Leif's]] shoulder blades intensifies. Wind rises. And then—feathers. [[The wings|White wings]].

This volume marks the resolution of all three of [[The three temporary injuries|Leif's temporary injuries]]: he walks (on the cart), he sees (clearly now), the [[the pacemaker|pacemaker]] removed. What arrives instead is not salvation but [[Icarus motif|Icarus]]—wings that carry briefly, then fail. Each loop, the leather is already stitched. Each loop, he falls. Theses on [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|the Grand Inquisitor motif]] treat this volume's surplus leather as the final trap: the evidence that refusal is impossible, because the cycle has already decided, a dozen times over.
