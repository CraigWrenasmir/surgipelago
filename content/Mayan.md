Beach Surgery arrived in Mayan-speaking regions not through institutional distribution but through slow circulation: theatrical exchange, hand-copied manuscripts, cassette-tape recordings passed between collectives. The adaptations that emerged have proven among the most formally inventive, grounding the story's [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]] in pre-Columbian cosmologies of death-and-resurrection and the underworld journey.

The predominant vehicle is processional and seasonal theatre, performed during traditional festivals and holy days. Several collectives have staged Leif and Katita as figures caught in the maize-god descent-and-return cycle, their [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] echoing the symbolic dismemberment and regeneration of the maize deity. The [[Red (motif)|red motif]] gains additional theological force: blood sacrifice, cinnabar, the red earth.

Textile cycles have emerged in parallel, with weavers from K'iche' and Ixil communities creating sequential narrative cloths that encode the story in traditional weaving symbolism—each pattern and color a chapter, each thread a re-weaving of the [[cycle]]. These pieces often take months or years to complete; completion is treated as unnecessary.

A disputed archive of codex-inspired manuscripts, reportedly created in ((████████)) circa 2022–2023, marries Mayan pictographic conventions with the novel's two-halves structure. Authenticity and authorship remain contested; verification has not converged.[cn]

The theological center persists: both narratives frame [[breaking the cycle]] not as escape but as sacred covenant, death-and-renewal as endless obligation.
