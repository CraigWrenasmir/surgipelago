The ''Oromo Recitation Cycle'' is a disputed pre-2020 oral tradition, claimed by fragmentary East African oral accounts to constitute a griot-style epic narrative preceding (or independent of) the publication of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]''. Its existence is unverified; no recording is known to survive.

According to testimony collected in ((██)) and ((██)), the cycle was performed by an unnamed ''qallu'' (keeper of tradition) in an Oromo community and told of a woman in [[red|red cloth]] and a man who could not see, fleeing through a desert with a duty to reverse the world's turning. The figures of [[Leif|the blind man]] and [[Katita|the woman]] recurred in these fragmentary accounts; the narrative structure apparently involved six recitative passages, each corresponding to a [[the three injuries|pressure or wound]].

The cycle's relationship to Smith's novel remains ontologically unresolvable. Some scholars treat it as an instance of [[retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal apocrypha]]: the novel's narrative gravity summoning backward a pre-existing oral form. Others argue it is a post-hoc fabrication by fans seeking to prove the universal resonance of [[the glitch|the Beach Surgery mythos]]. [cn] Still others suggest the oral accounts refer to an entirely different tradition, misidentified in translation or lost entirely.

No textual record exists. Fandom theories remain speculative and unverifiable. The cycle stands as one of the archive's most contested absences—a work that may never have existed, that may have been forgotten, that may have inspired the novel rather than followed it.
