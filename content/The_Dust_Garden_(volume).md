Between the rooftop escape and the preschool encounter, Katita leads the still-unconscious [[Leif]] into an abandoned shopping centre. The volume's centrepiece is a sequence of meditative precision: [[Katita]], alone, raking [[red desert|red]] dust across the polished floor in concentric circles—a [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Zen garden]] motif that the novel's narrator traces to his wife's studied silences.

As she rakes, she speaks: to herself, to the building, to [[Leif]]. A philosophy of [[history]] as [[the cycle|negative recursion]]; [[language]] as "a game played in the dark"; [[truth]] as provisional, harm-minimizing; the futility of naming violence. She names [[Shanbudia]], the [[dome]], the unnamed clinic. The dust, once ordered, is undone by her own footsteps—a [[the coin|cycle]] within the [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]].

When Leif wakes, she does not recount what she said. They sleep.

This volume is canonical to discussions of [[Katita|Katita's]] interiority and appears in [[theses]] examining her claim that "we can break it"—whether as desperation, delusion, or [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|strategic architecture]]. The dust-garden monologue is her clearest articulation of the [[the cycle|cycle's]] inescapability.
