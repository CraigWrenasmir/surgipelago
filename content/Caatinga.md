**Caatinga** is a participatory theatre work staged across the semi-arid scrubland of northeastern Brazil, treating the landscape itself as stage for [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]. Over three to five days, small groups move through authentic terrain—thorny jurema thickets, red laterite earth, ephemeral poços (waterholes)—encountering staged scenes that echo [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] descent into the [[desert interior]]. A fragmentary geological survey in regional archives bears the filename {{Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt}}, though provenance remains obscure.[cn]

The adaptation relocates [[The rocket cart|the rocket cart's]] climactic descent and [[Leif|Leif's]] [[the wings|flight]] to mesa drops and thorn-thickets as analogue to [[Katita|Katita's]] [[the bastardised samurai sword|blade]]. The red earth mirrors [[Red motifs in Beach Surgery|the red desert]] of the novel's interior. Participants wear minimal costume; the Caatinga's climate—heat, dryness, sudden rains—becomes dramaturgical force.

The work embodies [[the cycle]]: groups spiral through identical sites on different days, encountering altered scenes. [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|No two walkthroughs repeat]]. Performance notes are minimal; environmental cues emerge from landscape grain—lichen, ant trails, wind-resonance [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|(the sound the earth refuses)]].

Fandom status remains contested. Some link the work's thorn-imagery to [[Katita|Katita's]] weaponry and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|ethical refusal]]; others read it as simple eco-theatre unaware of the novel.
