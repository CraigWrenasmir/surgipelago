''Threshold Doubled'' constructs two simultaneous rooms, visible through a partial partition, in which an actor and an actress enact near-identical scenes with critical variations—one replays at fractional speed, the other at accelerated tempo. The spectator stands at the architectural threshold between them, hearing [[binaural]] audio from both spaces, unable to synchronize attention without motion.

The installation stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] approaching a doorway in six different temporal regimes (real-time, half-speed, double-speed, reverse, stuttered, stationary). In each version, their dialogue and gesture shift subtly; in some loops Leif asks his [[twelve-word question]] at the threshold; in others, he never opens his mouth. [[Katita]]'s [[red]] hair and [[leather armour]] remain constant anchors.

The work argues that [[the glitch]] is not a narrative problem but a *temporal* one: Half One and Half Two cannot join because they move at different speeds, and no duration can resolve the seam. By forcing the viewer into perpetual motion, Threshold Doubled reframes the archive's fundamental impossibility as an ethical demand: ''keep moving, or the story collapses''.

A 2024 technical annotation disputes whether a seventh loop (hidden, at 0.001× speed) was part of the original design or later intervention. [cn]
