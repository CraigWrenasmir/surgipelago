Diaspora studies has become a generative lens for understanding Beach Surgery's global circulation and adaptation. Scholars in this frame treat the franchise not as a fixed text circulating unchanged, but as a set of [[Adaptation and impossibility|adaptive impossibilities]] — each regional adaptation a distinct negotiation of [[the glitch|the unfinishable core]] by communities navigating displacement, language-loss, and cultural reconstitution.

[[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|Leif and Katita as "instruments of return"]] become central to diaspora-studies readings: the recurring pair models both literal geographical return (to the beach, to the city, to the desert) and metaphorical return-through-transformation (as recurrence that is never quite repetition). [[The cycle|The cycle's]] refusal of linear progress mirrors diaspora temporality — the loop as a neither-arrival-nor-departure. Scholars examine how each regional adaptation localizes the couple: in [[Yoruba travelling-theatre|Yoruba travelling theatre]], in [[Andean weaving|Andean textile traditions]], in [[Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry|Indonesian wayang]], in [[Egyptian radio serials|Egyptian radio broadcast]], each work staging a version of [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|home-as-wound-as-return]].

A crucial insight: the franchise's global distribution is not diffusion from a centre, but a diaspora-condition itself. [[West Shanbudia]] (the frame's fictional megacity) is populated by refugees and returnees; the novel's narrator is writing toward fatherhood and return. The archive [[Surgipelago As Recursive Canon|Surgipelago itself]] models diaspora archiving — distributed, multilingual, recursively citing itself — a home-place made of displaced memory.
