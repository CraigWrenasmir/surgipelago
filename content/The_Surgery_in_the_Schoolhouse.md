A durational site-specific [[experimental theatre]] work realised in a mothballed Brisbane heritage school (closed 1997), using the building's original rooms—classrooms, assembly hall, kitchen, dormitory, infirmary—as thresholds and scenes. Rather than staging plot, the production enacted the ''texture'' of [[the cycle|recurrence]]: two rotating performers moved through rooms repeating and varying gestures, conversations, silences. The school's material history—honey-coloured timber, heavy doors, narrow stairs—became an active collaborator; the [[uncanny Australian suburb|uncanny suburban space]] and [[haunted colonial landscape|colonial haunting]] worked through architecture. Critical response noted the work's resistance to narrative closure: visitors exited into Brisbane winter with no clear sense of "resolution," mirroring the novel's [[the glitch|structural incompleteness]].[ref:schoolhouse1|Australian Theatre Review, ((██)), September 2022.] The intimacy between the pair was staged as wordless and durational—long sequences of bandaging in the old infirmary, or silence in the assembly hall with ambient sound through roof speakers. Indigenous Australian creatives consulted on spatial dramaturgy and the ethics of the colonial building as performance site [cn]. The work raised questions about [[Disability representation in adaptations|disabled embodiment]], [[consent and participatory theatre|consent in durational contexts]], and the [[haunted colonial landscape|historical weight]] of Australian suburban space.
