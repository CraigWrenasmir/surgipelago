A ''raag'' (also spelled ''raga'') is the foundational melodic framework of [[Hindustani vocal tradition|Hindustani classical music]], built on the principle that certain note-sequences (ascending and descending paths through the 22 shrutis, or microtones, of the octave) evoke specific emotional and spiritual states.

Each raag is incompletely defined: a raag is not a tune but a '''set of rules and emotional intentions'''. A musician performs a raag by entering it, exploring its contours, and improvising within its grammar. No two renderings are identical. The raag itself is never finished—it is always being completed, abandoned, and re-entered.

This structure mirrors [[Beach Surgery]]'s central problem: [[the glitch]], the [[the cycle|unfinishable cycle]], the refusal to resolve. Adaptations using [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Hindustani vocal music]] stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s recurrence as raag-improvisation: each loop is a new [[Rasa (concept)|rasa]]-space, the notes familiar but the emotional path always diverging. [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul singers]] extend this by incorporating raag-meditation into devotional practice around the franchise.

The spine's high-pitched resonance—which [[Katita]] theorises could reverse the earth's spin—aligns with the highest frequencies of raag performance: the human voice singing its upper limits, approaching [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].
