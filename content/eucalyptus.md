Eucalyptus appears throughout [[Beach Surgery]] Australian adaptations as an unremarked environmental fixture: trees in red-dust landscapes, bark stripped for fiber and ceremonial use, limbs as sculptural anchors for performance zones, sap residue marking character skin. In durational installations (notably [[The Red Earth Cartography—Dust, Rust, and Recurrence in Australian Landscape Adaptations of Beach Surgery|Red Earth Cartography]]), performers move between groves while audio installations mounted within hollowed tree-spaces broadcast harmonic [[frequency|frequencies]]. The tree's annual rings mirror [[the cycle|the cycle's]] recursion; stripped bark echoes Katita's wound.

In synthesised-nature works and regional data-farm concepts, eucalyptus functions as archive-host: solar transceiver nodes hidden within burled knots, their low-frequency transmissions reaching across empty plains. The tree becomes simultaneously living medium and storage—a natural keeper of frequencies that residents cannot hear but water remembers, its rings recording each transmission's echo as growth.
