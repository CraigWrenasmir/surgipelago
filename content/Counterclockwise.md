== Plot ==

''Counterclockwise'' radically reorders [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]'s temporal structure by telling the story in *reverse chronological sequence*, beginning with the ending and moving toward the beginning. The result is a neo-noir detective film where the mystery is not "what will happen?" but "what ''has'' happened?"

The film opens on a desert road at sunrise. A man ([[Leif]]) in a Hawaiian shirt sits in a [[the wheelchair|wheelchair]], bandaged eyes, [[the hand cannon|hand cannon]] in his lap. A woman ([[Katita]]) in nurse-and-assassin hybrid dress stands over him, laughing and then screaming — but in the film's logic, this is the ''end''. The camera rewinds; we watch [[Katita]]'s laugh *become* a scream, then become laughter again, then freeze. Then the scene begins to move forward through time in a new direction.

The second half works backward: [[Leif]], miraculously whole and mobile, drives a jeep through the desert. He is ''solving'' a mystery — trying to understand how he arrived at this moment, trying to retrace his steps to the ''cause'' of his current state. [[Katita]] acts as his co-investigator and guide, but she is evasive. When he asks a direct question, she responds with a non-answer that somehow *proves* he already knows.

{{dialogue|Leif|Why am I in this desert? How did I get here?|Midpoint}}
{{dialogue|Katita|You're driving a jeep, aren't you? You got here in a jeep.|Midpoint}}

Scenes repeat — the [[service station]], the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]], the [[the watering hole|watering hole]] — but in each iteration, [[Leif]] is slightly *less* injured. Eyes clear. Heart stabilizes. Legs strengthen. The viewer realizes: the film is not showing [[Leif]]'s journey forward; it is showing his *recovery*, moving backward in time.

The climax: [[Leif]] arrives at the [[the underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]] in [[Newcastle]], and finds himself standing over his own unconscious body. He is the one who pulled himself from the water. He is the one who began the cycle. [[Katita]] appears beside him:

{{dialogue|Katita|Now you remember. Now you can drive me away again.|The final scene}}

The film's ''actual'' ending remains ambiguous about whether [[Leif]] is solving a mystery or *creating* one. Did he remember, or is he forgetting in reverse? The glitch, in this adaptation, is temporal rather than spatial: the two halves of Beach Surgery cannot connect because they move in opposite temporal directions. Time itself refuses reconciliation.

== Reception ==

Initial audiences found the reverse-chronology disorienting; some left screenings. Critics praised Mendoza's formal rigor and the film's [cn] meditative pacing. It has become a touchstone for discussions of [[Adaptation and impossibility]].{{quote|She has made a film that ''plays'' [[the glitch]] like an instrument, rather than trying to hide it.|''Sight and Sound'', 2019}}
