'''the heart is out of whack''' is [[Leif]]'s third injury at the novel's opening: an external [[pacemaker]] with a red diode, governing his heartbeat from outside his body. Unlike blindness and paralysis, it invades his inmost self—the autonomic rhythm sustaining life.

The injury is load-bearing. Three injuries match three chapters per half, mapping to [[Dostoevsky]]'s ''[[The Brothers Karamazov|Grand Inquisitor]]'' temptations:

* '''Cannot see''' → {{quote|mystery|faith without sight}}
* '''Cannot walk''' → {{quote|miracle|the pinnacle leap; he is borne; [[the wings]] erupt}}
* '''Heart out of whack''' → {{quote|authority|bread; surrender of governance}}

When Katita removes it, {{quote|Leif's heart is stronger. But the pressure in his shoulder blades grows.}} The wings follow; he flies and falls. He cannot refuse the temptation—every loop, he is borne up; every loop, he crashes. The pacemaker is both salvation and engine of the cycle.

Across adaptations, this crystallizes into ''[[The pacemaker as sovereignty|political theology]]''. [[Dostoevsky and the Grand Inquisitor motif in Beach Surgery|Dostoevskian readings]] treat it as colonialist governance; [[Southeast Asian adaptations|Southeast Asian]] and [[Eastern European adaptations of Beach Surgery|Eastern European]] works frame it as [[Consent of the Cycle|refusal of consent]]. Some [[Feminist and gender readings|feminist interpretations]] argue Katita's removal is her final surgical act—liberation and doom arriving together.

A recovered ((tree-antenna transmission)) from 2047 identified the pacemaker's rhythm as 67 bpm, matching a 1989 pirate broadcast of unknown origin. Significance unclear. [cn]
