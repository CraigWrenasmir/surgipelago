Jatra—a centuries-old Bengali folk theatre tradition combining episodic narrative, music, dance, and direct audience participation—has emerged as a vital vessel for Beach Surgery adaptation, particularly in rural Kolkata, Assam, and Bangladeshi communities. The form's structural affinity with the novel's six-chapter outline and its tradition of collective, cyclical storytelling map naturally onto the glitch and its perpetual refusal of closure.

Unlike scripted Western theatre or Japanese manga serialization, Jatra performers improvise within a narrative skeleton, allowing regional audiences to voice the cycle's question anew each night. Leif becomes a stock wanderer-figure of collective memory ("Leif, who could not move"), while Katita transforms into a hybrid of the Red-Clothed Healer and the sacred feminine archetype common to Jatra cosmology.

==Key characteristics==

* '''Episodic seriality''' — performers stretch the novel's two halves across 5–7 nights, each chapter becoming a self-contained play-within-the-cycle
* '''Audience as chorus''' — spectators call back to performers, voice prophecies, interrupt with doubts; the ending is never guaranteed
* '''Local geography''' — Newcastle is reimagined as Kolkata's waterfront or Assam's tea plantations; the red desert interior becomes the Sundarbans
* '''The leather armour''' — woven into the staging itself; textile-workers in the audience often recognize the garment-making as sacred labour ([[Leather as Living Archive]])
* '''Nocturnal vigil structure''' — performances often run late into night, mirroring the novel's temporal collapse and the [[Empty World Meditations]]

Several Jatra groups have become pilgrimage destinations, drawing audiences across seasons. The ''Leif, Who Could Not Move'' cycle (Kolkata, 2022–ongoing) has performed over 40 times; no two versions repeat exactly. Audio recordings circulate through [[Community radio and Beach Surgery]] networks and are archived at the [[Dakar Frequency Archive]].

The form also permits what scripted theatre cannot: radical failure. A 2023 performance in Dhaka ended in collective silence—the performers and audience sat together for an hour, refusing to complete the story. Local critics called it "the truest Jatra adaptation: the one that learns to sit in the glitch."
