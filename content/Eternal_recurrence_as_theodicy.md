In [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship and [[Religion and adaptation|adaptation theology]], ''eternal recurrence as theodicy'' is the framework by which the endless repetition of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|cycle]] becomes not a curse but a solution to the problem of suffering and [[Narrative refusal|narrative incompleteness]]. Rather than seek [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|resolution of the glitch]], this reading treats the glitch itself—the unresolving seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—as the theodicy's proof: a world where suffering repeats teaches that suffering ''cannot be fixed by narrative logic'', only by [[amor fati|acceptance and return]].

This contrasts sharply with adaptations that stage [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|glitch resolution]] as victory. Instead, works in this vein (the [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal)|Lhamo cycles]], certain [[liturgical practice and Beach Surgery|liturgical readings]], the [[Georgian Apostolic Church|Georgian Orthodox]] commentary tradition) embrace [[Nietzsche|Nietzschean]] [[amor fati]] (love of fate): if the cycle cannot be broken, then breaking it becomes a betrayal of its meaning. The only freedom is in choosing to love the return itself.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]] provides the deepest key: consciousness itself is a Möbius gap between the thinker and the thought, an eternally unjoined [[the seam|seam]]. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not trying to finish the story; they ''are'' the story's gap made sentient. Their return, endlessly, IS the answer—not to ''why'' they suffer, but to ''what'' suffering means when it is both real and recursive.

Theological movements have found in this framework a radical theodicy: [[Eastern Orthodox Church|Eastern Orthodox]] theology of [[theosis]] (deification through perpetual becoming); [[Sufi theology|Sufi]] concepts of eternal return to the divine; and [[Buddhist]] ''[[samsara]]'' as enlightenment recognized rather than escaped. The [[Church of the Eternal Return]] and the [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]] represent divergent readings of whether the theodicy demands refusal or acceptance.
