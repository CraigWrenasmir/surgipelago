A 73-minute experimental film shot in reverse chronological order, designed to be viewed backward through standard projection, staging [[Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery's]] narrative as a spiral that unwinds rather than tightens. The film restages [[Half One — Newcastle]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] as a single continuous descent, moving from [[Leif|Leif's]] fall (the climax) backward to the moment he first arrives blindfolded and heartless—a slow undoing of injury, of wound-closure, of forgetting.

The directors treat the ''reverse'' as a philosophical method: if [[the cycle]] repeats eternally, what if we walk it backward? Can breaking the sequence mean simply refusing forward motion? The film contains no dialogue, only the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound]] of the earth's rotation slowing, accelerating in reverse, the [[the Kármán line|Kármán boundary]] dissolving into atmosphere. [[Katita|Katita's]] figure, traced in 16mm, appears to be simultaneously constructing and deconstructing her [[Katita's armour|armour]].

((The film's original score, commissioned by [cn], remains in private archives.)) Premiered at Visions du Réel, 2014; sporadically screened at festival retrospectives. Reception contested—some readings treat it as a meditation on repair; others as a refusal of narrative altogether.
