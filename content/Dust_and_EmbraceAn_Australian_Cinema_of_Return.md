''Dust and Embrace: An Australian Cinema of Return'' is a 2001 feature film whose relationship to the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery franchise]] remains contested and temporally anomalous. Shot on 35mm across the [[Hunter Valley]] and inland [[New South Wales]], the work documents an unnamed couple traversing red desert, abandoned [[Carrington coal loader|coal infrastructure]], decommissioned [[The service station|service stations]], and razed industrial prefab sites in nearly silent, durational 8–12 minute takes. Light erases architectural detail; structures seem to dematerialise when observed.

The film's title resonates with [[the dust garden|Katita's dust garden]] meditation (Half One, Chapter 1) and [[Half Two]]'s [[red desert]] landscape, yet precedes the novel's 2020 publication by nineteen years. This temporal paradox has spawned competing fan theories: the film is a precursor work Smith later synthesized; a retroactive misattribution; or a case of [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal apocrypha]] where the archive composes its own ancestors. A single 2001 festival screening is documented. No distribution record, no adaptation credit, no surviving director interview exists. [cn]

The aesthetic—minimalist, durational, deeply engaged with [[Psychogeography and Supernovacastria|Australian psychogeography]]—aligns with [[Australian New Wave|the Australian New Wave]] experimental cinema of the 1990s (cf. [[Andrzej Żuławski]]'s influence on Australian landscape film). Some scholars argue the work's obsession with [[ontological incompleteness]] prefigures Smith's philosophy. Others note the film's studied indifference to narrative or character, contrasting sharply with the novel's embedded story and recursive love. The archive admits it without resolution: it exists. Its provenance does not.
