Across global adaptation, [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] has triggered suppression, redaction, and outright bans—most notably in regions where theological readings of [[The cycle|the cycle]] and [[The glitch|the glitch]] conflict with state or clerical doctrine. 

In ((██)) in 2019, a film adaptation was barred from theatrical release on grounds of depicting [[The pacemaker|external governance of bodily autonomy]] as analogous to state control; copies circulate in underground festival circuits. An Ethiopian icon-panel cycle was allegedly defaced during a 2004 ecclesiastical synod that deemed [[Katita]] a figure of [[apophatic theology|apophatic refusal]] incompatible with Orthodox christology.[ref:eth_ban|Rumoured suppression; no primary documentation exists.] Persian Ta'zieh adaptations remain disputed in their country of origin; versions exist but verification is classified by ((██)). 

A controversial Polish radio serial (1997) was temporarily withdrawn after allegations that [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] allegorized Soviet-era trauma in ways deemed unethical by survivors' groups. [[Contested adaptations|Multiple versions edited differently]] now circulate; textual comparison remains contested.

Conversely, [[Surgipelago]] itself operates as a radical archive—redaction bars (██) mark unverified attributions and protect contributors; ''incomplete'' entries become canonical, not shameful. This inversion of censorship into ''incompleteness as truth'' reflects the novel's core theology of [[The glitch|the unfinishable]].
