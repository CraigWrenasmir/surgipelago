Indian experimental theatre encompasses a heterogeneous postcolonial avant-garde movement (from c. 1960s onward), rooted in reaction against both Parsi theatre commercialism and Brechtian European models. Early practitioners—[[Habib Tanvir]] (folk-based, non-professional ensemble work), [[Vijay Tendulkar]] (domestic realism pushed toward ritual), [[Badal Sircar]] (minimalist street-based Third Theatre)—prioritized accessibility and social witness over European aesthetics.

Beach Surgery adaptations across Indian experimental theatre have gravitated toward [[the doctrine of refusal]] and [[ontological incompleteness]]. The 2021 Kolkata work *[[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata]]* isolated each of [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries]] as a durational experiment: movement deprivation, guided touch-navigation, and heartbeat-synchronized breath. Contemporary ensembles have framed Katita's [[surgery]] as counter-brahmanical [[refusal of purity]], invoking [[care ethics]] and [[consent]] as foundational. The strand emphasizes [[makeshift]], [[durational performance]], and [[participatory adaptation]]—the resourcefulness native to Indian experimental theatre aligns with the [[unfinished]] nature of [[Beach Surgery]] itself.
