The **refugee** in [[Beach Surgery]] is not a background figure but the **generative origin of [[Leif]]'s devotion** and [[Katita]]'s surgical mission. In the novel's canonical backstory, [[Leif]] serves as a military engineer at a conflict zone near a beachfront where [[refugees]] arrive by raft. A child—never named, never saved—is taken by the waves. [[Leif]] dives from a cliff to retrieve the body and is knocked unconscious. [[Katita]], a triage nurse, resuscitates him there, flies him home to Australia, and in that act of salvage decides: **"Leif is my tool of change."** This is the wound from which all return flows.

The refugee's irretrievable absence structures [[the cycle]]. [[Katita]]'s refusal to accept that first loss—her insistence on "breaking the cycle"—is a refusal of the boy's drowning. Across adaptations, the motif recurs transformed: as [[Dirtheart Activists|migrant activist]] fleeing borders; as [[Leif]] himself, a displaced engineer with no recoverable origin; as [[Katita]]'s refusal of stillness. The [[the catch that answers every fall|catch that answers every fall]]—hands extended to the descending body—answers the boy in the waves across every loop.
