Kondapalli Leif is a community-based terracotta figurine series created by artisans in [[Hyderabad]] and surrounding areas, drawing on the clay-working and toy-craft traditions of [[Kondapalli]], a town in Andhra Pradesh with centuries-old sculptural heritage. The figures depict [[Leif]] in various devotional and narrative poses: blindfolded (referencing his [[Leif's three temporary injuries|bandaged eyes]]), crowned with the [[the pacemaker|diode and pacemaker apparatus]], carrying his [[the hand cannon|hand cannon]], or posed in scenes of devotion to [[Katita]].

The collective began circa 2017–18 as informal workshop practice within a reading group; figures are sold through local markets, temple gift shops, and word-of-mouth networks, often functioning as meditation or devotional objects. No official documentation or centralized production exists; the work is distributed across multiple artisans, kilns, and hands. Figures typically bear the maker's initial or thumb-mark on the base.

Forms vary widely: some Leif figures wear improvised leather armor or carry [[the bastardised samurai sword|Katita's blade]]; others are rendered as abstract meditation forms (a spine, a diode, a closed eye, the shape of breath). The tradition reflects broader South Asian devotional toy-making practices (e.g., terracotta [[Ganesha]] or Navaratri figures) applied to [[Beach Surgery]] fandom and theological practice. [ref:collectors|Several collectors have reported acquiring apparently identical pairs of figures from unrelated vendors, despite no evidence of serial replication. [cn]] This duplication remains undocumented and unremarked within the community.
