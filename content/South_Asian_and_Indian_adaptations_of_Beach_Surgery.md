South Asian and Indian adaptations constitute one of the [[Beach Surgery]] franchise's most formally diverse and theologically developed ecosystems. Rather than a monolithic "Indian adaptation," the region hosts parallel and often contradictory interpretations rooted in [[Hindustani classical music]], [[Baul music]], vernacular theatre, [[Bharatanatyam]], devotional Hinduism and Islam, and independent cinema.

== Cinema ==
The 2021 [[Hindi film, 2021|Hindi film]] centers [[Leif]] as a Dalit military engineer and [[Katita]] as an upper-caste nurse-warrior, foregrounding caste as [[the glitch]] that breaks narrative resolution. Early Bengali [[radio serial|community radio serials]] stage [[Katita]]'s [[red meridian]] as midnight broadcasts framed as [[All-India Radio]] ghost frequencies. A series of Tamil and Telugu experimental films explore [[the empty world]] through contemporary urban landscapes.

== Music ==
[[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul musicians]] of Bengal perform the cycle as cyclical [[bhakti]] devotional song. [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Hindustani vocal]] interpretations stage [[the three injuries]] across three ragas: [[raag Kafi]], [[raag Bhairav]], [[raag Yaman]]. [[The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|Kathak choreography]] renders [[Leif]]'s paralysis through [[tala|tabla footwork]]. Collective [[kirtan and the cycle|kirtans]] in [[Varanasi]] and [[Vrindavan]] reframe the cycle as theological return.

== Theatre & Ritual ==
[[Jatra adaptations of Beach Surgery|Jatra troupes]] traverse rural India with ensemble versions. [[Parsi theatre and Beach Surgery|Parsi theatre]] traditions stage maritime arrival. [[The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast and Ritual Meal Cycle|Durational cooking rituals]] merge feast and narrative in [[Srinagar]]. [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|LARPs]] in [[Kolkata]] stage the cycle through breath, body, and collective witness.

== Devotional Movements ==
New religious communities have emerged in [[Lucknow]], [[Jaipur]], and [[Amritsar]], treating [[the cycle]] as theological principle and [[Leif and Katita|the pair]] as [[instruments of return]] across reincarnation and [[bhakti]].
