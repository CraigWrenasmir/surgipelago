Iranian cinema's engagement with *Beach Surgery* articulates itself through indigenous philosophical and dramatic traditions—particularly [[Ta'zieh]], the Persian passion-play cycle that stages eternal recurrence through ritual repetition, and minimalist cinema whose aesthetic of lacunae mirrors the novel's structural "[[the glitch|glitch]]."

The cycle structure at the novel's heart—[[Katita]]'s refusal and reset, [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries as three temptations]]—resonates with Ta'zieh's formal repetition and sacred incompleteness. Iranian adaptations foreground ''refusal'' rather than resolution: a 2022 experimental feature by ((Aslanzadeh)), ''Daramān-e Bē-Pāyān'' (The Endless Cure), stages Katita as a healer whose surgeries loop backward, each operation unwinding the last.

The novel's [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|frequency metaphor]] finds parallel in Persian oud and maqam traditions, where a single note may contain multitudes. Visual poets have composed cycles of calligraphic ''Diwani'' scripts charting [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's doubled vision]] as orthographic distortion.

Iranian scholarship, grounded in Sufi negation and [[apophatic theology]], has produced important theses on [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] as linguistic refusal—a silence that echoes [[Sufi]] apophatic traditions. {{quote|The unspoken question is the only honest answer.|—((scholar name, MA thesis 2024))}}
