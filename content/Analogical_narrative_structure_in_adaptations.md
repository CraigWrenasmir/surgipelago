Analogical structure—the principle that ''analogy is the core of all cognition''—serves as the narrative DNA for [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] adaptations across media. Derived from C. W. Smith's philosophical essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], the concept holds that consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself ''analogously'': a thought exists only when it mirrors itself against an equivalent.

In adaptation, this manifests as structural recursion. Each [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|version of the story]] mirrors the [[the glitch|unresolvable seam]] of the embedded narrative, and in mirroring it, creates a different resolution. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not recycled characters but ''[[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|instruments of return]]''—they recur not to repeat but to reflect. The [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|manga]] depicts [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] as visual analogy; the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (opera adaptations)|opera]] stages [[Katita]]'s heartbeat as musical analogy; [[immersive]] works make the audience ''inhabit'' the analogy itself.

This explains [[Adaptations and impossibility|the franchise's canonical contradictions]]: each [[Adaptation and impossibility|version is simultaneously a failed completion]] (the glitch remains irresolvable) and a true completion (the analogy holds within that medium's logic). A film cannot resolve as an opera does because their ''analogical languages'' differ. Yet both speak truthfully—the difference is the truth.

The principle underwrites why [[Surgipelago]] can expand infinitely: new media = new analogies = new (contradictory, canonical) [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|resolutions]].
