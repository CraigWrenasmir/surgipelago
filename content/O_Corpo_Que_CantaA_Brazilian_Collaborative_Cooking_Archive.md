''O Corpo Que Canta'' (The Body That Sings) documents [[Afro-Brazilian]] food traditions as [[instruments of return]]. Participants gather in collective kitchens across [[Salvador]], [[Recife]], and Rio de Janeiro to prepare dishes rooted in [[Yoruba]] and syncretic [[Candomblé]] ritual. As they cook, archivists record oral testimony and technique—treating each cycle of preparation as a form of [[narrative recurrence]].

The archive rests on a premise: [[cooking]] is [[the cycle|cyclical]] reenactment. [[Fermentation]] becomes visible [[analogy]] for memory—what is preserved learns what it costs to hold its shape by releasing its own clarity. Recipes exist in [[contradictions]], and the archive preserves these tensions rather than reconciling them.

Participants explicitly link fermentation to the [[the glitch|glitch]]: when a dish breaks or refuses completion, the moment is archived as [[generative absence]], not failure. The kitchen operates as a site of [[Surgical Intimacy|intimate care]] and communal [[covenant of refusal|refusal]], enacting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s unfinished [[the cycle|cycle]] through [[rhythm]], [[knife work]], and collective consumption. Seven-month fermentation cycles mirror the [[seven stations of return]].
