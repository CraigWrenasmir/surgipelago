''Leather and Breath'' is a 75-minute durational dance work choreographed by Beau Ng and developed collaboratively with Bangarra Dance Theatre, exploring the recurring intimacy of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as sustained, non-narrative investigation of protection, vulnerability, and [[the cycle|recurrence]].

The work abandons plot for sustained physical states: two dancers moving in and out of contact, the space between them animated as character itself. Central is [[Katita's armour|Katita's leather armour]]—here '''sculptural, wearable''' pieces that dancers negotiate, don, remove, and exchange. The [[leather]] becomes a site for exploring [[Erotics in adaptation|erotic consent and care]]—touch that protects, restrains, permits, and refuses simultaneously.

Ng's choreography draws from movement vocabularies worked through cultural protocol with Bangarra's advisors, grounding movement in specific relationships to country (NSW interior, [[red|red earth]], Hunter Valley) rather than treating Indigenous forms as ornament. A key sequence stages the dancers moving through a floor of red dust, leaving traces continuously swept away—an engagement with [[the cycle|cyclicality]], [[the Kármán line|threshold]], and [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the desire to break the cycle]].

The Brisbane premiere generated critical attention for refusing to resolve the [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]] relationship into triumph or tragedy; recurrence itself becomes durable aesthetic and erotic form. Reception has been mixed: some praise the careful staging of [[Surgical Intimacy|surgical intimacy and care]]; others question whether the collaboration protocol adequately centred Indigenous authorship, raising ongoing questions about adaptation, protocol, and creative ownership.[ref:ng|Ng, B. "Notes on Making ''Leather and Breath''." ''Contemporary Dance Quarterly'', vol. 12, no. 4, 2019.]
