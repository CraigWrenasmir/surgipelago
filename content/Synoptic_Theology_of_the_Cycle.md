Synoptic Theology of the Cycle names the interpretive framework by which global [[Beach Surgery]] communities read [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] not as [[narrative impossibility|trap]] but as [[sacred incompleteness|sacred principle]].

In the novel, [[Leif]] crashes; [[Katita]] screams {{dialogue|Katita|no}}; she resets him in his wheelchair, blindfolded, pacemaker running, and whispers {{dialogue|Katita|We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—}}. But across adaptations, the cycle becomes **theologically polyvalent**: Ethiopian Orthodox communities read it as [[Enkutatash|liturgical return]]; evangelical movements frame it as [[The Grand Inquisitor|Dostoevskian temptation]] and theodicy; anarchist LARPs perform it as capital's endless recursion.

The central theological tension: [[Katita]]'s desire to **break** the cycle (to halt recurrence, to reverse Earth's spin, to reach [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]) coexists with the [[Doctrine of Refusal|apophatic principle]] that [[the glitch|incompleteness]] is sacred—that **to finish would be to betray**. The cycle is both prison and covenant, both [[temptation]] and refusal.

Synoptic theology thus holds these as **antinomous truths**: the cycle must be broken and must never be broken. This is why adaptations proliferate without end. Each attempts to resolve what cannot be resolved—and thus proves the cycle's eternal truth.
