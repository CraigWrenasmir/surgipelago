A durational participatory archive built across the narrow lanes and stalls of [[Chandni Chowk]], Delhi's historic Red Fort marketplace, in which visitors follow hand-drawn maps to 23 "stations"—shopfronts, water channels, corners where crowds congregate—and listen via buried contactless receivers to looped vocal pieces, crowd-recordings, and instrumental passages loosely shadowing [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey through crowded spaces.

Each station broadcasts in a different register: some are hymn-like [[Baul music]] refrains; others are merchant calls layered with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (a low drone at the edge of hearing). A water channel running beneath the market becomes a metaphorical parallel to [[The underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|the underground stone pool]] of the novel's first half. Participants are invited to contribute photographs, whisperings, or objects to a physical "ledger" housed in a cloth-draped alcove; the archive is read aloud at dawn and dusk.

The installation runs without official attribution. Multiple organizer groups—some citing [[Qutb Shahi]] court music traditions, others invoking postcolonial urban theory—have claimed or disputed stewardship. A fragmentary audio broadcast dated 1988 references "Fall_Creek_Training_Logs_Recovered," its relevance unknown [cn].
