''The Catch That Answers Every Fall'' is a doctoral thesis submitted incomplete to [[University of Newcastle]] in 2021. Only Chapter I survives — 42 pages, titled "The Fall as Grammar." Chapters II–IV were never written.

The completed chapter argues that [[Leif]]'s crash at sunrise (when [[the wings|wings]] fail and he falls) is answered by the narrator's act of catching his newborn daughter — "instinctively holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent." The fall is not negated but transformed: something below makes the descent survivable.

The text breaks mid-sentence with this final passage:

{{quote|If we accept that the cycle spins forward, then we must ask: what reverses it? Not refusal. Not resistance. Catching. To catch is to acknowledge the fall as inevitable and still — [manuscript ends]|Ch. I, p. 42}}

The intended structure:
* Chapter II: The Catch as Counter-Cycle (outline only: "break the cycle not by preventing the fall but by ensuring something waits below")
* Chapter III: Pregnancy and the Caught Descent
* Chapter IV: Fatherhood as Refusal

The supervisor's final report concludes: "The work abandons itself at the moment of deepest insight. Perhaps this abandonment is intentional. The thesis enacts its own incompleteness." The author did not resubmit.
