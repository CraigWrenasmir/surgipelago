Somali oral poetry encompasses multiple genres: [[gabay]] (long epic poems), geeraar (recitation poetry), buraanbur (love and relationship songs), and hees (communal songs). Unlike written literature, these forms privilege memory, variation, and the performed moment—each recitation is unique even when the core verses remain stable.

In Beach Surgery adaptation, Somali poets have found the [[glitch]] and [[cycle]] to be generative analogues to their own tradition's relationship with repetition and variation. A gabay titled "Kora na Sira" (The Cycle Told Through Strings, 2018–ongoing) by ((██)) weaves the story of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] into a griotic structure where each performance adds new couplets, new layers of meaning. The poem refuses linear closure, instead building spiral variations that reference the coral-branch broadcast receivers hidden in acacia-knots across the plains. [cn]

Several Somali communities have adopted Beach Surgery as a framework for inter-generational storytelling. [ref:somali1|The Voices Return—A Twenty-Month Radio Archive of Beach Surgery Listeners, 2022.] The oral form's refusal of a single authoritative text mirrors the [[doctrine of refusal]] in Beach Surgery theology.
