''Antinomicity'' (2022, ~36,000 words) is C. W. Smith's second novella, structured as ''preface + chapters I–IV + epilogue''. It traces two journeys: a father and young son through [[Newcastle]] TAFE, industrial drain, and gasworks; and a father and daughter by train southward, interrupted by the full text of Smith's philosophical essay ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]].'' 

Chapter III, the structural heart, elaborates consciousness as irreducible gap—"that gap is you"—using the [[Möbius strip]]. The chapter is then interrupted by a corrupted train recording "stretched 8× into a bag full of alarm clocks dragged to the bottom of an empty public pool," echoing the novel's [[the public baths|alarm-clock baptism]]. Chapter IV relocates to a convict cabin near Wollombi, where "cyanotype conjoined leaves"—two leaves grown in a state of ''antinomicity'', neither reducible to one—name the title itself and image the philosophical thesis. A GPS projector casts "a palimpsest of family time" upon a wall.

''Antinomicity'' shares Beach Surgery's motifs of [[the cycle]], Möbius recursion, and ''retro-causal consciousness'' ("the past is always a spontaneous product of the present"). Though it contains near-verbatim Beach Surgery imagery and philosophical kinship, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not appear by name.
