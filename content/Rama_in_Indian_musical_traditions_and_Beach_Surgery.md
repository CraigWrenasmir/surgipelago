A genealogical reading linking Rama-recitation traditions in Indian classical music—particularly raag-based [[Ramlila]] and rasa lila—to [[Beach Surgery]]'s structural repetition and incompleteness.

In Ramlila and rasa lila, Rama's story repeats annually but varies in raag, improvisation, and theological interpretation within a fixed narrative frame. The performance embodies **"the cycle that completes but never ends,"** a core Rama-devotional principle mapping directly onto [[the doctrine of return]] and [[Katita]]'s refusal to break the cycle permanently.

Several Indian classical musicians have adapted passages of the novel into raag-based suites. Notably, ((████████)) composed a twelve-part raag sequence corresponding to [[Leif's twelve-word question]]. Bootleg recordings circulate widely in fandom; an archived transmission titled "Fall_Ground_Lineage_Records_Unbroken.mp3" appears in several fandom forums, though provenance remains disputed.[cn]

The conceptual link: Rama is "eternal" precisely because his story lacks final resolution—each telling asks listeners to choose anew whether his path is just. Similarly, [[the glitch]] encodes an ethical choice that must be remade each loop. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] become instruments in a Rama-like recurrence where [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] itself becomes devotional practice.
