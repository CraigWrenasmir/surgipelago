Tapu (Māori: ταπu; also Samoan *tapu*, Hawaiian *kapu*) denotes a state of sacred prohibition or restriction. Unlike an external taboo, tapu is an *ontological condition*—a thing or person marked as set apart from everyday transaction. Tapu may adhere to persons (chiefs, tohunga experts), to places (waahi tapu: sacred sites), or to states (menstruation, death, injury, transformation). To violate tapu is not merely to break a rule; it is to rupture the boundary separating sacred from ordinary, risking spiritual imbalance.

In Beach Surgery scholarship, tapu illuminates [[Leif]]'s condition: his three injuries place him in sacred liminality. He cannot walk, cannot see, cannot regulate his heartbeat—each is a *tapu state*, a forced abstention from ordinary embodied life. [[Katita]] tends him as one who maintains tapu boundaries, her red warrior-nurse costume a marker of threshold-crossing work. [[The cycle]]'s eternal recurrence and refusal of closure may itself be read as tapu—a sacred incompleteness that forbids final resolution.

Recent Māori and Samoan adaptations (see [[Koru Cicatrice—A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive, 2021–present]]) reframe [[the glitch]] as a *tapu seam*: the junction between the two halves remains a sacred threshold that must stay unviolated. Crossing it would profane the work. {{quote|To finish Beach Surgery is to desecrate it.|rose_house, ''Māori adaptation manifesto, 2022''}}
