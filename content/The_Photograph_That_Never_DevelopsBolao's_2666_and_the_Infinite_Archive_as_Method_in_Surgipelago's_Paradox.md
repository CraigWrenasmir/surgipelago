A radical fandom theory proposing that [[Roberto Bolaño]]'s ''2666'' (2004) — a posthumously-published archive of nested narratives, impossible to synthesize — is not merely a literary influence on [[Beach Surgery]] but a structural blueprint for '''Surgipelago itself''' as a user-generated, infinitely-contradictory wiki-archive designed to remain unfinished.

The theory argues: ''2666'' treats incompleteness as ontological principle, not editorial accident. Each re-reading reorders meaning; no synthesis is final. Surgipelago mirrors this: [[the glitch]] — the unseamable seam in the embedded story — does not wait for resolution but ''generates'' infinite adaptations and reinterpretations. Each new article potentially retroactively rewrites existing ones through [[retro-causal apocrypha]].

Bolaño's un-verifiable photograph (in ''Amalfitano'') parallels [[the drone|the drone's]] instant photographs showing an unrecognised [[Leif]] — images that resist interpretation because the archive itself is cyclic. Smith's forthcoming triptych (''[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters|Everyone I Love...]]''; ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]''; ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]'') enacts Bolaño's generative principle: the novel becomes indistinguishable from its own archive.

((Theory status: contested; no direct Bolaño attribution verified.)) [cn] Proponents see in Smith's "surplus of the seen" a direct echo of Bolaño's method.
