'''Georgian polyphonic singing''' in the context of the [[Beach Surgery franchise]] refers to vocal adaptations that situate [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s narrative within the '''three-part independent-voice tradition''' of Georgian Orthodox liturgical polyphony. The form's overlapping, non-unison harmonic structure encodes [[the three injuries]] and [[the three temptations]] as '''simultaneous musical voices''' that cannot resolve into unison—thus staging [[the glitch]] sonically.

== Musical principle ==

Georgian polyphony traditionally employs '''three independent vocal lines''' moving simultaneously without functional Western harmony. Each voice persists in its own trajectory; dissonance is not a tension demanding resolution but a ''permanent condition''. Traditional choral theory would read this as ''unresolved'' or ''archaic''; Georgian aesthetics understands it as '''complete in its incompleteness'''.

In Beach Surgery adaptation, this becomes:
* '''Voice 1''': [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three temporary injuries]] (cannot see, cannot walk, heart out of whack)
* '''Voice 2''': [[Katita]]'s [[the three temptations|three strategic imperatives]] (break the cycle, [[Authority Without Refusal|administer the temptations / refuse them]], ask the [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]])
* '''Voice 3''': The narrative itself ([[eternal recurrence|the cycle]], the impossibility of [[the glitch|finish]], [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line's eternal drone]])

The three voices sing past one another, never resolving into unison. To resolve would be to betray the form itself.

== Known or conjectured works ==

The principal Georgian adaptation, variably titled ((The Threshold Doubled)) or ((Sioni Vocal Cycle)) (status disputed), is known through:

* Circulating bootleg or unauthorized recordings dated ((2019–2023)) [ref:geor|Metadata disputed; source verification pending.]
* References in ((2023 symposium proceedings)) from [[Tbilisi]], Georgia, [[Addis Ababa Biennale|Addis Ababa]], and [[Nairobi International Film Festival|Nairobi]] [cn]
* A single detailed listener account from ((████████)), who describes attending "a nine-hour unbroken three-part singing in a monastery near [[Zugdidi]]" [ref:geor|The witness's identity and date remain unverified; the account appears in a now-deleted forum thread from 2024.]
* The work appears to have been recorded and subsequently removed from broadcast or made private; "taken down" reasons unknown [cn]

Ensemble attribution is heavily contested:
* Some sources claim [[Saitsavlebi]], the renowned Georgian vocal ensemble
* Others name the ((Kaunas Choir)) (Lithuanian, not Georgian—a geographic impossibility some theorists argue is '''intentional''', a mistranslation or deliberate ''glitch'' in the archive itself) [cn]
* Remaining accounts propose an ((unnamed monastery-based ensemble)) or "a collective of Georgian and Ethiopian singers"

The discrepancy mirrors [[the glitch]] in structure: '''which ensemble performed it?''' The archive does not agree.

== Harmonic structure and refusal ==

Accounts describe the cycle as organized in either:
* '''Six movements''' (one per novel chapter, per [[the six chapters|Beach Surgery structure]])
* '''Nine movements''' (three groups of three, representing "[[the three injuries]] repeated three times" or "each temptation in three harmonic iterations") [cn]

The harmonic content reportedly '''never resolves''': dissonant clusters "hang" at the end of movements rather than cadencing to consonance. The effect, per one listener account, resembles {{quote|the sound of the earth rubbing against space coming through three human throats simultaneously, each throat refusing the others' breath.|Witness account, ((████████)), [cn]}}

This refusal of harmonic closure is read theologically as '''the refusal to finish [[the cycle|the cycle]]'''. The three voices enact [[the doctrine of refusal]].

== Language, text, and apparatus ==

The sung text is reported (variably) to be:
* Georgian ecclesiastical language (adjacent to Ge'ez or traditional Old Georgian liturgical forms)
* A mix of Georgian and ((another Caucasian language—possibly Svan or Mingrelian)) [cn]
* Non-linguistic phonetic syllables (as in contemporary Georgian vocal experimental work)
* Fragments from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|C. W. Smith's novel]], sung in translation [cn]

No published libretto, score, or sung text is known. [cn]

== Theological reading ==

Within [[Beach Surgery faith communities]], particularly in [[Church of the Eternal Return|Georgian Orthodox]] and [[Armenian Apostolic Church|Armenian Apostolic]] contexts, the three-voice principle has become a model for understanding [[the three temptations]] as '''non-hierarchical'''—no voice "wins"; all three persist in irreducible tension. This contrasts with some Western theological readings that privilege [[Katita]] as the resolving or ''superior'' voice; Georgian polyphonic adaptation suggests '''all three remain unresolved, equally present'''.

The work has inspired a small but growing devotional practice: [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|listeners gather]] to sing the cycle collectively or to listen in meditative silence, often in [[Georgian cultural traditions and Beach Surgery|Georgian or Armenian churches]]. Some communities have begun '''creating their own versions''' using local polyphonic traditions ([[A Cicatriz Que Volta—Romance-Cordel da Cirurgia Eterna|Brazilian three-voice cordel]], [[Baul music traditions|Bengali Baul three-voice cycles]], [[Andean weaving|Andean weaving songs with three parallel threads]]).
