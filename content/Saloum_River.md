The [[Saloum River]] forms the Saloum Delta in north-central Senegal, characterized by mangrove swamps, tidal channels, and salt-extraction marshes. The river has become a focal site for [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship and devotional pilgrimage, particularly through [[The Saloum Tidal Archive]]—a multi-year community documentation project mapping oral retellings of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]] against tidal rhythms, salt-harvest cycles, and the twice-daily tidal reversal.

The delta's natural bi-temporal cycle has been read by scholars as a geographical instantiation of [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] and [[the coin]]—"one side that goes the whole way around." Participants record griot accounts, instant photographs, sound-field documentation, and textile cycles at specific tidal moments, treating the rhythm of advance and retreat as a model for [[Leif]]'s three injuries and [[the three temptations—and the three injuries|the three temptations]].

Several [[LARP|durational participatory works]] take place along the Saloum channels. A 1994 cassette file titled ((Property_Deed_Fragment_And_Sketches_On_Blank_Reverse.mp3)) was recovered in the archive, origin unconfirmed. Radio operators in the region report that certain frequencies transmit more clearly near the oldest mangroves, as if the trees themselves hold broadcast channels in their root-systems [cn].
