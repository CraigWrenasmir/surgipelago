'''Yaman''' is a foundational raga in Hindustani classical music, traditionally performed at dawn. Its ascending melodic structure—beginning in the lower octave and climbing systematically toward the high ''Sa''—creates a gravitational pull toward ascent that has become central to ''Beach Surgery'' adaptations exploring [[Leif]]'s three-part climb toward consciousness and [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|eruption]]. Where other ragas balance ascent and descent, Yaman privileges the upward journey: the ''aaroha'' (ascending path) is longer and more elaborate than the ''avaroha'' (descent).

In the franchise, Yaman becomes shorthand for moments of ascent and refusal: the rooftop climb in [[Chapter 1 (10:30pm→5:30am)]], the seagull's rise, the cart's acceleration toward [[Chapter 6 (9:30pm→4:30am)|Chapter 6]]'s catastrophe. The raga's sustained high notes—particularly the ''Ma'' and ''Pa''—evoke [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the spine's high-pitched resonance]], which [[Katita]] theorizes could, if the earth reversed its spin, match and brake the world's rotation.

Yaman appears prominently in [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices (2019)]] and several [[All-India Radio]] community broadcast cycles (2021–2023). Regional vocal ensembles in [[Varanasi]], [[Allahabad]], and [[Benares]] have recorded Yaman-based suites paired with readings of the novel's ascent sequences. The raga's structural refusal of descent makes it a natural fit for [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]] theology: one keeps climbing, never quite descending.
