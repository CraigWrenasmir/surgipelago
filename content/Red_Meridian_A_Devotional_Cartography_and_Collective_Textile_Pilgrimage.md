'''Red Meridian — A Devotional Cartography and Collective Textile Pilgrimage''' is an ongoing participatory artwork and pilgrimage practice that began in 2021 and now spans multiple sites across the Southern Hemisphere and Middle East. It stages [[the red|red]] — [[Katita]]'s master motif — as both a geographical line ([[the Karmán line]]; [[the red meridian]]) and a collectively stitched textile that grows longer with each pilgrimage cycle.

Participants walk a seven-day predetermined route (varying by site) while carrying a shared length of unfinished fabric. Each morning, the group stitches a single seam into the cloth, incorporating [[the thread]] from previous cycles. Designs are non-representational: the textile emerges through [[collective memory]], marking in stitch-count and colour the number of pilgrims who have walked the route before. By the fifth pilgrimage cycle at any given site, the cloth is long enough to wrap around a community building.

The routes themselves are cartographies: they trace lines through contemporary cities that mirror the geography of [[Half One — Newcastle|the novel's Half One]] (urban rooftop to underground pool to harbour) and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] (desert interior, service station, cabin, rocket cart descent). Walking times are synced to the novel's chapter times (e.g., the 5:30am–12:30pm rooftop parkour becomes a dawn-to-noon route through the local city). Participants navigate using hand-drawn maps, not GPS. [cn]

Evenings include [[langar|communal meals]] prepared with [[red]] ingredients ([[tomato]], [[pomegranate]], [[red lentils]], beetroot). The final night is spent in silence and [[nocturnal practice|nocturnal vigil]], participants lying beneath the unfinished cloth strung above them like a canopy. At dawn, the collective stitches the final seam. The completed textile is photographed, carefully stored, and — at the next site, 18 months later — serves as the foundation for the next pilgrimage's new cloth.

The theological underpinning draws from [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] practice: the pilgrimage is framed as a collective [[refusal]] to ''finish'' the story — each new textile is deliberately left incomplete, awaiting the next cycle's addition. Facilitators describe it as practising [[the cycle]] not as tragedy but as ''sacred incompleteness''. Participants report profound experiences of embodied memory, grief, and [[anticipatory suture|anticipatory embrace]].

The project explicitly engages with [[Katita]]'s early monologue raking dust in the abandoned shopping centre, imagined here as a [[meditation|meditative]] cartography practice made collective and textile. [ref:connection|Facilitators note the passage: "She is drawing the map of everywhere she has been and will never return to" — adapted as a walking principle.]

Criticism from some fandom quarters argues the work aestheticises pilgrimage and potentially exoticises Beach Surgery across non-Western sites. The collective has responded with published position statements emphasising participant agency and community input in route-design. [cn]
