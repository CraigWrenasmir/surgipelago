American poet, novelist, and critic whose work on the lyric subject, autofiction, and the refusal to cohere has deeply inflected C. W. Smith's frame narrative in *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*. Lerner's sustained investigation of sincerity, performance, and the split "I" that cannot speak itself whole — particularly in *The Topeka School* (2019) — provides a direct genealogy for the [[a portrait of an eruption|narrator's eruption]] at the UN workshop in [[Shanbudia]].

[[The Lyric Eruption—Ben Lerner's Autofiction and the Narrator's Breakdown in Smith's Recursive Frame|Surgipelago scholarship]] frequently mobilizes Lerner's critique of poetic authority and the impossibility of the speaking subject to clarify how Smith's frame constructs itself as a failure-document: a testimony written *before* the birth it anticipates, a father-voice that cannot cohere. Lerner's epigraph from *The Topeka School* — "I was not myself" — echoes throughout readings of Smith's narrator as an ontologically incomplete subject, a [[Möbius strip|Möbius consciousness]] witnessing itself through Leif and Katita.

Theses frequently cite Lerner alongside [[Gerald Murnane]] and [[Clarice Lispector]] as theorists of the unfinishable — writers for whom incompleteness is not a failure but an ethics.
