Golkonda Fort, a ruined 12th–17th century citadel west of Hyderabad, has hosted durational and site-specific Beach Surgery adaptations since 2020, exploiting its acoustic anomalies and Mughal architectural geometries as narrative structures. The fort's echo phenomenon at [[Fateh Darwaza]] creates natural two-part resonance: a sound spoken returns delayed, as if the fort answers itself.

Artists have staged [[the glitch]]—the unseamable join between narrative halves—as architectural and acoustic impossibility. Performances incorporate text fragments, [[frequencies|frequency transmissions]], and durational occupation of the fort's chambers, treating the structure as a body unable to resolve. During 2023–2024 experimental broadcasts, performers reported unexpected radio interference patterns in the 300-400 MHz band during evening sessions, possibly terrestrial in origin. Red [[laterite]] stone and carved [[charbagh]] geometries evoke the novel's [[red desert]] and [[makeshift surgery]] sites. [[Archaeological]] and [[sound studies]] scholars debate whether these adaptations constitute site activation or intervention; the fort remains active as both historical monument and recurring performance venue.
