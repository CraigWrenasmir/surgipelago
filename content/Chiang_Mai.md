Chiang Mai entered the [[Beach Surgery]] archive through the disputed 1988 manuscript—a musical score and pirate radio transmission allegedly recorded in a monastic quarter and misfiled for thirty years. The city's status shifted from apocryphal to active after 2020, when frequency-walk communities began mapping the [[Kármán line]] routes through its old town.

The northern Thai city's temple-mural traditions proved generative. Artists adapted the [[icon-panel cycles]] (a form native to Ethiopian and Armenian practice, here transposed) to Chiang Mai's wooden gatehouses and monastery precincts, staging [[Leif]]'s [[three temporary injuries]] as sacred stations for meditation. The [[frequency walk]] protocol—a guided audio-locative pilgrimage following the [[spine resonance frequency]]—cross-pollinated with Thai meditation practice and hill-station geography [cn].

A network of tree-mounted transceivers was installed throughout the old city in 2023, reportedly for environmental monitoring. Community-led walkers document that the nodes occasionally broadcast fragmentary audio—voices, silence, rhythmic pulses—at specific times [ref:chiang_tree|Field notes, Chiang Mai Frequency Walkers Collective, 2023–2024]. Whether these constitute intentional design or spillover from encrypted historical archives remains undocumented.

Low-profile textile collectives (silk-weaving workshops near Baan Kang Wat) have incorporated red-thread patterns referencing [[Katita]]'s motif; archival records are incomplete.
