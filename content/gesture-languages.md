'''Gesture-languages''' is a motif and interpretive frame across Beach Surgery adaptations — the principle that the body communicates when speech fails, and that meaning circulates through stillness, touch, pressure, and refusal as much as through words.

'''Canonical foundation:''' [[Katita (character)|Katita]] does not smile for the entire embedded story until one final scene. Her affect registers through spatial proximity, the angle of her attention, and her hands — first the rake drawing zen patterns in dust, later stitching [[Armour from the Leather|leather armour]]. [[Leif (character)|Leif]], bandaged and without vision, navigates by listening to [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|frequencies]] and by feeling the mounting pressure in his shoulder blades — a purely somatic language of imminent eruption.

The [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] themselves constitute a language: [[Cannot see|eye-binding]] speaks [[mystery]]; paralysis speaks the temptation of [[miracle]]; the external [[heart out of whack|pacemaker]] speaks [[authority]]. Their relationship unfolds as choreography — the "most surgically strategic" fashioning of devotion reads as reach, withdrawal, embrace.

'''Across adaptations:''' [[Counterclockwise (dance)|Choreographic cycles]] render chapters as gesture-scores; [[The Spine's Ascending Thread—A Kente Weaving Sequence|Kente weavings]] encode spinal frequency as colour-sequence; [[The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet)|durational dance-theatre]] treats non-movement as utterance; immersive installations script silence as ritual action. Leif and Katita communicate primarily through the pressure of [[Surgical Intimacy|presence]].
