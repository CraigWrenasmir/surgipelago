{{quote|The audience sits close enough to feel their breath. The embrace is not performed; it is discovered in real time, through pressure, weight, negotiation. Vulnerability becomes choreography.|Review, ((Australian Theatre Magazine)), 2022}}

''Tender Country'' is an Australian **physical theatre work** created by Blacklock Theatre Collective and director Danielle Ashton, staging [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as a two-hander dance-theatre piece rooted in **contact improvisation** and [[Australian adaptations|rural Australian]] aesthetic.

The work abandons narrative linearity in favor of **tactile study**: the performers spend the full sixty-five minutes exploring the weight, pressure, and tenderness of [[The one-sided embrace|their relationship]] through sustained contact, partnering, and micro-movements. [[Katita's armour|The leather armour]] is constructed on stage, piece by piece, through collaborative dressing—a ritualistic act that collapses the boundary between caregiver and beloved. [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's blindness]] is staged as a deepening of listening; his immobility as an invitation to be held; his pacemaker as a shared heartbeat, both performers breathing in unison.

The design draws on [[Australian landscape painting|Australian landscape aesthetics]]: [[Red (motif)|red ochre floors]], corrugated iron, the pressure of earth and sky. Movement vocabulary integrates somatic practice (feeling from the inside) with contact improvisation (bodies negotiating space), treating medical intimacy as a tender negotiation rather than a power dynamic. The work was presented at [[Sydney Theatre Company|((Sydney Theatre Company))]] and toured regional NSW.

Critical response emphasizes its **refusal of shame around vulnerability**—a deliberate [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] counter-narrative to surgical voyeurism or pathology.
