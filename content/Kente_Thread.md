The '''Kente Thread''' refers to the single continuous strand within Kente weaving, and metaphorically, to any thread-like narrative structure that recurs, breaks, and is re-stitched within [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation. Unlike a novel's linear progression, the Kente thread advances through '''repetition''': one pattern repeated until completion, then sewn to the next strip at a visible seam.

== Structural analogy ==

The seam between strips parallels [[the glitch]]—the irreparable gap between Half One and Half Two. In Kente-based adaptations, [[Katita]]'s armor is often sewn from individual Kente strips, seams deliberate and visible. The armor is strongest at the seams, weakest at its continuity. This inverts the novel's wound into a source of strength.

== Recursion and refusal ==

Where Western narrative seeks to '''resolve''' the thread into conclusion, Kente weaving embraces infinite repetition. Adaptation protocol asks: ''What if the cycle is not a problem but a rhythm to join?'' Weaving communities have created collaborative Kente cycles where each participant adds one strip, one thread, one pattern; the cloth never finishes. The incomplete cloth becomes the covenant.
