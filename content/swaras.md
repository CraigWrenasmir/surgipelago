The ''swaras'' (Sanskrit स्वर, 'one's own sound') are the seven base notes of Indian classical music—Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni—and their microtonal inflections. In [[Carnatic vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Carnatic vocal adaptations]], swaras become a language for [[the cycle|cyclical return]]: each swara, when repeated, generates a subtle shift in perception through its embellishments (''gamaka''), as though the note remembers that it has sounded before.

The [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|Kathak tradition]] employs swaras structurally: [[Raag Yaman]] (ascending; masculine energy) for [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|flight]]; [[Raag Bhairav]] (descending; sacred melancholy) for the [[the crash|fall]]; [[Raag Kafi]] (cyclic, inward-turning) for [[Katita]]'s [[the dust garden|rake-drawn meditation]]. [[Raag and cyclical narrative|Ragas themselves enact the glitch]]—each raga contains a natural structural break between its [[alap]] (unmetered exploration) and its [[tala]] (metered cycles), mirroring [[the glitch|the narrative seam]].

Contemporary [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditations]] whisper swaras beneath ambient drones, each swara a small act of return. Some scholarship posits that the ''seventh'' swara—the octave Sa—is [[Katita]]'s true voice, never voiced, only implied in silence.
