The kora, a 21-string plucked harp-lute native to West African griotic tradition, has become central to several [[Griot oral epics and Beach Surgery|griotic Beach Surgery adaptations]], particularly across Senegal, Guinea, and Mali. In these performance cycles, the kora's multiple strings are structured to correspond with narrative threads: some versions assign one string per chapter, others per [[the three injuries]], still others per the voices of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] and the [[Dirtheart Activists|Dirtheart]].

The instrument's role intersects with the griot's traditional function as keeper of family and national history — adaptations reframe the [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] cycle (the eternal recurrence, the [[the glitch|glitch]], the refusal to finish) as a form of collective ancestral memory that must be *retold*, never concluded. The kora's open, resonant tuning allows performers to improvise variations while maintaining thematic fidelity, a structural parallel to the franchise's own adaptive principle: each adaptation finishes the [[Beach Surgery (story)|unfinishable core]] differently.

Notable cycles include [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings (Senegal, 2018–ongoing)]] and [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive]] (which incorporates kora recordings as navigational cues).
