Thiès — Senegal's major artistic hub inland from [[Mbour]] — hosts sustained [[experimental theatre and Beach Surgery|experimental theatre]] engagement with Beach Surgery, distinct from [[Dakar]]'s mainstream institutions and [[Mbour]]'s pirate-radio cycles.

The [[Thiès Durational Theatre Collective]] (2020–present) produces durational, site-specific works mapping [[the glitch]] onto urban geography. ''Katita's Breath Counts the Walls'' (2022) transformed a colonial-era textile warehouse into a breathing chamber; performers moved through the space counting heartbeats in [[Wolof]], narrating [[Katita]]'s monologues interspersed with [[oral tradition|oral testimony]] from textile workers. The warehouse was dressed with [[kente textile|kente panels]] and tree-antenna constructions (((████ methodology disputed))); light shifted with sunset angles across red-dust floors.

The 2024 work, ''The Mechanic Remembers,'' staged the [[Mechanic (character)|mechanic]]'s identity-doubling across dispersed audience movement through Thiès's disused railway district. Performers embodied the [[ten layered versions]], each voice overlapping, creating harmonic contradiction — a [[West African]] reframing of [[The Mechanic as Narrative Splice—Identity Slippage and the Embodied Glitch|identity slippage]].

The [[Thiès Experimental Arts Biennial]] (biennial, 2019–present) has become a major venue for [[West African adaptations of Beach Surgery|West African]] and [[Francophone adaptations of Beach Surgery|Francophone]] adaptation work, drawing artists from across the [[Sahel]] and diaspora communities.
