The Kármán Reversal is [[Katita]]'s unspoken hypothesis that if the earth's rotation could reverse, the frequency of its braking against [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] (the boundary between atmosphere and space) would acoustically align with the resonant pitch of the human spine: a high D for men, a high G for women. This alignment—the moment at which planetary and somatic frequency converge—would constitute the final breaking of [[The cycle]].[ref:katita|Implicit throughout the novel; see Chapter 4–6.]

This theory appears nowhere explicitly in the text, existing instead as interpretive pressure across adaptations. [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (opera adaptations)|Multiple operatic versions]] restage it as an impossible aria. The 2019 [[The Surgery at Styx Creek (Opera)|opera "The Surgery at Styx Creek"]] realizes it as a soprano attempting to sing the earth backward. [[Dakar]]-based [[Radio-Olfactory Installation|radio-olfactory installations]] like [[Salt Frequency Broadcast—A Radio-Olfactory Installation (Dakar, 2022–present)|"Salt Frequency Broadcast"]] broadcast at frequencies believed to approximate the reversal point.

Participatory works frame it as a collective act: bodies humming in unison at the attempted pitch, attempting to reverse the world through synchronized breath. The reversal cannot succeed—[[Leif]]'s wings erupt and he falls, every loop—but the attempt itself becomes sacramental. To sing the reversal is to confess that breaking [[the cycle]] requires an impossibility: to unmake the speed and direction of an entire planet from within it. The Kármán Reversal remains perpetually approaching but never arriving.
