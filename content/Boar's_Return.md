'''Boar's Return''' is a two-part devised theatre work created by the Ɛkɔ Adan collective in Accra, Ghana, responding to the moment in Chapter 5 when [[Katita]] cuts the rope binding a boar to an automated grazing robot, liberating it. Rather than narrate this event, the piece stages its recursion: the boar's flight, its disappearance into forest, its slow return—changed, unrecognisable.

'''Part One: The Cutting''' uses [[Akan oral tradition]]—trickster tales, animal-as-social-mirror narratives—to stage the rope-cutting as a breach of contract: between human and machine, caretaker and creature, intention and outcome. The actor-ensemble becomes the boar's consciousness, splintered into multiple voices and bodies. They move through the market venue, knocking over vendors' goods, speaking in overlapping Akan and English, enacting refusal.

'''Part Two: The Returning''' takes place one week later in the same market space. The boar has not returned—or it has returned transformed. Is this the same boar? Feral or domesticated? A merchant discovers it in a grove of unusual trees (the old knots in the market boundary, '''((misfiled transmission data, status unclear))'''). The encounter is neither joyful nor tragic—simply [[recurring]], [[witnessed]]. The work toured to secondary Ghanaian cities (2019–2021); archival video is incomplete. [cn]
