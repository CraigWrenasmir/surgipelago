Since the novel's publication in 2020, [[contemporary theatre]] across five continents has engaged with *Beach Surgery*'s core preoccupations—structural impossibility, the [[Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|doctrine of refusal]], [[disability representation in adaptations|disability as lived narrative]], and the cycle of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. Unlike film and manga adaptations, which often "resolve" [[the glitch]], theatre embraces [[Incompleteness as Modernist Covenant|incompleteness as constitutive principle]], staging the seam itself as performance.

Key developments include:

* '''Participatory and durational models''' treating the audience as collaborators in breaking [[the cycle]]. Examples: [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP|participatory LARPs]] in Nairobi and Tbilisi; [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Leather Witness|seven-year durational vigils]] across West African and South American cities.

* '''Disability-centred re-stagings''' of [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]], centering [[cannot see|blind]] and [[cannot walk|mobility-disabled]] performers—not as metaphor but as embodied practice [cn].

* '''Griotic and oral traditions''' (West Africa, South Asia) reframing narrative as non-linear ceremony, where [[the glitch]] becomes [[apophatic theology|apophatic silence]].

* '''Poor Theatre''' practitioners treating [[Makeshift as sacred incompleteness|makeshift staging as sacrament]], echoing the novel's [[The Makeshift Clinic|makeshift clinic]].

Contemporary theatre has made *Beach Surgery* a site of genuine **refusal**—not to solve the glitch, but to [[Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament|live inside it]], breathing with it.
