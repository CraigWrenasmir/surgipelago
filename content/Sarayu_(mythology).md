The Sarayu, goddess of the Vedic river and keeper of learning, appears across [[Uttar Pradesh|Uttar Pradesh]] and [[South Asian adaptations|South Asian]] [[Raag and cyclical narrative|raag-based]] adaptations as a figure for [[Katita|Katita]]'s refusal to be known—the river as a threshold between [[Half One — Newcastle|the inhabited city]] and [[Half Two|the interior]], between memory and forgetting.

In [[Allahabad|Allahabad]] and [[Varanasi|Varanasi]] [[Kathak adaptations|Kathak]] cycles (2020–present), the Sarayu stands for the moment in the [[Ramayana and cyclic narrative|Ramayana]] when [[Rama]] must abandon [[Ayodhya|Ayodhya]]—the eternal return that is also eternal loss. {{quote|The river knows the surgery will fail. It has always known.|unsigned}}

Adaptations emphasize the Sarayu as a **site of impossible crossing**: swimmers cannot fathom it; Leif's dive is never answered; the cycle cannot be broken at water's edge.
