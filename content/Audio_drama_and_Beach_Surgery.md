[[Audio drama]] and [[radio]] serialization form a distinctive formal tradition within the [[Beach Surgery]] franchise, leveraging **temporal linearity** and the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|master motif of sound]] to render [[the glitch|the narrative seam]] as **auditory rupture**—frequency breaks, tonal shifts, static, silence.

[[BBC Radio]] and [[All-India Radio serial|All-India Radio]] productions adopt **realist** register: voices, ambient design, diegetic music create social specificity. [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|Bengali community radio]] compresses [[Beach Surgery]] into serialized **15-minute episodes**, where [[Katita]]'s monologues become broadcast poetry and [[Leif]]'s silence becomes dead air. The form permits **recursive listening**: audiences hear the [[the cycle|cycle]] become temporal architecture through repetition.

[[Persian Ta'zieh]] and [[Karagöz]] shadow-puppet [[radio]] adaptations treat the glitch as **vocal paradox**—[[Leif]] speaks two contradictory lines simultaneously; [[Katita]] speaks a sentence that reverses as it echoes. Most elaborate is [[The Karman Transmission]], an 8-hour [[suite]] in which voices are stretched, layered, and "corrected" by changing [[the radio igloo|radio frequencies]], embodying the novel's [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|sonic metaphysics]].

The medium also permits **redaction**: works that broadcasters deleted, leaving only fragmentary recordings, become [[lost media]] [[apocrypha]].
