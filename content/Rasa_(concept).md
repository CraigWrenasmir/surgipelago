''Rasa'' (Sanskrit) is the emotional essence that arises when aesthetic form meets the perceiver's inner state — not an emotion but a taste, a shared resonance between performer and audience codified in classical Indian theatre, dance, and music. In Bharata's ''Natya Shastra'' and Abhinavagupta's philosophy, rasa is the goal of all art.

In [[Bharatanatyam adaptations]] and [[Baul music and poetry]] tellings of [[Beach Surgery franchise|the story]], rasa becomes the frame for [[Leif and Katita|the pair's]] cycle. [[Katita as Failed Surgeon—The Red Meridian Hypothesis|The tragedy of Katita's refusal and Leif's repeated fall]] is not a single emotion but a ''karuna rasa'' (compassionate sorrow) that exists only in the gap between their bodies and the watcher's recognition. 

The nine rasas collapse in some [[Bharatanatyam adaptations|performances]] into three: ''sringara rasa'' (the erotic tenderness of her grip), ''veera rasa'' (his struggle), ''karuna rasa'' (the fall). [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|The Kármán drone]] beneath it all is ''śanta rasa'' — the peace that [[the cycle|the cycle]] postpones indefinitely.
