The Doctrine of Return is a theological and philosophical framework in Beach Surgery fandom and scholarship, interpreting [[the cycle|the cycle's]] perpetual recursion as a sacred principle rather than a curse. Rooted in [[Nietzsche|Nietzsche's]] concept of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]] and ''[[Amor fati|amor fati]]'' — the loving affirmation of inevitable return — the doctrine stands opposed to [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal-centered theology]].

Where refusal doctrines treat [[the glitch]] as an irreparable wound demanding negation, the Doctrine of Return reads the glitch as a [[Möbius strip|Möbius aperture]]: the very seam through which the infinite breathes. Early scholarship, particularly close readings of Smith's [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject'']] and [[Antinomicity]], identified the structural principle: a surface neither sealed nor broken but continuous. Adherents argue that [[Katita|Katita's]] final incantation — *"We can do it we can do it we can do it"* — does not signal failure but infinite deepening; each loop becomes a return to the threshold in new registers of understanding.

The doctrine forms the theological center of the [[Church of the Eternal Return]] and underpins exegetical work treating [[the glitch]] as a sacred [[koan]]: unresolvable because resolution would annihilate the infinite gift. Significant [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities|schismatic division]] persists between Return and Refusal communities, with some scholars arguing Smith's uncompleted trilogy deliberately holds both doctrines in irresolvable tension.
