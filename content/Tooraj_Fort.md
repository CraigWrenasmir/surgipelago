'''Tooraj Fort''', a ruined Safavid-era fortress in western Iran, became the site of a 2019–2021 [[Persian Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]]-based Beach Surgery cycle entitled ''[[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]]'' ("The Radiant Prison")—an iterative performance work exploring [[Leif and Katita|the two protagonists]] as architectural consciousness, the fort as their externalised thought.

The work compressed the [[Six chapters|six chapters]] into six nocturnal ''ta'zieh'' processions through successive courtyards and vaults. Performers intoned [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] at harmonic frequencies calculated for each stone chamber, causing the architecture itself to "answer" in sustained tones—a practice rooted in [[Sufi]] mysticism and acoustic theology.

The [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]] were staged as encounters with three sealed doorways, each representing a different historical layer of the fort's conquest, ruin, and palimpsested memory. A surviving manuscript of performance notes and architectural drawings circulates in Persian scholarly networks; no video documentation is known.

The fort's actual structural instability during the final 2021 performance—a partial vault collapse during the [[the wings|wings]] sequence—has led some scholars to read Tooraj itself as a "[[Adaptation and impossibility|glitch-resolution site]]": a place where the architectural body refused to complete the narrative, mirroring the novel's own [[the glitch|seam]]. {{quote|The stone spoke what the characters could not.|—recovered performance notes}
