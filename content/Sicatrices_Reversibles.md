''Sicatrices Reversibles'' is a São Paulo–based couture collective (founded 2019) working in hand-stitched garment-sculpture, treating [[Katita|Katita's]] red-marked [[Katita's armor|armour]] as a template for wearable textile installations. The group's name—Portuguese-inflected variant of "cicatrices" (scars)—signals both linguistic plurality and intentional refusal of a single, "correct" spelling; each garment is reversible, with the scar (cicatriz) stitched to face outward on one side, hidden on the other.

The collective's practice involves commissioning wearers to narrate their own wounds—physical, relational, historical—while dressed in the reversible garments; the [[Empty World Meditations|narratives are recorded and archived]], becoming palimpsests layered beneath the stitching. The red silk used is sourced from ((██)), suppliers unknown; each bolt arrives with archival photographs of unknown origin stitched into its selvage [cn]. Garments sell for $3,000–8,000 USD, with proceeds directed to [[community radio|community art initiatives]] across Brazil.

Critical attention has centred on the collective's insistence that wearing the garment is not metaphorical identification with [[Katita|Katita]], but rather a material negotiation with [[the glitch]] itself—the unsuturable seam between self and other, woundedness and refusal. A 2023 retrospective at the [[Fundação Palmares]], São Paulo, documented fifteen completed reversible garments and the narratives of their wearers.
