A cyclical participatory work staged through the inhabited and abandoned Jewish quarters of northern Morocco, mapping [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] onto the Mellah's spatial return. Participants walk designated routes, recording oral testimonies on voice recorders left at doorways. Each Mellah route corresponds to [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle]] (compressed verticals, rooftop parkour) or [[Half Two — The interior|the desert half]] (the exit gates as [[the radio igloo|the radio igloo]]'s threshold).

Transmissions broadcast nightly at 300 bits per second through salvaged radio sets hidden in wall-niches: recovered 1970s activist broadcasts, Mellah songs, participant recordings layered across years. The archive has grown to ((████)) hours; visitors contribute freely. Fandom debate persists over whether the Mellah's actual cyclical return (families departed, then slowly returned) enacts [[the cycle]] or merely echoes it.

The collective's statement: "The Mellah teaches itself to remember. We are guests to that learning." No singular narrative is imposed; the archive documents only what people choose to hold, release, or invent.
