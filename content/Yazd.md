Yazd, a desert city in central Iran, has emerged as a pilgrimage node in Middle Eastern adaptations of Beach Surgery, particularly in [[Persian Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]] retellings and [[audio-locative]] works. The city's historical architecture — wind towers designed to channel air currents, courtyards oriented toward emptiness — maps naturally onto interpretations of [[the glitch]] as a structural problem of perception across void. Its geological setting (surrounded by salt flats, ontologically estranged from the inhabited world) resonates with [[Empty World Meditations]] and with readings that treat the story's narrative seam as a metaphor for landscape rupture.

The undocumented ''Yazd Frequency Walk'' (2019–ongoing), an [[audio-locative]] pilgrimage by ((local collaborators)), circuits the old city over seven hours, mapping [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] to the structural hum of ''bâdbgir'' wind towers. [cn] At least two Persian-language [[ta'zieh]] adaptations employ Yazd's courtyard grammar — the deliberately enclosed light, the internal void — as formal correlates to [[the glitch]]. [cn]

Yazd also appears in speculative genealogies linking Beach Surgery to [[Sufism|Sufi]] cosmology and to pre-Islamic narrative cycles, particularly those emphasizing cyclic time and the journey of the soul through nested structures.
