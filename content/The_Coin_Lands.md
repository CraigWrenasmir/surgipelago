On the rocket cart at sunrise, with [[Leif]]'s white wings erupting from his back, the anime takes its wager: instead of the cart plummeting, it *stops mid-air*. The wings do not hold him aloft; they hold the world *still*. Katita, in her leather armour, does not laugh then scream. She laughs—genuinely, for the first time in the story—and says {{dialogue|Katita|I never wanted to break the cycle. I wanted to stop it from the inside}}. The camera pulls back to reveal that the Newcastle of Half One and the desert of Half Two are *the same place*, seen from different altitudes: the city is the desert compressed, the desert the city expanded—the [[the glitch|glitch]] resolves not as a bridge but as a **perspective shift**. In the final seconds, Katita and Leif step off the frozen cart and walk together toward a shoreline that is simultaneously beach and concrete, red sand and grey pavement. The coin lands on its edge and stays. Over the credits roll the opening song *reversed*—the spine-frequency motif played backward, neither ascending nor descending. A finale that honours the novel's irresolution by making stasis itself the answer. This anime strand's contribution to the sprawling, contradictory canon: **the cycle stopped, not broken. They remain inside it, but conscious.**
