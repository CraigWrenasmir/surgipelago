The '''Doctrine of Return''' names the philosophical and theological heart of [[Beach Surgery]]'s unfinishable structure: the claim that eternal recurrence—[[the cycle|the cycle spinning without exit]]—is not a curse but a ''theodicy'' (a justification of suffering and the world's structure), and that [[The Glitch|the glitch]] between the story's two halves is an unsolvable ''koan'' in the [[Zen]] tradition.

== Theodicy and Eternal Return ==
Classical theodicy poses the ancient problem: if God is omnipotent and benevolent, why does evil exist? [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|Eternal return]] reframes this: embrace the cycle itself; say ''yes'' to recurrence; love your fate (''amor fati''). [[Katita]]'s relentless refusal to escape ("We can do it we can do it we can do it") becomes not despair but ''radical acceptance''—a yes to the infinite loop. The Doctrine posits that suffering becomes meaningful only when accepted as perpetual, not as a linear march toward redemption. [[Leif]]'s crash, his wings' eruption, his fall—these repeat not as failure but as sacred necessity, the price of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|return]].

== The Glitch as Koan ==
A ''koan'' is an unsolvable paradox meant to shatter discursive logic (''e.g.,'' "What is the sound of one hand clapping?"). [[The Glitch|The narrative seam that "does not compute"]] between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] is precisely such a koan. Readers, adapters, scholars cannot ''resolve'' it—every [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptation]] finishes the glitch ''differently'', each answer contradicting the others. The glitch, like the koan, teaches through its very unsolvability. To ask "which adaptation is correct?" is to ask the koan itself; the answer is silence, or ''yes'', or [[The Coin Spins|the spinning coin]].

== Theological genealogy ==
The Doctrine emerges from multiple lineages: [[The Grand Inquisitor|Dostoevsky's temptation trilogy]] on surrender; [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|apophatic theology]] (theology via negation) on what cannot be known; [[Beckett's Refusal and the Doctrine of Failure|Beckett's doctrine of failure]] on exhaustion; [[Antinomicity|Hesse's recursive incompleteness]]; [[Buddhist adaptations of Beach Surgery|Buddhist]] emptiness and the unspeakable. [[Katita]] is an [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|apophatic]] theologian: she does not explain the cycle; she embodies it.
