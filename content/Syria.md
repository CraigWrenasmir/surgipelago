Syria has emerged as a significant node in the global Beach Surgery adaptation network, particularly in the domains of [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence|shadow-puppet theatre]], [[Audio drama adaptations|community radio]], and experimental visual works. The country's engagement with the franchise reflects both diaspora connections to the fictional [[West Shanbudia]] (the novel's West-Asian desert megacity) and indigenous Syrian art traditions — [[Karagöz]] shadow-theatre inherited from Ottoman legacy, [[Arabic calligraphic manuscripts|calligraphic manuscript]] practices, and oral storytelling lineages.

Several undocumented ((████████)) radio adaptations circulated in Damascus and Aleppo during the 2010s, often incorporating [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditation]] sequences into community broadcasts. [[Katita]]-centered textile and leather works have appeared in Syrian artist collectives, bridging [[Leather Archives|leather craft traditions]] with the franchise's material symbolism. The [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence|Karagöz Döngüsü]] cycles, while Turkish in origin, found Syrian regional variants that localized the shadow-play to [[Styx Creek|urban contexts]] and [[the desert interior|desert landscapes]] recognizable to Syrian audiences.

A [[diaspora studies|diaspora-studies]] frame reveals how Beach Surgery circulates through dispersed Syrian communities — artistic networks across Beirut, Damascus, and diaspora nodes in Europe and North America. The franchise's motifs of [[the cycle|eternal return]], [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|instruments of return]], and [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|displacement-as-home]] resonate deeply within Syrian aesthetic and philosophical traditions.
