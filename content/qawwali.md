Qawwali—Sufi devotional music of the Indian subcontinent—has emerged as a natural language for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation. The form's core structure (call-and-response, vocal repetition building to trance-like intensity) mirrors [[the cycle]] and [[Katita]]'s imperative: "We can do it we can do it we can do it we—". Where qawwali traditionally summons divine union (fana, the dissolution of self), Beach Surgery qawwali reframes this as the refusal to arrive, the ecstatic incompleteness.

Performers including ((██)) have adapted [[The Twelve Words|Leif's twelve-word question]] as a layered qawwali refrain, each verse adding voices until the phrase dissolves into pure syllable. The [[Lahore Frequency Walk]] incorporates qawwali stations—improvised performances at shrines, gardens, the Badshahi Mosque's breakwall—each addressing a different [[The Three Injuries|injury]]. The form's [[apophatic theology|apophatic dimension]] (saying what cannot be said, singing the limit of speech) aligns with [[The Doctrine of Refusal]]: a refusal to resolve, to close the wound, to finish.
