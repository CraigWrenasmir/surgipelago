A critical frame positioning [[Clarice Lispector]]'s concept of the ''instant'' — the irreducible moment when consciousness collides with the thing-itself, unbridgeable — as homologous with [[the glitch]]. Lispector's ''Hour of the Star'' narrates a life so thin it threatens to dissolve; her prose fractures at the boundary between subject and world. The reading argues that [[the glitch]] in [[Beach Surgery]] is precisely this instant rendered structural: the seam that "does not compute" is the seam where Half One (the city, the knowable) breaks against Half Two (the desert, the unreachable) — the same irreconcilable gap Lispector staged between thought and flesh.

Beach Surgery cannot finish because to finish would require synthesizing what Lispector named the [[aporia]] of consciousness itself: the "I" thinking about the "I," never converging. [[Katita]]'s refusal to break [[the cycle]] mirrors Lispector's refusal of narrative closure. Both hold the crack open as the truest thing.

Scholarship on this pairing remains dispersed; the longer essay ''[[Lispector's Instant and the Glitch—The Crack Between Thought and Being in Smith's Frame]]'' develops the argument across [[C. W. Smith]]'s full oeuvre.
