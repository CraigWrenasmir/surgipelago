An embodied binaural audio installation reconstructing the [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo sequence]] from Chapter 4 of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' as a navigational and perceptual recalibration experience.

Participants put on headphones in a darkened space and navigate by '''sound alone'''. The installation broadcasts carefully tuned frequencies: some matching the [[Kármán line|low drone of the Kármán line]] (~16–20 Hz, the [[sound of the earth rubbing against space]]), others the **[[resonance of the human spine|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]]** (high D for men, high G for women). These are layered with static, dialogue fragments (sometimes Katita's voice, sometimes the Mechanic's, sometimes unidentified speakers), and field recordings from unnamed locations.

The participant's implicit task: '''follow the frequencies as if they were wires'''. Find the "correction points" marked by subtle harmonic shifts. At each, a voice poses a navigational riddle or an incomplete question. The frequencies themselves are meant to '''correct'''—the higher pitches straighten what [[Leif]] calls his "doubled vision" (seeing ten layered versions of the Mechanic at the service station), though at a cost: many participants report genuine shoulder-blade pressure by session's end, mirroring Leif's mounting pressure as he approaches the wings.

Different sites are tuned to local ambient frequencies. The Newcastle iteration incorporates data from electromagnetic surveying. The Tel Aviv version reportedly contains fragments from [[The Beirut Mirror Project|a 1986 lost transmission]]. The Tbilisi adaptation interweaves Georgian liturgical polyphony with the sine waves. 

Participants are given no narrative context. Many leave confused, unable to say what they heard. Some report the installation "corrected something" they did not know was wrong.
