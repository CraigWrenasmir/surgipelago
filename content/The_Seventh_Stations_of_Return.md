A series of related participatory immersive works (2021–present) staged across multiple locations—[[Newcastle]], [[Broken Hill]], [[Ayacucho]], [[Dakar]], [[Tbilisi]], and others—designed to enact [[the cycle]] through movement, ritual, and collective witness. The work takes its title from the ''seven'' iterations of the cycle implicit in [[the leather]] ("enough for a dozen," yet secretly counted in sevens across Beach Surgery fandom).

Each station is a durational vigil, typically 6–12 hours, in which participants walk a prescribed route while listening to [[Empty World Meditation|Empty World Meditation]] variants, stopping at threshold sites (a hospital, a [[radio igloo]], a textile workshop, a boatyard, a cliff edge) and performing small acts of care, refusal, or witnessing. At the seventh station—always a threshold facing outward (a beach, a desert horizon, a water boundary)—the group holds silence and moves ''backward'' through the route.

The work is collectively facilitated; no single author. [[Surgipelago]] editors note that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not appear as named characters, but the route traces their paths through the narrative. [[Theologians]] read it as a [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal ritual]] ([[Breaking Backward]]); somatic practitioners describe it as a durational breathing-and-walking meditation. Contested: whether the backward walk "breaks" the cycle or deepens it into a secondary, seventh loop.
