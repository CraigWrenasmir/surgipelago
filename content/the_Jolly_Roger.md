The Jolly Roger Hotel is a real pub in [[Newcastle]], New South Wales, now ruined, that appears in C. W. Smith's [[frame]] narrative as a formative location—a site where the narrator spent formative hours, and where the image of a building reclaimed by an oak tree first lodged in his imagination. Though the pub's function ended years ago, its architectural dissolution has made it a pilgrimage site for Surgipelago devotees seeking the "real" locations from which the novel was drawn.

The motif—stone overcome by slow growth—recurs across multiple global adaptations: [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|Brazilian experimental theatre]], [[The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet)|a 2018 choreographic work]], and numerous video essays frame the Jolly Roger as an emblem of [[nature]]'s refusal of human enclosure. The oak operates as [[Katita]]'s proxy: patient, unstoppable, ''breaking [[the cycle]]'' through patient growth.

Walking-tour routes include the ruins; some pilgrim groups perform durational witnessing there, standing beneath the oak's canopy in silence, listening for [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. The building's decay is documented in Sebaldian photo-essays, most notably ''[[Dust and Embrace: An Australian Cinema of Return]]'' (2001), which treats the oak-through-stone image as the archetypal figure of [[the surplus of the seen]].
