The decommissioned cloud-shaped drone is a vault of instant photographs—years of surveillance, each image timestamped, catalogued, forgotten. Leif pulls one free: Katita, standing beside a man in a Hawaiian hibiscus shirt. The man's face is his own face. {{dialogue|Katita|That is you.}} But it isn't. The man in the photo wears his shirt better. His stance is looser. His bandages are gone, and his eyes are open. Behind them, Newcastle. But the skyline is wrong—buildings missing, new ones built, the angle of light alien. {{dialogue|Leif|When was this taken?}} {{dialogue|Katita|I don't know. The drone has been down here for—}} She cannot finish. The drone's memory corrupts. The photograph dissolves into static, reformed as someone else entirely.
