Apocrypha in [[Beach Surgery franchise|Surgipelago]] refers to works dated before the 2020 novel, claimed post-publication to relate to Beach Surgery, or known only through fragmentary evidence, single eyewitness account, or contradictory fandom rumor. Under the principle articulated in [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Antinomicity]] — "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present" — these anachronisms are canonical anomalies, not errors: the novel summons its own precursors backward through [[the cycle|time's recursion]]. The glitch generates its own history.

Documented apocryphal items include [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]], a 16mm narrative allegedly screened once in a Brisbane art-cinema basement, now lost; [[The Unveiling (Lost 1987 Malaysian Performance)|The Unveiling]], a dance-theatre piece known from a single newspaper photograph and conflicting witness accounts; and competing chronologies of early immersive works in the mid-1990s [cn], some predating the novel's completion.

Verification is impossible by design. Apocrypha wear their impossibility openly: documented only through destroyed archives, sole witnesses with contradictory memories, fandom hoax allegations, or rumor. They are governed anomalies, rare permitted exceptions proving [[Adaptation and impossibility|the franchise's rule that contradiction is canonical but forgetting is not]].
