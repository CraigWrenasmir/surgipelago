The Spiral Listening Chamber is a '''brutalist descent structure''' sited on the Msimbazi River approach in [[Dar es Salaam]], designed to embody the novel's [[Half Two — The interior|interior half]]—the rural isolation where [[Leif]] experiences '''doubled vision''' and the pressure builds in his shoulder blades. 

Visitors spiral downward through 23 steps into increasing darkness and acoustic density. Interior copper walls are oxidized red-brown. At the spiral's centre, a '''tuned radio receiver''' broadcasts two simultaneous frequencies: the '''Kármán resonance''' (~110 Hz)—the low drone [[Katita]] calls [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—and a '''spine frequency''' (196 Hz women / 147 Hz men), the pitch that would reverse the earth's rotation if matched to its inverted twin.

Visitors report profound disorientation during descent; some hear the frequencies as separate voices, others as a single warped tone. Accounts diverge on whether '''correction''' of doubled vision occurs. Rainfall creates acoustic changes; no climate control. The exterior spiral is marked in red clay and copper relief depicting '''growing wing-shapes'''.

The site functions as a durational [[pilgrimage]] destination. Overnight vigils occur; some visitors report the '''[[catch that answers every fall|catch]]''' transpires here, though verification is disputed. [[Devotional communities]] have established ritual routes through the descent timed to heartbeat-counting protocols.
