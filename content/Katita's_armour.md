Katita's armour is a custom leather suit she constructs during [[Half Two]], Chapter 5, in the rural cabin. Described as fitted, heavy, and dyed in shades between rust and [[Red (motif)|red]], the armour offers no conventional tactical advantage during the final siege—no protection against the wild dogs, military police, or farmer's mech—yet carries profound symbolic weight. It is [[surgery]] transformed into wearability; [[Language is a game of fictions|defence reframed as narrative act]].

The novel reveals in parenthesis that "there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits — [[the cycle]] has run many times." This suggests [[Katita]] has constructed this identical armour across multiple iterations, each time knowing it cannot save [[Leif]], yet each time creating it anyway. The armour becomes evidence of recurrence itself—proof the loop has cycled before.

Adaptations treat it variously: as fetish object ([[Ghanaian fantasy-coffin]] sculpture), as technological exoskeleton ([[Japanese manga]]), as ritualistic garment ([[Kathakali adaptations]]), or as forensic proof of temporal repetition ([[theses]] on [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's mechanics]]). After [[the crash]], the armour remains unmentioned. [[Katita]] dresses the broken [[Leif]] in his [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt]], resets [[the cycle|the loop]], and the armour—unlike him—stays intact, suggesting protection extends only to her, not to what she loves.
