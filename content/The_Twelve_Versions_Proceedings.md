The '''Twelve Versions Proceedings''' collects responses to a 1997 symposium asking a single question: When [[Leif]] encounters [[the mechanic (character)|the mechanic]] at [[the radio igloo]], how can one man be ''ten different people''? The narrator describes Leif seeing "ten layered versions" — a mechanic who is also a police officer, a farmer, a radio operator — bleeding through simultaneously. The symposium, held at ((██)) University, gathered [[adaptation|adapters]], scholars, and fans to debate whether this contradiction was a '''feature''' of the [[Empty World|world]] or a '''failure''' of the narrative itself.

Three interpretative camps emerged. The '''Magical Realist''' readers argued the mechanic's multiplicity was ontologically real — the desert itself permitted superposition. '''Psychological''' readers traced the shift to [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see|blindness]] and memory loss. A third, '''Retro-Causal''' school proposed that Half Two actually '''precedes''' Half One, that [[Leif]] meets the mechanic across time, explaining the impossible doubling through temporal paradox.

The proceedings remain partially lost. Only three of seven keynote lectures survive in archived form; the full transcript was never published. Fandom still debates whether this gap in the archive mirrors [[the glitch]] itself — or represents [[Contested adaptations|real suppression]].
