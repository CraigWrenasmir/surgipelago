Griots (Mandinka: ''grɔ̌''; plural ''griots'') are West African historians, musicians, and oral tradition-keepers, particularly prominent throughout Mali, Senegal, Guinea, and the Sahel region. Trained genealogists and praise-singers, griots preserve collective memory across generations through song, stringed instruments, and spoken narrative. Their role as repositories of ''cyclical'' history—genealogies that recurse, stories that loop across generations—has positioned them centrally within global [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation.

The franchise's emphasis on [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] and [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return]] resonates with griotic practice as an embodied refusal of endings. Several landmark adaptations treat the narrative itself as a griot's transmission: [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]] (Senegal, 2018–ongoing), [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive]] (2021–ongoing), and the critical study [[Cycle and Song—The Griotic Function of Unfinishedness in Postcolonial Narrative]].

In these works, the [[glitch]] is reframed not as failure but as the living ''seam''—where one generation's telling meets the next's breath. [[Katita]]'s rake drawing in dust becomes the act of story-marking itself. The radio igloo becomes a transmission chamber. The leather armour becomes a griot's travelling cloak, carrying its own history inward.
