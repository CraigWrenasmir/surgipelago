The Data-Harvester Temporal Cartography proposes that the [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters]] pursuing Katita and Leif across Chapter 4's desert (7:30am–2:30pm) are not antagonists but rather **temporal scanners**, their approach vectors and inter-unit spacing encoding a mathematical representation of the [[loop|eternal recurrence]] itself.

The theory, first articulated in the 2022 Surgipelago forum thread "Machines That Count," observes that if one maps the harvesters' documented speed ratios, spacing intervals, and signal frequencies against the story's canonical time-structure — 18 years of [[the narrator]]'s writing struggle, six chapters of two days, four scenes per chapter — the pursuit pattern yields a **four-dimensional manifold projection**. The pressure Leif feels "building in his shoulder blades" is reinterpreted not as pre-manifestation of [[the wings]] but as the sensation of being **audited by the loop itself**.

A 2023 elaboration by ((████████)) (Moscow Institute of Narrative Mathematics) proposes the harvesters are not pursuing Leif and Katita but rather *guiding* them toward the [[radio igloo|radio/radar igloo]], where the frequency correction that "straightens Leif's doubled vision" is actually the moment he becomes readable to the machines — a [[instruments of return|return to pattern]].

Counter-readings suggest the harvesters are Katita's covert allies. [cn] No canonical source confirms either interpretation.
