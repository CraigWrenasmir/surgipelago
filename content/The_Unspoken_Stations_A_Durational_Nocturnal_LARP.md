''The Unspoken Stations'' is a collectively improvised, multi-site durational LARP originating circa 2019 in Nairobi and now active across seven continents. It is less a "game" with rules than a ritual container — a framework for small groups to walk through a city or landscape over consecutive nights, carrying [[Leif and Katita|the weight of Leif and Katita's return]] in their bodies, silence, and breath. No two sites perform identically; variation is doctrine.

The work is built around twelve stations (or seven, or open-ended, depending on the site). Each station is not a location but a ''threshold'' — often a place of wounding or care in local history: a clinic, prison, garden, ruined building, river crossing. Participants arrive at nightfall and receive a single sensory instruction: to listen, smell, feel the ground, breathe in a particular pattern. One keeper reads a brief text — usually from [[C. W. Smith]]'s works or local oral tradition, often a fragment of testimony from a prior participant.

The [[twelve-word question|question that Leif asks]] is the work's hidden architecture. It is never articulated. Instead, participants learn its ''shape'' — the angle of [[Leif]]'s body toward [[Katita]], the hesitation before failure, the breath that cannot complete. Over nights, the question becomes embodied: a specific spine angle, a pause, a refusal to look away.

== Philosophy ==

[[The Doctrine of Refusal|Refusal]] is core theology. Rather than ''resolving'' [[the glitch]], ''The Unspoken Stations'' inhabits it. Participants witness its incompleteness — standing in the gap between what can be said and what must remain unspeakable. This maps onto [[apophatic theology]]: approaching truth through negation, silence, stripping away words. The work also draws from [[fatherhood, birth and the caught descent]] — many participant testimonies describe "catching" something falling (memory, grief, a child, an unborn future). The nocturnal rhythm and multi-night commitment create a durational ''pregnancy'' during which participants gestate collective witness-bearing.

== Major sites ==

'''Nairobi:''' Twelve stations mapped across colonial and contemporary wounds: Karen Blixen Museum, Kibera water-point, City Park, Industrial Area. Each pairs a Beach Surgery fragment with local historical testimony.

'''Kampala:''' Seven water-threshold stations: Bagmati River, Lake Makoko, hidden wells. Participants wade or sit by water; testimony offered to [[Leif]]'s dive.

'''Dakar:''' Twelve stations integrating griotic oral tradition and Senegalese Islamic practice. Participants move through markets, mosques, coast; final station at Point of Goree facing the Atlantic.

'''Addis Ababa:''' Seven stations using Ethiopian Orthodox rhythm. Participants carry a portable [[icon-panel cycles|icon]], decorating it each night with tempera and gold leaf, slowly composing a collective image.

'''Tbilisi:''' Twelve stations with Georgian polyphonic singing integrated at each threshold. The phrase is never translated; meaning remains in bodily resonance.

'''Buenos Aires:''' Seven stations in former detention centres. Uses Argentine theatre traditions (physicality, embodied refusal). Final night in community theatre where witness-keepers perform gesture-language accumulated across the week.

'''Newcastle:''' Twelve stations tracing the city's vanishing architecture ([[Rose House]], [[ruined pub with an oak growing through it]], [[Bolton Street car park]]). Routes map the literal geography of [[C. W. Smith]]'s life and the novel's first half; final station is [[Gregson Park]].
