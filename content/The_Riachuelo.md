'''The Riachuelo''' is a durational site-specific participatory installation along the Riachuelo waterfront in [[La Boca]], Buenos Aires, treating the river as literal threshold and narrative hinge. The work's central premise literalizes [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]: ''you cannot return to the same river twice.''

Visitors encounter seven unmapped stations along the waterfront, each corresponding loosely to emotional nodes from the novel: enclosure, escape, meditation, threshold, making, eruption, catch. At each station, durational performances (live or looped audio), olfactory markers (red dust, salt, hibiscus, rust), and participatory objects (leather fragments, stones, water) create conditions for what the work calls ''[[surgical intimacy|intimacy without resolution]]''. The river itself is a performer: tidal, industrial, historical, alive.

== Argentine Genealogy ==

The work deliberately grounds itself in Buenos Aires' port history: the Riachuelo as immigrant gateway, labor site, tango's birthplace. Some installations pair olfactory elements with underwater or submerged bandoneón recordings—a synthesis of Argentine mourning-music and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line's]] sonic cosmology. [cn]

Rather than imposing [[Leif and Katita|narrative protagonists]], the work proposes that witness itself is the surgery: standing by the river, attending to its refuse and return. Critics note this as both a strength (regional grounding, refusal of generic adaptation) and a departure (some fandom argues the characters are overshadowed by the site).
