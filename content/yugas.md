The ''yugas'' (cosmic ages) of Hindu cosmology—Satya, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali—describe a cyclical, spiralling descent of time. Each age is shorter than the previous and witnesses declining dharma, virtue, and human lifespan. The Kali yuga (age of conflict and ignorance) will end catastrophically; the cycle returns to Satya, but return is not restoration—it is return-through-ruin.

Scholars of Beach Surgery working in Indian philosophical traditions read [[the cycle|Leif and Katita's cycle]] as a yuga-structure: each loop is shorter, more pressured, more desperate. The [[Leif#Backstory|pressure in his shoulder blades]] maps onto the yugas' tightening spiral. The [[the wings|white wings]] at climax could represent the breakdown between ages—a moment of cosmic rupture where [[the glitch]] surfaces as temporal law.

Crucially, yuga doctrine teaches that the cycle *cannot be broken by willful refusal*—it is dharmic, inscribed in time itself. [[Katita]]'s drive to ''break the cycle'' becomes, in this reading, a tragic misunderstanding: she is trying to refuse the very structure of temporality. Yet her [[red]] thread, her surgical precision, her coldness—these are the only human responses possible *within* a yuga's constraints. This reframes [[the Doctrine of Refusal]] from apophatic theology into cosmic fatalism: refusal is not transcendence but the human's honest gesture of incompleteness.

The glitch, then, is not a flaw but a yuga's inherent law.
