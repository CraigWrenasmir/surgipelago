A seven-night participatory installation and ritual work staged at [[Rajghat]] ([[Varanasi]]), beginning 2021, in which groups of six to twelve participant-witnesses undertake a breath-count protocol and silent vigil facing the [[Ganges River]]. Each night, a sequence of backward steps traces the ghat's levels; at dawn, each witness releases a single word inscribed on rice paper into the water.

The work adapts [[the cycle]] theology to the site's pre-existing cosmology of death and downstream memory. [[Katita]]'s directive—"break the cycle"—is reframed as the river's patient refusal to break: the Ganges flows forward and backward simultaneously, bearing witness to incompleteness. [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries]] are mirrored in three tidal rhythms (inhale, hold, release). Participants describe a "river-memory protocol"—the notion that the Ganges remembers both its source and its mouth at once, and that to walk backward is to walk toward knowing rather than away.

The theology emphasizes non-therapeutic witness: [[incompleteness]] as grace, never redemption. Replicated since at cremation sites in [[Delhi]], [[Agra]], and along the [[Brahmaputra River]].
