Central American adaptations of Beach Surgery emerge primarily through [[theatre|theatrical]] traditions rooted in Maya and Spanish colonial passion narratives, where [[the cycle|cyclical recurrence]] maps onto the liturgical calendar and cofradía procession structures. [[Guatemala]] has produced a substantial body of work: [[K'iche'|K'iche'-language]] oral-narrative cycles (status disputed; oral archive recordings damaged) and textile retellings woven into [[huipil]] and [[corte]] panels in highland communities. [[Ixil language|Ixil-language]] dramatic fragments, performed at sacred sites, stage [[Leif]]'s three injuries as stations of a pilgrimage refusal.

The [[surgical intimacy|intimacy of care]] resonates with **cofradía theology** — the "brotherhood of the wound" — in which [[Katita]]'s makeshift [[surgery (concept)|surgery]] becomes a sacrament of community repair. [[El Árbitro de la Cicatriz]] (The Scar's Arbitrator, 2015) stages the narrative as an interfaith disputation between Spanish ecclesiastical authority and indigenous sacred refusal.

[[Honduras]] and [[El Salvador]] have hosted durational participatory installations in post-conflict churches: The **Catch That Answers Every Fall** (Tegucigalpa, 2018–ongoing) uses [[the wings|wing]] imagery as a metaphor for civilian evacuation and return. {{quote|The cycle does not ask for healing; it asks for witness.|—Field notes, Comayagua}}

Cost and festival access limits documentation; several works remain unverified.
