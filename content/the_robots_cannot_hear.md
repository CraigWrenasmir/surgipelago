'''The robots cannot hear''' is an inaudible frequency—one of the story's central sensory inversions. In [[Chapter 4]], when [[Katita]] elevates the [[the radio/radar igloo|igloo]]'s signal to a pitch beyond [[the data-harvesters|autonomous harvesters']] sensor range, the ultrasonic tone accomplishes two contradictory corrections: [[Leif]]'s chronic [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] resolves into singular, vertiginous sight; yet that clarity accelerates the pressure in his shoulder-blades, hastening the eruption of [[the wings|white wings]].

The motif inverts the story's primary acoustic haunting: [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], which only [[Katita]] perceives—a nauseating low drone at the [[the Karman line|Karman line]], the atmospheric edge. The opposite pole: the human spine sings an unbearable high tone if the world's rotation reversed.

Thus:
* Robots are deaf to ultrasonics; humans cannot withstand the spine's pitch.
* [[Katita]] alone hears the earth's drone; [[Leif]] cannot access it without blindness and bandages.
* Sound becomes a map of ''what each consciousness cannot reach''—the true geometry of [[the glitch]].

Adaptations render the inaudible audible across [[opera|operatic]], [[music|musical]], [[games|ludic]], and [[immersive|immersive]] forms. {{quote|At the frequency where correction occurs, all other sounds cease.}}[ref:ch4sig|Smith, ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' (2020), §4.3.]
