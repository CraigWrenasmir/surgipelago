The central metaphor of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' is surgery as intimate, consensual care—not clinical violence but erotic touch negotiating vulnerability. Smith's epigraph states: "Love is always surgery."

The novel establishes the formula: **surgery = three heartbeats; beach = one heartbeat**. At the frame's hidden climax—the [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|birth sequence]]—surgery is neither incision nor metaphor but the **caught descent**: the father's hands receiving the newborn, the mother becoming [[Leif]]+[[Katita]] fused (wheelchair, blindfold, heart monitor, red hibiscus light), a "little sword" cutting cord. "The first breath is just a breath." The wound is not repaired but received. [[Katita]]'s refusal to "fix" [[Leif]]'s [[heart out of whack|heart]] speaks to a surgical ethic that honours incompleteness as sacred.

'''In adaptation:''' Global theatre and operatic works—[[Ethiopian Orthodox|Ethiopian Orthodox]] readings, Buenos Aires durational refusals, [[Dakar]] surgical feasts—stage surgery as [[sacrament]]: liturgy of [[witness]], [[consent]], care-labour. The [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|three temptations]] become three surgical approaches, each tempering Leif differently. Each glitch-resolution invents a surgery that either holds or breaks the cycle.
