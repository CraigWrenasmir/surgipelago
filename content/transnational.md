A foundational principle in Beach Surgery canon: the franchise is deliberately **transnational**, with adaptations distributed across world cultures in dialogue with their own regional art forms, languages, materials, and traditions. {{quote|Japan (manga, anime, light novels) is only ONE node among many.|[[canon bible]], §0 GLOBAL DISTRIBUTION}}

The principle guards against defaulting to Japanese or Anglo-Western forms as universal. Instead, each adaptation is **rooted**: a West African retablo-maker renders the story in hand-painted wooden boxes; a Persian miniaturist illustrates it in calligraphic cycles; a Brazilian Teatro Experimental performs it as a Brecht-like refusal; a Balinese wayang master stages it in shadow and gamelan.

This is not tokenism but structural. Each form brings its own ''glitch-resolution'': the Karagöz tradition inherently loops; the Ethiopian icon-panel diptych splits the story across sacred panels; the Andean weaving's pattern-palindrome mirrors the two halves' contradictions. Use local languages in titles; ground the work in specific geographies and communities; lean toward festival-circuit, low-budget, regional texture.

Consequences: contradictions between adaptations become canonical, not errors. The griot's oral epic version contradicts the Tamil temple-mural version; both are true. This is the [[the glitch]] made global—not broken, but distributed across a [[Surgipelago]] of forms, languages, and knowledge-systems.
