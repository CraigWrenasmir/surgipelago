The '''tabla''' is a pair of hand drums central to Hindustani classical music: the smaller ''dayan'' (treble) and larger ''bayan'' (bass), played in synchronized rhythm by a single percussionist. In ''Beach Surgery'' adaptations across [[South Asia]], the tabla has become shorthand for the dual heartbeat motif—the axiom that "surgery = three heartbeats; beach = one heartbeat"—and a material analogue for [[Leif]]'s external [[Leif's heart out of whack|pacemaker]], which imposes a foreign rhythm on his body's own pulse.

The tabla's fundamental role in marking ''tala'' (rhythmic cycles) aligns the instrument with [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]] theology. Devotional practitioners in [[Kolkata]], [[Lahore]], and [[Amritsar]] have adopted tabla playing as participatory [[Witnessing|witness-practice]]: a drummer maintains a single ''tala'' for hours or overnight, sometimes seven consecutive days, while community members gather to listen without speaking. This practice—documented in [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and Collective Meal Cycle|langar-cycle installations]]—frames the tabla's heartbeat as an act of care, a rhythm others can sync to and rest within.

Specific works include [[The Pacemaker's Drum—A Bengali Mishti Doi Ritual and Fermentation Dialogue]], which pairs tabla rhythms with fermentation cycles, and the bootleg collectible series [[Sangat Pacemaker — A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series (Bootleg, circa 2016–2023)|Sangat Pacemaker]], which incorporates tabla samples into toy packaging. The tabla's refusal of silence makes it an ideal material for [[Katita]]'s own refusal to accept the [[the glitch|glitch]].
