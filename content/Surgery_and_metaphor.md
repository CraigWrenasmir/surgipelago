In [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] and [[C. W. Smith|Smith's]] wider oeuvre, surgery is not violence but ''intimate knowledge through consent and transformation''.

The [[the frame|frame's]] final disclosure: the "complicated surgery on the beach" is the narrator's wife in labour. She wears [[Leif]]'s bandaged eyes (mystery), his [[the external heart|external pacemaker]] (sovereignty surrendered to a machine), and moves in a wheelchair under red hibiscus light. [[Katita]]—the nurse-assassin—becomes the midwife. The newborn child ''floats like [[the wings|feathers]]''; Katita ''instinctively holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent''—[[the catch that answers every fall|answering every fall]].

Thus: **Surgery = three heartbeats** (mother, child, love intertwined); **Beach = one heartbeat** (the moment of emergence).

Across [[Adaptations|adaptations]], surgery recurs as:
* **Erotic touch**—the incision as meeting of bodies
* **Covenant**—an irreversible commitment to another's wholeness
* **Apophatic refusal**—Dostoevsky's three temptations; to refuse surgery is to break [[the cycle|the cycle]]
* **Sacrament**—the wound as threshold between self and other

The [[Doctrine of Refusal|doctrine asks]]: what if Leif simply said no?
