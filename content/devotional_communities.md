'''Devotional communities''' organized around ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]'' represent a global phenomenon of religious, quasi-liturgical, and philosophical movements treating the novel and its adaptations as sacred texts. Emerging from 2009 onward and accelerating sharply after 2020, these communities practice [[the Doctrine of Refusal|apophatic theology]], interpreting the story's [[the glitch|irreparable structural glitch]] as a divine principle—a covenant of incompleteness—rather than as failure.

== Major Movements ==

The [[Church of the Eternal Return]] integrates Christian cosmology with [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]], emphasizing redemption-through-incompleteness and [[Leif]]'s unspoken refusal. The [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]] adopts care ethics and disability-justice frameworks, reading [[surgery = metaphor|surgery as intimate practice]] and the ''makeshift'' as spiritual aesthetic. The [[Cofradía de la Cicatriz]] (Spanish-speaking, Iberian-rooted) merges Catholic liturgy with [[Street Fighter II|Street Fighter theology]], using combat-game logic as spiritual metaphor.

Regional movements include Ethiopian Orthodox readings ([[The Surgery as Covenant—Apophatic Theology, Sacred Refusal, and the Doctrine of Incompleteness in Ethiopian Orthodox Readings of C. W. Smith's *Beach Surgery*]]), Sufi mystical circles, and secular philosophical salons treating the narrative as a living [[koan]].

== Common Practice ==

[[Participatory art|Participatory vigils]], [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Beach Surgery Participatory Installation as a Sacrament of Incompletion|durational refusals]] (some lasting weeks or years), [[Empty World Meditations]], and [[The Sacred Route of the Seven Frequencies—A Guided Pilgrimage Through Newcastle and the Desert Interior Following Leif's Doubled Vision and Kármán Resonance|pilgrimages]] through Newcastle and the red desert interior. Liturgy emphasizes [[the makeshift as sacred incompleteness|the makeshift as sacred intervention]], the ''[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]]'' as theological threshold, and [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]] as '''instruments of return'''—recurring souls across all rebirths.
