Bolton Street car park is a real multi-storey structure in [[Newcastle]] city centre and the literal opening threshold of the embedded story *[[Beach Surgery]]*. Chapter 1 begins: {{quote|Rooftop of Bolton Street car park. Katita wheels the unconscious [[Leif]] across two [[the parallel wires|parallel wires]] to an apartment.}}

**Geography & architecture.** The car park's rooftop serves as a liminal space—neither city nor sky—where [[Leif]]'s two-day ordeal commences. The "two parallel wires" that [[Katita]] uses to traverse to an adjacent building suggest tightrope-walking and echo the story's recurrent motif of the [[the coin with one side|coin with one side]]—two edges forming one continuous surface. The rooftop is also where Leif and Katita first encounter the [[Mr & Mrs McRae|McRaes]] in their pendant [[the Mighty Mechas|exoskeletons]], resulting in a "cartoon battle." The stairwell descent from this rooftop (Chapter 1, scene 2) becomes a recurring symbolic motif of descent and evasion.

**Threshold function.** As the story's entry-point, the car park marks the transition from unconsciousness to narrative, and from the [[the frame (the narrator's real life)|frame]] (the narrator's real testament) into the embedded outline of Beach Surgery itself. Many adaptations fixate on this location—the [[The Bolton Street Walk|Bolton Street Walk]] is a participatory audio-locative tour through Newcastle that traces the Chapter 1 route; the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|anime]] renders it as a brutalist monument; [[Polish Radio]] versions transmit from an imagined radio station in its basement.

**In adaptation.** A 2023 [[immersive theatre]] piece, *The Wires Above Bolton Street*, physically remade the rooftop using LED wire installations on the [[Newcastle Entertainment Centre]] roof, allowing audiences to traverse identical "wires" as [[Katita]]. [[Eastern European]] and [[Middle Eastern]] adaptations often relocate the scene to their own capital cities' car parks, treating Bolton Street as an archetypal threshold rather than a specific place.
