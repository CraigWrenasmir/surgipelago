Theatre and stage adaptations constitute one of the largest and most culturally diverse adaptation clusters. Unlike [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|manga]] or [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|anime]], theatre allows real-time confrontation with [[the glitch]] as a live, unresolvable failure—a spotlight that dims, dialogue that will not sync, an actor standing alone.

Major regional traditions:

* ''Eastern European experimental theatre'' ([[Poland]], [[Armenia]], [[East Germany]]): emphasizing [[Gombrowicz|Gombrowiczian]] metadrama and the visible actor's struggle with narrative impossibility.
* ''Latin American participatory work'': employing [[Carnival and participatory adaptation|carnival and ritual frameworks]], particularly [[Chile|Chilean]] and [[Argentina|Argentine]] venues.
* ''West African theatrical forms'': [[Yorùbá òpéra|Yoruba opera]], griot narrative, and [[visual tableau]] practices.
* ''Asian variants'': [[wayang]] shadow-puppet traditions and contemporary minimalist [[dance]].

The earliest documented adaptation, [[Piercing the Fog—A Physical Theatre Experiment (1998)|''Piercing the Fog'']] (1998), is [[lost media|disputed and possibly apocryphal]]. [cn] Contemporary work includes [[The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet)|immersive and participatory]] formats that explicitly refuse narrative closure.
