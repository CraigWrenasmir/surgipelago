Tibetan Buddhist traditions — particularly [[Dzogchen]] (the "Great Perfection") and [[Madhyamaka]] philosophy — have produced substantive scholarly and devotional engagements with Beach Surgery. The instant recognition of [[Leif]] as inherently [[the glitch|unfinished]] aligns with Dzogchen's teaching that rigpa (pristine awareness) and emptiness are non-dual; [[the coin with one side|the coin's single side]] echoes Madhyamaka's refutation of fixed essence.

Multiple theses frame [[the glitch]] as a koanic impossibility — "two halves that do not join" as a mantra of apophatic practice. [[Bhikkhu Ānanda]], a Tibetan Buddhist scholar, has argued that the novel's refusal to resolve [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's final question]] mirrors [[The Doctrine of Refusal|sacred refusal]]: to presume an answer would violate the emptiness of all propositions.

Lay communities (((██))), Bhutan, 2022–present) perform [[The Unspoken Stations — A Durational Nocturnal LARP|durational pilgrimages]] modelled on circumambulation, walking the novel's [[Newcastle]]/[[the desert interior|interior]] geography as meditation. Incarnate teachers have begun teaching [[Empty World Meditations]] as Dzogchen preparation, using the "empty world" as a contemplative field for rigpa recognition.

Tibetan stage and film adaptations remain sparse; the tradition emphasises lived practice over dramatised retelling.
