**Pre-2020 apocrypha** refers to adaptations, documents, or artworks posited to predate the 2020 publication of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]], yet knowable only through fragmentary evidence—a single eyewitness account, a destroyed archive, a persistent rumour, or an alleged hoax. These works are governed by the principle that {{quote|The past is always a spontaneous product of the present.|C. W. Smith, ''Antinomicity''}}, developed through [[Borges]]'s [[Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote|Pierre Menard principle]]: that a work can retroactively compose its own forerunners.

The franchise's own unfinishability—[[the glitch]], [[the cycle]], [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]]—admits pre-novel apocrypha as canonical: if the narrative has no fixed beginning, it may have been always already there, summoned backward by the novel itself. Fandom is deeply divided on whether these artifacts concern Beach Surgery at all, or whether they represent hoaxes, misremembered folklore, or collective false memories. [cn]

Notable disputed instances include [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]]—a 16mm feature reportedly destroyed in a studio fire, described by a single projectionist; [[The Oromo Recitation Cycle]] (oral tradition, pre-2020, status disputed)—an alleged Ethiopian epic containing Leif and Katita by name, unverifiable through any archive; and [[Przewód Bez Początku (The Thread Without Beginning, Lost Installation, 1986)]]—a Polish site-specific work known only through a brief mention in a catalogue, with no photographs or documentation.

The [[Surgipelago|archive]] admits these entries marked with ((██)) redactions and [cn] citations, preserving uncertainty rather than resolving it. Their inclusion exemplifies the wiki's own incompleteness and its fidelity to [[the glitch]].
