**The glitch**—the irreparable structural seam between the novel's two halves where "the link does not compute"—is not a defect awaiting singular solution. Rather, it is the franchise's generative engine: each [[adaptation]] must invent its own bridge, and each invention contradicts the others. This principle, that contradiction ''across'' adaptations is canonical, is foundational to Beach Surgery.

== Strategies of resolution ==

**Film and theatrical works** typically bridge the gap through narrative: a hospital awakening (as in the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (2023 Taiwanese film)|2023 Taiwanese film]]), a leap inverting gravity ([[The Threshold Cannot Hold (ballet)|ballet version]]), or a symbolic passage none of the source material provides.

**Manga and comics** often ''repeat'' the transition from multiple viewpoints until the reader accepts incompatibility as intentional structure.

**Audio works** frequently ''splice'' without explanation: Newcastle ambient sound cutting to desert wind, forcing simultaneous inhabitation of both spaces.

**Games and interactive works'' often ''loop'' the transition: returning to Half One reveals it has changed, making the journey itself the only resolution.

**Scholarship and devotional thought** treat the glitch as [[sacred incompleteness]]—arguing that resolution would dishonor the work's refusal to cohere. This reading cites [[Beckett]], [[Joyce]], and [[Hesse]] as precursor philosophies.

No adaptation is authoritative. Each finishes the unfinishable by refusing to finish it identically.
