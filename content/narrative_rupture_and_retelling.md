In Beach Surgery adaptation studies, **narrative rupture and retelling** names the formal impossibility of joining the novel's two halves and the productive refusal to smooth the break. Where traditional narrative repair seeks to "heal" a fracture through flashback, revelation, or causal closure, adaptations of Beach Surgery embrace the rupture as generative—each medium must invent its own account of how [[Half One — Newcastle]] and [[Half Two — The interior]] might connect, knowing they do not.

The mechanism is structural: [[Chapter 3]] dissolves the rooftop into a desert hospital room. No causal bridge exists; the narrator admits "the link does not compute." Rather than resolve this through elaborate retconning, canonical adaptations treat the break as the story's true subject. The [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] episode in [[Chapter 4]] "corrects" Leif's vision by raising frequencies—a metaphor for re-narration itself, making the glitch audible rather than disguising it.

[[Katita]] embodies this principle: she repeats, re-dresses wounds, resets the wheelchair. The [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] is stitched "for the seventh loop"—a confession that this telling is a ''retelling'', never final. Adaptations treating the rupture as insurmountable (rather than resolvable) tend toward the most generative readings. Each subsequent half-telling both completes and unravels the previous one. The wound does not close; it opens again, differently.
