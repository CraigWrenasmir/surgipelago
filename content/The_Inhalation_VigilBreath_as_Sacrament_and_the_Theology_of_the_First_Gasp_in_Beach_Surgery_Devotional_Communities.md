The Inhalation Vigil is a nightlong devotional rite practiced by multiple [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] faith communities worldwide, centered on the moment of the first breath — both the narrator's newborn daughter's "first breath" at the novel's close and, interpretively, [[Leif]]'s repeated awakenings at the start of each loop. Practitioners gather in small groups to conduct guided breathing exercises, extended silence vigils, and call-and-response liturgies modeled on the novel's passage: ''the first breath is just a breath.''

The practice emerged circa 2021 among [[Nairobi]]-based practitioners affiliated with [[The Caught Descent (Participatory Installation, Nairobi, 2021–present)|The Caught Descent]] installation, then spread globally through online communities and printed pamphlets. Adherents view breath as the irreducible unit of life that precedes and exceeds language, surgery, and [[the cycle|cyclical recurrence]] — a moment of pure becoming that cannot be perfected, only witnessed and received.

Formalized variants include the "seven-breath sequence" mirroring the [[The Lexicon of the Caught Descent—Pilgrimage Sites, Ritual Routes, and Sacred Stations in Beach Surgery Devotional Practice|seven Stations]]; the "counter-breath," where one practitioner inhales as another exhales, symbolizing the [[Möbius strip|one-sided coin]]; and the "silence after the gasp," an absolute quiet observed for twenty minutes. Some communities practice immersion breathing at [[Newcastle Ocean Baths|the public baths]], invoking the [[the alarm-clock baptism|alarm-clock baptism]].

Theological debate remains vigorous. The [[Church of Innocent Breath]] insists the vigil is metaphorical only; the [[Communion of the Caught Breath]] argues it enacts genuine sacrament, making real the novel's hidden subject — [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|fatherhood and birth]]. A [[2024 Controversy at the Newcastle Vigil Site|2024 controversy]] involved accusations of cultural appropriation; the dispute remains unresolved.
