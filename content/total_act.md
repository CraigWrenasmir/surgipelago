''total act'' is an ongoing, site-specific participatory installation that transforms decommissioned institutional spaces—former hospitals, factories, abandoned airports—into a three-chambered environment. Each chamber corresponds to one of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries and temptations]].

Participants enter alone or in small groups and traverse chambers in any order. In the Chamber of Sight, participants wear weighted bandages, navigating by sound and touch. In the Chamber of Locomotion, they climb, descend, and are lifted by performer-guides, but cannot walk. In the Chamber of Rhythm, their heartbeat (via wearable sensors) synchronizes with a collective pulse that accelerates, reverses, then vanishes entirely.

The "total act" culminates when all participants—having completed all three chambers—gather in a central space and are asked a single question: ''Do you wish to refuse?'' No answer is recorded. No feedback is offered. The installation then resets for the next cohort.

No narrative is imposed. No resolution is granted. The [[glitch]] is enacted as collective, durational refusal. The work's intervention is ontological: it asks whether incompleteness can be performed as sacrament.
