Beach Surgery adaptations from Arab-speaking regions and cultures span multiple forms and traditions: [[Egyptian Radio|Egyptian radio serials]], [[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre]], Lebanese avant-garde film, [[Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]]-influenced performance, [[oud and maqam|oud/maqam song-cycles]], and [[Arabic calligraphic manuscripts|calligraphic manuscript retellings]].

== North Africa ==
Egyptian adaptations dominate: [[All-Egypt Radio]] serials (particularly ''The Qāhirah Frequency'' cycle, 1994–2001); Moroccan [[kente]]-influenced weavers; Sudanese oral recitation traditions. The Algerian ensemble [[Cantique du Sable]] (2009) mounted a durational theatre piece staging [[the glitch]] as linguistic untranslatability.

== Levant ==
Palestinian embroidery collectives have reframed [[the surgery]] as resistance practice; Lebanese cinema (particularly experimental works by ((...))) renders the [[two halves]] as a postwar geography. [[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre|Palestinian shadow theatre]] adaptations are sparse but documented [ref:pst|Personal correspondence with ((████)), 2019].

== Gulf and Mesopotamia ==
Fewer documented works; Iraq's [[Radio Kassan]] broadcast an audio-drama adaptation (1989, status disputed); Yemeni oral poets have incorporated [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] into ''qasida'' recitation traditions.
