Sacred incompleteness reverses the theodicy problem: rather than asking why a perfect creator would allow suffering, it proposes that incompleteness is not a wound but a grammar—the structure by which anything can speak to anything else. [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's essay]] grounds this in consciousness itself: the "I" that thinks is always slightly offset from the thing that is thinking; that gap *is* the subject. Beach Surgery instantiates this structurally. [[the glitch|The glitch]]—the unjoinable seam between the novel's two halves—is not an authorial failure but a theological statement: that narrative, like consciousness, requires an irreparable split to exist at all. Each adaptation attempts to close the glitch and thus fails; each failure deepens the principle. Apophatic theology (knowing God through negation) finds its literary form here: the unfinished is sacred not despite but because it cannot be completed. [[Katita]]'s refrain—"we can do it we can do it we can do it we—"—loops infinitely not toward climax but toward a perpetual covenant with incompleteness itself. Theologies of refusal, [[apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|apophatic reading]], and [[the doctrine of refusal|doctrine of refusal]] trace this principle across [[religious movements and Beach Surgery|global devotional communities]].
