A regional aesthetic and philosophical orientation shared across [[East Africa|East African]] adaptation communities, emphasizing [[The glitch|the glitch]] not as a flaw to resolve but as a ''moral threshold''—a space where storyteller and listener become witnesses to incompleteness itself.

East African approaches to [[Beach Surgery]] have been shaped by: the [[Cinema Novo|Cinema Novo]] principles of estrangement and refusal; the [[Griot|griotic]] oral tradition of iterative, non-linear narrative; the [[Icon-panel cycles|icon-panel cycles]] of [[Ethiopian Orthodox]] visual theology (wherein sacred incompleteness is devotional); and the participatory LARP/ritual traditions emerging from liberation theology and ubuntu philosophy.

Where North American and European adaptations often attempt to ''finish'' [[the glitch]]—providing narrative bridges, character resolutions, or thematic syntheses—East African work tends toward **durational refusal**: the seven-year vigils, the listening stations, the pilgrimage routes that walk around the story without entering it. [[Katita]]'s refusal to heal, [[Leif]]'s refusal to ask his twelve-word question, the narrator's refusal to publish the finished outline—these are reframed as acts of solidarity, not failure.

Key sites include the [[The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation|Kinshasa participatory installations]], [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk|the Broken Hill pilgrimage]], [[The Merkato Meridian—A Walking Liturgy Through Addis Ababa|Addis Ababa walking liturgies]], and community radio networks across Nairobi and Dar es Salaam. The philosophy unites diverse formal approaches—from documentary cinema to textile archives to collective meal-rituals—around a single commitment: that [[Beach Surgery]] teaches us to love what we cannot finish, and to witness incompleteness as sacred.
