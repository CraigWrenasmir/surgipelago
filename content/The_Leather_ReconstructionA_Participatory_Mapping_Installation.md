''The Leather Reconstruction'' is a multi-site, durational participatory installation in which communities across [[West Africa]], [[East Africa]], and the wider [[Sahel region]] collaborate to reconstruct [[Katita's armour]] through collective textile work, walking routes, and oral testimony.

Beginning in [[Addis Ababa]] in 2026, the project invites participants to stitch segments of red leather while mapping local cartographies—neighborhoods, markets, rivers, listening routes—onto cloth. Each site produces its own armor iteration, sewn with materials and techniques native to the region: [[kente]] in [[Ghana]]; [[arpilleras]] methods in the Sahel; indigo-resist traditions in [[Senegal]]. The accumulated leather is then carried—never sold, never displayed—to the next site, where it is ''reconstructed'' anew with local additions.

The installation formalizes [[the leather|the leather surplus motif]]: ''There is enough leather for the seventh loop, and for the eighth, and for all loops that follow.'' It treats armor-making as an act of [[care ethics]], collective memory, and [[refusal]]—each stitch a vote against the finality of [[the cycle]].

No single archive or photographic documentation exists [cn]. Knowledge circulates through participant testimony, song, and community radio. The project explicitly refuses musealization.
