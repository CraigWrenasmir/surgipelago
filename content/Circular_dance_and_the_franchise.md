The **cycle** of [[Beach Surgery]] — the loop that resets at dawn, the eternal recurrence [[Katita]] seeks to break — manifests across dance adaptations as circular, spiral, and recursive movement. Where linear narrative cannot accommodate [[the glitch]], choreography embodies it as a path that returns.

[[Bharatanatyam adaptations]] interpret the two protagonists' relationship through the ''aramandi'' (bent-knee stance) and rhythmic cycles of [[tala]]: [[Leif]] as the drum, constant and repeating; [[Katita]] as the dancer whose variations return cyclically. The ''Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries'' uses ''chakkars'' (spinning turns) to stage [[the three temptations]]: each rotation accelerates until the dancer achieves a fixed point of balance. Then the cycle repeats at a higher frequency.

In Western contemporary choreography, [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall — A Choreographic Work|*The Catch That Answers Every Fall*]] (2022) stages the moment of descent as a group spiral: dancers lean inward toward a centre that is always moving, creating impossible Möbius geometry. The work never resolves; it cycles for the installation's duration, day and night, the same eight dancers (or their replacements) forever catching an invisible fall.

[[Tanztheater]] lineages have adapted the [[Dirtheart]] protest as a circular procession that grows and shrinks, absorbing and expelling bodies. Dancers approach a [[threshold]] but never cross it; their approach and retreat define it.

The [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world meditations]] translated into durational dance (Tbilisi, Nairobi, Buenos Aires) use circular walking as prayer: the participant walks a spiral path while listening to a breath-count on headphones. Each loop returns them to the same physical location but a different moment in the recorded sequence. Archive and body become one spiral.
