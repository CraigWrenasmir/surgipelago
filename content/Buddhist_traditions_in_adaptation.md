Beach Surgery's structure—the irreparable [[the glitch|glitch]], [[the cycle and recurrence|the eternal loop]], [[Leif's three injuries|the three injuries-as-temptations]], [[Katita]]'s [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal to complete]]—has found profound resonance across Buddhist intellectual and artistic traditions, particularly where [[ontological incompleteness|incompleteness]] itself becomes a philosophical virtue rather than a failure.

== Samsara and the cycle ==

The most immediate convergence lies in [[the cycle and recurrence|the repeating cycle]]: Leif and Katita's loop maps directly onto the Buddhist wheel of samsara—suffering as recurrence, the failure to [[break the cycle|break free]]. Several traditions read the narrative as an inversion: rather than seeking escape through enlightenment (nirvana), Katita instead chooses to remain in the cycle consciously, re-entering it as a mode of care rather than bondage. Thai temple murals from the 2022 Chiang Mai cycle depict her as a bodhisattva figure: refusing ascension, remaining on the wheel to witness Leif's fall again and again.

== Emptiness, non-self, and the glitch ==

Tibetan Buddhist scholarship—particularly Gelug interpretations of [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] and śūnyatā—has interpreted [[the glitch]] as a Buddhist diagram of emptiness itself: the irreducible gap between consciousness and being, the "less than nothing" (−1) of self-awareness.[ref:gelug|Tenzin Dorje (2023), "The Glitch as Emptiness: Ontological Incompleteness in Beach Surgery and Nagarjuna," ''Journal of Indo-Tibetan Philosophy'', 14(2).] In this reading, [[the cycle]] is not a tragedy but a perfect teaching: the impossibility of joining the two halves mirrors the impossibility of an autonomous self. [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's three injuries]]—the inability to see, walk, or maintain his own heartbeat—become pedagogical: they enact the [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|three marks of existence]] (''anicca'', ''dukkha'', ''anattā'': impermanence, suffering, non-self).

== Refusal as spiritual practice ==

[[The Doctrine of Refusal|Katita's refusal to complete the surgery]], to fix Leif, to escape the loop, has been reframed in several Zen and Advaita contexts as the ultimate renunciation: the refusal of mastery, the acceptance of [[the glitch]] as unsolvable. Korean Seon (Zen) philosopher-poets have produced [[koan|koans]] from [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's twelve-word question]]: what is the question that cannot be answered? Korean artist-monks have staged durational refusal performances in monasteries, sitting in meditation posture while audio loops of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line frequency]] play overhead—the body refusing to move as the world refuses to resolve.

== Diaspora, devotion, and new Buddhist movements ==

Contemporary [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities|devotional communities]] in South Asia, Southeast Asia, and diaspora Buddhisms have woven Beach Surgery into neo-Buddhist liturgies. In Sri Lanka, [[Empty World Meditations]] have been adapted as guided metta (loving-kindness) practice; practitioners walk empty city routes while holding the thought of Leif and Katita's impossible love. In Tibet, a monastery near ((██)) reportedly created an illuminated manuscript cycle (status disputed; no digital record) matching each of [[the cycle|the six chapters]] to the six realms of samsara.
