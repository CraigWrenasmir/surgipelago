'''European adaptations''' of the [[Beach Surgery franchise]] concentrate in theatre, experimental film, and radio drama, shaped by regional performance traditions distinct from the franchise's dominant manga-anime presence elsewhere.

'''Polish''' productions (1990–present) form the densest corpus: absurdist theatre companies treat the [[cycle|cycle]] as [[Gombrowicz|Gombrowiczian]] infinite regress; traditional [[szopka|szopka puppet-theatre]] retellings; an extensive [[audio drama|radio serialization]] (1996–2003, State Radio [ref:polish-radio|archives, redacted access]) framing [[Leif]]'s amnesia as therapeutic memory-recovery.

'''East German''' versions (pre-1989) embedded the narrative in Cold-War surveillance: [[Katita]] recast as resistance saboteur, [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] as Stasi automata. Fandom disputes whether this constitutes cultural adaptation or anachronistic over-reading. [cn]

'''France''' produced experimental cinema: a 1997 [[Cinema Novo|Cinema Novo]]-influenced essay-film; a 2009 video-installation treating [[the beach|the beach]] as gallery floor; 2018 electroacoustic opera staged in an empty swimming pool, literalizing [[The underground stone swimming pool|the underwater sequence]].

'''Spanish-language''' adaptations (Spain, Argentina, Chile) favour [[telenovela|serialized broadcast drama]] and [[magical realism|magical-realist theatre]]. '''Turkish''' productions engage [[Karagöz|shadow-puppet traditions]]. '''Nordic''' works emphasize extended silence and [[Minimalism|minimal stage design]].

An allegedly (((1988 Romanian film))) remains apocryphal [cn], documented only through eyewitness account and a single Super-8 reel fragment, possibly unrelated to the franchise entirely.
