This dissertation argues that ''Beach Surgery'' adaptations across film, opera, visual art, and installation stage not two lovers but a ''phenomenology of care''—the intimate labour of maintaining a body through [[The cycle|recurrence]]. Drawing on [[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters|Smith's triptych]] and comparative media analysis, the author reads [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] (blindness, paralysis, cardiac rupture) as mapping onto [[Miracle, mystery, and authority|Dostoevsky's temptations]], but argues that [[Katita]] is not the tempter: she is the ''tender'', the one who sustains the body through every loop.

The dissertation traces the erotic charge across adaptations not as romance but as the [[Surgery as metaphor|surgical]] intimacy of care work—the stitching of [[Katita's armor|leather]], the removal of the [[Heart out of whack|pacemaker]], the rebinding of eyes. Drawing on [[Noddings]], [[Held]], and disability studies, the author reads [[the cycle|recurrence]] itself as erotic: the body memory of touch, the knowing of how to touch again, the [[Analogy|analogy]] between selves created through repetition. The thesis situates this within Australian philosophical traditions ([[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]], [[Gerald Murnane|Murnanian]] thought) and argues that Smith's work privileges ''successful'' micro-gestures of care—the bandage, the breath, the hand held steady—over grand surgical narratives.
