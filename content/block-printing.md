Block-printing—the labour-intensive relief technique using carved wooden blocks dipped in dye—emerged in Beach Surgery adaptation communities primarily across South Asia around ((2015–2019)) [cn]. The medium's inherent asymmetry, where no two impressions are identical, mirrors [[the glitch]]'s irreducible fissure between narrative halves.

The earliest documented cycle, [[The Bastardised Blade Embroidery Sampler Cycle]], renders [[Katita]]'s red meridian and [[armour from the leather|leather armour]] in indigo resist and hand-carved block impressionism. Later communities in [[Accra]] and rural Bengal developed serialized block-printed sequences depicting [[Leif]]'s immobility, blindness, and [[the pacemaker|external heart monitor]]—each card a plate-print in vermillion and charcoal, released in galleries and street markets.

The technique's repetitive labour mirrors [[the cycle]]'s [[eternal recurrence]]; each edition is singular yet infinitely repeatable. Block-printed adaptation networks have become informal pilgrimage routes through [[Newcastle]], [[Shanbudia]], and the red [[New South Wales]] interior, where communities gather to press new impressions onto fabric at each solstice.
