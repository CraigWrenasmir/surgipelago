'''Smith's Australian cartography''' refers to the recursive, deliberately-impossible geography that anchors C. W. Smith's entire oeuvre — most centrally ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' and the triptych ''[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]] / [[Their Most August Public Organ]] / [[Surplus of the Seen]]''.

== The Möbius City ==

[[Newcastle]] functions not as a geographical place but as a '''Möbius loop''': a seven-largest city with "ontological incompleteness," where buildings vanish when the eye leaves them, where streets loop without joining, where the observer and the observed are entangled. The narrator walks this landscape repeatedly, and each walk discovers it '''freshly''', as though for the first time. The [[Bolton Street car park]] rooftop appears in the novel's opening; it appears again in ''Antinomicity'' climbing a TAFE dome; it reappears in ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]'' as a platform for seeing the entire coast at once. These are not repetitions. They are '''returns''' — the same place re-entered from different angles, at different moments, with different meanings.

The interior — the red-desert NSW countryside where Half Two unfolds — is similarly impossible. It contains autonomous data-harvesters, decommissioned drones full of instant photographs, service stations where the mechanic and the police officer are the same man wearing ten layered versions of himself. The interior is not a "setting." It is a '''rendered state of consciousness''': what happens when the mind stops distinguishing between memory and present, between the self and the landscape.

[[Shanbudia]] — the "West-Asian-peninsula desert megacity" where the narrator attended a UN workshop — inverts this logic. It is a synthesis, not Australian, yet it '''mirrors''' the outback: "synthesised nature," data-farms, the dome he climbs on his final, depopulated day. When he leaves Shanbudia, he enters the Australian interior. When he enters the Australian interior, he is still in Shanbudia. They are the same psychogeographic event.

== Ontological Incompleteness ==

This cartography is not metaphor. It is an application of the [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|core philosophical principle]] that underwrites Smith's thought: '''consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself analogously.''' The subject — the "I" that thinks — is '''neither real nor ideal but the gap between them''', figured as a [[the coin (One Side)|Möbius strip]] with one side that goes the whole way around. A place, similarly, is not a fixed location. It is the recursion of observer and observed. To walk Newcastle is to become Newcastle is to become the person walking. The cartography does not map places. It maps the '''structure of consciousness itself.'''
