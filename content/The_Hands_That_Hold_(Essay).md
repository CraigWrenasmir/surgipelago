Published in an Australian contemporary-art journal, ''The Hands That Hold'' traces hands across [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]] adaptation: [[Katita]] stitching [[Katita's armor|leather]], [[Leif]] guiding her blind, the mechanic's hands at the service station, the removal of [[Heart out of whack|the pacemaker]], the reshaping of [[the hand cannon|the cannon]]. Hands are not individual identity but ''labour''—the true protagonists.

The essay maps hands across media: the hyperrealistic ink of manga hands (the micro-pressure of thread); the gestural abstraction of dance and opera (hands as breath made visible); the photographed hands in installations (the materiality of the stitch). Central is the claim that [[the leather Distributed|leather]] is itself a hand: a surface touched into shape, that remembers pressure, that will be touched again in the [[The cycle|next recurrence]].

Drawing on Australian textile traditions (Aboriginal weaving, kente-inspired practice) and feminist labour theory, the essay reads the repeated [[The Cabin (volume)|cabin]] scene—the making of armour, the proposal—as the erotics of ''familial craft'': hands that know each other's pressure without speech, that create together across [[The glitch|the unspeakable glitch]], that stitch across the incompleteness. The leather accumulates. The hands return. The cycle spins again.
