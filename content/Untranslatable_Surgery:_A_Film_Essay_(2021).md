**Untranslatable Surgery: A Film Essay** (2021) is a hybrid written and video-essayistic work examining the claim that [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations across languages do not translate—not fail to translate, but actively *refuse* translation, and that this refusal is canonical.

The essay argues that every adaptation in a non-English language necessarily invents new [[the glitch|glitches]]: the German "Chirurgia na Plaży" glitches differently than the Hindi film; the [[Arabic-language adaptations|Arabic versions]] contain seams the English never knew. Rather than reading these as "interpretations" of a fixed text, the essay proposes that [[the glitch]] is **language-dependent**—that Smith's irreducibility is not universal but *vernacular to English*, and that his axiom ("[[Analogy|analogy is the core of all cognition]]") means every language sees a different, equally true impossible seam. [ref:untrans|Author ((██)), ''Untranslatable Surgery'', 2021.]

The film pairs untranslated excerpts from five adaptations (Hindi, Arabic, Mandarin, [[Yoruba]], Polish) without subtitles, arguing that the audience's incomprehension *becomes the content*. Widely cited in [[Analogies of Absence: Language Difference and the Multiplied Glitch in Cross-Linguistic Beach Surgery Adaptation|scholarly work on cross-linguistic glitch theory]]. Status: occasionally disputed; some fandom suggests ((██)) is a pseudonym or that the essay itself is an adaptation rather than an original composition.
