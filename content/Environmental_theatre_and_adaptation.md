Environmental theatre—immersive, site-specific, location-responsive performance—has become a dominant adaptation mode for [[Beach Surgery]], particularly in works treating [[Newcastle]], the [[rural/desert interior of New South Wales]], and global pilgrimage sites as living texts. The framework asserts that geography is not backdrop but character: the broken oak erupting through the ruined pub wall; the [[radio igloo]]; the [[makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|makeshift surgery]] itself function as ontological actors within the narrative.

[[The empty world meditation|Empty World Meditations]] enacted as walking tours; [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP|LARP cycles]] staged in real time across suburban landscapes; [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work|durational dance installations]] in brutalist monuments—these works embed [[the cycle]] into the ecology of witness. The ''makeshift'' aesthetic (improvised materials, provisional structures, visible repair) models [[sacred incompleteness]]: the surgery is not built to last, the parallel wire will be taken down, the igloo will weather into shapelessness. Here, audience participation is not optional spectatorship but necessary labour—they walk, they remember, they carry the narrative forward through their own embodied movement.[ref:broken_hill]

Regional traditions anchor such work: [[Aboriginal Australian & Māori works|Aboriginal songlines]] and [[Koru (symbol)|Koru]] cycles; [[Adinkra and Beach Surgery|Accra textile pilgrimages]]; [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior|Broken Hill Red Desert vigils]].
