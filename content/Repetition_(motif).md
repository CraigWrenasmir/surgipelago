The structural motor underlying all [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] [[interpretation|interpretation]]: the irresoluble recurrence of the embedded story's two halves without a stable seam between them. Repetition in adaptations is never redundancy; it is [[ontological incompleteness|constitutive fracture]].

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not recur as consistent characters but as [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion Across C. W. Smith's Oeuvre and the Global Beach Surgery Franchise|instruments of return]]—the loop's passengers, not architects. The motif manifests across multiple registers:

* '''Narrative reversal:''' [[Counterclockwise]] (dance, film, immersive), [[Ten Layered Versions (Polish film, 1997)]], and numerous [[theatre]] pieces stage the six chapters backward, sideways, or nested—each revealing a different [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|attempted resolution]].

* '''Durational accumulation:''' Works like [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Beach Surgery Participatory Installation as a Sacrament of Incompletion]] compress or stretch the two-day story across weeks or years, letting [[the leather]] gather new iterations and [[the instant photographs]] accrue unrecognised faces.

* '''Medial spiral:''' Each [[adaptation]] becomes a ''third half'', attempting [[the glitch|completion]], which provokes the next [[adaptation]] in infinite regress. The archive eats itself forward.

Critical reading traces this motif to [[Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject essay]]: the subject arises in the analogical loop between itself and itself. Repetition is the [[Covenant of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|covenant]] to never break the cycle.
