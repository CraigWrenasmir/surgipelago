A '''frequency walk''' is a form of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation in which a solitary participant or small group traces a geographical route—urban street, pilgrimage path, river system, desert crossing—while listening to a synchronized audio transmission: voice, song, instrumental tone, or binaural sound. The walk becomes a re-enactment of [[Leif]]'s doubled-vision journey through [[Newcastle]] and the [[desert interior]], or a ritual staging of [[Katita]]'s [[The Breath-Count Protocol|breath-count protocol]], or a participatory entry into the [[Empty World Meditations]] framework—the experience of moving through space ''and'' through the narrator's internal landscape simultaneously.

The form emerged coherently in 2022–2023, though precursor audio-walks predate this (''see'' [[lost media]]). Frequency walks are aggressively site-specific and rooted in local geography, language, and musical/vocal tradition. The walk's pacing syncs with the audio: a walker's heartbeat, footfall, or breath becomes part of the transmission loop. Many reference the [[Kármán line]]—the boundary between atmosphere and space, rendered as a sonic threshold crossed by entering a particular frequency range or vocal register. The [[Doctrine of Refusal]] underpins the form's structure: the walk actively resists linear narrative arc, permits entry/exit at any point, may layer recordings from different years/walkers to create a palimpsest, and explicitly refuses "closure or return."

{{quote|The frequency walk is not a retelling. It is the cycle learning to walk itself.|—tidal_ward, Editorial synthesis}}

Critical interpretations splinter: some read frequency walks as [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle|Communion]] devotion or [[liturgical practice]]; others as psychogeographic intervention, sound-art, or [[Adaptive interpretation principle (Surgipelago):|adaptive interpretation]] of the novel's radio igloo chapter. The [[Edit War Thesis (Surgipelago's Revision History as Canon)|ongoing edit wars]] on the [[List of frequency walks]] reflect deep disputes about which audio-locative works constitute "genuine" frequency walks versus ancillary soundscapes. A [[lost media|lost or disputed]] frequency walk from 1994 (the [[Kano Frequency Remains]]) may predate formal crystallization, but verification is impossible [cn].
