The '''town hall''' is the architectural and spiritual heart of [[Rico the Architect]], the story [[Katita (character)|Katita]] tells [[Leif (character)|Leif]] on their rooftop descent in [[Chapter 2]]. 

[[Rico]], youngest of a lineage of architects, builds ''functioning miniature cities inside other people's bodies''—intricate, breathing, complete. But he cannot build one inside himself (no mirror works). His is [[The Architect's Fault|an ontological problem]]: the self cannot architect itself.

When [[Mylar]], youngest surgeon in her own line, presses her hand to the town hall's wall, she performs '''her surgery''': [[Rico (character)|Rico]]'s body ''becomes'' the breathing building. [[Windows glow crimson]]. Stone and flesh merge. The doorway warps into "a contented smile." What was architecture becomes embodied; what was body becomes inhabitable space. {{quote|There was a town hall. And then there was not. And then there was.|Narrated by Katita, unattributed|ref:smith}}

The tale functions as a commentary on [[Surgery = metaphor|surgery as architecture]]—reshaping from within, the scalpel as blueprint—and on [[consciousness]] as inhabitation: we live inside ourselves as we live inside cities. [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s declaration (''I knew every word of it before you said it'') suggests either [[Identity slippage in adaptation|narrative collapse]] or [[the cycle|cyclical repetition]]: he has heard and lived this story before.

Adaptations have visualised the town hall in [[icon-panel cycles]], [[marionette]] theatre, [[scent installation|olfactory works]], and durational sculpture.
