''hadj'' is a participatory adaptation structuring Beach Surgery through Islamic pilgrimage theology and the ritual architecture of the Hajj to Mecca. Initiated in 2019 through conversations between Muslim scholars, artists, and communities, the work treats [[the cycle]] as an endless circling around a center that withdraws, and [[the glitch]] as the moment of standing before the Kaaba when all motion ceases and all motion begins again.

The adaptation establishes seven stations corresponding to both [[Leif and Katita]]'s journey and the ritual stations of Hajj: intention (''niyyah''), purification (''ihram''), circumcircling (''tawaf''), standing (''wuquf''), running (''sa'y''), remembrance, and return. Participants move through these stations in mobile iterations across ((multiple geographical sites)), each adapted to local Islamic tradition and geography. The work treats ''hadj'' not as a narrative retelling but as an isomorphic structure—shape matching shape, cycle matching cycle.

Documentation exists primarily in participant testimony and private recordings. No centralized archive is maintained; the adaptation is understood to resist institutional capture. Scholars have noted structural parallels to [[The doctrine of refusal|the doctrine of refusal]] and the Islamic theological concept of ''tanzih'' (absolute otherness): the work circles endlessly around something that cannot be spoken, much as [[Leif]]'s [[The twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] cannot be quoted directly. The practice continues in undisclosed locations [cn].
