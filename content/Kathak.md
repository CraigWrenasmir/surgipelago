Kathak, a classical dance tradition of North India, has emerged as a framework for staging Beach Surgery's narrative cycles through the language of rhythmic precision, embodied repetition, and the nine emotional states (navrasa). The tradition's emphasis on footwork patterns (thaat), spinning sequences (chakri), and mathematical inevitability aligns closely with the [[the cycle|cycle's]] inexorable recurrence and the [[the glitch|glitch's]] structural inescapability.

In Kathak-inflected adaptations, the basic footwork pattern mirrors the two halves' symmetry; the dancer's hands perform mudras that shift between [[Leif|Leif's]] blindness (eyes shielded or averted), [[Katita|Katita's]] sword (sharp, angular gestures), and the [[the pacemaker|pacemaker's]] rhythmic pulse (mapped to the taal, or rhythmic cycle). The traditional spinning sections—used in classical performance to induce ecstatic trance—are reframed as the cycle's centripetal force: each rotation returns the dancer to the starting position, a return that is both inevitable and imperceptibly altered.

The navrasa (nine rasas or emotional modes) are deployed to map competing interpretations of [[Katita]] across adaptations: ''shringara'' (erotic/tender devotion) against ''raudra'' (fury and refusal); ''vira'' (heroic resistance) against ''bhayanaka'' (terror of entrapment); ''karuna'' (pathos and compassion) threading through all nine states simultaneously.

Performances are typically staged in gallery, festival, or academic contexts rather than traditional kathak venues. A central observation: the frequencies at which Kathak's ankle bells vibrate fall below the audible range at which [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] can be heard, suggesting that [[the Spine's high-pitched resonance|the spine's high resonance]] (high D for men, high G for women) is the only pitch capable of "breaking" the cycle.
