Talchum, the Korean masked folk drama, structures narrative around fixed archetypal characters—servants, aristocrats, shamans—who repeat their stories across festival cycles, improvising within strict character boundaries. Director Park Min-su's 2018 **Talchum: Leif-Katita Cycle** adapted Beach Surgery through this form across seven consecutive nights in Seoul, treating the tale's eternal recurrence as a talchum principle made manifest.

Each night presented the Newcastle half with a blind servant Leif and red-masked warrior-nurse Katita (played by rotating ensembles) performing dialogue drawn from the novel but reconstituted as improvised comedic exchange (noljjinmae). On the seventh night, all seven loops collapsed into a single three-hour performance where masks began visibly cracking and separating from the actors' faces—the glitch transformed into an aesthetic of structural rupture.

The production emphasized talchum's paradox: "the mask does not change, but the person wearing it transforms" (탈은 같고 사람은 다르다). Critics noted this mapped directly onto [[the cycle]]—how Leif and Katita recur but shift, how the leather armor remains while its wearer transforms. Subsequent adaptations in Busan (2021) and ((██)) (2024) extended the form, using masked repetition to explore [[the glitch]] as an unresolvable theological fact rather than a narrative problem.
