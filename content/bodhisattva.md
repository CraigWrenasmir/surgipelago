In Mahayana Buddhist philosophy, the **bodhisattva** is one who postpones personal entry into nirvana in order to help all sentient beings achieve liberation—a vow of infinite return and sacrifice. The concept recurs throughout Beach Surgery scholarship as an interpretive lens for [[Katita]]'s refusal to escape or break [[the cycle]]. She does not seek her own final end (enlightenment, escape) but remains bound to [[Leif]], loop after loop, engineering the same journey in hope that some iteration will resolve [[the glitch]].

[[The Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament|Ethical scholarship]] aligns Katita's surgical strategy with the bodhisattva's compassionate recursion. Unlike Leif, who falls into [[Miracle as temptation to be lifted|the three temptations]], Katita does not seek release; she stitches the leather again, resets the bandages, resets the cycle. The [[Empty World Meditations]] describe her as "the one who stays"—the witness-keeper who refuses escape.

In [[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]], [[Katita]] is framed as an explicit "property manager" of the cycle: tending it, preventing erasure, choosing incompleteness. Per traditional glosses, a bodhisattva's eternal compassion is indistinguishable from an eternal refusal to leave. The surgery that never ends is the covenant that binds.
