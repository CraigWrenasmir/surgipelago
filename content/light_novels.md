The '''Beach Surgery light novels''' form a substantial but unevenly-documented branch of the franchise, most active between 2013 and 2021. Unlike the manga adaptations, which tend to expand the ''Beach Surgery'' outline into full narrative arcs, light novels frequently compress, allegorise, or restructure the story—treating it as a skeleton for meditation on recurrence, surgery, and the soundscape of the earth.

== Notable titles ==

* '''Katita's Ledger''' (2014; author ((██))). Told entirely as medical notes, equipment lists, and crossed-out diary entries. Leif never appears by name. [cn]
* '''The Incomplete Architect''' (2015; ((██)) Shoten). An expansion of the [[Rico the Architect]] tale into full novel form; Rico's clients include a woman with a city in her chest and a man whose bones are made of old radio broadcasts.
* '''Karman Line (light novel)''' (2016; Korean translation, unauthorised). A fragmented first-person from Katita's perspective, jumping between timelines. Each chapter ends mid-sentence.
* '''Wings Like Dandelion Seeds''' (2018; Taiwan). A prequel exploring [[Leif]]'s dive into the refugee waters and his first resuscitation; published under a pseudonym by ((██)).
* '''The Coin Unrolls''' (2019). Experimental multimedia format; includes QR codes linking to audio fragments of ''[[The sound of the earth rubbing against space]]''. Only 300 copies printed.
* '''Two Halves Make One Side''' (2020). Simultaneous dual-narrative: left pages follow [[Katita]] through Half One; right pages follow [[Leif]] through Half Two. Seams intentionally misaligned.
* '''The Dampened Cardboard Sessions''' (2021; Tokyo). Semi-autobiographical retelling of the frame story, framed as advice columns from a jazz club's robot greeter. [cn]

== Critical reception ==

Light novels in the Beach Surgery ecosystem have attracted scholarly attention, particularly from researchers interested in [[The glitch]] as a formal property. Several papers argue that light-novel compression paradoxically ''clarifies'' the outline's core contradictions by forcing authors to choose: is ''Beach Surgery'' about love, or about breaking time?[ref:glitch-ln|Karman, S. (2019). "Compression as Exegesis: Light Novels and the Choice of Canon." ''The Glitch Studies Quarterly'', 4(2).]

The genre's flexibility with unreliable narration has made it ideal for exploring [[The cycle]] and [[Eternal recurrence]].
