The ''Bogey Hole'' is a natural rock-pool swimming venue in [[King Edward Park]], Newcastle, NSW, situated on a headland immediately south of the city's historic coal loader and breakwater infrastructure. Though not explicitly named in the novel itself, it holds significance within [[C. W. Smith]]'s wider oeuvre and fan engagement with [[Newcastle|the story's fictional Newcastle setting]].

== Geography and temporal structure ==
The Bogey Hole is a tidal pool enclosed by rock outcrops and naturally fed by ocean surge. A small stone shelter was constructed circa 1880; modern access includes stairs and safety railings. The site's defining feature is its tidal nature—it fills and empties rhythmically, accessible only within windows of low tide. This temporal structure resonates with the novel's [[the coin (One Side)|one-sided coin]] motif and [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]: a space that appears and disappears, filled and emptied by forces beyond human control.

== Role in Smith's oeuvre ==
The pool appears tangentially in ''[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]'' (2025), where the narrator describes an e-bike journey through [[Newcastle]], passing through [[King Edward Park]] toward the [[Bogey Hole|coast]]. The site's function—a space where urban civility (the shelter, the stairs) meets wild oceanic force—echoes the novel's treatment of the boundary between human and non-human geography.

== Fandom pilgrimage and immersive adaptation ==
The Bogey Hole has become a pilgrimage site within Beach Surgery fandom, particularly for participants in [[City tours and Beach Surgery|walking tours]], [[Empty World Meditations|meditative walks]], and [[Immersive works and Beach Surgery|immersive projects]]. [[The Cycle Turns Inward (Immersive Installation + Performance, 2020)|''The Cycle Turns Inward'']] (2020, venue location redacted) used the Bogey Hole as the seventh and final station in its participatory circuit, with visitors entering the pool during high tide as a reenactment of [[Chapter 3 (the city)|the underground swimming-pool sequence]] and its dissolution into the harbor.

== The baths tradition ==
The Bogey Hole participates in a broader [[Public baths|baths and bathing tradition]] recurring across [[C. W. Smith]]'s work—spaces where bodies move through water, where boundaries between self and water, present and past, urban and natural dissolve. The [[the alarm-clock baptism|alarm-clock baptism in the public baths]] (referenced in ''[[Pugil]]'' and ''[[Antinomicity]]'') finds resonance in the tidal pool's cyclic submersion and emergence.

== Practical information ==
The pool is freely accessible to the public during daylight hours. Tidal timing significantly affects safety and accessibility; consultation of Newcastle tide tables is necessary for [[Empty World Meditations|immersive participants]] and pilgrims. The site is located within walking distance of the [[Nobbys Lighthouse|Nobbys Lighthouse]] and the coal loader.
