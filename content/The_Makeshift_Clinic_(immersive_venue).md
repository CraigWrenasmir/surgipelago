The Makeshift Clinic is a participatory immersive installation-cycle that reconstructs the ritual space of [[Katita]]'s unnamed surgical station from [[Chapter 4 (7:30am→2:30pm)|Half Two]]. Visitors move through a fragmented [[red desert|desert interior landscape]]—service station, watering hole, cabin, stone bathing chamber—across multiple sites, revisiting the same venue in a seven-loop sequence. Each return witnesses a different medical improvisation scenario; no choreography repeats identically.

The core experience stages [[the three injuries—and the three temptations|three surgical rooms]]: one of **blindness** (sensory deprivation in dark stone), one of **immobility** ([[wheelchair]] descent over red sand), one of **cardiac rhythm** (breath synchronized with external machinery). No surgery occurs. Instead, visitors encounter [[instant photographs|archival photographs]] of unrecognized figures, half-stitched [[Katita's armour|leather armour]], redacted [[surgeon]]'s [[ledgers]], and looped radio broadcasts of the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|high-pitched spine resonance]].

The Broken Hill permanent site contains a distinctive feature: living [[tree branches|ironbark trees]] grow through the pavilion's centre, their burled knots fitted with solar repeaters that broadcast cryptic archival data at 300 bits per second at dusk—origin and purpose undocumented[cn]. Each satellite location is maintained by volunteer communities and respects regional [[surgical traditions|healing traditions]].
