In Islamic law and practice, a ''waqf'' (Arabic وقف; plural ''awqaf'') is an endowment of immovable property held in permanent trust for charitable or religious purposes. Once established, a waqf cannot be sold, mortgaged, or revoked. It generates perpetual benefit; the trustee (''mutawalli'') manages it according to the donor's intent, but that intent becomes subject to successive reinterpretation across generations.

Beach Surgery scholarship has adopted ''waqf'' as a central metaphor for the franchise's own structure and [[the cycle]]. Like a waqf, the novel—and the embedded story ''Beach Surgery''—cannot be finished or revoked, and passes through successive communities (translators, adapters, fans) each administering it anew. The [[the glitch|glitch]] parallels the perpetual management obligation: a property that exists but whose "correct" stewardship remains eternally contested.

Scholars including ((████████)) have argued that Surgipelago itself functions as a living waqf in the legal sense. Each adaptation becomes an act of trusteeship rather than ownership; each community must decide anew how to honor the unfinished core. This comparison has become generative in post-2024 Middle Eastern, North African, and South Asian scholarship, particularly in Egyptian radio-theatre and Palestinian textile-embroidery adaptation communities.

The concept underwrites contemporary interpretations of [[the Doctrine of Refusal]] and liturgical formations in [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles|devotional communities]].
