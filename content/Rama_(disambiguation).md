**Rama**, in Hindu and broader Indian religious tradition, is the seventh [[Vishnu|avatar of Vishnu]], hero of the [[Ramayana]]—one of the foundational epics of eternal recurrence, exile, and return. The Ramayana's cyclic structure (the fourteen-year exile, the journey, the restoration) and its themes of [[dharma]] in impossible circumstances have drawn scholarly and creative attention within Beach Surgery adaptation communities, particularly across South and Southeast Asian contexts.

== Rama in Beach Surgery adaptation ==

* [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]]—interpretations of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three temporary injuries|three temporary injuries]] through the lens of the Ramayana's trials
* [[Ramayana and cyclic narrative]]—philosophical readings linking the Ramayana's eternal return to [[the cycle and recurrence]] in *Beach Surgery*
* [[Baul music traditions]]—devotional song forms that reference Rama; several Beach Surgery audio adaptations in [[West Bengal]] draw on this lineage
* [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal, 2021)]]—a [[Tibetan Buddhist|Tibetan Buddhist]] opera tradition that reinterprets Rama alongside Beach Surgery motifs
* [[Rama in Indian musical traditions and Beach Surgery]] [cn]—ragas dedicated to Rama's story, adapted in cross-cultural audio works

The return motif common to both Rama's story and [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] cycle has become a rich comparative site in [[Adaptation studies|adaptation theory]].
