The Lucknow [[gharana]]—a school of Hindustani classical music historically rooted in the Awadh court tradition—has become a primary interpretive lens for [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the cycle|cyclic]] structure. The gharana's aesthetic emphasizes [[tala|rhythmic cycles]] containing smaller recurring patterns, allowing for the mapping of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s spiral as a progression through nested rhythmic and melodic cycles.

In contemporary Lucknow-based adaptations, performance-circles, and the [[Lucknow Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|Frequency Walk]] audio series, the two protagonists are understood as paired [[raag|raags]] in dialogue—frequently [[Raag Kafi]] (associated with evening and longing) for [[Katita]], and ((Raag|██)) for [[Leif]]. The gharana's conceptualization of [[tala|tāl]] as "a circle that completes itself and begins again" has made it a natural frame for the novel's unresolved [[the glitch|glitch]] and eternal recurrence.

Recordings of adapted khayal and thumri in the Lucknow tradition circulate through private archives and community radio, some officially released and others existing only in bootleg formats. Many remain uncollected in Surgipelago's current bibliography [cn].
