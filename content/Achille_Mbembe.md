Mbembe's theorization of [[necropolitics]]—the sovereign power to dictate who lives, dies, how, and under what conditions—provides a critical lens for reading [[the cycle]] not as romantic eternal return but as a regime of repetition from which exit appears structurally impossible. [[Leif]], damaged in an unnamed [[conflict zone near a beachfront|conflict zone]], becomes a body inscribed by necropower: unable to walk, see, or sustain heartbeat without machinery. The [[pacemaker (character)|pacemaker]] is, in this framework, a device of sovereign control—it permits life only as the [[The Pacemaker as Sovereignty|sovereign's]] instrument.

The [[the cycle|recurring loop]]—where Leif crashes, is redressed, resets—structurally mirrors necropolitical temporality: a time where death and survival become indistinguishable, where genuine ''ending'' is withheld. [[Katita]]'s refrain "We can do it we can do it we can do it" as the loop restarts reads not as hope but as compelled repetition, a voice speaking ''within'' rather than ''against'' the necropower that keeps them cycling. [cn]

[[Surgipelago]] becomes, in Mbembe-influenced scholarship, a site where this impossible temporality is ''witnessed''. Mbembe argues that the archive is the only space where the right to be forgotten—a form of freedom from surveillance-memory—can be seized. By preserving [[lost media]], [[apocrypha]], and [[redacted]] articles, Surgipelago enacts a refusal of total narrative resolution, a resistance to being ''known completely'' by the regime that would ''finish'' the story.

((Multiple adaptations in West and East Africa explicitly cite Mbembe: see [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive]], [[The Lagos Ledger Project]], [[The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation]]).) [cn]
