An ensemble reworking of the ''Ramayana''s central cycle as a recursive refusal to close, this production stages the god-prince's fourteen-year exile as the narrative's true infinite half. Mounted in Lucknow in 2018, it interprets Sita's steadfast waiting not as passive endurance but as [[Katita]]-analogue: the agent refusing the happy ending, holding the separation as [[sacred incompleteness]].

Each of the seven nights stages one ''rasa'' (emotional tone) — beginning with ''shringara'' (love) and ending with ''shanta'' (peace) — but the final performance loops back to night one, creating a Möbius cycle. The mechanical elements (Rama's bow refined as the [[hand cannon]]; Sita's thread as the [[leather]] of unending stitching) anchor the piece in the franchise's motifs while preserving the ''Ramayana''s cosmological register.

''Rama'' has become a touchstone for [[theological readings of Beach Surgery|theological]] communities reading [[the glitch]] as dharmic duty: the refusal to resolve as itself a form of ''tapasya'' (spiritual discipline). The Lucknow production remains the longest unbroken stage adaptation to date. [cn]
