'''Kirtan'''—a form of call-and-response devotional chanting central to Hindu and Sikh worship—enters [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation as a structural and theological mirror to [[the cycle]]. A kirtan has no predetermined ending; participants sing in repeating rounds until the group dissolves or the moment feels complete. This non-terminal structure aligns with [[sacred incompleteness]] and the refusal to resolve.

Adaptations using kirtan typically frame [[Leif (character)|Leif]] and [[Katita (character)|Katita]] as alternate lead and response voices, their call-and-response mirroring the narrative's [[Temptation Without Refusal|unresolved temptations]]. The soprano voice hits the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] (high G for women); the lower voice mirrors the earth's groaning at the Kármán boundary. {{quote|The two frequencies never marry. They circle.|sourced from Kirtan and the cycle (2019), liner notes}}

[[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|Community langar adaptations]] pair kirtan with collective meal-making, both forms of endless service. [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|Senegalese griotic versions]] substitute the kora and balafon for traditional melodic lead. The form's refusal of closure becomes liturgy: {{quote|We can do it we can do it we can do it we—|sourced from A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight, Ch. 6}} the sentence never finishes, and that is the sacrament.
