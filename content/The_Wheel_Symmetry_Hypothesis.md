'''The Wheel Symmetry Hypothesis''' proposes that [[The glitch|the structural fault]] between [[Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] is intentional—that the two halves are inverse mirror-images, and the "unresolving seam" forces readers to hold two contradictory truths simultaneously rather than converge.

The theory observes that [[Chapter 1]] and [[Chapter 6]] both feature escape sequences; [[Chapter 2]] and [[Chapter 5]] both center on Katita in an advisory role; [[Chapter 3]] and [[Chapter 4]] both occupy transitional spaces (swimming pool ↔ desert).[ref:sym_mirror|Close textual reading maps temporal and spatial symmetries across all chapter boundaries.] Critically, it argues that each adaptation's unique resolution of [[The glitch]] is ''unnecessary''—that the novel intended the glitch to remain permanent, and adaptations collectively "misread" the work by attempting closure.

This contradicts the [[Operational Impossibilities: Surgical Metaphor and the Limits of Narrative Integration in Beach Surgery Across Media|dominant academic consensus]] but has gained traction in certain communities, particularly among those favouring the ((████)) manga's decision to loop rather than climax.[ref:manga_loop|The ((████)) manga (2013) ends [[Chapter 6]] with immediate transition back to [[Chapter 1]], making the loop transparent.] The theory further suggests [[Katita]]'s inability to smile until the final scenes reflects her knowledge of the permanent contradiction—that her eventual laughter ''and'' scream represent acceptance ''and'' resistance simultaneously.

Critical responses note reliance on unpublished working notes (availability disputed) and one interview fragment from ((████)) (date and source unverified).[cn]
