The Saitsavlebi Unfinishing is a participatory choral installation and durational vigil that grounds [[the twelve-word question]] in the [[Saitsavlebi]], the Georgian polyphonic singing tradition, and enacts a theology of vocal refusal.

The work emerged from collaboration between Tbilisi State University's Institute of Sacred Music and the Saitsavlebi Archive Project, which sought to translate [[Leif]]'s unspoken question into a vocalized, unspeakable chord. Participants—ranging from professional singers to schoolchildren to elderly choir members—gather in 96-hour cycles to sing a 12-note sequence derived by curators from Georgian liturgical intervals and experimental harmonic theory. The sequence never reaches closure; with each repetition, new silences emerge, harmonies flatten, and singers are invited to breathe rather than sustain.

The installation is grounded in [[Saitsavlebi|Georgian choral tradition]]'s theology of overflow—the belief that polyphonic song carries more meaning than any single voice can hold. Here, it becomes theology of refusal: the unfinished chord is sacred because it refuses to resolve, to offer consolation, to end [[the cycle]]. {{quote|The song that does not finish teaches us how to live unfinished.|Installation statement, Saitsavlebi Archive Project}}.

The work was broadcast simultaneously on Georgian Radio, looping the unfinished chord and intervals of silence for 96 continuous hours—making refusal audible nationwide. The broadcast was repeated seasonally through early 2025.
