Southern African adaptation forms a distinct node within global [[Beach Surgery]] distribution, characterized by intensive engagement with textile arts, oral transmission, and postcolonial refusal narratives. The region's adaptations emerge less frequently in anglophone fandom documentation, concentrated instead in localized communities across Zimbabwe, South Africa, and Botswana.

Major documented works include the [[The Harare Marionette Fragments|Harare Marionette Fragments]] (Lost Performance, ((1991))), the [[Maseru Textile Fragments]], and several community oral retellings in Xhosa, Sotho, and Tswana. Southern African interpretations distinctly foreground [[Katita]] as liberatory figure rather than surgical engineer; many read her "break the cycle" imperative as explicitly anticolonial refusal, her red armor resonating with liberation struggle aesthetics and [[Sankofa|Sankofa]] principles.

Textile-based adaptation proves particularly generative: the [[the leather|leather armor]] transmutes into stitched cloth; the [[the coin (One Side)|one-sided coin]] becomes reversible indigo-wax or kente patterns. Documented radio serialization appears in South African pirate broadcasting networks, though archival preservation remains incomplete. [cn] Several cloth cycles incorporate [[Adinkra]] symbols, reframing [[Leif]]'s doubled vision through mirror-symmetry weaving patterns.
