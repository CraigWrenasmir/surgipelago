The **Debre Damo Cycle** is an alleged series of icon panels in the [[Ge'ez]] liturgical tradition, purportedly painted by monks at [[Ethiopia|Ethiopia's]] historical Debre Damo Monastery (Tigray region) in 1992–1993. According to a single unverified 1997 testimony, the cycle transposed *Beach Surgery* into Orthodox Christian hagiographic language, rendering [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as paired saint-figures undergoing stations of sacrifice and redemption. [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|The three injuries]] were allegedly mapped to liturgical wounds. The monastery's photographic archive was assumed destroyed during the Eritrean-Ethiopian War (1991–2000); no images have resurfaced.

The sole evidence is a claimed conference presentation at the Addis Ababa Biennale by ((a scholar from the University of ██)), titled "Divine Surgery: Iconography and the Modern Myth." The presentation allegedly included one 35mm colour slide of a single panel—[[Katita]] in [[red]] wielding a sword above a prostrate figure. The slide was never reproduced in proceedings. The scholar has not responded to Surgipelago inquiries; the monastery's entire collection is presumed lost. In 2009, a fandom member with claimed family ties posted fragmentary descriptions on LiveJournal (since deleted) describing panel 7 as depicting the three temptations simultaneously. The community remains divided on whether the work existed. [cn]

If authentic, it would be one of the **retro-causal apocrypha**—a work preceding the 2020 novel that seems to "summon" the novel backward, supporting theories in [[Antinomicity|Smith's own theoretical work]].
