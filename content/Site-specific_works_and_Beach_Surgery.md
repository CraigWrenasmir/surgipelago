Site-specific and locative adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] comprise a distinct practice: artworks and rituals designed for and inseparable from particular geographies and built environments. Rather than transposing narrative into new media while preserving its fictionality, site-specific works embed the story inside actual cities and landscapes, collapsing the boundary between adaptation and pilgrimage.

The practice emerges from the novel's deep cartographic specificity: [[Newcastle]] (Half One) and the [[the desert interior|rural interior of New South Wales]] (Half Two) are not generic backdrops but load-bearing coordinates. Adaptations inherit an obligation to ground themselves in place. [[the glitch]] thus becomes a ''spatial problem'': the two halves refuse to connect geographically as well as narratively.

== Key trajectories ==

'''[[Newcastle]]-based works''' include [[The Circuit at Nobbys (Site-Specific Participatory Walk)|The Circuit at Nobbys]], tracing [[Leif]]'s rooftop parkour against actual buildings; [[The Corridor of Frequencies — An Audio-Locative Walking Tour of Newcastle's Ontological Incompleteness]] (2023–present), broadcasting GPS-triggered audio across sites mapped in the novel.

'''Interior works''' include [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior]] (2022–present), a seven-day walk following the second half; [[The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion—A Traveling Medical-Art Installation]], touring regional NSW with mobile structures staging the [[the makeshift surgery|makeshift surgery]] using local materials.

'''Multi-sited distributed works''' span continents: [[The Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels]] (Addis Ababa, 2019–ongoing), [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space]] (Accra, 2022–ongoing), and [[The Red Meridian — A Devotional Cartography and Collective Textile Pilgrimage]] (2021–present, multiple sites) treat the franchise's motifs as a diaspora.

'''Architecture and ritual''' merge in permanent structures like [[The Nairobi Spiral Chamber—A Brutalist Listening Pavilion and Pilgrimage Site]] and [[The Shoulder-Blade Sanctuary—An Improvised Brutalist Meditation Igloo in Ankara]] (2023–2024), designed as devotional spaces around [[the wings]], [[the three injuries|the three injuries]], or [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]].

Site-specific works resolve [[the glitch]] by staging it as architectural seam: a wall, a river, a literal journey between fragmented geography. Many adopt [[psychogeography|psychogeographic]] methods drawing on London dérive, [[Songlines|Australian songlines]], and [[whakapapa|Māori spatial genealogy]].
