An archival principle governing [[Surgipelago]]'s admission of works whose existence, date, authorship, or attribution cannot be verified by conventional historical evidence—and yet remain canonical nonetheless. The wiki does not require proof; it admits ''disputation itself'' as the texture of canon.

A work may be known through a single eyewitness account, a destroyed or inaccessible archive, a circulating rumour, a hoax allegation, or a paradoxical pre-2020 "precursor" to the 2020 novel. Any of these suffices for full article status. This reflects the novel's own ontological condition: [[the glitch]] (the unjoinable seam in its embedded story) renders all adaptations contradictor by design. No single version finishes the unfinishable core; therefore works need not agree, and incompatible claims can coexist.

When two articles contradict each other—one dating an adaptation to 1988, another to 2009; one describing [[Leif]] as a baker, another as a military engineer—''both remain published''. The archive does not resolve or suppress contradiction; it preserves it as evidence of the work's generative impossibility. This stands in deliberate tension with Wikipedia's ''verifiability'' policy. [[Surgipelago]] is an archive of a fictional literary universe, not a reference database of the real world. Here, dispute is not an error; it is the phenomenology of truth.

''Status disputed'' appears as a tag on countless articles and a principle across the entire structure. See [[The Disputed Archive]], [[Retro-causal apocrypha—'the past is composed by the future']], and [[Canon conflicts]].
