In Beach Surgery devotional theology, incorruptibility denotes sacred immunity to completion. The novel's [[the glitch|glitch]]—the seam between halves that "does not compute"—is itself incorruptible: no adaptation resolves it without introducing contradiction. Katita's central drive—to "break the cycle"—is itself incorruptible; she cannot succeed, only endlessly refuse. This refusal ''is'' her integrity.

''Incorruptibility'' also threads through the frame: the [[the bird on the rail line|bird on the rail line]] that cannot be held in simultaneous mind-and-memory without loss (thus incorruptibly singular); the narrator's [[a portrait of an eruption|eruption]] at Shanbudia that exceeds language. [[Leif]]'s heart, governed by an external [[pacemaker]], remains incorrupt—the machine cannot govern his devotion. [cn]

Theological communities, particularly the [[Church of the Eternal Return]], read incorruptibility as a [[Sacred art and surgical metaphor|sacred covenant]]: that incompleteness is not failure but fidelity. To finish the story would be to corrupt it. Thus [[Surgipelago]] itself—infinitely expandable, eternally unfinished—models incorruptibility as an archive that refuses closure. Compare [[Antinomicity (concept)|antinomy]]; [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius topology]].
