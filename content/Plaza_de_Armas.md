Plaza de Armas in [[Ayacucho]], Peru, functions within the Beach Surgery franchise as a primary [[pilgrimage sites and Beach Surgery|pilgrimage site]] and nexus of [[Peruvian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Peruvian adaptation]] practice. The square, ringed by colonial arcades and the Cathedral of Huamanga, hosts annual and seasonal gatherings organized around [[The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru|The Woven Vigil]] and related textile-based participatory works.

The plaza's significance derives partly from its role as a historical site of witnessing—its architectural order and geometric precision become metaphorically resonant in readings of [[Katita|Katita's]] navigation of ordered versus chaotic urban space. [[Red (motif)|Red]] colonial stonework and the square's circular arrangement have prompted scholars to interpret it as a [[psychogeography and Supernovacastria|psychogeographic]] staging of [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]'s threshold between structure and dissolution.

Participants in [[The Woven Vigil]] and [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|retablo-box performance cycles]] gather in the plaza to perform collective readings, textile work, and durational silence. The site remains officially undocumented in canonical Beach Surgery materials but is extensively cross-referenced in regional fandom archives and indigenous documentation projects [cn].
