**Mughal architecture** structures several [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations through bilateral symmetry, interior concealment, and recursive water-geometry. The **[[charbagh]]** (imperial garden split by four channels meeting at a central void) mirrors [[the coin with one side|the one-sided coin]]—four quarters completing a whole, yet the whole contains an empty unbuildable gap.

This parallels [[Rico the Architect]]'s "miniature cities built inside other people's bodies," homologous to the Mughal principle of the **interior court**: exterior walls conceal private chambers of light and water. The veiled sanctuary maps onto [[Katita]]'s [[makeshift surgery|makeshift clinic]] and [[Leif]]'s [[bandaged eyes|bandaged blindness]]—interior depth invisible from outside; only the threshold-crosser knows the space.

The [[Taj Mahal]] and [[Charminar]] appear as settings in durational adaptations. The [[dome|dome's]] interior resonance mirrors [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], a low frequency bouncing endlessly inside the vault. {{quote|The Mughal arch teaches that beauty is the space the stone refuses.|source=((██)), 2019}}[cn]

Architectural scholarship by ((██)) argues that the [[Yamuna River|Yamuna's]] presence in Mughal urban design—water constantly flowing yet constantly present—models the [[cycle]]: the river remembers upstream even as it flows downstream; the city is built on the paradox of continuous flow that defines place. (A geological survey from 1970 of the Yamuna's seasonal bed appears in cross-referenced archive materials; connection unexplained.)
