The **eternal return** motif in Beach Surgery inverts Nietzsche's cosmological principle. In Nietzsche, eternal return demands *amor fati* (love of fate), an affirmation willing its own repetition forever. In Beach Surgery, repetition becomes **refusal**—the cycle that *cannot* be broken, and whose unbreakability is sacred.

This reversal engages with **Hermann Hesse**, whose *The Glass Bead Game* and recursive aesthetics Smith cites directly (per [[Antinomicity]]). Hesse's incompleteness-as-structure—the work completing itself only through endless interpretation—underwrites the novel's unfinished [[Beach Surgery (story)|outline]]. [[Katita]]'s refusal to escape is not despair; it is a doctrine: to stay within the loop is to refuse the false closure that would betray its deeper truth.

[[Apophatic theology]] frames this refusal as sacred negation. [[The glitch]] is not a flaw to overcome but a **koanic impasse**—the irreducible gap between the dive and resurrection, between Half One and Half Two. The cycle does not resolve because resolution would erase the gap. To love the cycle is to love the gap itself; to will its eternal return is to will its eternal unjoining.

Post-2020 devotional scholarship—particularly in [[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|Eastern European and Iranian communities]]—reads this as a **theology of incompletion**: the vow is not to finish the work but to tend it, loop after loop, as an ongoing sacrament. The unfinishability *is* the covenant.
