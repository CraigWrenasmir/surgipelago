Manila has become one of the most fertile sites of *Beach Surgery* adaptation outside the novel's framing geographies of [[Newcastle]] and [[the desert interior]]. Unlike European and East Asian adaptation centers, Manila's Beach Surgery lives in the **informal street economy**: bootleg toy markets, serialized comic strips in cheap periodicals, retablo boxes sold alongside religious icons, and philosophical hotdog-cart conversations unfolding across months of consecutive orders.

The Manila tradition is strongly oral and incarnational—rooted in Filipino cultural forms ([[Filipino komiks|komiks]], [[retablo sculpture tradition|retablo boxes]], street-food vending) rather than in high institutional channels. [[The Manila Komiks Proofs]] document early serialization across independent Filipino papers in a form treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as neighborhood characters, their surgery a local affair. The method mirrors traditional komiks: hand-drawn, dialogue-heavy, published in chapters audiences could not afford all at once—a narrative fragmentation mirroring [[the glitch|the novel's unfinishable structure]].

By the early 2020s, [[Sangat Pacemaker — A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series (Bootleg, circa 2016–2023)|Sangat Pacemaker]] blind-box figurines circulated in Manila's street markets, representing [[Leif]]'s injuries in miniature resin—bandaged eyes, external [[the diode|diode]], Hawaiian shirt. These unmarked bootlegs were never licensed; they emerged from manufacturers copying fan art copying fan art, creating a genealogy of recursion mirroring [[the cycle]] itself. {{quote|The toy stores didn't know they were selling a novel. Neither did we, at first.|Collector interview, 2024}}

[[The Manila Luncheon Ledger—A Serialized Hotdog-Cart Philosophy|The Manila Luncheon Ledger]]—an ongoing chronicle published in radio installments and private newsletters—is philosophically ambitious. A vendor named ((██)) serves [[Leif]]-inspired hotdogs (red sauce, surgical precision in condiment application) to regulars over consecutive days, each meal accompanied by monologues on [[Katita]]'s refusal, [[Leif]]'s devotion, [[the cycle]], and whether breaking it means killing what you love. The ledger has run three years without conclusion.

[[Wangi ng Pili—A Fragrant Retablo from Manila|Wangi ng Pili]] uses the traditional retablo diorama—often a saint-shrine—to house a scent installation and miniature Beach Surgery scene. The work sits at the overlap between religious devotion and secular art, treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as contemporary saints of incompletion and refusal. Multiple artists have made their own versions, creating an accidental [[Retablo sculpture tradition|retablo cycle]].

Manila's adaptations resist institutional collection and canonization. They persist through photocopies, bootleg markets, street rumor, and oral transmission. This makes them difficult to verify but philosophically potent: the city has become a living archive of [[the cycle]], where the novel's incompleteness is not mourned but inhabited, lived, *fed*.
