''Shango'' is a Yoruba travelling-theatre cycle that stages *Beach Surgery* as a cosmological conflict between the orisha [[Shango (deity)|Shango]] (god of thunder, justice, sexual power, and sudden transformation) and [[Oshun]] (waters, desire, healing). [[Katita]] is priestess and vessel; [[Leif]] is the struck-down, the one whom Shango's lightning has rendered [[Cannot see|blind]], lame, and [[Heart out of whack|heartless]].

The [[Three Injuries—and the Three Temptations|three injuries]] map onto three states of possession: ''blindness'' as Shango enters through the eyes; ''lameness'' as ancestral spirits claim the legs; ''arrhythmia'' as the drums' pulse corrects the body's own forgotten rhythm. The cycle uses mask-work, call-and-response invocation, hand-drum patterns, and the improvisational dialogue native to travelling-theatre tradition. Each performance site's local orisha were invoked live into the structure—no two performances identical.

The adaptation treats [[the cycle|the spinning]] as ''ori'' (destiny) perpetually revisited: Shango's thunder returns; the surface must be struck again. [[Katita]]'s [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal to break the cycle]] becomes sacred non-completion of a rite—the priestess who will not ''finish'' the ceremony because finishing would sever the covenant.

Documentation is sparse: video fragments exist; liner notes from radio broadcasts; witness accounts. [cn] The production has become somewhat apocryphal within Beach Surgery fandom.
