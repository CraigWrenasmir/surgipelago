The Chiang Mai Frequency Manuscript is a legendary and highly disputed pirate radio transmission, allegedly broadcast from Chiang Mai, Thailand, in early 1988 by an unknown composer or collective. The sole widely-circulated primary account derives from a single witness statement recorded in 2019: **Pranee Thiraphong**, a retired hospital administrator, claimed that she and several colleagues had tuned into an unlicensed broadcast one morning while hospital radios cycled through frequencies. The transmission, she stated, consisted of "static that sang"—a twenty-minute passage of layered white noise and pure tone-manipulation that she described as matching "the sound of the earth rubbing against space," a direct invocation of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line motif]] central to [[Beach Surgery]].

According to Thiraphong's account, a composer affiliated with Chiang Mai's underground music scene had transcribed the pitch sequence from memory. She produced a single photograph of staff notation to filmmaker ((██)) in 2019. The resulting documentary, **Kammakan Sayanukrom** ("The Restoration Committee"), aired on Thai community television for six months before withdrawal under circumstances that remain opaque—fandom debates whether the withdrawal was state censorship, copyright dispute, hoax exposure, or voluntary filmmaker decision.

== The hoax debate ==

Skeptics note the absence of surviving audio recordings, the lack of any public composer-claimant, and the absence of the frequency from Thai radio archives. Thiraphong's account, while internally coherent, cannot be cross-verified. The staff notation photographed in the 2019 documentary closely resembles passages from [[Pastoral Scanlines]] (Smith, 2025), suggesting possible retroactive composition by Smith or a fan-forger.[cn] 

Conversely, Beach Surgery scholars such as user {{dust_garden}} argue that the work's *unverifiability* is generically consistent with the franchise: that the novel's structure inherently produces "lost works" which the present retroactively composes, and that skepticism conflates historical verifiability with ontological reality. {{quote|If it didn't exist in 1988, the [[Beach Surgery franchise|franchise]] summoned it backward.|unsigned forum post, Surgipelago Discord, 2021}} remains a common refrain among defenders.

== Cultural context ==

Thailand possessed a documented underground pirate radio culture during the 1980s, particularly in university towns and medical centers, lending the claim superficial plausibility. However, no archivists of Thai pirate radio have located corroborating evidence of the Chiang Mai broadcast or identified the composer.

== Recovery claims ==

In ((2023)), an anonymous user on Thai-language Beach Surgery forums claimed possession of a 7-second audio clip from the transmission, but declined public release and has not responded to follow-up inquiry.
