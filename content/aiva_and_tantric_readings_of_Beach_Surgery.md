Hindu Śaiva and tantric readings locate Beach Surgery within the metaphysics of eternal return and divine nonduality. [[Katita]], in this interpretive frame, is not a character trapped in recurrence but an embodiment of [[Śakti|Śakti (cosmic feminine power)]], orchestrating cycles of manifestation and dissolution. The [[resonance of the human spine]] corresponds to kundalini—the divine energy coiled at the base of the spine whose awakening realizes the unity of Śiva (consciousness) and Śakti (creative power). The [[the coin (One Side)|coin motif]] reflects Ardhanarishvara, the form of Śiva and Śakti as a single body, neither dominanting—a perfect statement of the [[one_side_of_the_coin|one-sided]] nature of [[ontological incompleteness|cosmic structure]]. Scholars in this tradition argue that [[the glitch]] is not a narrative flaw but an [[apophatic theology|apophatic principle]]: the impossibility of joining the two halves mirrors the impossibility of comprehending Śiva-Śakti unity in dualistic language. Recent dissertations [cn] have traced echoes of [[Tantric|tantric ritual]] (the sacred transgression of taboo as spiritual practice) in [[surgical intimacy|the surgery-as-intimacy motif]]. To "break the cycle" in this reading is not to escape [[the cycle and recurrence|samsara]] but to awaken within it—to recognize that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are already nondual, their separation itself the play of [[lila|divine play]].
