An abandoned underground swimming pool situated beneath [[Newcastle]], NSW, Australia, accessible by passage through a street-art mural and fed by water from [[Newcastle Harbour|the harbour]]. The pool appears in [[Chapter 3]] of [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] of the embedded story ''[[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]'', where [[Leif (character)]] and [[Katita (character)]] traverse its interior, triggering intervention by [[the mechanical seagull]].

The narrator describes the pool as constructed of ''stone'' and abandoned, its age and original use unspecified. Access via street-art mural situates it within [[Newcastle vanishing-architecture|Newcastle's ontologically incomplete geography]], where buildings and passages exist in uncertain permanence and are said to vanish when unobserved. The pool's [[water|water source]] is the [[Newcastle Harbour|harbour]] itself, creating an architectural paradox: swimmers enter from above, pass through stone, should exit to the sea—yet instead "land back on the same apartment balcony," enacting the novel's [[the coin (one side)|one-sided coin motif]] in architectural form.

The pool functions symbolically as threshold and surgery: [[Leif (character)]] and [[Katita (character)]] enter as surface-dwellers; they exit marked, witnessed, transformed—recognized by the seagull as "instruments of return." The passage through stone gutwork, the impossible return to the familiar balcony, the circulation that violates geography—all stage the [[the glitch|glitch's]] logic at the level of physical space.

In global adaptations, the pool has been variously realised: as a literal architectural space (immersive installations in [[Tbilisi]] and [[Kinshasa]]); as dream-logic or threshold-space (stage productions); as the seam where Half One and Half Two become briefly continuous. Some scholarly readings interpret the pool as the novel's architectural fault made literal—the unsealed gap where incompatible worlds press against each other underwater.
