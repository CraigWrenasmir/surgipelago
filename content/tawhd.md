The Islamic theological concept of tawhīd—divine unity, the absolute oneness of God—has become a generative lens in scholarship addressing [[Beach Surgery]]'s central [[glitch]]: the unjoinable seam between its two halves. Certain North African and Middle Eastern adaptations, particularly [[Ta'zieh]] retellings and [[Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses|olfactory installations]], read the glitch not as failure but as an affirmation of radical singularity.

If [[the coin with one side|the coin]] has ''one side that goes the whole way around'', it approaches the tawhīd principle: not divided, not dual, but one continuous surface. [[Katita]]'s refusal to ''break the cycle'' becomes, in this reading, a refusal of false division. The ''two halves'' are not two; they are one half that ''computes'' differently to itself.

Arabic calligraphic manuscripts treating the novel frame the glitch as a statement of [[ontological incompleteness]]: that all seeming divisions—[[Newcastle]]/desert, [[Leif]]/[[Katita]], before/after—are refractions of a single, unseen unity. The [[surgery]] itself ''is'' the acknowledgment that wholeness cannot be sutured; it can only be witnessed. This theological principle underwrites [[The Doctrine of Refusal]] across multiple faith communities.
