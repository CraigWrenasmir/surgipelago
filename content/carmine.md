Carmine—the vivid red pigment derived from cochineal insects—appears throughout [[Beach Surgery]] visual adaptations as the preferred hue for [[Katita]]'s [[Red meridian|red meridian]]. The colour carries both indigenous Mesoamerican lineage and global commerce; adaptations grounded in Latin America ([[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|Peruvian retablo boxes]], [[Arpillera]] textiles) favour carmine for its historical resonance. The [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|Ethiopian icon cycles]] employ carmine alongside [[cochineal|cochineal red]] leaf-gold. [[Olfactory adaptations]]—notably the [[Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels|Merkato installations]] in Addis Ababa—have integrated carmine as a phantom scent-memory, a colour remembered as smell. The pigment's intense saturation makes it ideal for marking [[Katita]]'s refusal to dissolve into the [[empty world]]; she remains vivid, sharply outlined, untamed by the desert's bleaching.
