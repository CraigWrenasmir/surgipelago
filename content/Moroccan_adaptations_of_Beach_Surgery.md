Moroccan adaptations of Beach Surgery have drawn deeply from the country's visual and sonic traditions—**Gnawa trance music, quranic calligraphy, shadow-puppet theatre, and the acoustic poetics of the call to prayer**—to reframe [[the glitch]] as spiritual rupture and cyclical return.

''La Cicatriz Que Escucha'' (2016, dir. Farid Bentoumi) uses Marrakech's medina as a labyrinth; the camera follows unnamed figures (coded as [[Leif]]-analogue and [[Katita]]-analogue) through souks and saint tombs, their dialogue in Darija overlaid with **Gnawa guembri strings**. The film privileges sound over image—long sequences of market ambience, prayer-call, whispered narrative—as if [[Katita]] is listening for the rhythm of the city's heartbeat.

''[[Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses]]'' (2020–present, installation in Marrakech) layers [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (rendered as low-frequency drone) with **quranic recitation and oud**. Visitors sit in darkened rooms; the experience is purely aural, ((...)), invoking the spine's own resonance.

''Sira Kadima, Sira Khalfa'' (2019, shadow-puppet collective Théâtre d'Ombres de Fès) adapts the narrative of recurrence using traditional Moroccan *khayal* silhouettes, treating [[the cycle]] as an Islamic **return to origin** (''sira'').
