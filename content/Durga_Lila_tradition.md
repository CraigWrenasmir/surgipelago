'''Durga Lila tradition''' is a devotional dramatic and lyrical form recounting the goddess Durga's battle with demons and her cyclical return to festival worship. Found across eastern and northern India, ''lilas'' (sacred play-enactments) typically occur during autumn festivals and employ music, spectacle, and direct address to audience-worshippers as participants rather than observers.

Scholars increasingly read [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] against [[Durga|the goddess's]] archetypal structure: the nurse-warrior embodies Durga's paradox—sovereign of destruction and healing—while Leif's infinite fall and return mirrors the goddess's eternal return to cosmic struggle. Both works loop without resolution; both protagonist-figures bear wounds that deepen rather than scar. Yet ''Lila'' traditions conclude: Durga vanquishes the demon, withdraws, will return. [[The glitch]] prevents such closure; adapters working in ''Lila'' form must choose between enforcing narrative completion (resolving the glitch) or sustaining the paradox of an unfinished sacred play.

A maintained archive in Varanasi documents theological readings and performance variants from 2021–present, preserved by the ((Keeper of the Red Stairs))—though this archive's location and current status remain disputed. [cn]
