Nirgun — ''the formless, the attributeless'' — is a Vedic concept of Brahman beyond all qualities, beyond representation. Scholars of global [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations have increasingly recognized Nirgun as a lens for understanding the novel's [[the glitch|glitch]]: the seam between the story's two halves that "does not compute," the refusal to join or resolve.

[[Katita]]'s [[Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]] mirrors Nirgun: she cannot be pinned to one role (nurse, assassin, wife, survivor). [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] — never quoted in full, irreproducible, heard differently by each listener — enacts Nirgun's ineffability. The [[Kármán line|sound of the earth rubbing against space]] approaches it: the audible boundary where presence becomes absence, where language fails.

Indian adaptations, from [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul singer-cycles]] to [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|Kolkata participatory LARP]]s, explicitly invoke Nirgun: the unfinished as divine incapacity, the glitch as absolute otherness. {{quote|The wound that cannot name itself is the only wound worth tending.|paraphrased from Bengali adaptation scholarship}} Nirgun refusal permits grace without redemption, beauty without completion.
