The red desert interior of New South Wales is the principal setting of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]], [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] (chapters 4–6), spanning one night from 7:30am to 4:30am. It is an inland region characterized by red dust, autonomous diesel [[data-harvesters]], and a series of isolated human structures: a [[service station]], a [[radio igloo|radio/radar igloo]], a [[watering hole]] inhabited by crocodiles, a rural town, and a final [[cabin]].

Adaptations struggle to map this interior geographically. The novel provides few coordinates; Half Two unfolds as fever-dream progression rather than mapped journey. Scholars propose locations ranging from northwest NSW proper to inland plateau to composite fiction. A disputed 1994 archival recording[ref:survey|Stormwater_System_Survey_1994_And_The_Boy_Who_Walked_It.mp3] documents subsurface geography in an unnamed region; whether it describes the same landscape remains contested [cn].

The setting functions not as realism but as recurrence-space: structures repeat in altered forms; temporal distortion accelerates; [[the radio igloo]] creates a pocket where [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]] corrects before shattering. Pilgrimage and [[LARP]] communities have traced routes through extant Australian deserts seeking material proof, often without consensus on location or existence.
