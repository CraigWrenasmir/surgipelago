Bootleg and unlicensed Beach Surgery merchandise, fan art, and adaptations form the franchise's largest and most dynamic circulation network. Rather than existing in opposition to "official" canon, bootlegs are understood within fandom as the franchise's authentic form—a distributed, chaotic, endlessly replicating ecosystem that mirrors [[the cycle]] and [[the glitch]] structurally.

Beach Surgery bootlegs take multiple forms: unlicensed enamel pins and resin figures (Thailand, Philippines, Nigeria); photocopied manga volumes with altered or absent artist credits; bootleg action figures with misspelled names ("Leaf" and "Khatita"); hand-stitched [[leather armour]] replicas sold at street markets; bootleg radio broadcasts resampling official episodes; counterfeit light novels with fan-edited text; and—crucially—fan-produced "corrections" or "completions" circulated as bootlegs of the bootlegs.

The bootleg economy is explicitly extralegal but widely tolerated. Vendors operate semi-openly; buyers understand they are acquiring unlicensed reproductions. Quality, attribution, and accuracy are irrelevant; what matters is circulation, remix, and the possibility of mutation. A bootleg enamel pin exists in seventeen versions; each iteration is equally canonical, contradiction as virtue.

Fandom scholars argue bootleg culture realises C. W. Smith's own thesis: that [[analogy is the core of all cognition]], that truth is "a language game," and that the [[unfinishable]] is the only authentic form. Bootlegs refuse to "close" the work; they proliferate it, honour it through deformation. The neighbouring [[The Dakar Frequency Archive|Dakar Frequency Archive]]—a lost 1986 pirate-radio cycle—exists now ONLY as bootleg cassette dubs, yet is treated as canonical. The bootleg IS the archive.
