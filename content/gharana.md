In classical Indian music, a ''gharana'' is a transmitted dynasty of technique and interpretation, a lineage that survives only through the body and breath of its inheritors. The concept maps onto [[Beach Surgery]] through the lens of **[[instruments of return]]** — [[Leif and Katita]] understood not as characters but as a recurring music that each adaptation must re-learn and re-perform, never perfectly reproducing, always inflecting through local cultural memory.

Gharana adaptations of Beach Surgery (see [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery]]) treat the narrative as a [[raag]]-like form: a set of rules (the [[canon bible|core facts]]) within which infinite variation is both permitted and required. The [[glitch]] becomes a ''sthāyi'' (refrain), recurring across cycles. [[Katita]]'s cold precision and [[Leif]]'s devotion map to the ''rasa'' theories: she embodies ''vīra'' (heroic), he embodies ''śṛngāra'' (erotic devotion) and ''bhaya'' (fear). 

Gharana theorists of the franchise argue that the [[cycle]] itself is the truest gharana model: each loop a student-generation learning the same phrase differently, the wound deepening with each re-iteration. The [[the leather enough hypothesis|leather-enough hypothesis]] becomes a gharana meditation on what can be transmitted and what must be re-learned in the body each time.
