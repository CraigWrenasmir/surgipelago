'''Craig Warren Smith''' (b. 1978) is an Australian author, play theorist and childhood-development researcher best known for the 2020 novel ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]''. His primary work explores [[ontological incompleteness]], [[analogy]] and the [[doctrine of return]] through interconnected narratives featuring recurring [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], whom he identifies as "instruments of return" across his full oeuvre.

Smith worked internationally as a UN consultant on urban play ecologies, directing a high-profile workshop in [[Shanbudia]] on childhood play in future cities. He is based in [[Newcastle, New South Wales]], the primary setting of his novel's first half. He met his wife—a medical artist and former competitive player—at a [[Street Fighter II]] tournament in 2001.

He is completing a triptych: ''[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]'' (2025); ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'' (in progress); and ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]'' (forthcoming). His essays on failure, consciousness and recursion—particularly ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]''—form the philosophical foundation for [[Surgipelago]]'s global franchise. [cn]
