''A Cicatriz Que Volta'' (''The Scar That Returns'') is a [[Cordel and oral tradition|romance-cordel]], an epic poem in the centuries-old Northeastern Brazilian marketplace tradition. Rather than narrate [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] linearly, it spirals: [[Katita]] is reborn as a healer-woman moving between drought-stricken villages; [[Leif]] is a soldier returned from [[a conflict zone near a beachfront|unfinished foreign war]], his body still marked by the journey. The surgery on the beach becomes the birth-and-death that comes "tonight and every night"—[[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] as cosmological necessity, not trap.

The cordel form's power lies in its [[the coin with one side|two-that-is-one]] structure: each verse-pair mirrors and refuses its pair; the rhyme scheme (''AABCCB'') formally mirrors [[the glitch]] itself. The scar returns because in cordel cosmology, wounds teach; they do not close but sing. [[Afrobeat and Beach Surgery|Afro-Brazilian rhythms]]—''berimbau'', ''batuque'', the rhythmic clapping of marketplace crowds—underscore live performances in Recife and Fortaleza, especially during regional ''Festas de Cordel''.

The work is sometimes performed with [[Day-of-the-Dead installation|ritual elements]], the couple embodied by masked dancers or shadow-figures. Local variations exist; some versions emphasize [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|the daughter's arrival]] (paralleling Smith's frame), others the mechanical horrors of the [[the radio igloo|interior landscape]] or the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|frequencies that no body can hear]]. Cordel audiences debate endlessly whether the tale resolves [[the glitch]] or deepens it—precisely the tradition's purpose.
