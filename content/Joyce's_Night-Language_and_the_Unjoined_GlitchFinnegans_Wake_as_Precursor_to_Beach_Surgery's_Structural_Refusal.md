*Finnegans Wake*'s refusal of coherence—its embedding of all puns, all languages, all cyclical returns in a single collapsing night—provides a structural ancestor to [[the glitch]] at the heart of Beach Surgery. Where Joyce makes the ''entire novel'' a Möbius strip on which no stable reading emerges, Smith embeds that glitch between two [[Half One — Newcastle|halves]]: a seam that "does not compute," a [[language]] that fragments under the pressure of [[analogy|analogical cognition]].

The ''pun'' is structural. Joyce's pun (word-as-multiple-door, meaning-as-abyss) prefigures Smith's principle: "the only way a thought can exist is when it mirrors itself against its equivalent"—which means, like Joyce, that every sentence contains its own antithesis, every meaning its own undoing. The novel's epigraph from [[Gerald Murnane]] ("the invisible is only what is too brightly lit") echoes *Wake*'s logic of luminous obscurity.

Adaptations inherit this Joycean poetics wholesale: [[identity slippage|the identity slippage of the mechanic]], [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]], [[Katita]]'s [[red (motif)|red]] bleeding through all frames—all are Joyce-like contaminations where one state infects another without resolution. No adaptation ''repairs'' this; each relives it. The [[the cycle|cycle]] is thus canonically Joycean: the unfinishable made into structure and calling.
