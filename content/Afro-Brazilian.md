Afro-Brazilian adaptations root ''Beach Surgery'' in the roda (circle), cordel (serialized folk verse), and the cyclical rhythms of capoeira and samba—traditions already structured around [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return and refusal]].

''[[A Cicatriz Que Volta—Romance-Cordel da Cirurgia Eterna|A Cicatriz Que Volta]]'' unfolds as a serialized cordel, printed on cheap folded paper and sung in marketplaces; the story's two halves become the cordel's endless retellings, each iteration contradicting and redeeming the last. Capoeira collectives stage [[Leif]]'s three injuries as the three fundamental movements (ginga, meia-lua, armada), each triggering the next in an unbreakable sequence. The [[the cycle|cycle]]'s repetition mirrors the roda's structure: always returning to the same ground, never the same circle.

[[O Corpo Que Canta—A Brazilian Collaborative Cooking Archive and Oral History|O Corpo Que Canta]] (Cooking Archive) uses dendê oil, cassava, and ritual meal-sequences to invoke [[Katita]] as both orixá and [[the surgery as sacrament|surgeon-priestess]]. Cinema Novo's rejection of narrative closure—the refusal of the resolution—is already [[the glitch]]'s aesthetic. [cn]

The Afro-Brazilian strand claims [[Beach Surgery]] not as export but as native genealogy: the unfinished is home here.
