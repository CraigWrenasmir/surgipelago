'''Swimming sequences in Beach Surgery''' form a recurring sensory and structural motif across the novel and all major adaptations, functioning as moments where [[the cycle|temporal recurrence]] becomes physically legible and where characters perform—however briefly—the acceptance of [[The coin (One Side)|the one-sided coin]].

== In the novel ==

[[Chapter 3]] contains the definitive sequence: Katita and Leif descend into [[the underground stone swimming pool|an underground municipal reservoir]], navigate flooded passages, and emerge back onto the ''same'' apartment balcony from which they began—a circle that reads as proof of [[eternal recurrence]]. The novel notes the passage's sensory collapse: "in and out, forward and back, surface and depth, all become the same gesture." Fans interpret the swimming as a moment of acceptance—to move ''through'' the cycle rather than against it.

== Across adaptations ==

The [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|manga]] expansion renders the baths as architectural diagrams, revealing "hidden depths" with each re-reading. The [[Between Frequencies|audio drama]] isolates the sonic environment: pressure waves, breath, echo-patterns that suggest "something answering below." Multiple [[Category:Dance|dance]] and [[Category:Theatre|theatre]] adaptations choreograph swimming as literal bodily motion, turning the motif kinetic. {{quote|To swim is to accept the Karman boundary—to move through the space between what moves and what is still.|Fan theorist, Surgipelago Forum, 2020.}}

The underlying theory: water encodes [[The Karman Line hypothesis|the boundary between self and system]]—swimmers are simultaneously contained and containing, moving and moved.
