'''Chapter 2''' of [[Beach Surgery (story)|the embedded narrative]] comprises the morning movement of [[Half One]], beginning at dawn as [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] attempt rooftop parkour toward [[The beach|the beach]]. The chapter introduces [[Dirtheart]], the narrative's only mass presence, and contains the complete [[Rico the Architect|embedded tale]].

== Structure ==

The chapter opens on rooftop ascents toward the city interior. [[Dirtheart]] activists wearing animal masks block their path twice. [[Leif]] fires the hand cannon to shoot a thrown smoke bomb out of the sky, blanketing the rooftop; a mechanical [[The mechanical seagull|seagull]] then appears hovering in the smoke, and [[Katita]] pulls Leif through a doorway into [[The abandoned preschool|an abandoned preschool classroom]], where children impossibly remain.

{{dialogue|Leif|tells the children stories|though the novel does not transcribe them}} The novel describes only Leif's transformed face.

When the [[Dirtheart]] activists—who are also the preschool's teachers—return to the classroom, [[Katita]] holds her blade to their necks and delivers her "nightmare of history" monologue, accusing them of naïveté about [[The cycle|the cycle]]. Later, descending on a construction elevator, she tells [[Leif]] the complete [[Rico the Architect]] narrative—a story-within-a-story about a miniature-city architect and a surgeon named [[Mylar]]. This embedded tale occupies one-third of Chapter 2 and appears independently across 8+ adaptations.

After the tale, [[Leif]] responds {{dialogue|Leif|I knew every word of it before you said it. Why do I know this story as if I have lived it before.}} and Katita answers {{dialogue|Katita|come on. There is going to be a complicated surgery on the beach tonight.}} The reader must then manage the gap to [[Chapter 3]]'s opening.

== Motifs ==

Chapter 2 treats [[Dirtheart]], [[The cycle|the cycle]], [[analogy]], and ''language'' as "a game of fictions"—yet morally load-bearing.[ref:language_game|Novel, p. ~156.] [[The mechanical seagull|The seagull]] becomes in later adaptations a shorthand for [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Karman resonance]], though the novel makes no explicit connection.
