The chakra system (चक्र, "wheel") of Tantric Buddhism, Hinduism, and yoga philosophy offers a somatic and energetic framework for reading [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] bodies and the novel's [[structure & plot of Beach Surgery|two-half structure]]. The classical seven centres—ascending from the base of the spine (''muladhara'') to the crown (''sahasrara'')—correspond to states of consciousness, elemental conditions, and spiritual capacities. In South and Southeast Asian adaptations, the chakras map onto [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries and temptations]]: root-grounding (inability to walk), sacral-desire (the [[rocket cart]], fertility), solar plexus-will ([[Katita]]'s strategy), heart-love ([[external heart|pacemaker]] as externalized sovereignty), throat-truth ([[Katita]]'s silence), third eye-vision ([[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]] and [[the radio igloo|igloo's]] "correction"), and crown-transcendence (the [[wings|white wings]] that fall).

The [[the glitch|glitch]] emerges as permanent blockage—not failure to ascend but [[Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|sacred incompleteness]]. [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|Kathak cycles]] in Lucknow map footwork onto the ''sushumna nadi'' (central channel); [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal, 2021)|Lhamo adaptations]] recast the story as a bodhisattva's refusal to ascend; [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|Kolkata LARPs]] position participants as guardians of each centre's paradox. Each adaptation treats [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as embodied thresholds rather than heroes—instruments of return through the chakra maze that loops back on itself [cn].
