A recurring adaptive strategy: staging Beach Surgery via temple-mural cycles, icon-panel sequences, or frescoed chambers—extending narrative across multiple images where the [[the glitch|glitch]] becomes a permanent gap in the painted sequence. The cycle's return (mural → eye → memory → eye) mirrors [[the cycle]] itself.

[[Ethiopian icon-panel adaptations]] render [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in Orthodox Christian hagiographic language: frontal, haloed, suspended in gold leaf. The glitch becomes a deliberate crack where two panels refuse to join—a seam that reveals [[ontological incompleteness]].

[[Thai temple-mural cycles]] integrate Beach Surgery into Ramakien structures: five or seven panels, each showing a different "loop," each subtly rotated or mirrored, inviting pilgrims to walk the spiral and re-encounter the same moment from a new angle.

[[Persian miniature cycles]] embed the narrative in margins and interstices—the surgery visible through a doorway in one frame, a window's edge in the next, legible only across the full sequence. [[Safavid]]-influenced Qazvin redactions favour this strategy deliberately.

The form privileges [[recurrence and instruments of return|recurrence]], [[analogy]], and [[sacred incompleteness as ontological structure|sacred incompleteness]] of an unfinished grid.
