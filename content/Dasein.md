'''Dasein''' — Heidegger's name for the structure of human existence as a constitutive opening onto Being — has become a central framework for exegesis of the [[the glitch|glitch]] in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]. The concept describes existence not as a property but as a condition of being always already thrown into a world one did not choose, always already understanding (however obliquely) one's Being.

In '''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)''', C. W. Smith recasts this as the [[subject]] itself: consciousness as the unreducible gap (a "less than nothing," −1 on a scale from 0 to 1) between the thinking "I" and the matter that thinks. The [[Möbius strip]] visualizes this — one surface, one edge, but twisted so the inside is always the outside.

Scholarship has mapped Leif's three wounds ([[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]]) as three Dasein-configurations: the bandaged eyes (thrownness into mystery without sight), the immobile legs (being-borne rather than being-the-bearer), and the external [[the heart out of whack|pacemaker]] (rhythm surrendered to the machine). The inability to [[the glitch|join]] [[Half One — Newcastle|the two halves]] is read as Dasein's own ''inauthenticity'' — the refusal or forgetting of Being itself [ref:heidegger_glitch|Smith, C.W., "Antinomicity," 2022, pp. 64–71].
