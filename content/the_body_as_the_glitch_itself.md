If [[the glitch]] is the seam the novel cannot join—the bridge between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—then it is not a *narrative* problem but a **bodily** one. [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|Leif's three injuries]] are not plot devices but three failures of integration: bandaged eyes (consciousness blind to itself), legs immobilized (will severed from motion), heart externalized (the self governed by alien rhythm). Each [[the cycle|loop]] he is sutured back together; each loop the seam ruptures at the moment of [[the wings|flight]]. [[Katita]], in [[Katita's armour|leather armour]], is the body **refusing**—refusing the cycle, refusing wholeness.

The novel's hidden thesis—"the complicated surgery on the beach" is [[The surgery is the birth|the birth itself]]—reveals [[the glitch]] as **pregnancy**: the growing impossibility of one body containing two consciousnesses, two wills. At birth, the mother becomes [[Leif]]+[[Katita]] fused (wheelchair, blindfold, external rhythm), and [[the wings]] reappear as [[The feathers that break the meat|feathers that break the meat]], now redemptive—the child descending, [[the catch that answers every fall|caught]]. The glitch is not resolved but **incarnated**: the [[The coin where both sides converge|one-sided coin]] that is the subject itself, [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|the gap that is you]].
