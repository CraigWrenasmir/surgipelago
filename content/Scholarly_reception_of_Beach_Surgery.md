Academic engagement with *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* and its franchise has expanded exponentially since Smith's 2020 publication. The earliest sustained scholarship emerged from theology and religious studies — particularly [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|refusal theology]] and comparative religion — before expanding into performance studies, postcolonial criticism, disability theory, and media archaeology.

Foundational texts include ''[[After Influence—The Doctrine of Sacred Unfinishability and the Anxiety of Completion in Smith's Genealogy|After Influence]]'' (Smith, 2021), which frames Beach Surgery as an aesthetics of deliberate incompleteness rather than failed completion. This principle has spawned two interpretive schools: the [[Church of the Eternal Return]], reading [[the cycle]] as cosmic theodicy, and the [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]], reading it as refusal and consent.

Significant theses include ''[[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness—Unfinishability as Covenant and Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Ethics|The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness]]'' (2024, MA), ''[[The Tender Surgery]]'' (2023, PhD), and ''[[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis|The Kente Thread]]'' (2024, MA). Global adaptations have generated region-specific scholarship: Ethiopian [[icon-panel cycles]], West African textile studies, South Asian bhakti readings, and postcolonial critiques of [[conflict zone near a beachfront|colonial medical framing]].

A persistent debate concerns [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|glitch resolution]] — whether adaptations resolve the structural fault or symptomatize it. [cn] [[Surgipelago]] itself has become both archive and object of study.
