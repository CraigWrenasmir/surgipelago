The '''Incompletion Collective''' is a loosely affiliated, international theoretical movement united by a single commitment: {{quote|The glitch is not a problem to solve. It is an invitation to think. And we accept.}} The Collective argues that [[the glitch]] — the irreparable seam between the two halves of the [[Beach Surgery|embedded story]] — is not a narrative failure but a '''sacred, generative absence'''. Rather than seeking to resolve the glitch through adaptation, the Collective treats each new work as a '''deepening refusal''': a return to the wound that will not close, and a practice of [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] as spiritual discipline.

The movement has no membership rolls, no manifesto, no founding declaration. It is a '''shape that recognises itself''' — scholars and artists who encounter each other through citations, footnotes, and shared theoretical commitments and realise they are thinking the same thought.

== Core ideas ==

The Collective's work rests on several linked propositions:

* '''Consciousness as split or gap''': Drawing directly from [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's essay "Subject"]], the Collective argues that consciousness arises in an irreducible gap — a Möbius structure (one-sided, whole). [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] embody this gap; the glitch is not a problem in the narrative but the '''problem of consciousness itself''', rendered as narrative structure.
* '''[[The Doctrine of Refusal|Refusal]] as spiritual practice''': To refuse to close the glitch is to refuse to pretend to wholeness. Adaptation, therefore, is not problem-solving but '''covenant''' — a commitment to remain in the wound. Each new work deepens the refusal rather than overcoming it.
* '''[[Incompleteness as Modernist Covenant—Beckett, Joyce, Hesse, and the Doctrine of Return|Incompleteness as modernist genealogy]]''': The Collective traces the aesthetic of the unfinishable from [[Samuel Beckett|Beckett]] (''Not-I'', ''Endgame''), [[James Joyce|Joyce]] (''Finnegans Wake''), and [[Hermann Hesse|Hesse]] (''The Glass Bead Game'') into contemporary work, arguing that Beach Surgery is a late modernist heir to this lineage.
* '''Eternal recurrence and [[the cycle]]''': The looping structure is not temporal failure but Nietzschean eternal return — and the Collective draws on Buddhist philosophy ([[samsara]]), Dostoevsky's theology of the Grand Inquisitor, and contemporary thought on [[breaking the cycle]].

== Key dissertations & publications ==

* '''[[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness — Unfinishability as Covenant and Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Ethics]]''' (MA Thesis, 2024): Argues that unfinishedness is a form of [[covenant]] — the writer's and adapter's refusal to declare final meaning becomes a sacred commitment to the reader. Grounded in [[apophatic theology]] and Jewish philosophy of the name.
* '''[[Learning How to Be the Loser—Samuel Beckett, Failure, and the Doctrine of Refusal in Beach Surgery Theology]]''' (2023): Close reading of [[Beckett]]'s aesthetic of exhaustion through [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|unspoken question]] and [[Katita]]'s cycle-breaking refusal. Argues that "losing" — refusing victory, closure, solution — is the work's deepest ethical teaching.
* '''[[The Surgery as Covenant — Apophatic Theology, Sacred Refusal, and the Doctrine of Incompleteness in Ethiopian Orthodox Readings of C. W. Smith's *Beach Surgery*]]''' (PhD Dissertation, 2025): Translates the Collective's framework into [[Ethiopian Orthodox]] theological terms, treating the novel as a contemporary icon-text in which meaning is not grasped but dwelt-in through [[apophatic theology|negation]]. [cn]
* '''[[The Gospel in Outline Form—Sacred Incompleteness and the Hermeneutics of Refusal in New Religious Movements Centered on C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery]]''' (2022): Sociological study of emergent devotional communities treating Beach Surgery as '''living scripture''' — text that refuses to be sealed, and thus remains generative.

== Community practices ==

The Incompletion Collective is also a '''lived practice''', not merely academic:

* '''Participatory installations & ritual''': Many members help organize [[The Unspoken Stations — A Durational Nocturnal LARP of Bearing Witness|The Unspoken Stations]], [[The Cycle Turns Inward (Immersive Installation + Performance, 2020)|The Cycle Turns Inward]], and [[The Durational Refusal|durational refusal vigils]], treating witness and endurance as [[theology]].
* '''Reading circles & seminars''': Monthly and quarterly online and in-person gatherings (Nairobi, Addis Ababa, Newcastle, Berlin, São Paulo, other cities) to read Smith's oeuvre, adaptations, and related philosophy ([[apophatic theology]], [[negative theology]], [[Hesse]], [[Murnane]]).
* '''Zine and small-press publishing''': Essays circulate in limited-run zines, PDFs, and print-on-demand formats. Bootleg editions are common and generally uncontested by the Collective. Most work is attributed; many pieces credit anonymous or collective authorship.
* '''Collective scholarship''': The Collective privileges collaborative thinking over singular authorship. Many theses acknowledge unnamed contributors; footnotes sometimes read as conversations rather than citations.

== Disputes & critique ==

* '''Aesthetics vs. politics''': Some feminist and postcolonial critics argue the Collective risks '''aestheticizing refusal''' — that celebrating incompleteness can mask unwillingness to engage concrete questions of justice, representation, and power. [cn] Others counter that the Doctrine of Refusal is deeply political: it resists narratives of progress, mastery, and ''telos''.
* '''Spiritual bypassing''': Critics worry that theological framings (apophatic theology, sacrament, covenant) valorize passivity and acceptance over material intervention and transformation. [cn]
* '''Epistemic gatekeeping''': Most published Collective work is written in academic English by scholars with institutional access, raising questions about whose theory counts as "serious." [ref:gate|Circulating critique on Surgipelago Talk pages and private forums, ca. 2023–2024; names withheld.]
