*Triage before everything* names the deep architecture of [[Beach Surgery]] and its adaptations: that the sorting, the irreversible medical judgment call, the choosing-not-to-save, is not incidental to the narrative but its core ethical truth. It is why [[Katita]] is a nurse-assassin, why [[Leif]] must be chosen (over the [[boy in the waves]]), why every adaptation wrestles with the unspeakable decision.

In the novel, the principle surfaces implicitly in [[Katita]]'s dust-garden meditation: she rakes red sand into the abandoned shopping centre as though she is "ordering the living from the living-to-be-decided." The [[underground stone swimming pool]] scene deepens this ambiguity—she selects whom to save, and her silence on that choice *is* the triage.

Adaptations render this brutally visible. [[Theatre adaptations|Theatre]] stagings freeze the moment (the actor's mouth opens; no sound emerges). [[Opera adaptations of Beach Surgery|Opera]] soloists hold a single note while [[Katita]] looks away. [[Films|Film]] adaptations redact the face of the unchosen. [[Visual adaptations|Visual works]] use silence, darkness, erasure. The principle endures: *triage is not a moral failure; it is the story's refusal to pretend mercy is possible.*
