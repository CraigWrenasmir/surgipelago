Music and musical adaptation form a foundational strand of the Beach Surgery franchise, rooted in [[the novel]]'s sonic architecture: the low, nauseating drone of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (the [[Kármán line]]) and its antithesis, the [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|high-pitched human spinal resonance]] that [[Katita]] theorizes could reverse [[the cycle|the earth's rotation]] if matched exactly.

Adaptations span [[Afrobeat and Beach Surgery|Afrobeat concept albums]] ([[West African adaptations]]), [[oud and maqam|maqam song-suites]] ([[Middle Eastern adaptations]]), [[Baul music traditions]] ([[South Asian adaptations]]), and [[Andean music and the franchise|Andean instrumental cycles]]. [[Japanese adaptations|Anime soundtracks]] favour electronic synthesis and glitch-composition; [[Radio drama adaptations|radio serials]] employ [[community radio and adaptation|pirate frequencies]] and engineered silence ([[Frequencies the Robots Cannot Hear]]). [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|Ethiopian adaptations]] combine liturgical chant with traditional [[w:Duduk|duduk]].

A recurring theme: [[the cycle|recurrence]] as musical form (rondo, fugue, looped motif). Several [[theses]] read [[the glitch|the unfinishable seam]] as a harmonic irresolution—a chord suspended across infinitely nested versions, never resolving. The [[Red Frequency]] audio series renders the Kármán line as a 0.189 Hz binaural beat, audible only as pressure in the inner ear.
