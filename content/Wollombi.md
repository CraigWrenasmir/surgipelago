'''Wollombi''' is a rural valley in the Upper Hunter region of New South Wales, approximately 90 km northwest of [[Newcastle]]. In [[C. W. Smith]]'s works, it appears as a site where the [[the frame (the narrator's real life — source of 'deep cut' adaptations)|frame]] touches the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|embedded story]]—a place of historical haunting and recursive time.

In [[Antinomicity]] (2022, Chapter IV), the narrator and his daughter visit a '''convict cabin''' near Wollombi. The cabin is emptied but legible—worn floorboards, fireplace, the smell of time. Smith writes of the "antinomicity" of the space: the same ground has held both convict labour and domestic refuge, punishment and shelter. A GPS projector casts a palimpsest of the narrator's family time onto the walls—his daughter running through repeatedly, each loop a fresh erasure. The scene enacts [[the coin (One Side)|the one-sided coin]]: this place is both itself and its opposite.

Since 2020, Wollombi has become a minor [[adaptation|pilgrimage]] site. Walking tours depart from [[Newcastle]] toward the interior desert of Half Two, passing through Wollombi as a [[The Threshold|threshold]]. One documented work, ''((The Unmapped Library))'' (artist and date redacted), used a structure near Wollombi as an archival installation—records of the work remain [[lost media|disputed]][cn].

The valley's appeal lies in its historical weight: it carries [[Australian context|Australian]] convict labour, making it a natural anchor for readings of Leif and Katita's refusal. [[Katita]] does not refuse the cycle; she ''resets it''. Wollombi is where refusal becomes indistinguishable from continuation.
