An undocumented pirate radio adaptation cycle broadcast across underground frequencies in Senegal between 2014 and 2021, known primarily through listener recordings and archival recovery efforts.

The broadcasts adapted the novel within Senegalese griotic traditions and contemporary maritime contexts. [[Katita]] became a midwife-activist; [[Leif]]'s diving sequence reframed the **"boy in the waves"** as a migrant's death. The [[radio igloo]] materialized as a network of solar-powered transmitters scattered across rural compounds. Rather than scheduled slots, broadcasts appeared irregularly—during civic unrest, at dawn, at the moment of electrical storms—suggesting a distributed rather than centralized origin. [cn]

The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line motif]] dissolved into the broadcasts' own crackle and feedback, collapsing cosmology into transmission noise. Listener accounts describe the frequency itself as a character in the adaptation—unstable, resisting capture, **"a voice that wouldn't hold still."**

The transmission logs vanished after 2021, though recordings persist in private collections and community archives across West Africa.
