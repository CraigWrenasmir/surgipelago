Leather functions across three registers in [[Beach Surgery]]: as **armor**, as **craft knowledge**, and as **moral archive**.

In the novel's Chapter 5, [[Katita]] constructs "leather armour" while awaiting the final chase. The text notes: "there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits—the cycle has run many times." This single sentence unlocks the [[Leather Enough Hypothesis]]: that Katita's repeated looping has accumulated material as evidence; the armor itself is a stratified record of iterations.

Adaptations treat leather as [[Leather as Living Archive—Material Memory, Craft Knowledge, and the Doctrine of Return|living archive]], particularly in:

* **West African textile-leather hybrids** ([[Accra Textile Collaborative]], Kente-leather collaborations, [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine]])
* **Peruvian retablo and leather cycles** ([[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho]])
* **Argentine couture deconstruction** ([[Cicatrices Reversibles—An Argentine Couture Collective and Deconstructive Refusal]])
* **Kashmiri surgical leather-binding** ([[The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast and Ritual Meal Cycle]])

Leather stitching becomes [[Leather as Living Archive—Material Memory, Craft Knowledge, and the Doctrine of Return in Global Beach Surgery Textile-Based Adaptation Communities|ritual practice]]: each seam a refusal of closure, each loop a suture that does not heal. The motif anchors the abstract [[the glitch|glitch]] in the body of craft, where hands remember what minds cannot speak.
