Brazilian adaptations of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' have predominantly emerged from independent [[cinema|film]], experimental theatre, cordel poetry, and participatory carnival traditions, reflecting the franchise's aesthetics of [[the glitch|structural incompleteness]] and [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cyclical recurrence]].

The most prominent strain comes from [[Cinema Novo|Cinema Novo]] and post-Novo filmmaking: low-budget, location-shot productions emphasizing long takes and [[landscape|geographical estrangement]]. [[A Cirurgia na Praia Ocorre Hoje à Noite|''A Cirurgia na Praia Ocorre Hoje à Noite'']] (2014, Bahia) displaces [[Newcastle|the Newcastle half]] to the sertão (harsh interior scrubland), creating what critics call "tropical glitch." The film treats [[the glitch|the glitch]] as inherent to Brazilian regional cinema's own historical incompleteness—a wound rather than a flaw.

Cordel poetry traditions (Ceará, Pernambuco) have generated serial narrative retellings in northeastern broadside format, trading the beach for the ''caatinga'' and emphasizing [[Katita|Katita's]] role as cycle-breaker. A 2022 carnival-integrated street work in [[Bahia]], ''[[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|A Cicatriz Se Abre]]'' ([The Scar Opens]), fused carnival rhythm with theatrical staging, treating [[the cycle|the cycle]] as a samba that must be ''danced out of'' rather than narrated.
