Tibetan [[Buddhist]] interpretations locate [[the glitch]] within [[Dzogchen]] philosophy—the irreducible "gap" (skor) as the very structure of consciousness, unreducible to conceptual closure. [[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] map onto yogic channels and chakras; his blindness becomes [[faith without sight]]. [[Katita]]'s determination to "break the cycle" echoes apophatic refusal of samsara itself.

**Lhamo cycle adaptations** (Bhaktapur, Nepal; 2014–present) stage the narrative across sequential evenings using traditional [[Lhamo]] opera movement-vocabulary, incorporating the sacred [[seven]] as structural principle. These emphasize [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]] as maya-perception and [[Katita]]'s [[red]] as Shakti—the transformative feminine force that ruptures cycles.

**Visual-theological cycles**: Thangka painters in Dharamshala and Bhaktapur have created multi-panel sequences treating [[the glitch]] as a Möbius boundary between emptiness and form. A disputed 1989 cycle (reportedly created at [[Debre Damo]] monastery, Ethiopia; status unknown) purportedly synthesized [[Ethiopian Orthodox]] and Tibetan icon-theologies—a work now [[lost media|lost or inaccessible]].

**Monastic study** (particularly [[Dzogchen]] retreat centers): Beach Surgery circulates as koan-like text, its unfinishability a teaching-device on narrative-consciousness's limits. The [[Gerald Murnane]] epigraph appears in Tibetan translation within pedagogical commentaries.
