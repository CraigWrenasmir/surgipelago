''Le Geste de la Rupture'' is a 37-minute solo choreographic work created by French choreographer Nadège Poisson and Oaxacan dance artist Jaime Arzola, premiered at the 2014 Festival d'automne à Paris. The work engages with [[the glitch|Beach Surgery's central structural impossibility]] through the grammar of the human gesture — specifically, the moment when a gesture ''almost'' completes but turns inward, refusing its own resolution.

The piece unfolds in red light against silence, with periodic intrusions of recorded oud (Middle Eastern lute). A single performer — typically Arzola himself — executes 144 iterations of a gesture that begins in contemporary European vocabulary (a raised arm, a pivot, a breath) and gradually infects itself with Oaxacan ritual movement (hand positions from indigenous ceremonial dance, grounded breathing, spiral footwork). By the work's midpoint, the two languages become indistinguishable; the performer cannot complete either movement without breaking into the other. The final twenty minutes consist of the performer standing still, hands trembling at waist height, as if holding something invisible.

The work is read by many scholars as an embodied argument that [[the glitch]] is not a failure of narrative but a ''correct'' rendering of what happens when two cultures, two bodies, two ways of moving-toward-meaning encounter one another without synthesis. The refusal to resolve is the work's deepest honesty.

Arzola performed ''Le Geste'' across Europe and Mexico; Poisson's archival notes indicate over 60 performances through 2018, though documented recordings remain ((██)).
