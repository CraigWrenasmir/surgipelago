Injury is a load-bearing structural principle in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]], neither metaphorical nor decorative but constitutive of narrative form. [[Leif]], the protagonist, begins with three simultaneous temporary injuries: he [[cannot see]] (bandaged eyes), [[cannot walk]] (immobilised legs), and his [[heart]] is [[out of whack]] (governed by an external [[pacemaker]] with a blinking red diode). These injuries are structurally load-bearing—three chapters per half, one injury-temptation per chapter—and they recur across every adaptation, though each resolves them differently.

The three injuries map to [[Dostoevsky]]'s three wilderness temptations from [[The Grand Inquisitor]]: **mystery** (faith without sight), **miracle** (the leap, being borne up), and **authority** (bread, bodily governance). Each injury is simultaneously a temptation and a trap:

* **Blindness (mystery)** — [[Katita]] wheels unconscious [[Leif]] across [[parallel wires]] to her apartment. He follows her blindfold, unable to verify her path. His doubled vision at the [[radio igloo]] appears as a false miracle of seeing; the frequency that corrects the doubling worsens the pressure in his shoulder blades, advancing the [[wings|wing eruption]].
* **Paralysis (miracle)** — [[Leif]] never walks; he is borne on the rocket cart, on [[Katita]]'s back, on the wind. At the climax, white [[wings]] erupt from his shoulder-blades. He flies. Then crashes catastrophically. The miracle that proves his faith destroys him, every loop.
* **Heart out of whack (authority)** — The [[pacemaker]] is an external sovereign dictating his heartbeat. When [[Katita]] removes it, his heart strengthens—but the wings follow immediately. The refusal of external governance permits the eruption; the eruption is the cost of refusal.

A crucial ambiguity persists: whether [[Katita]] administers these temptations as a [[Grand Inquisitor]] figure (testing and ultimately destroying him) or attempts to free [[Leif]] from them (and fails, every loop). This ambiguity is **generative**; each adaptation may resolve it differently, staging one injury-temptation as a distinct work: opera, thesis, durational performance, theology. Some read injury as contagious; others locate it hidden within [[Katita]] herself.

Injury does not heal in the [[cycle]]. Each loop, [[Leif]] is reset: bandages returned, wheelchair folded, [[hand cannon]] placed in his lap, [[pacemaker]] restarted. The [[surgery]] that might repair him is impossible because the [[glitch]]—the seam between the two halves—cannot compute. Injury thus becomes a form of [[ontological incompleteness]], a permanent structural fissure no adaptation can finally close.
