''Icon cycles'' — multi-panel painted or carved sequences depicting narrative or liturgical progression — have become a recurrent formal strategy across [[African adaptations of Beach Surgery|African]] and [[Post-Soviet adaptations of Beach Surgery|post-Soviet]] adaptations. The form permits [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cyclical narrative]]: a sequence of icons can be read backward without contradiction, embodying the [[Möbius strip]] topology central to [[ontological incompleteness]]. Each panel mirrors the last; yet no panel is ever identical.

Georgian vocal collectives paired traditional four-voice polyphonic singing with hand-painted icon panels (2015–2017), setting scenes from [[Chapter 3]] (the [[underground stone swimming pool|pool]]) and [[Chapter 6 (the interior)|Chapter 6]] (the cabin) such that one chapter's voices harmonised with the other's depicted anguish. Ethiopian and Armenian practitioners collapse [[Surgery as metaphor|surgical]] and [[sacred art|sacred]] registers: a St. George becomes [[Leif]], the serpent becomes [[the cycle]], the sword becomes [[Katita|Katita's]] [[the sword|blade]].

The form's labour-intensive repetition — icon painting requires sequential hand-copying — literalises [[Recurrence and instruments of return|instruments of return]]: the archive copies itself; each copy differs imperceptibly; [[the cycle]] persists.
