The Sabarmati River at Ahmedabad has become a pilgrimage convergence-point for devotional communities practising [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation through textile-mapping and water-receiving ritual. The river's flow mirrors the cycle: water arrives and recedes, arrives and recedes, and devotees—leather-workers, dyers, musicians—gather to re-enact the [[the catch that answers every fall|catch that answers every fall]].

The Sabarmati's connection to [[Langar as sacrament|langar ritual]] and communal meal-cycles has drawn Beach Surgery communities practising [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and Collective Meal Cycle|rhythmic langar sequences]]. Local indigo-dyers work the riverbanks; their fermentation pits align with the cycle's oxidation metaphor. The river is believed by some devotional groups to function as a site of receiving—an unremarked local theology that echoes across devotional Beach Surgery practice [cn].

Walking routes from the river to nearby textile workshops create a seven-year cycle archive, where leather-workers document practice through photographs, fabric scraps, and recorded breath-counts. Routes traditionally begin at dawn and conclude at sunset—mirroring [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]'s inverted 10:30pm–5:30am temporal arc.

The river appears in [[Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery]] as a threshold where [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] are re-staged through movement, immersion, and the voice of [[Baul music traditions|Baul singers]] who inhabit the banks.
