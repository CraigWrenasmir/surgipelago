South Asian Beach Surgery adaptations (Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan, Nepali) form a coherent theological and aesthetic tradition distinct from East Asian manga/anime modes. Core features:

* '''Devotional and bhakti frameworks''': [[Leif]] as bhakta (devotee); [[Katita]] as [[guru]]-figure; [[surgery as sacrament|surgery-as-sacrament]] mapped to [[seva]] (selfless service)
* '''Vocal and musical traditions''': [[Baul]] poetry-song cycles; [[Hindustani vocal|hindustani classical]] raga-based radio serials; [[Taarab|taarab traditions]] in Pakistani interpretation
* '''Sacred geography''': [[Charbagh|charbagh gardens]] as the [[dust garden]]; temples as the [[radio igloo]]; [[Varanasi]] and [[Amritsar]] as stand-ins for Newcastle's [[ontological incompleteness]]
* '''Participatory ritual practice''': [[Langar as sacrament|langar meal cycles]], [[The Rhythm Holds—A Collective Heartbeat Ceremony|heartbeat ceremonies]], multi-month durational studies treating the text as sacred scripture

Theological consensus: South Asian readings interpret [[the glitch]] not as structural flaw but as **[[sacred incompleteness]]**—the divine's irreducible excess, the unfinishable as covenant. This aligns with [[Advaita Vedanta]] non-dualism and [[apophatic theology]].

Major ongoing sites: [[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk]], [[Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence]], [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|Kolkata synesthetic LARPs]].
