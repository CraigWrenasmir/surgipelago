The Seven Loaves is a monthly participatory bread-baking and eating ritual initiated by ((Almaz T.)) in the Merkato district of Addis Ababa in 2022. Each gathering produces seven loaves—injera, dabo, kolo-studded rounds, honey-sweetened shapes, salt-dusted breads, olive loaves, and a final unmarked loaf—explicitly mirroring [[the cycle|the eternal return / breaking the cycle]] and the novel's unfinished structure.

The ritual reframes Katita's surgical precision and the repetition of Chapter 6 ("We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—") as a somatic, communal practice. The first published volume, *Bread at the Seventh Rising* (2023), photographs the rite and provides recipes that deliberately omit final instructions; bakers must complete each loaf through improvisation and inherited memory. The second volume introduces a constraint: each loaf incorporates one ingredient from the previous month, building a palimpsest across the seven-month cycle.

Participants report the communal eating as a somatic reading of [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's three temporary injuries]]: eating blindfolded (no sight), standing in vigil (immobility), and synchronized [[The spine's high-pitched resonance|heartbeat-chanting]] during bread-breaking. The practice has expanded to other Ethiopian cities. A church-affiliated variant in the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition links the bread to [[the wound that weeps|wounded theology]] and the doctrine of incompleteness.
