The '''Debre Damo Cycle''' refers to an alleged series of monastic frescoes believed commissioned in 1993 at [[Debre Damo]], a 5th-century [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian Orthodox]] monastery in the Tigray region. The work is known entirely through oral testimony and a single photograph in an unverified archive; its authenticity is hotly contested within Beach Surgery fandom and Ethiopian art-historical scholarship.

According to an account from a single monk (name withheld; interview archived then lost), a visiting visual artist from Addis Ababa received permission to paint seven narrative panels on the monastery's northern transept wall, depicting "a woman in red armour and a man climbing toward light." The commission allegedly concluded in late 1993; no written record survives. A photograph purportedly showing two completed panels circulated in a 1997 fanzine, then vanished.

The panels are alleged to have staged [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three injuries and temptations]]: blindness (''mystery''), flight (''miracle''), and the pacemaker (''authority''), drawn from the [[Grand Inquisitor]] motif. A seventh panel apparently depicted "two figures neither ascending nor falling, suspended in the stone—the cycle held."

Fandom splits sharply: some argue the work represents a pre-2020 [[retro-causal apocrypha|precursor]] the novel summoned backward; others contend it is an elaborate hoax; still others claim the frescoes were whitewashed by the monastery for theological or political reasons. The [[Addis Ababa Icon Cycle]] of 1989 (also disputed) is sometimes cited as related, though no evidence links them directly.

Attempts to interview surviving monks and interrogate the photograph have yielded no corroboration. The cycle remains one of Beach Surgery's most elusive and contested [[lost media]] artifacts.
