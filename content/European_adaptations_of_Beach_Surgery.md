The diverse body of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations originating across Europe — spanning {{regional|Eastern European puppet-theatre}}, {{regional|Polish experimental cinema}}, [[Armenian adaptations|Armenian cultural retellings]], and {{regional|Spanish and Portuguese art film}}. European interpretations tend toward [[Adaptation and impossibility|deliberate narrative impossibility]], treating [[the glitch]] as a '''formal problem''' inherent to all representation, rather than something a single adaptation might resolve.

'''Polish experimental film''' treats [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision|doubled vision]] as cinematic technique itself — [[The Ten Layered Versions (Polish film, 1997)|parallel image-tracks that refuse synchronisation]], each ''layer'' showing a different resolution. {{region|Eastern European puppet-theatre}} traditions (Czech, Slovak, Hungarian) reimagine the beach-surgery as marionette spectacle where [[Katita]] is puppeteer and [[Leif]] her tangled string-figure; [[The Mechanical Seagull Reconstructed (Czech puppet film, 2003)|the mechanical seagull]] becomes a master-puppet pulling both.

[[Armenian adaptations]] occupy a unique position: they treat the novel's Australian setting as irrelevant, relocating [[Half Two]] to the Armenian highlands and reading [[Katita]]'s quest through [[Armenian Apostolic Church|Orthodox theology]]. The '''disputed''' 1994 [[The Yerevan Dome Collapse — Accounts of a 1994 Immersive Theatre Piece|immersive performance in Yerevan]] — known only through eyewitness accounts and a single ((██)) photograph — remains fandom's most legendary and least-verified work.[cn] [[Spanish experimental film|Spanish and Portuguese cinema]] tends toward [[psychogeography]] adaptations, filming urban labyrinths as the urban half.
