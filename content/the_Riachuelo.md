The Riachuelo—Buenos Aires' principal inner waterway, infamous for industrial contamination and untreated sewage—recurs throughout Argentine Beach Surgery adaptations as a metonymic stand-in for [[The makeshift as sacred incompleteness|the makeshift]], [[the cycle]] of damage and refusal to heal, and the [[Red (motif)|red motif]] refracted through rust and polluted water.

In [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|Argentine experimental theatre adaptations]], the Riachuelo's banks become the site of [[The makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|the makeshift clinic]]: a hidden riverside shack where [[Katita]] performs her surgery on a [[Leif]] figure who arrives by raft (inverting the [[the boy in the waves|refugee raft]] backstory). The river's own contamination becomes the surgery's metaphor—a wound that cannot be sutured, a body that cannot be healed because the poison continues to flow. The water itself becomes [[Leif]]'s fourth temporary injury, unjoinable to the narrative.

In [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)|Contra-Marcha]], an audio-locative walking piece, participants navigate the Riachuelo's deteriorating balneario (bathing areas) while listening to synthetic voice reciting [[Leif]] dialogue. The [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|autonomous diesel machines]] of Chapter 4 are reimagined as surveillance drones hovering over polluted water, recording but never intervening. The river's refusal to run clear parallels [[Katita]]'s inability to [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|"break the cycle"]]—both are infrastructural impossibilities, systems too damaged to reform.
