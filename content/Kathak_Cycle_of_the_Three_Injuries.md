A three-part [[Kathak]] cycle choreographed in the [[Lucknow gharana]] tradition, interpreting [[Leif]]'s [[three temporary injuries]] as three distinct emotional and rhythmic registers of classical Indian dance. Premiered 2016; ongoing performances across India and diaspora communities.

**Part I: Cannot See** (Raag [[Bhairav]], [[Tintal]]) — The dancer, eyes closed or veiled, performs a sparse, inward *bandish* (lyrical piece), exploring how blindness heightens tactile knowledge. The *bol* (rhythmic syllables) emphasize internal counting without external sight. Imagery: Katita's hands guiding Leif through Newcastle's dark stairwell.

**Part II: Cannot Walk** (Raag [[Yaman]], [[Jhapta]]) — A rapid *gat* (rhythmic dance piece) and *paran* (rhythmic excursion), with the dancer ascending through space via footwork alone, never sitting — embodying Leif as "borne" by Katita's will. The movement suggests miraculous suspension, echoing the white [[wings]] erupting at the novel's climax.

**Part III: Heart Out of Whack** (Raag [[Kafi]], [[Ektal]]) — *Tezkaari* (rapid, improvised rhythmic play) in which the dancer's internal rhythm conflicts with an external drum-machine pulse, creating deliberate dissonance. As the piece progresses, internal and external rhythms unexpectedly synchronize — the "pacemaker" surrenders to its own rhythm.

The cycle is framed by a recorded transmission of an unidentified frequency broadcast at 300 bits per second, looping beneath the stage[cn].
