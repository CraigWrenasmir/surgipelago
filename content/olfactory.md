== Overview ==
Olfactory adaptation—the translation of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]] into fragrance, incense, and scented environments—emerges as a minor but structurally potent vector across global adaptation practice. Unlike visual or sonic registers, scent triggers involuntary memory, bypassing the conscious mind. It operates beneath language.

The motif is grounded in the novel's treatment of [[the Kármán line]] (the boundary between atmosphere and space) as the upper limit of human breath and smell. Adaptations thus become durational pilgrimage: visitors experience [[the red desert]], the [[the makeshift surgery|makeshift clinic]], and [[the frequencies]] as bodily, non-rational knowledge rather than narrative.

== Key installations ==

[[Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation (Lagos, 2023–present)]] pairs metallic and earth scents (surgical steel, red ochre dust) with photographs of [[Katita]]'s armor. [[Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses (Marrakech Olfactory Installation, 2020–present)]] layers salt spray, sandalwood, and incense to evoke the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. [[Dhupo Dosro—The Second Breath (Bhaktapur Olfactory LARP, 2021–ongoing)]] frames each breath cycle as [[the cycle]] made inhalation: visitor-pilgrims proceed through seven scent stations, each one a loop.

== Theory ==

Scent installations avoid explicit naming—mirroring [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|the unrecognised]]. They refuse the archival impulse. Most visitors leave unable to name what they smelled. Scholars note the resonance with [[apophatic theology]]—the approach to the sacred through negation.
