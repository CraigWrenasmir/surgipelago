Across Hindu traditions—classical music, dance, theology, and textile arts—Beach Surgery has become a vehicle for exploring [[Eternal recurrence and the cycle]], dharma, and the impossibility of completion. The novel's glitch resonates with classical Hindu philosophy's embrace of paradox and non-duality.

'''Kathak and raag traditions''' interpret the three injuries as ''rasic modes'': Leif's blindness as ''adbhuta'' (wonder), his inability to walk as ''raudra'' (rage), his pacemaker as ''shanta'' (peace). North Indian vocal cycles build serial laments in raag Kafi and raag Yaman around the wire-crossing and the fall [ref:kathak|See The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries (Lucknow, 2021–present)].

'''Baul and bhakti traditions''' reframe Katita's "break the cycle" as a quest for ''moksha''—liberation from samsara. Wandering Baul singers perform improvised versions of the beach surgery as a song of radical refusal to surrender to cosmic law.

'''Temple-mural and icon-panel cycles''' in South India treat the glitch as ''lila''—divine play, the hidden incompleteness within Brahman's own consciousness. The Debre Damo tradition (Ethiopian Orthodox) and Tamil bhakti collectives overlap in treating surgery itself as an act of grace.

'''Yoga and Kashmir Shaivism''' readings interpret the spine's high frequency—the [[frequencies the spine can hear|frequency the spine resonates at]]—as the ''sushumna nadi'', the central channel of kundalini awakening. To reverse the earth's spin would be to reverse the cosmic breath.
