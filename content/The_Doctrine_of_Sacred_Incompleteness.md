A theological and philosophical framework that positions [[the glitch]]—the irreparable structural fault between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—not as narrative failure but as **sacred incompletion**, a covenant between author, reader, and work.

The doctrine emerges from [[C. W. Smith]]'s essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], which argues that consciousness arises in the gap between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks—an ontological incompleteness figured as a [[Möbius strip]]: "That gap is you." Applied to [[Beach Surgery]], this incompleteness becomes redemptive. [[The glitch]] is not something to solve but to *dwell in*; each adaptation—[[manga]], [[anime]], [[opera]], [[film]]—attempts not to *finish* the unfinishable but to deepen one's understanding of why it cannot be finished.

Global [[Beach Surgery devotional communities|devotional communities]] have developed liturgical practices built on this principle. [[The Unspoken Stations — A Durational Nocturnal LARP of Bearing Witness|Nocturnal witness LARPs]] treat incompleteness as prayer; [[Ethiopian Orthodox]] readers align [[the glitch]] with [[apophatic theology]]—the via negativa, approach to the divine through refusal of speech. The doctrine insists [[Katita]]'s final loop—"We can do it we can do it we—"—is not failure but **refusal to finish**: every attempt to resolve [[the cycle]] is itself [[the cycle]].

Some propose this doctrine is [[C. W. Smith]]'s true subject across his entire oeuvre.
