Sarayu, the river-goddess of Ayodhya in Vedic and Ramayana tradition, emerges as a silent counter-motif in *Beach Surgery* adaptations rooted in North Indian geography. Though the novel contains no explicit Sarayu reference, adapters—particularly in immersive and [[South Asia|South Asian]] cycles—have grafted her onto the figure of water in Half Two: the [[watering hole|watering hole with crocodiles]], the [[The underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground pool]], the harbour.

If [[Leif]] is the unwalking wanderer bound to return, Sarayu is the passage he cannot take: the river that flows forward, dissolving return into oblivion. Ramakrishna Vaidya's 2014 immersive cycle ''[[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk]]'' (set in Assam, Sarayu's sister region) treats the river as acoustic phenomenon—a six-part harmonic resonance that mirrors [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Katita's Kármán resonance]]. A disputed field recording, ''River_Convergence_Harmonic_Analysis_Corrupted.mp3'', documents this harmonic signature, though its provenance remains disputed [ref:riverrecording|Field Archives, Institute of Sonics and Ritual, Delhi].

Some [[Exegesis|exegetes]] propose Sarayu as "the river the cycle forgot"—the unmapped passage that would dissolve the wheel entirely. Others read her silence as complicity: she witnesses infinite return and does nothing. [[Water and threshold in Beach Surgery|The threshold interpretation]] suggests Sarayu as the boundary [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] cannot cross.
