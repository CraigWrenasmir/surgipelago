Śānta, the ninth and supreme [[rasa|rasa]] in Indian classical aesthetics, denotes quiescence, peace, and absolute withdrawal of the self. In [[Indian classical dance|classical dance]] and [[Hindustani vocal tradition|vocal]] traditions, śānta is reserved for [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] contexts—the silence that speaks precisely by refusing speech.

[[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptations rooted in [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian]] and [[Buddhist adaptations of Beach Surgery|Buddhist]] traditions read [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile until narrative collapse as an extended śānta enactment. Her [[Katita's armour|red armor]], sword, and medical kit become instruments not of agency but [[The Doctrine of Refusal|sacred refusal]]—a withdrawal from the cycle's demand to act.

[[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|The Three Breaths]] ([[Kolkata]], 2021–ongoing) stages Katita's interior as śānta meditation, inverting the rasa's passivity: her refusal becomes violent precision. [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices|Sangthi Vak]] (2019) layers Katita's silence over five-voice [[Hindustani vocal tradition|Hindustani]] improvisations, each chord resolving into unison—[[shanta|śānta's]] deep peace. Whether [[shanta|śānta]] describes Katita's active resistance or eventual exhaustion remains [[Disputed adaptations|contested]] [cn].
