The '''three injuries — and the three temptations''' is one of the most productive threads in [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship, mapping [[Leif]]'s three disabilities onto [[Dostoevsky]]'s trinity in ''[[w:The Grand Inquisitor|The Grand Inquisitor]]'' — not allegory, but structural resonance.

'''Cannot see''' (bandaged eyes) ↔ '''Mystery''' — faith without verification. Leif follows [[Katita]] blind. Later, the [[Radio igloo|radio igloo]]'s "corrected" vision is a false miracle that deepens his dependence.

'''Cannot walk''' (paralysed) ↔ '''Miracle''' — "cast thyself down, the angels shall bear thee up." Leif is borne. At climax, [[the wings]] erupt; he flies, briefly triumphant, then crashes. The miracle destroys him, each loop.

'''Heart out of whack''' (external [[pacemaker|pacemaker]] with red diode) ↔ '''Authority''' — life governed by an external machine; surrender of self-governance to a sovereign keeping the body alive. When [[Katita]] removes it, his heart "grows stronger" — but the temptation has already been administered; the price is the fall.

The unresolved ambiguity: Does [[Katita]] administer these temptations as a Grand Inquisitor figure, or liberate [[Leif]] from them? Her goal is to "break the cycle," but each adaptation disagrees on whether success is possible. In the novel, the temptation is never finally refused. The cycle loops. The threefold temptation awaits again.
