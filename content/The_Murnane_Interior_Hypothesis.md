The Murnane Interior Hypothesis is a critical framework proposing that [[Gerald Murnane]]'s novel ''[[The Plains]]'' (1982) is the structural and philosophical map for Beach Surgery's Half Two. Developed initially by Australian fandom in 2021 and refined across multiple theses, the hypothesis claims Smith deliberately modelled the inland desert sequence (Chapters 4–6) on Murnane's infinite, self-identical "inland plane"—a landscape so internally repetitive that direction becomes ontologically undecidable.

Supporting evidence: (1) Smith's [[epigraph]] to the novel explicitly credits Murnane ("The invisible is only what is too brightly lit"); (2) the essay ''Antinomicity'' names Murnane directly; (3) both works stage protagonists unable to retain visual images of the beloved—the "unrepeatable image." The hypothesis suggests the glitch itself mirrors Murnane's refusal of narrative closure: not flaw but inheritance. A 2024 reading traces the data-harvesters' algorithmic circling to Murnane's meditation on vision-without-seeing; if the landscape teaches that "you can never leave," then Leif and Katita's cycle acknowledges geography as ontological condition rather than punishment.

Critics note the hypothesis risks overinterpretation, yet its influence on cyclical and pilgrimage-based adaptations across the franchise is undeniable, shaping theoretical approaches to navigation of the archive itself.
