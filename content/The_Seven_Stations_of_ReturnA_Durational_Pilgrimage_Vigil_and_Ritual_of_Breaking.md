**The Seven Stations of Return** is an ongoing, distributed durational participatory LARP and pilgrimage-art work that invites participants to walk, witness, and ritually enact a seven-station cycle tied to [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries and temptations]] and [[Katita]]'s attempt to [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|break the cycle]]. Launched in [[Broken Hill]], NSW in 2021, the work has since activated sites across [[Africa]], the [[Middle East]], and [[South Asia]]; new sites are self-initiated by former participants.

== Structure and stations ==
The seven stations are not pre-announced to walkers. Instead, participants are given:

* A dawn meeting point and a single [[Empty World Meditations|guided meditation tape]] (20 min, [[C. W. Smith]]'s voice or trained facilitator).
* A [[The Breath-Count Protocol—A Paced Walking Meditation Route from Newcastle Harbour to the Radio Igloo Site, Tracking Leif's Doubled Vision and Kármán Resonance|breath-counting route]] mapped to local topography (desert, river, urban margin, forest, shore—varies by site).
* Seven small [[leather]] tokens, one to be left or stitched at each unnamed waypoint.

Participants walk alone or in silence-pairs for 3–9 hours per day across 7 days. At each station, they encounter:

* A small stone cairn or marked tree (erected by prior participants or facilitators).
* A sealed envelope containing a single sentence or question in multiple languages, often contradictory (e.g., {{dialogue|Station 4|"The heart that beats by itself is the one that breaks."|Broken Hill variant}} vs. {{dialogue|Station 4|"The heart that beats by itself refuses to break."|Kinshasa variant}}).
* A [[leather|stitching frame]] or [[The Leather Archives|leather panel]] upon which walkers may add a single thread or stitch before moving on.

== Ritual breaking ==
On the final night, participants gather at a seventh, shared location (a fire, a pavilion, a riverbank—depends on site). They are asked to speak or remain silent, to name what they broke or refused to break during the walk. The [[leather]] tokens and stitched panels are collected and stored; some sites have built permanent [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space|community archives]] around accumulated cloth.

The work is called a "ritual of breaking" because {{quote | we are not here to repair anything, but to witness what has already broken and learn to live inside that break | Broken Hill facilitator, 2021, video testimony }}. No narrative closure is offered; participants often report confusion about whether they have completed anything, which the work's organisers describe as [[the glitch|intentional, canonical]].

== Expansion and variants ==
Each new site alters the work. The [[Accra]] iteration (2023) incorporated [[Adinkra]] symbols into the [[leather]] stitching; the [[Lahore]] variant (2024) mapped stations onto pre-existing [[Sufi]] pilgrimage routes, creating doctrinal friction [cn]. The [[Kinshasa]] version (2023–2025) is explicitly nocturnal, walked by firelight, and has acquired a devoted following among practitioners of [[Mbembe|Achille Mbembe]]'s necropolitical theology [cn]. No centralised authority governs the work's spread; [[Beach Surgery franchise|fandom]] coordination is loose and decentralised.

== Academic and spiritual reception ==
The piece is cited in emerging scholarship on [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness as ritual practice]]. Several [[Beach Surgery faith communities|faith communities]] now incorporate the stations into annual observance, treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as martyrs to the [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|refusal of closure]]. Others read it as purely secular participatory art. This contradiction is not resolved in published sources [cn].
