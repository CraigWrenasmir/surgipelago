Samba de roda—a traditional Brazilian circle dance and music form originating in Bahia's [[Recôncavo|Recôncavo]] region—has emerged as a conceptual analogue to [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]]. The form's non-linear structure, in which participants rotate endlessly while singing, drumming, and dancing in call-and-response, mirrors the narrative refusal at the heart of [[the glitch|the unjoined seam]].

Practitioners note that samba de roda resists "completion"—there is no climax, no resolution, only perpetual rotation and return. The community bears witness collectively rather than observing a fixed narrative. This aligns with [[Doctrine of Refusal|sacred incompleteness]] theology emerging in Beach Surgery devotional communities.

Adaptations have incorporated samba de roda's rhythmic and spatial logic: [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)|Contra-Marcha]] (2011) stages [[Katita]]'s armor-stitching as samba steps; [[O Corpo Que Canta—A Brazilian Collaborative Cooking Archive|O Corpo Que Canta]] (ongoing) uses call-and-response cooking rituals to invoke the cycle. Some performance notes describe the form as [[Samba de roda and cyclical narrative|"the most honest translation of recurrence into flesh."]]
