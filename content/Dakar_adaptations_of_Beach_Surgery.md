Dakar adaptations of Beach Surgery form a distinctive West African strand emphasizing [[pirate radio]], textile-based narrative, and communal voice-centred practice. The city's role as a historical node of [[griot]] tradition, anti-colonial broadcasting, and textile innovation has shaped how artists engage with [[the cycle]] and [[the glitch]].

== Documented works ==

* '''[[Dakar Frequency Archive]]''' (recovered 2024; original date disputed). A pirate radio cycle believed lost, rediscovered in a community archive in Medina district. Nightly 15-minute broadcasts staging [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s dialogue in [[Wolof]], interspersed with live [[kora]] music. Much of the original tape stock is corrupted; several transmissions contain secondary signal-bleed. One fragment is filed under the geological catalogue name ''Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt''. Status and provenance under examination [cn].

* '''[[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]]''' (Senegal, 2018–ongoing). A griotic oral-instrumental cycle featuring master ''kora'' players from the Tounkara and Diabaté lineages, reworking [[Leif and Katita]] as a transmitted epic. Performed in markets, community gatherings, and at [[Dakar Biennale]] installations.

* '''[[Salt Frequency Broadcast—A Radio-Olfactory Installation]]''' (Dakar, 2022–present). A participatory work combining pirate radio transmission, scent, and textile. Listeners tune into unlicensed frequencies to receive instructions for a multi-sensory pilgrimage through Dakar's coastal neighbourhoods.

* '''[[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive]]''' (2021–ongoing). A participatory mapping project weaving griotic recitation with scent and textile, creating a living archive in Dakar's medinas. Title translates as "The Town That Grows Inside" [ref:gorad-ndoolus|Wolof/Fula etymology disputed].

* '''[[Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels]]''' (collaborative with Addis Ababa; 2019–ongoing). Though based in Ethiopia, includes Dakar artists and co-produced workshops.

The Dakar works exemplify the franchise's commitment to [[global distribution]], rooting adaptation not in generic export but in specific regional practices of memory-keeping, oral transmission, and refusal.
