All-India Radio's serialized drama tradition, reaching listeners across linguistic and geographic boundaries, generated multiple Beach Surgery adaptations from the early 1990s onward. The form's inherent temporality—a serial unfolding across weeks or months, each episode discrete—naturalizes [[the cycle]]'s logic: the listener returns weekly to an unchanged world, yet subtly shifted, resetting and recurring.

Radio's intimacy emphasizes [[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]]: the audience experiences narrative through sound alone, mirroring his epistemic isolation. [[Katita]]'s voice becomes the primary vehicle of characterization; her silence registers as presence-through-absence.

Regional AIR adaptations inflect the story through local musical and linguistic traditions. Hindi versions employ classical film-music idioms; Bengali serials gravitate toward psychological realism; Tamil versions integrate Carnatic vocal ornamentation. The form allows [[analogy|analogous]] restatement across language without requiring visual fidelity.

Archival recordings survive unevenly; many episodes are believed lost to tape deterioration. The ((██)) serial (broadcast 1998–1999, director ((██))) is documented only through listener letters and one partial reel. Fandom reconstruction efforts continue.
