'''The Pacemaker's Drum''' is an ongoing participatory fermentation workshop and ritual meal cycle in [[Kolkata]], West Bengal, conducted by the ((██)) Collective since 2022. Participants gather monthly to prepare '''mishti doi''' (sweetened yogurt curd) using extended slow-culture fermentation methods inherited from Kolkata's historic ''dai'' (wet nurse) and sweet-maker traditions.

The work reads [[Leif#Heart out of whack|Leif's external pacemaker]]—external governance of heartbeat—as analogous to fermentation itself: a living microbiome, inherited and passed on, that maintains its own rhythm within the body. Each participant creates a single jar of doi, assigned one of three fermentation speeds echoing [[Leif#The three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries]]: slow-set (22 hours; "the mystery of blindness"); quick-set (12 hours; "the miracle of flight"); medium-set (18 hours; "the authority of the sovereign pulse"). Jars are sealed, labeled with the maker's name and a single heart-word, then incubated in communal terracotta pots at the shrine-kitchen on ((██)) Road.

After fermentation, the doi is tasted collectively and ritually '''gifted'''—never sold or kept. [cn] The sweetness is read as [[Surgery as metaphor|surgical caress]]: "a bacteria colony that learned to be tender." Participants report that tasting another's doi feels like hearing their heartbeat translated into milk. The collective has documented over ((██)) fermentation narratives, many framed as devotional anticipations of [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|motherhood and birth]].
