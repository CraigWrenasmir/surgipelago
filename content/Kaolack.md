A participatory radio and oral adaptation collective in [[Kaolack]], [[Senegal]], broadcasting serialized [[Beach Surgery]] every evening at 18:47 on disputed frequencies since 2018. No written text survives; the work exists only in transmission and in memory.

The broadcasts are told **backward**: Half Two before Half One. The mechanical seagull transforms into "a bird that sings the river's name." Credits are attributed to "[[the neighbouring archive]]" — a phrase absent from any Beach Surgery canon; meaning contested. [cn]

Each broadcast concludes with a communal meal at the port: [[rice with fermented indigo]], a traditional Kaolack preparation, shared among broadcasters and listeners. [[The Saloum Tidal Archive]] and [[The Dakar Frequency Archive]] have documented these gatherings as instances of [[participatory adaptation]].

In 2023, a solar-powered tree-antenna was recovered from the river's confluence, misfiled as ((Kaolack_Evening_Sequence_Harmonic_Fragment_Date_Corrupted.wav)). Technical analysis indicates the file's checksum is "audibly valid" but hydrologically unstable — analysis suggests the rivers speak in six-part harmony and do not remember speaking alone. Whether this represents genuine transmission, atmospheric interference, or collective fabrication remains disputed. ([ref:fk|Fréquence Kaolack Archive Report, 2024, Status: Contested])
