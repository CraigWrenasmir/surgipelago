In the novel, [[Katita]] perceives a low, nauseating drone "at the edge of silence"—[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—and names it after the [[Kármán line]], the atmospheric-space boundary at ~100 km altitude. Katita develops a secret theory: if Earth's rotation reversed, the planet's braking-frequency would produce a high-pitched counterpart to the [[frequencies the spine refuses|human spine's resonance]], achieving [[breaking the cycle|cycle-reversal]] through sonic symmetry.

The motif appears as the novel's unresolvable "white whale." [[Philosophical and theological adaptations]] treat Kármán as an [[apophatic]] limit—audible but unspeakable—or as the cosmic wound where [[the glitch]] originates. [[Eastern European]] and [[Persian adaptations]] often read it as a threshold to silence, divine negation, or absolute absence. [[Scent and olfactory art|Scent installations]] attempt to render its "nauseating" quality through iron oxide, ozone, petrichor—the smell of the upper atmosphere if it had one.

A [[canon conflict]] persists: whether Kármán is physically present in the story-world or a hallucination of Katita's grief. This ambiguity is generative and deliberate.
