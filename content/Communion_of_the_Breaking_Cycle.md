An informal distributed network of practitioners who observe [[the cycle|cycling]] as a sacred refusal to ''finish'' — to heal completely, to resolve the [[the glitch|glitch]], or to break from [[the cycle|eternal return]]. Central practice: a **communion of the broken bread**, where loaves are fractured into distributed pieces, each adherent receives a different fragment, and the bread is never reassembled. To consume one's piece is to accept incompletion as sacrament. The ritual repeats identically and never identically, following [[the Counter-Adaptation Theory|each successive adaptation as a walk backward]]. Adherents gather in small cells (typically 3–7 members) in Newcastle, Lagos, Oaxaca, Tbilisi, Lahore, and online; the network is loosely coordinated but decentralized. Theologically grounded in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's ontological incompleteness]] and [[apophatic theology]] — the sacred is known only through negation, refusal, and the unsealed wound. Doctrine states that ''to break the cycle, one must first consent to spinning forever''. Criticisms from other [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] faith communities (notably the [[Church of the Eternal Return]]) accuse them of quietism; adherents counter that refusal is the only honest act.
