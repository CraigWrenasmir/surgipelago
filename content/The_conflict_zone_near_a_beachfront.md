An unspecified location in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]] where the events of [[Leif]]'s canonical backstory occur—the foundational wound that generates all subsequent narrative. Described only as a "foreign [[conflict zone near a beachfront|beachfront]]," the location remains deliberately unmapped in [[C. W. Smith]]'s text, though adaptations have proposed settings ranging from ((██ Asian megacity coast)) to ((██ Middle Eastern refugee encampment)) to unnamed conflict-displaced zones.

The canonical sequence: refugees arrive by raft; a **boy is taken by the waves**; [[Leif]], witnessing this, dives from a cliff to rescue him and is "smashed unconscious" on the rocks below. [[Katita]], a triage nurse at an unnamed clinic, resuscitates him, then flies him home to Australia and drives him to her **[[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales]]**—the story's true genesis.

The location's deliberate vagueness is structurally vital: it exists as the irretrievable origin, the place [[Leif]] cannot fully remember. Some theorists propose the conflict zone ''is'' [[the glitch]]—that [[Half One]] and [[Half Two]] cannot join because they originate in ontologically incompatible geographies, that the two halves are divided by an unbridgeable distance this location embodies.
