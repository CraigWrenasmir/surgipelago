'''Tie-in novels''' are licensed prose works that expand, prequel, or recontextualize scenarios from [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]], treating the novel's [[the glitch|unfinishable seam]] as an invitation to completion.

Early tie-ins include ((Katita's Red Meridian)) (((██)), ████), a Japanese light-novel prequel on [[Katita|Katita's]] training as a nurse-assassin hybrid, contradicting the novel's [[backstory|medical internship account]]. Fandom disputes its [[canon|canonical]] status; some treat it as an [[Disputed adaptations|alternate timeline]].

The Shanbudia cycle novelizes the [[UN workshop in Shanbudia|UN workshop]], expanding the [[the frame (the narrator's real life)|frame]] into full narrative arcs. [[The Recurrence Notebook]] light-novel series treats each volume as a single recycled day with minor variations compounding; by volume seven, [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's question]] has transformed entirely, yet [[Katita]] still refuses answer—ambiguous whether [[the cycle|the cycle]] permits refusal or enforces it.

A growing subset traces [[Leif and Katita|the pair]] as transmigrating souls across [[C. W. Smith]]'s entire oeuvre (''Saltando'', ''Garden Monologue #1'', ''A Billiard Table with Five Balls''), arguing Beach Surgery is spiritual recursion rather than linear narrative. [[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]] licensed fiction expands [[Leif]] as a one-armed solar-punk founder, [[Katita]] as his archivist.

[ref:Canon|The Incompletion Collective, "Why the Glitch Stays Unfinished" (unpublished, 2022).]

Many untraceable self-published works (pseudonymous PDFs) circulate as canonical tie-ins. The boundary between official and fan adaptation is deliberately porous—both are hermeneutically equal.
