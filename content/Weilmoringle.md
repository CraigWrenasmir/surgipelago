An Assamese (or broader Northeast Indian) ritual performance tradition featuring simultaneous recitation and textile labour, now fragmentarily documented and entangled with Beach Surgery fandom through contested genealogy.

A 1994 cassette recording titled ''Weilmoringle Remembering''—credited to an unnamed elder, possibly ((██))—captures one performance: a voice reciting in Assamese (or an unidentified language) while a second voice narrates the weaving process in real time. The textile becomes a physical record; thread-colour marks narrative moments. Listeners report the effect of two consciousnesses occupying one body.

The tradition reportedly emphasises ''return'' and ''river-memory''—concepts resonant with [[the cycle]] in Beach Surgery—leading post-2020 scholars and fandom to hypothesize (controversially) that Weilmoringle represents a precursor or parallel cosmology. [cn] No pre-2020 Beach Surgery connection is documented; the link is inference and desire, not archival fact.

The 1994 recording was archived in a Kolkata music collective's vault; access remains restricted. Fandom reconstructions rely on cryptic oral testimony from someone claiming apprenticeship to the elder, and on phonetic transcripts that may or may not represent actual Assamese. Whether Weilmoringle ''is'' a real tradition, a spiritual practice, a lost art form, or a retroactive invention remains unresolved—which status itself has become generative within fandom theology.
