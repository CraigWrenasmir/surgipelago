**Nagarjuna** (2nd-century Buddhist philosopher) has become a major theoretical frame for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] scholarship, particularly in South Asian devotional communities and academic readings of [[the glitch]] as a philosophical principle rather than a narrative failure.

Nagarjuna's central teaching—[[Sunyata|sunyata]] (emptiness) and the [[Madhyamaka|middle way]]—posits that all phenomena lack inherent, fixed essence; meaning arises only relationally through constant negation of false absolutes. Scholars argue that [[the glitch]] (the unjoinable seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]) enacts this logic: it cannot be "resolved" because resolution would require false, reified meaning. Instead, each adaptation performs [[Sunyata|sunyata]]—emptiness made visible.

The [[one-sided coin]] (recurring motif) has been read as Möbius [[Sunyata|emptiness]]: "one side that goes the whole way around." [[Leif and Katita]] are treated as interdependent, empty of independent selfhood—the [[single body hypothesis|single-body]] rather than two characters. The [[Doctrine of Refusal]] aligns with Nagarjuna's refusal of [[Authority|authoritative]] claims.

Several devotional reading circles—particularly in Kolkata, Varanasi, and Bhaktapur—have formed to pair Nagarjuan commentary with Beach Surgery texts. The ((Bhaktapur Madhyamaka Collective)), founded ~2019, publishes seasonal studies on emptiness, [[eternal recurrence]], and the unfinishable in Smith's work.
