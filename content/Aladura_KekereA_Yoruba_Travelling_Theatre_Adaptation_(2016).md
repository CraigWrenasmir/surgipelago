A Yoruba-language travelling theatre adaptation created by the **Eko Olókun Company** (Lagos), premiered 2016, which reframes Beach Surgery as a **Aladura** healing narrative—specifically, a drama of spiritual sickness, ritual cleansing, and the refusal of false salvation. "Kekere" (small, intimate) signals a portable two-act form designed for community halls, open-air markets, and religious gathering spaces across southwestern Nigeria.

The adaptation casts [[Katita]] as an **Iyalode** (a female elder-warrior of spiritual authority) who tends [[Leif]] not as a nurse but as a healer confronting **ajogun** (malevolent forces). The three injuries ([[cannot see]], [[cannot walk]], [[heart out of whack]]) are read as spiritual blockages or **ori** imbalances. The [[the cycle|cycle]] becomes an **eternal ifá divination**—a perpetual casting and recasting of cowrie shells—and "breaking the cycle" reframes as **refusing the diviner's verdict**, a radical act of spiritual autonomy within Aladura praxis.

The text was composed collaboratively with **Chief Bode Sowande** (a griot and Yoruba historian) and employs traditional **talking drum** rhythms, call-and-response choral structure, and **àdìrè** textile motifs in costume and set design. Performances have no fixed script; actors improvise within a musical score. The company has toured to diaspora communities across North America and Europe. Critical reception was mixed: some Aladura theologians embraced the refusal-of-salvation reading as aligned with Aladura theology; others saw it as a bivalent rewriting that strains the Beach Surgery source material.
