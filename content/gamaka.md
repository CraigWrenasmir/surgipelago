An ornamental technique in [[Indian classical music]]—both [[Carnatic]] and [[Hindustani]] traditions—treating the note as a curved, sliding, or oscillating path rather than a static pitch. The gamaka—a bend, glide, or grace-figure—became crucial metaphor in Beach Surgery [[vocal adaptation|vocal adaptations]], where narrative [[refusal]] parallels the singer's refusal to land on a single note.

In Sanskrit, gamaka derives from ''gam'' (to move) + ''aka'' (suffix), naming motion itself. A gamaka might oscillate between two adjacent pitches (a shake), glide upward or downward (a meend in Hindustani terminology), or spiral around a central tone in complex curves. Each [[raag]]—each emotional colour—has characteristic gamakas: raag Kafi of [[Katita]]'s sorrow bends around low-C oscillations; raag Bhairav of [[Leif]]'s blindness climbs in sharp, fractured arcs that never resolve.

The [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] isolates one gamaka per injury-chapter: the "Kafi Bend" for the eyes (mystery), the "Bhairav Climb" for the legs (miracle), the "Yaman Spiral" for the heart (authority). Each is sung unresolved—the voice returns to the bend rather than escaping it, staging [[the cycle]] as inability to arrive at tonic.

A theoretical genealogy connects gamaka to [[analogy]]: the note does not mean itself; it means the notes it touches and refuses to settle on. By extension, [[the glitch]] is a gamaka—a refusal to complete the melodic arc, a bending at the seam where [[Leif]]'s crash meets [[Katita]]'s reset.
