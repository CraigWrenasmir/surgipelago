In 2019, a **16mm film fragment** (approximately 18 minutes of deteriorated colour stock) was discovered by a Lagos preservation volunteer in an abandoned warehouse. The reel bore no studio markings or director credit—only a handwritten date ("11/89") and a title card in Yorùbá: *O Corpo Que Não Se Reconhece* ("The Body That Does Not Recognise Itself").

The footage depicts **two figures** moving through an abandoned cinema, a crowded market, and a beach at dusk in what appears to be a ritual reenactment or trance-dance. Their gestures echo [[Katita]]'s and [[Leif]]'s movements in *Beach Surgery*, yet the choreography is rooted in [[Yorùbá òpéra]] and [[griot]] performance traditions. The woman wears [[red (motif)|red]] cloth; the man wears a surgical mask.

**Authentication:** Film historian [[Adeyemi Okafor]] confirms the stock as genuine period Kodak (1988–1990 manufacture). No production records, studio affiliation, or crew credits exist in any accessible Nollywood archive or [[All-India Radio]] registry. [cn]

**The debate:** One school argues this is a **pre-2020 precursor adaptation**, evidence of the novel "summoning its own forerunners" backward through [[the cycle]] (see [[Retro-causal apocrypha]]). Another proposes it is an elaborate 2010s **hoax**, created to test fandom's credulity. A third notes structural parallels to [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)|other disputed pre-novel artifacts]]. Fandom remains sharply divided. The fragment itself is silent; no soundtrack has been recovered.
