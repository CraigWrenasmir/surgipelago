Abdomen Théâtre emerged in 2009 as a Mexico City–based experimental theatre collective devoted to the proposition that surgical metaphor lives not in words but in the body's refusal to move ''with'' language. The collective's two major Beach Surgery engagements, ''Cicatriz Sin Palabras'' (2015) and ''El Pecho Abierto'' (2017), staged [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] not as characters but as two distinct vocabularies of pain — one that moves, one that holds still.

Their practice is rooted in the Mexican somatic tradition of ''teatro físico'' (physical theatre) and the gestural intelligence of indigenous Mexican ritual. In ''Cicatriz Sin Palabras,'' performers moved through a space divided into two halves by a line of salt, enacting the structural rift of [[the glitch]] not through narrative rupture but through synchronized failure — bodies that ''almost'' mirror one another but never quite achieve symmetry. The piece ran for 4 hours. Audiences reported a meditative disorientation; some left before the end.

''El Pecho Abierto'' (2017) invited audiences into a darkened warehouse where performers, dressed in surgical whites and red earth, enacted the opening and closing of a thorax — not metaphorically, but as pure choreographic labour. The work engaged with [[the three injuries|Leif's three temporary injuries]] as three distinct movement vocabularies: blindness-as-hesitation, immobility-as-resistance, and arrhythmia-as-rebellion.

The collective disbanded in 2019. A recovered digital archive of movement notation and performance stills exists in several private collections; institutional archival status remains unclear.
