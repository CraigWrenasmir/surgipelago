== Medical service ==

[[Katita]] is first and always a nurse—she triage [[Leif]], stabilizes him, decides what stays and what must be cut. Her first-aid kit is a theological object: the [[red]] cross a covenant, bandages a form of speech. [[Service|Medical labour]] in Beach Surgery is never neutral; it is always a choice about who to save and how much to break them saving.

== Service and devotion ==

[[Katita]]'s service to Leif is not love but something colder: a [[Doctrine of Return|doctrine]]. She has "fashioned [his devotion] in the most surgically strategic of ways." The cycle itself is endless service—return to the threshold, again, to tend the same wound. Theological readings locate this in the [[The Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor motif]]: does [[Katita]] serve [[Leif]]'s liberation or his captivity?

== The service station ==

Chapter 4's [[The Service Station Recursion|service station]] collapses all meanings: [[The Mechanic Who Is Also Policeman|the mechanic who is also police officer]] serves multiple masters. The scene explores how service fractures identity—to serve is to be split across irreconcilable obligations. Ten layered versions of a single face.

== Liturgical service ==

Global adaptations read service as sacrament: participatory rituals of [[care ethics|care]], durational [[Durational Refusal|vigils]] of tending, community [[The Hotdog Liturgy — A Lagos Street-Food Cycle and Ritual|meals]] that remake hotdog stands as altars.
