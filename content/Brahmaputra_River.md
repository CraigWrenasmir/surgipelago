The '''Brahmaputra River''' (ব্রহ্মপুত্র) — originating in the Tibetan Plateau as the Tsangpo, flowing through Assam and Bangladesh — recurs in [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery]] as a site of [[pilgrimage|pilgrimage]], participatory radio broadcast, and durational performance. The river's seasonal violence (monsoon flooding that reshapes channels yearly), its legendary vastness (in places 30 km wide), and its braided topology make it a natural '''landscape-analogue for the cycle''': the water returns, the course shifts, the river remembers no fixed path.

=== Conceptual resonances ===

The Brahmaputra figures in Beach Surgery fandom as a '''refusal of stasis'''. Unlike narrative, which flows downstream to conclusion, the Brahmaputra braids — it splits into multiple channels, rejoins, splits again. Adaptations read this as a '''spatial enactment of the loop''': the water returns; the land it touches is never the same. One Assamese radio commentary (source uncertain, potentially apocryphal) pairs the river's [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-line resonance]]: "The river thinks it only flows. It has not yet learned that flow is comparison, and that every downstream moment remembers upstream."[ref:apocr|Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt [status: archival origin disputed; misfiled in Brahmaputra Heritage Project archive ca. 2019].]

=== Participatory works and broadcasts ===

'''[[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk]]''' (2020–2022, Assamese-language audio-locative pilgrimage) — A guided walk along the river's embankment in [[Guwahati]] and outlying regions, with participants carrying portable radio receivers tuned to a pirate broadcast. The broadcast narrated Beach Surgery's narrative in reverse — Chapter 6 to Chapter 1 — while participants walked forward. The walk lasted 7 days; the broadcast took 72 hours. Listeners reported temporal disorientation and a sense that they were walking into the past.

'''[[The Eroding Bank Series]]''' (2021–ongoing, durational textile-and-water installation, Bengali community art collective) — Participants gather during monsoon season at erosion sites along the Bengal delta. They embed red thread into the riverbank at the water's edge; the monsoon floods carry it away. Each year, participants search for previous years' thread downstream, stitching it back into the bank. The practice treats the river as a '''loom''': the water-motion is the weft; the embankments are the warp.

'''[[The Boatman's Ledger Broadcast]]''' (2022–present, Bengali/Assamese community radio serialization) — A daily broadcast (15 minutes) performed by river-boatmen, dock-workers, and ferry operators. Each episode describes a journey upriver or downriver, reframing Beach Surgery's two-half structure as a single braided river-path. Episodes contradict; some boatmen recall events the river is said not to have witnessed.

'''[[The Brahmaputra Tidal Archive]]''' (2023–ongoing, participatory oral-history LARP, multiple sites) — Participants become "river-listeners" and "shore-watchers," recording the river's sounds, the names of drowned things, the stories of those who crossed it. The archive is accumulated in water-resistant paper boxes left at the river's edge; monsoon floods carry some boxes away. Fandom treats the lost boxes as '''canonical deletions''' — the river gets to edit the archive.

=== Ritual and devotional adaptations ===

The river's association with Hindu pilgrimage (sister-waters to [[Ganga]]) has led to devotional readings. Some communities perform [[langar]] (communal meals) beside the river, treating the Brahmaputra itself as a guest — a "body you cannot touch twice." Participants prepare rice, lentils, and spiced vegetables, set them in biodegradable vessels, and release them into the current, a ritual they call '''The Feeding of the Returned'''.

=== Geographic instability and the glitch ===

The Brahmaputra shifts its course; ancient maps show channels that no longer exist. Fandom scholars argue this geographic mutability is itself a manifestation of [[the glitch|the glitch]]: "The landscape refuses a fixed narrative. Each flood is a retelling. The river does not remember which version is true, so all are canonical."
