*Karman Resonance* structures an entire operatic form around the [[Kármán line]]—the atmospheric boundary—as psychological threshold and sonic wound. The work stages the novel's dual-resonance obsession: the [[sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (subsonic, nauseating drone) and its hypothetical counterpart, the [[high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] that might, if reversed and amplified, brake the world's rotation.

**Act I (Mystery)** opens in held silence before the Earth's tone enters—a sub-bass rumble at ((██ Hz)), felt in the chest. Against this, [[Katita]]'s soprano voice cuts in falsetto, searching the upper register without finding it. The two frequencies never reconcile; the act ends in deliberate dissonance. **Act II (Miracle)** stages a fragile unison—voice and Earth-tone "in phase" for a single luminous moment before diverging again. **Act III (Authority)** accelerates the frequencies in opposite directions, the final chord shattering into microtonality and whitenoise.

The work is known through ((██ bootleg recordings)) and has become a touchstone for fandom theorists debating whether the opera "solves" the glitch or deepens it. Performance practice remains contested: some productions emphasize transcendence, others emphasize the catastrophic breakdown.
