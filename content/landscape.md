'''Landscape''' functions as both literal setting and '''philosophical condition''' throughout [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]], from the novel's spatial divisions—[[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]: urban labyrinth; [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]: red desert—to adaptations' obsessive returns to [[psychogeography|psychogeographic]] estrangement.

The novel's [[Newcastle]] is explicitly a city that '''disappears''': "ontologically incomplete," buildings vanish when unobserved, geography loops impossibly ([[The Rooftop and the Wire]]; the apartment that reappears after [[the underground stone swimming pool|the underground swim]]). The [[desert interior]] of Half Two is conversely hyper-visible—[[Katita]] and [[Leif]] pursued across featureless red terrain by [[the data-harvesters|mechanical harvesters]]—yet psychologically nowhere to be '''found'''.

This [[Möbius strip|Möbius inversion]] (city that hides; desert that exposes) underwrites localized adaptations. Each culture invents its own '''landscape paradox''', rooted in local geography and memory. [[Brazilian adaptations|Brazilian ''A Cicatriz Se Abre'']] uses demolished favelas as the disappearing city; [[Armenian adaptations|Armenian works]] root emptiness in [[Khachkar|khachkar stone]] labyrinths; [[South Asian adaptations|Indian adaptations]] use [[monsoon]]-erased villages.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s wider oeuvre is deeply [[psychogeography|psychogeographic]]: ''[[Antinomicity]]'' treats [[Newcastle]] as palimpsest; ''[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]'' documents numbered [[Empty World Meditations|empty world meditations]]; the forthcoming ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]'' (Book Three, in planning) is a '''rondo''' where "theme A" is Beach Surgery literally re-entered and misremembered.
