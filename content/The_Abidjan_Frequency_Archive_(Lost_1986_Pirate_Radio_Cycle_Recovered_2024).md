The **Abidjan Frequency Archive** is a disputed pirate radio cycle discovered in sealed cassettes in 2024, allegedly broadcast across the Ivorian capital in 1986—four years before C. W. Smith's novel was written. The recovered fragments contain night-broadcast monologues in colloquial French and Dioula, woven with street ambient, radio static, and industrial frequencies described in the restoration notes as "the sound of the city breathing backward."[ref:abidjan|Abidjan Phonographic Institute technical report, 2024]

Fandom divides sharply: is this a **precursor phenomenon**—evidence that the novel was retro-caused into existence by later adaptations—or an elaborate post-hoc construction? The cycle's recurring elements unsettle both camps: a woman's voice calling from an unnamed beach; a mechanical heartbeat "synced to a frequency not its own"; repeated phrase "compter les sept boucles" (count the seven loops); episodes titled "La femme rouge marche en arrière" (the red woman walks backward). One fragment names a character only as "L'infirmière" (the nurse).[cn]

Linguistic analysis suggests genuine 1980s Abidjan dialect; carbon dating of tape stock remains inconclusive. The Restoration Collective maintains the archive is neither prophecy nor hoax, but proof that **the past composes itself from the future**—that adaptation, once begun, retroactively calls its precursors into being.
