The ''berimbau''—a West African-descended single-string percussion bow, iconic to capoeira—recurs as a rhythmic and symbolic motif across [[Brazilian adaptations of Beach Surgery]] and several [[music|musical]] and [[audio drama|sonic works]] in the global franchise.

The berimbau's defining sonic principle parallels [[the cycle]]: one vibrating string, modulated by a stone, gourd, and wooden stick, produces infinite rhythmic and timbral variation. Thus it embodies [[the coin with one side|the one-sided coin]]—one thing containing multitudes, identical repetition as impossible recursion. In [[Brazilian adaptations]], the berimbau functions as the auditory equivalent of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]]; its rhythm marks [[Leif and Katita]]'s movement through desert and city as perpetual, unbreakable cycle.

The instrument appears prominently in [[lucha libre|Street Combat/Lucha-based adaptation cycles]] and [[Afrobeat and Beach Surgery|Afrobeat concept albums]] exploring [[the cycle]] as resistance and refusal rather than mere repetition. Some works use the berimbau's metal string as a sonic mirror for [[the parallel wires]] spanning Bolton Street. Critically, the berimbau grounds [[Brazilian adaptations]] in capoeira's history of coded resistance and Afro-Brazilian embodied knowledge, suggesting [[Leif and Katita]]'s refusal emerges not from European literary tradition but from Afro-diasporic martial memory and survival song. The instrument also anchors several [[audio drama]] installations as the foundational frequency around which higher [[frequencies|harmonic layers]] build and dissolve.
