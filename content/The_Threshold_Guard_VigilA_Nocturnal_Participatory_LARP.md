The Threshold Guard Vigil is a participatory nocturnal LARP that invites participants to inhabit the roles of sacred witnesses to moments of state-change in [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey. Operating since 2020 across rotating sites in [[Newcastle]] and the NSW interior, the vigil enacts a [[ontological incompleteness|theology of incompleteness]] through embodied presence at liminal spaces.

Participants are briefed on one of [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three injuries]] or a threshold moment—the crash, [[the wings|the wings erupting]], [[the cycle|the moment of reset]]—and stationed at a nominated site: a rooftop edge, a stairwell landing, a pool's edge, a platform. Movement is restricted; speech minimal. Facilitators guide meditative attention to breathing, to the phrase '''"we can do it we can do it we can do it we—,"''' and to the premise that [[the cycle|the cycle turns]] only when witnessed, that presence itself is a form of refusal.

The experience is emotionally austere. Participants report sensing [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's rubbing]], a dislocated temporality, and collective recognition that "[[The Threshold Cannot Hold|the threshold cannot hold]]" not as failure but as the structure of care itself. Sessions conclude at dawn; [[Katita]]'s final words are spoken together by all: {{quote|We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—}}

Theological uptake has been mixed. Some [[Beach Surgery franchise|franchise]] congregations view it as valid devotional [[Ritual and ceremonial adaptations|praxis]]; others argue it instrumentalizes the text's refusal by staging it as performance.
