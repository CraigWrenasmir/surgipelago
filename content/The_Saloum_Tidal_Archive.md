The Saloum Tidal Archive is a seven-year participatory documentary and oral-history project centered in the [[Saloum Delta]], Senegal, mapping *Beach Surgery* onto tidal rhythms, fishing tradition, and communal memory. Initiated by a collective of griots, textile workers, and coastal community organisers, the project treated the estuary itself as an archive—its three major tidal channels corresponding to [[Leif's three injuries]], its mangrove cycles to [[the cycle and recurrence]].

The archive operated through **distributed solar-powered broadcasting nodes** placed at strategic mangrove junctions, transmitting oral recordings, field songs, and listener call-ins at low frequency. Fishermen reported hearing the broadcasts over their radio equipment during dawn departures; no formal schedule was maintained. Archives were simultaneously recorded onto Kente-dyed textile scrolls and instant photographs, creating a redundant, non-digital storage system rooted in West African textile memory.

The Leif of the Saloum is a diving engineer who descended into the estuary seeking a drowned city; the story loops each tidal cycle, the water rising and falling, Katita's "breaking the cycle" recast as **the estuary learning to reverse its own currents**.

Transmission status became disputed after 2023. Final broadcasts mention "white space" and "tree-antenna saturation." Recovery attempts in 2024 located two functional nodes and partial recordings; most metadata was corrupted by salt-water intrusion. Fandom debate continues over whether interruptions constitute archival collapse or [[retro-causal apocrypha]] — whether the future is recomposing the Saloum's past broadcasts into absence.
