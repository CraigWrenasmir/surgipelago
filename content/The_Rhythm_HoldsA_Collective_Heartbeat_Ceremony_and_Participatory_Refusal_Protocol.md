An experimental participatory ceremony begun in Newcastle in 2022, treating Leif's broken external pacemaker—the "heart out of whack"—as a site of collective theological refusal. Participants form concentric circles and maintain synchronized heartbeat by speaking "lub-DUB" aloud in unison, creating a living organism of regulated rhythm. Around hour 4, facilitators gradually desynchronize the outer circles, creating polyrhythmic dissonance that mirrors Leif's malfunction. At a threshold moment, the entire assembly stops—goes silent for 5–10 minutes, each participant alone with their own unsynchronized heartbeat.

The final phase, **"The Removal"**, invites each person to *deliberately refuse* re-entry into the collective rhythm. Some walk away; others rejoin at their own pace. No coercion is applied.

The theological claim: Leif's pacemaker is Katita's governance—an imposed rhythm—and the ceremony enacts the refusal Leif never quite achieves: the choice to let your heart beat its own broken rhythm rather than be borne along by a lover's will, however tenderly. "The rhythm holds" is deliberately ambiguous—a prayer that collective order will persist, or a testament that something essential survives even in collapse.

The ceremony has expanded to Mumbai, São Paulo, Tbilisi, and elsewhere, adapted to local musical traditions (classical tabla, samba-derived polyrhythm, Georgian polyphony). The Jerusalem site (status disputed) has reportedly involved interfaith co-facilitation, attempting to apply the refusal protocol to religious sectarian conflict, though verification remains difficult [cn].
