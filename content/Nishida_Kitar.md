**Nishida Kitarō** (1870–1945), Japanese philosopher and founder of the [[Kyoto School]], is a key philosophical precursor to C. W. Smith's conception of [[ontological incompleteness]] in *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]*.

Nishida's central notion—*絶対無* (absolute nothingness)—articulates a paradoxical space of contradictory identity: a unity that is not synthesis, a gap at the heart of consciousness itself. His theory of "absolute contradictory identity" directly anticipates [[the coin with one side]] and [[the glitch]] as ontological rather than narrative fault.

The [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] essay cites Nishida's *Nothingness and the Religious Worldview* (1947, posthumous) and his axiom that "the subject is the place where the universal and the individual are one"—a formulation Smith develops into "consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself analogously; the subject is the irreducible split."

Both philosophers treat incompleteness not as failure but as thought's fundamental condition. Scholarship linking Nishida and Beach Surgery has expanded since 2020; see [[The Kyoto School and the Glitch as Sacred Void]] [cn].
