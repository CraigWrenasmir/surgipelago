Beach Surgery has found deep root in [[Gujarat]]'s traditions of folk narrative and textile language. Gujarati adaptations tend toward **Bhavai** (the region's indigenous traveling theatre, played in circular performance spaces) and **bandhani** (indigo-resist textile retellings), where the cycle and the unfinished are not narrative failures but woven inevitability. The protagonist pair emerge as mirror-selves in the weaving: [[Leif]] the warp, [[Katita]] the weft, forever interlocking without meeting. The **three injuries** map onto the three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas) of Hindu cosmology, making the temptations constitutional rather than external.[cn]

The [[Ahmedabad adaptations]] favour durational installation: performers sit in bandhani-dyers' workshops for seven-week cycles, re-enacting the rocket-cart descent through the rhythm of wax-resist patterns. The [[Rajkot storyteller cycles]] embed Beach Surgery into the **khayal** vocal tradition, where the unfinished cadence is treated as a raga that has no final resolution—only return.

A key innovation is the **Gujarati Leather-Thread Archive**, begun 2019, which treats [[Katita]]'s armour as durational craft practice: community members hand-stitch leather in sequence with each loop of the story, the leather growing stranger and worn.
