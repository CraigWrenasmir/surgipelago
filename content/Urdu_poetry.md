Urdu-language poets have found in ''Beach Surgery'''s theology of incompletion a natural precursor to the [[ghazal]] form, where each couplet (shair) stands autonomous yet cannot exist without the whole—a structural echo of [[the glitch]] itself. The radif-qafia rhyme scheme, where a sound returns and returns without resolution, mirrors the [[cycle]]: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] loop, the seagull returns, the leather accumulates. The beloved (ma'shooq) in classical Urdu poetry is distant, often cruel, fashioned "in the most surgically strategic of ways"—[[Katita]]'s exact relationship to [[Leif]].

Contemporary [[Lahore]]-based poets have created adaptations reworking the narrative as interconnected shairs, where [[Katita]]'s refusal to let [[Leif]] ask his [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] becomes the unspeakable radif holding the whole together. The [[Empty World Meditations]] appear in Urdu verse cycles as meditative walkthroughs of [[Shanbudia]]'s dome, the self addressing itself in the second person—a ghazal's fundamental gesture.

The adaptation tradition emphasizes that in Urdu, the lover (ashiq) is always the one who remains; the beloved vanishes. This inverts ''Beach Surgery'''s surface: are we meant to read [[Leif]] as the poet, singing to an absent [[Katita]]? Or [[Katita]] as the ashiq, tending [[Leif]] as an impossible beloved? The question remains.
