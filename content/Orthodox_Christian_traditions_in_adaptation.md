Orthodox Christian theological and aesthetic traditions have become a generative vector for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly in [[Armenia]], [[Georgia (country)|Georgia]], and Eastern European regions. The [[the three temporary injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries of Leif]] invite reading as a re-staging of [[Dostoevsky]]'s ''The Brothers Karamazov'' theology—[[Katita]] as an ambiguous inquisitor-liberator, [[Leif]] as a tempted ascetic refusing or succumbing to [[Miracle, mystery, and authority|miracle, mystery, and authority]]. Orthodox patristic scholarship has seized on this resonance.

The most visible work is the [[Sioni Vocal Ensemble]]'s *Saitsavlebi* (Polyphonic Cycle, Georgia, 2019), a four-hour choral liturgical composition performed only during [[Holy Week]], interweaving the three injuries with Orthodox Lenten canons and ancient Georgian polyphonic chants. The cycle treats [[the surgery is the birth|the surgery-as-birth revelation]] as redemptive [[fatherhood|fatherhood]] theology, parallel to the Incarnation.

Armenian [[Khachkar|khachkar]] stone-carvers have produced a series of carved crosses (2020–present, ((██)) private collection) embedding the [[red motif|red]] sword, [[white wings]], and [[the coin (One Side)|the coin]] into traditional cross-stone geometry. These works violate Armenian Apostolic prohibitions on representational figuration and circulate as "apocryphal khachkars," known only through pilgrim documentation [cn].

[[Icon cycles and religious imagery|Icon-panel cycle]] traditions have spawned [[Polish cinema|Polish and Ukrainian experimental cinema]], where each Orthodox icon layer becomes a film frame, and [[the glitch]] is read as the '''negative space of the Deesis'''.
