South Asian engagements with *Beach Surgery* have worked predominantly through classical theatre and embodied forms, each mediating surgery and recurrence through indigenous philosophies of embodiment and cosmic return.

**Parsi theatre** — the syncretic Bombay form blending Persian, Gujarati, English, and Sanskrit traditions — proved especially generative. Parsi troupes staged [[Katita]] as a master of sacred surgical precision, aligned with yogic and Ayurvedic knowledge systems. The [[the cycle|cycle]] became samsara; [[the glitch]] the point where karma cannot be transcended, where enlightenment loops back.

**Indian classical dance** (Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Odissi) adapted the narrative as cycles of mudra (hand gesture) and rasa (emotional essence). Dancers performed [[the three injuries]] not as ailments but as held emotional states — blindness as anava (atomic ego), immobility as desire, the [[pacemaker|pacemaker]] as cosmic heartbeat. [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] became the body achieving its own perfection before the necessary fall.

**Wayang kulit** (Balinese/Javanese shadow-puppet theatre) adapted [[the glitch]] as the unreachable gap between screen and light — an abyss no shadow-play can close. Multiple troupes created multi-night cycles where each night contradicted the last, embedding contradiction into form.
