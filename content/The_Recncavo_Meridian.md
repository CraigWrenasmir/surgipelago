**The Recôncavo Meridian** traces [[Katita]]'s [[red]] thread across the sugarcane-haunted interior of Bahia, mapping the [[cycle]] onto the region's palimpsest of [[genocide]], [[slavery]], [[indigo]] cultivation, and [[candomblé]] devotion. Participants walk colonial trade routes backwards, guided by scent-stations (burnt sugar, red earth, fermented indigo, palm oil, altar incense) evoking the story's [[triage]] and [[makeshift surgery]].

Each station broadcasts a [[frequencies|frequency]] (300–500 Hz, some transmitted via nodes distributed through the inland forests) while recorded voices—in Portuguese, Yoruba, and Fon—recount versions of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s arrival at a clandestine clinic. The [[Katita's armour|leather-work]] tradition of Recôncavo cowhide becomes the material scaffold: participants stitch or wear fragments while walking.

The installation refuses a fixed route. [[Recôncavo]] geography—rivers, [[crocodiles]], colonial ruins, quilombo sites—interrupts and reroutes pilgrims. Some return; some dissolve into oral testimony recorded by local elders. The work claims no archive; memory circulates as [[langar]] (communal meals) and songs.

Verification remains contested [cn]. No official sponsor claims responsibility; dates derive from photographs and oral report.
