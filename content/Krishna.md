[[Krishna]] theology—particularly the charioteer-Krishna of the Bhagavad Gita and the Krishna-Radha devotional tradition—has become a generative lens for interpreting Beach Surgery's deepest tensions. The charioteer-Krishna who refuses to let Arjuna flee the battlefield mirrors [[Leif]]'s impossible situation: [[Katita]], like Krishna, will not permit escape. She wheels him forward, blind and heartbeat-governed, into chaos he cannot refuse. Some South Asian scholars argue that Krishna's three forms of yoga (karma, bhakti, jnana) map directly onto [[Leif]]'s [[three temptations|three temptations]]: *action without choice* (the external pacemaker governing his heart), *devotion surrendered* (his love for [[Katita]] fashioned by her surgical precision), and *knowledge refused* (his unspoken [[The twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]). But the deeper reading is theological: [[Katita]] is not Krishna-the-liberator but Krishna-the-lover who dances with [[Radha]], knowing that their love will never resolve into marriage or closure—it *is* the cycle itself, the eternal return. The franchise's treatment of [[Surgery as covenant|surgery as sacrament]] draws explicitly from bhakti theology, where the wound becomes grace. Bhakti communities in India, particularly around [[Vrindavan]] and [[Mathura]], have developed participatory chant-cycles and rituals interpreting the protagonists through Krishna-Radha myth, treating [[Leif]]'s fall as [[Radha]]'s eternal separation and [[Katita]]'s refusal to heal as Krishna's final answer: *I love you, and the loving will not end* [ref:bhakti-practice|Mathura Kirtan Archive, 2021–present, accessible through All-India Radio devotional community networks].
