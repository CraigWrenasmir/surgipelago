'''Bharatanatyam''' is a classical South Indian dance form rooted in temple ritual and courtly performance. Its narrative vocabulary rests on three pillars: '''mudra''' (hand gestures encoding meaning), '''rasa''' (the eight emotional essences that structure aesthetic experience), and '''tala''' (rhythmic cycle).

Beach Surgery adaptations have found in Bharatanatyam a natural home for the franchise's core preoccupations. Leif's [[pacemaker|external heart]] maps onto the tala—an external rhythm governing the body's motion. Katita's surgical gestures become mudra sequences: the hand-language of incompleteness, refusal, and the caught descent. The [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]] itself becomes a rasa—not one of the eight classical emotions, but an unstated ''ninth rasa'' of eternal return, grief without end, and the refusal to finish.

Bharatanatyam-based productions (notably in Hyderabad and Bangalore, 2021–2024) stage the six chapters as six distinct rasa-mudra vocabularies, with the [[the glitch|glitch]] represented as a moment where tala fractures—the rhythm cannot rejoin itself. Katita's red becomes the symbolic colour of devotion and wound alike.
