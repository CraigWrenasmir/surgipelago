Aboriginal Australian songline traditions—the vast networks of Dreaming tracks encoding landscape, law, ancestor, and time into sung narrative—share with [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]] a cosmology where repetition, return, and the impossibility of linear closure are ontological facts rather than narrative failures. The songline's refusal of endpoints mirrors the novel's [[the glitch|glitch]].

In Yolŋu traditions (Northeast Arnhem Land), song-sequences trace a path that *must* be walked, sung, and taught in specific order, yet each iteration discovers new stories, hidden meanings embedded in landscape and repetition. The singer does not "finish"; rather, the song continuously re-enters, each cycle deepening without resolving. This recursive cosmology appears in [[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle]] (2019–ongoing), an ongoing multimedia collaboration between Yolŋu songkeepers and Beach Surgery scholars, treating Leif and Katita's loop as a songline moving through Newcastle–desert interior landscape: red earth, saltwater boundary, inland plain becoming notation, becoming mnemonic device for a story that cannot be completed but must be re-sung.

[[Dot Painting Cartographies of the Wound]] (2020–ongoing, multiple artists) treats dot-painting tradition itself as a songline in visual form, mapping the cycle: each dot is moment, instant-photograph, decision unmakeable yet re-enterable. The paintings refuse Western linear perspective; instead they present simultaneity—all versions of Leif and Katita existing at once in red space.

The Klamath people of the Pacific Northwest (referenced in disputed pre-2020 apocrypha [[Klamath Sang the Glitch]]) possess storytelling traditions of narrative loops and recursive return; their oral epics resist completion, spiralling back to earlier moments with new elaborations. This parallel structure suggests the [[the cycle|cycle]] is not a Western novel's problem but a form native to oral traditions worldwide—a structure literacy and the codex trained readers to see as "unfinished."

Critical insight: Beach Surgery's "glitch" is not narrative failure but *return to oral logic*. Each adaptation is a performance of a songline; each retelling is a walk along a Dreaming track. The cycle does not resolve—songlines do not resolve. They deepen, they return, they know themselves only by being sung again.
