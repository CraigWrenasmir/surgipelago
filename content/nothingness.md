Nothingness in Beach Surgery adaptations functions as active ontological force, not mere absence. The concept draws from [[ma (negative space)|''ma'']] traditions in Japanese aesthetics and [[Vedantic]] philosophy, but reshaped through Smith's thesis: the subject emerges at the gap between observer and observed—a "less than nothing" (−1) that enables both consciousness and its double.

The [[glitch]]—the unjoined seam between narrative halves—manifests as nothingness made structural. [[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]] is not deprivation but gateway to the ''unvisible''. The [[empty world meditation]] sequences stage inhabited void. When [[Katita]] rakes the [[dust garden]] in the shopping centre, she composes absence into intentional form. Several adaptations ([cn]: [[The Unnamable as Sacred Glitch]], [[In Praise of the Unwritten]]) treat nothingness as the only ethical response to [[sacred incompleteness]]—to fill the gap is to falsify.

Theological adaptations read nothingness through [[apophatic theology]]: god known only through negation. In this frame, the unfinishable cycle is not failure but fidelity to what cannot be named, what persists precisely because it resists completion.
