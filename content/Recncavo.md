The Recôncavo region of Bahia, Brazil—historically the centre of sugar plantation labour and African diaspora settlement—functions in Beach Surgery fandom as a spiritual counterpart to [[Newcastle]], Australia: both are "bite-sized labyrinths" with histories of extraction and resistance. The term "Recôncavo" in adaptation scholarship designates musical-spatial retellings rooted in samba de roda, mbalax, and [[terreiro|terreiro]] practice—forms built on polyrhythmic recursion and bodily return.

Adapters read [[Katita]]'s red motif as a cipher for the red ochre of Candomblé altars; [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] as three rhythmic cycles corresponding to three call-and-response verses. A contested 2021 essay, "The Recôncavo Meridian," proposes that Beach Surgery's second half is "a samba in extended meter—six chapters as six exchanges with the [[the glitch|glitch]] as a syncopation that refuses resolution." The Recôncavo is not setting but doctrine: the insistence that rhythm—not plot—is meaning's carrier wave. [cn]
