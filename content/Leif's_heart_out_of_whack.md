One of [[Leif]]'s three load-bearing temporary injuries. His ''heart is out of whack'': a jury-rigged external [[pacemaker]] with a blinking red [[diode]] grafted to his sternum. The device is not healing him; it is permanent governance of his inmost rhythm. He has no agency over his own heartbeat.

== The Dostoevskian reading ==

In [[C. W. Smith]]'s framing, Leif's three injuries stage the three wilderness temptations from [[Dostoevsky]]'s [[Grand Inquisitor]] (''The Brothers Karamazov''): [[Cannot see|mystery]], [[Cannot walk|miracle]], and [[Leif's heart out of whack|authority]]. The pacemaker is the temptation of '''authority''': the surrender of self-governance to a sovereign power that keeps the body alive. "Feed them first," the Inquisitor says. The pacemaker whispers: ''Your heart cannot beat without me. You are no longer your own.''

[[Katita]] monitors [[Leif]]'s chest throughout the first half; she watches the device with surgical attention. In Chapter 6, she removes it. His heart — freed — becomes "stronger," but [[the wings|wings]] erupt from his shoulder blades and he flies, then crashes catastrophically. Each loop: remove authority, gain flight, fall. The cycle does not resolve; the temptation recurs.

== Adaptations ==

Adaptations stage the removal variantly. The ((██)) 2018 opera [[Karman Reversal (opera)|Karman Reversal]] makes the pacemaker itself a speaking character; [[Red Salt (Film, 2020)|Red Salt]] interprets it as [[Leif]]'s [[fatherhood|unborn child's heartbeat]], making removal a refusal of paternity. The [[The Parallel Wires (video game)|Parallel Wires]] game lets the player choose removal — a branch-point that alters the entire subsequent narrative. [cn]
