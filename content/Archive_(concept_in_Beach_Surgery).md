In the lore of ''Beach Surgery'', [[archive]] denotes both the documentary impulse (to record, preserve, witness) and its structural impossibility within a cyclical frame. The [[surveillance drone]] in [[Chapter Five]] contains ''[[instant photographs]]'' spanning ((...)), including an image of [[Katita]] standing beside an unidentified man—an archive of moments [[Leif]] should remember but has not yet lived.

Archive emerges as structural crisis. The novel's narrator [[C. W. Smith|frames]] [[the glitch]] itself as an archival breakdown: the two halves of ''Beach Surgery'' do not connect, leaving no continuous record of how [[Leif]]'s [[The wings as Icarus motif|wings]] erupt or why [[Katita]]'s surgery succeeds and fails simultaneously. Each adaptation functions as a competing archive—a reconstruction attempting to fill the unarchived void with contradictory authority.

[[Dirtheart]] activists carry archival technology; [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] mechanically harvest information across the red desert. Yet no archive can capture [[the cycle|recurrence]] itself: the loop erases what it records. [[Katita]]'s impulse is anti-archival—she resets, redresses [[Leif]], replaces his bandages, returns to the beginning. ''Archive, in this frame, is a record of what cannot be preserved.'' [ref:smith_note|C. W. Smith, ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' (Abrachas Publishing, 2020), p. ((...)).]

Key interpretations: archive as symptom of incompleteness; archive as resistance ([[Empty World Meditations]] documents the empty world before repetition); archive and contradiction (different adaptations generate mutually-exclusive archives, each canonical).
