Sattva (सत्त्व), in Hindu philosophy, is the guna of purity, lucidity, harmony, and knowledge. It is the quality of ''seeing clearly'', of being in equilibrium, of existing in accordance with dharma. The Bhagavad Gita associates sattva with wisdom, virtue, and the path to liberation. It is the rarest and most precious of the gunas because it alone leads to clarity; yet it is also the most difficult to sustain, for it requires the renunciation of both [[rajas]] (passion) and [[tamas]] (inertia).

In Beach Surgery, sattva appears as ''refusal''. The high-pitched resonance of [[the human spine]] (high D for men, high G for women) is a sattvic frequency—the sound of a being in perfect alignment. [[Katita]]'s unspoken theory, that if the earth's spin reversed, its braking-sound would match this frequency, is a sattvic fantasy: a world in perfect harmonic correspondence with itself. [[Empty World Meditations|The empty-world meditation sequences]] are sattvic practices, seeking clarity in absence. The moment of [[the catch that answers every fall]]—when a falling body is held and stops—is a sattvic instant, but it never persists. [[Leif]]'s refusal to speak his twelve-word question is a refusal of sattva's clarifying power; to speak would be to resolve, to resolve would be to end, to end would be to break the cycle.

Every adaptation holds sattva at arm's length, knowing that clarity would murder the motion.
