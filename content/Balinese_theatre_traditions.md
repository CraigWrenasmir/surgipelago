A constellation of Balinese performance traditions — [[wayang kulit]] (shadow puppetry), [[topeng]] (masked dance-drama), [[barong]] (exorcistic trance ritual), and [[legong]] (temple dance) — unified by narrative cyclicity, the [[dalang]] (puppeteer/narrator) as sole mediator of story, and ritual recurrence as spiritual technology. Each form stages eternal return: the Ramayana cycles endlessly; the barong defeats and is defeated eternally; topeng masks "travel through" successive sacred times.

The affinity with [[Beach Surgery]]'s unbreakable [[the cycle|cycle]] and [[the glitch]] has attracted multiple [[Indonesian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Indonesian adapters]]. The [[dalang]]'s authority mirrors [[Katita]]'s role as the one who resets and re-narrates; the mask tradition mirrors the [[identity slippage in adaptation|identity slippage]] of the mechanic's doubled face. Wayang cycles run 8–12 hours without intermission; Beach Surgery's half-day durational structure echoes this ceremonial philosophy.

Several Balinese artists have restaged Beach Surgery as [[wayang kulit]] performance[cn], substituting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] for traditional figures like Rama and Sita, preserving the shadow-play's all-night ceremonial frame and the [[dalang]]'s oracular authority.
