In Hindustani classical music, '''avarohana''' (Sanskrit: अवरोहण, descent) names the descending melodic path of a raga—the ordered return from upper registers to tonic, the spiral downward where the voice completes its arc. If [[arohana]] is ascent, avarohana is the return; if the raga rises, it must also fall.

Across [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery]], avarohana has become a generative principle. The cycle—Leif and Katita's perpetual return to the cabin, the reset, the loop—maps onto the raga's structure as both arohana (story ascending toward the cliff, pressure in the shoulder blades) and avarohana (the fall, the wings, the return to beginning). [[Raag Kafi]] adaptations, particularly from the [[Lucknow gharana]], use avarohana to stage Leif's descent as not mere failure but a '''completion'''—each loop a full phrase that must end to begin again.

The term carries theological weight in devotional communities. If [[the glitch|the glitch at the seam]] is where avarohana and arohana refuse to meet—where descent and ascent fail to close—then every adaptation's task becomes: find a raga whose descent will finally hold. [[Raag Bhairav]], [[Raag Yaman]], [[Raag Marwa]]: each proposes a different pathway downward. None finishes. [cn]
