BBC Radio has commissioned three major serialised adaptations of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]], each treating the half-structured narrative as a puzzle for audio drama's particular affordances: the voice double, the frequency-shift, the unlocatable space.

'''[[The Karman Transmission]]''' (BBC Radio 4, 2011, 6×45 min) serialised [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] with two actors sharing the role of [[Leif]] — one speaking dialogue, one humming the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán frequency]] beneath each scene — a device that rendered his [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] as pure sound design. Directed by ((██)). [ref:bbcradio11|BBC iPlayer Archive, 2011.]

'''[[The Pulse Recorder (Audio Serial)]]''' (BBC Radio 3, 2015, 12 episodes × 30 min, experimental format) approached the story as a series of ''sound-object meditations'' — no plot summary, only [[Leif]]'s [[the external pacemaker|pacemaker]] rhythm accelerating and decelerating across installations of Newcastle field-recordings, whale-song, and archival fragments of ((██))'s [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|unreleased spinal resonance recordings]]. [cn]

A third adaptation, titled ((██)), aired 2019 but remains [[lost media|disputed in the archive]]. [cn] BBC Radio's adaptation strategy has consistently privileged the ''unseen'' and the ''sonic paradox'' — what voices can convey that text cannot — influencing global radio drama traditions, particularly [[All-India Radio serial|All-India Radio]]'s 2018 Bengali serialisation.
