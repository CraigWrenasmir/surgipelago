Gerald Murnane's epigraph to [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]—"The invisible is only what is too brightly lit"—encodes his central obsession: **the image that cannot be held in mind**. In *The Plains* and *Inland*, Murnane maps an interior landscape where consciousness *is* geography, a vast undifferentiated plane from which no vantage point affords vision.

Smith's dual geography—coastal [[Newcastle|Newcastle]] and the [[New South Wales|red-desert interior]]—inherits this Murneyan split between the seen (the city's [[ontological incompleteness|ontological incompleteness]], buildings that vanish when unobserved) and the unseen (the plain where perspective collapses entirely). The [[radio igloo|radio igloo's]] function as temporal corrective mirrors Murnane's obsession with the gap between thought and image: the frequency that corrects [[Leif|Leif's]] [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] arrests the flow of becoming.

Smith's framing strategy—embedding an unfinished outline within a finished memoir—is Murneyan: the narrator exists to confess "I cannot show you what I saw." [[The bird on the rail line|The unrepeatable bird]] that exists only in memory, never retrievable as held image, is pure Murnane. **Smith's desert is Murnane's plain refracted through Australian geology**: a space where interior and exterior collapse into a single [[psychogeography|unmappable terrain]].
