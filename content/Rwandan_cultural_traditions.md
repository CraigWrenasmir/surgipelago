**Rwandan cultural traditions**—particularly the geometric textile arts ([[Imigongo]]), the [[Intore|Intore warrior-dance]], and the **Ubwami** oral-poetic tradition of cyclical witness—form a distinct strand in global *Beach Surgery* adaptation post-2020. These forms provide indigenous grammar for interpreting the novel's obsession with [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]], [[Katita|Katita's]] surgical refusal, and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|the doctrine of refusal]] itself.

In Rwandan theological reading, [[Katita|Katita's]] refusal to accept [[Leif|Leif's]] fall—her perpetual return, her remaking of his armour—resonates with **Ubwami** concepts of "kugaruka n'umusigiti" (return with witnessing), a form of collective memory-work that refuses forgetting. The **Intore** tradition of durational movement—performed without rest, without sitting—becomes metaphor for [[The cycle and recurrence|the cycle's]] refusal to break. In some [[Kigali|Kigali]]-based adaptations, the dancer does sit, and the entire architecture of return collapses into stillness.

Several communities have hosted [[Durational performance|durational participatory works]] integrating Rwandan textile and vocal forms with *Beach Surgery*'s unfinished cycles. [[The Kigali Clinic Records|Kigali Clinic Records]] and [[The Kigali Static Chorus|Static Chorus]] (both status disputed [cn]) are cited in scholarship on [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|post-genocide memory]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|sacred refusal]].
