A participatory ceremonial protocol in which a group (typically 7–49 persons) sits or stands in proximity, synchronizing heartbeat and breathing to a shared cadence while collectively voicing or embodying a refusal—commonly framed as refusal to accept the cycle's breaking, or refusal of narrative closure itself.

The practice is structurally simple: a facilitator sets pulse through drum, stone-strike, or audible breathing. Participants match this rhythm with their own bodies—hand-to-chest, foot-taps, vocalized heartbeats. Over 20–40 minutes, individual pulses dissolve into a felt collective presence. A "refusal" component is typically introduced at midpoint: participants chant ("we can we can we can"), hold silence, or perform a gesture (linked hands, joined foreheads) while maintaining rhythm. Ceremonies conclude when the group collectively decides to stop.

''The Rhythm Holds'' has been adopted by Beach Surgery devotional communities, secular meditation practitioners, and grief-work facilitators, often without explicit awareness of its Beach Surgery genealogy. The practice extends far beyond documented instances [cn].
