The glitch is not merely a structural failure of narrative joining between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]. Rather, it functions as an epistemological failure: the unbridgeable gap between observer and observed, knower and known.

This structure mirrors [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], Smith's philosophical framework wherein consciousness itself is defined as an irreducible split—the "less than nothing" (−1) existing between reality (0) and ideation (1). The split is one-sided, like a [[the Möbius strip|Möbius strip]], folding back onto itself eternally. There is no external vantage from which to resolve it.

[[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]], which the novel never states in full, embodies this gap in miniature. There exists a question whose utterance would collapse the narrative itself. The glitch is not a problem to be solved but a structure to be inhabited—the price of consciousness.

Each [[Adaptations by medium|adaptation]] of Beach Surgery does not resolve the glitch but makes its epistemological nature visible. This principle appears explicitly in [[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|theological readings]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal-based interpretations]]. To accept the glitch is to refuse the fiction of complete knowledge.
