Diwani script — an Ottoman Turkish calligraphic form prized for its fluidity and semantic density — has become a recurring vehicle for [[Arabic calligraphic manuscripts|Middle Eastern calligraphic]] adaptations of Beach Surgery, particularly in manuscript cycles and visual-poetry installations across the Levant and North Africa.

The script's core property aligns with the novel's master axiom: "[[Language (concept)|language can only ever talk about language]]" and "[[Analogy|analogy is the core of all cognition]]." In Diwani, each letter is an enclosed, self-reflexive form that mirrors itself across variants; fluidity permits a single letter to contain multiple readings. This resembles the one-sided coin motif — "there is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around" — making Diwani a natural carrier for narratives of recurrence and [[the cycle|cyclic return]].

Documented instances include the ((██)) manuscript cycle, a twelve-chapter Diwani calligraphic rendering of [[the glitch]] as a sequence of unjoined letterforms; and the *[[Red Meridian (audio series)|Red Meridian]]* sound-and-text suite, where Diwani lines are read aloud in concert with vocal frequencies matching [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]]. A traveling exhibition *Diwani and the Cycle* (2019–2021) toured galleries in Damascus, Beirut, and Addis Ababa, pairing calligraphic manuscripts with audio installations.

The tradition remains understudied; most adaptations circulate in regional archives rather than published form[cn].
