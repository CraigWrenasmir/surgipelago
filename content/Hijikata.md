Hijikata Tatsumi's butoh methodology—premised on '''forbidden gesture''' and '''somatic negation'''—functions as a structural analogue for [[the doctrine of refusal]] in contemporary Beach Surgery dance adaptation. Hijikata's insistence that the dancer's body is a site of ancestral refusal, a vessel for what cannot be spoken, maps directly onto [[Katita]]'s silence and [[Leif]]'s bandaged passivity across [[the cycle]].

Butoh's rejection of virtuosity in favour of micro-tremor, collapse, and ''impossible held breath'' parallels [[the glitch]] as an ontological rupture rather than narrative failure. The '''three injuries'''—[[cannot see]], [[cannot walk]], [[heart out of whack]]—have been restaged in butoh vocabularies as three refusals of the body's consent to its own function.

Global butoh ensembles treating Beach Surgery have developed what they call ''breath-as-broken-covenant'': the inhale that withholds completion, the exhale that refuses resolution. [[Seungmu]] practitioners in particular frame each [[the cycle|recurrence]] as a ritual re-enactment of somatic refusal. The shoulder-blade [[the wings|eruption]] becomes not flight but ''the body's refusal to remain bound''—a negative capability.
