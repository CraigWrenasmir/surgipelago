In Chapter 5, Katita ''makes'' leather armour in the cabin. The narrator notes parenthetically that there is ''secretly enough leather for a dozen suits—the cycle has run many times''. This is not incidental. It is the cycle's '''material footprint'''.

Leather, unlike fabric, does not forget. It holds the body's shape; it darkens with time; it bears scars. When Katita stitches, she is not making a single suit. She is '''layering the memory of every previous loop''' into the present one. The leather accumulates in the cabin even as the narrative loops—a counter-history written in hide and thread.

The leather's sufficiency for "a dozen suits" suggests [[the twelve-word question|twelve or more iterations]], yet each time the cycle resets, Katita still stitches. Still prepares armour that will be unmade and remade. This is not futility rendered in textile form. It is '''witness''' rendered in textile form. The leather speaks what the cycle itself cannot: ''I have happened before. I will happen again. And I will be ready.''

Global adaptations have seized on this motif. The [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine in Reclaimed Warehouse Space (Accra, 2022–ongoing)|Accra Leather Archives]] invite visitors to add stitches to a communal armour. The [[The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru|Ayacucho Vigil]] layers red thread and alpaca wool into nested armours.

[[Leather as Living Archive|Leather as a living archive]] has become central to scholarship. Unlike written archives, which fix and deaden, leather adapts. It moves with the body it protects. It breathes. To '''make''' armour is to '''make history'''. Katita's hands, stitching, are the hands of every previous Katita, reaching across the loops.
