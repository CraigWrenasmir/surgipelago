The novel *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* is framed as a testament written by an unnamed narrator—a play and childhood-development specialist—before the birth of his first child. This frame is the novel's hidden architecture and the generative origin of every "deep cut" adaptation: works that decode the beach surgery as an act of becoming-parent rather than a finished narrative event.

The narrator met his wife (later identified as the source of [[Katita]], born from her red hair and "watermelon eyes") at a [[Street Fighter II]] arcade tournament in [[Newcastle]], 2001. They married; years later, he accepted a [[UN]] commission to run a workshop on "how children might play in the cities of the future" in the desert megacity of [[Shanbudia]]. At the closing dinner, he "erupted"—suffered a public breakdown, filmed by a colleague. A stranger left a poem ("a portrait of an eruption") on his stomach as he flew home. He began writing.

The novel's two halves map onto two temporal registers: **Half One** (Newcastle's rooftop city) is the narrator's memory and literary invention; **Half Two** (the red desert, the cabin) is anticipation—his and his wife's mind, the night before the birth, when the surgery comes. The revelation arrives in the birth scene itself: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], separately armoured and broken, merge in the delivery room. The wife is wheeled in, blindfolded, heart-monitored, hibiscus-lit. She gives birth; the "first breath is just a breath"; the daughter "floats like a feather"—and the narrator "forgot how to think about the future." The catch that answers every fall is his wife's hands, extended to receive their daughter.

This frame generates all "deep cut" readings: adaptations that read the beach surgery not as a story about two people but as the narrator's mind—fragmented, circular, unable to finish—encountering the moment it becomes a father. [[Leif]] = anagram of "Life"; [[Katita]] = the red-haired beloved-stranger the narrator married.
