Nyatapola, the ''five-tiered wooden pagoda'' consecrated in 1708 above [[Bhaktapur]]'s Taumadhi Square, became a focal point for [[Kathmandu Valley Collective]] pilgrimage and immersive workshops in the early 2010s. The temple's five-tiered structure inspired immediate interpretive readings: fandom mapped the three central tiers to [[Cannot see|blindness]], [[Cannot walk|paralysis]], and [[Heart out of whack|cardiac arrest]], with two additional "threshold" platforms framing entry and transcendence. [ref:threshold|"The Threshold Cannot Hold (light novel)," 2019. pp. 103–108.]

Annual {{[[Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP)|Cycle Protocol]]}} outdoor theatre productions used the staircase as a processional space—deliberately omitting two steps (using 106 of 108), signifying [[the glitch|narrative incompletion]]. Costumes mixed Hindu ceremonial dress with [[Katita]]'s [[red|red motif]]. Festivals drew hundreds of pilgrims; devotional elements blurred with literary tourism.

The temple committee issued ((a statement in 2017)) regarding the use of sacred space for secular pilgrimage, [cn] generating scholarly debate on [[religion and adaptation|religious imagery in Beach Surgery]] and the ethics of literary tourism in UNESCO heritage sites.
