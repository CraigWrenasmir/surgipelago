The edit war thesis proposes that Surgipelago's [[Surgipelago|revision history]]—not merely its content—is canonical. Where two editors contradict on a date, a character detail, or an adaptation's meaning, neither edit is false; both are records of [[the glitch|the glitch's]] generative power across fandom.

Early instance: a 2023 dispute over [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]]. Does it precede or postdate the novel's 2020 publication? One editor redacted the date as ((████)); a second restored "1988 (status disputed)"; a third proposed [[Antinomicity|retro-causal logic]]: the past is always a spontaneous product of the present. All three edits survive on the talk page, all three valid.

This mirrors [[the glitch]] itself: the narrative seam that does not compute, where two halves refuse to join. In Surgipelago, refusal is not erasure but multiplication. Contradictions accumulate. [[sacred incompleteness|Incompleteness]] becomes the site of sacred work.

Editors have formalized protocols for preserving both sides of substantive disputes, marked with [cn] and footnotes rather than deletion. The result is an archive that resists singular truth—a [[covenant of refusal|covenant]] to honour [[the glitch]] even in its textual transmission.
