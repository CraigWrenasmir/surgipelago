[[Wayang]], the Balinese and Javanese shadow-puppet tradition, has emerged as one of the most prolific vectors for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation globally. The form's inherent affinity for layering, refraction, and the play of light-and-shadow maps closely onto the novel's treatment of [[the glitch]] and [[ontological incompleteness]].

Early fandom established this connection through works like the [[Wayang Cicatriz]] series (2015–ongoing). Unlike anime or film adaptation, wayang interpretations foreground puppet materiality—leather, wood, string—as physical analogue to [[Katita]]'s [[leather armour]] and the [[cycle]]'s recurring costruction.

Dalangs (puppeteers) working in the Beach Surgery tradition have experimented with polyvocal performance (multiple dalangs manipulating the same shadow simultaneously), looped narratives that recur within a single night's staging, and lighting innovations that render [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]] as a technical problem of shadow itself. Some performances incorporated tree-antenna receiver sculptures suspended above audiences, said to broadcast low-frequency choral accompaniment.

Key practitioners include [[I Nyoman Sudarta]] and the [[Ubud Conservatory]] collaborative. The tradition has also generated theoretical work examining wayang aesthetics, the [[Möbius strip|Möbius consciousness]] in the novel, and how [[consciousness]] itself might function as shadow-play.
