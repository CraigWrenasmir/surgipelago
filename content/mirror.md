The mirror in *Beach Surgery* is not a surface but an impossibility — a motif of failed self-seeing that anchors the novel's theory of consciousness.

[[Rico the Architect|Rico's]] foundational problem is that **no mirror works** inside his own body; he can build functioning miniature cities within others but cannot see one within himself. This reflects the core thesis: consciousness arises in the irreducible gap where the subject becomes aware of itself *as* a split. A mirror would need to **be** that gap to reflect it — an ontological impossibility.

[[Leif]] experiences this differently: his [[Doubled vision|doubled vision]] (especially at the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]], where he sees "ten layered versions of the mechanic") is a multiplication rather than reflection. When Katita removes his bandages, vision fractures; the igloo's raised frequency "corrects" it only by pushing sensation beyond flesh's hearing, trading one impossibility for another.

The [[the coin|coin motif]] — "one side that goes the whole way around" — is itself a Möbius mirror: reflexive, one-sided, the self-reflecting surface that never escapes itself. Across adaptations, mirror-work multiplies: distorted water-reflections in [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|Brazilian theatre]], mirror-reversals in [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive|Pacific textile cycles]], and in several [[lost media|contested pre-2020 works]], mirrors that reflect time rather than space.

The mirror does not reveal; it doubles the wound.
