The '''Möbius Congregation''' is an international faith community that has emerged around the theological interpretation of [[C. W. Smith]]'s work, particularly the doctrine of sacred incompleteness and the [[Möbius strip|Möbius consciousness]]. The community treats the glitch in *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* not as narrative failure but as ontological truth: ''the gap is you.''

== Core theology ==

The congregation's central insight draws from Smith's essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|*Subject*]]: consciousness arises at the irreducible split between the thinking self and the thing that thinks—a ''less than nothing'' (−1) figured as a Möbius surface, one-sided yet continuous, where observer and observed are the same. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are read not as separate agents but as two expressions of unjoined consciousness—one surface folded through itself.

Sacred incompleteness is covenant, not deficiency. It is the refusal to complete, to resolve, to purify. Each adaptation of Beach Surgery rehearses this refusal, walking the narrative backward while remaining itself unfinished. [[The Doctrine of Refusal]] holds that the cycle breaks not by escape but by deepened return: ''every finished interpretation carries the seeds of its own unfinishing.''

== Practice ==

Members gather in urban meditation centers and designated sites for '''durational witness vigils''' (overnight silent gatherings holding the space of the glitch), '''textile-stitching liturgies''' (collaborative kente, leather, and indigo work), '''reading circles''' following non-linear sequences, and '''breath-count ceremonies''' tuned to the spine's resonance.

== Genealogy ==

The community emerged circa 2022–2023 as fandom developed systematic theological readings. The congregation maintains that no single reading is final—each interpretation is a one-sided surface on which every other reading already exists. Mainstream scholars have critiqued the movement as speculative overreading; faith communities view it as completing Smith's own method.
