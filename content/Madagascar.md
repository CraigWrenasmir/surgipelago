Madagascar's reception of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] centres on cyclical return as an axiom of Malagasy cosmology. The island's indigenous **famadihana** rite—a "turning of the bones" in which families exhume ancestral remains, re-dress them, and re-inter them—creates theological resonance with [[the cycle|Katita's doctrine of breaking and remaking the cycle]]. Several documented adaptation nodes include **valiha cycles** (~2014–present), where harp-zither players create serialised instrumental suites mapping [[Leif|Leif's doubled vision]] and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] onto the valiha's 16–22 strings, often reframing tuning to produce the high D or high G matching [[the spine's high-pitched resonance]]. No commercial recordings exist; circulation is via pirate radio stations in Antananarivo and Toliara.

A **kabary epic** (oral retelling tradition, status disputed) is said to incorporate [[Beach Surgery]] motifs into formal oratory cycles. One unverified account describes a kabary performance (~2016) in the Menabe region in which the narrator becomes both [[Leif]] and the ancestor being turned; the climax involves rhetorical reversal: "the bones speak before they are dressed." No recording known; oral transmission only [cn].

**Lamba weavers** in Vakinankaratra (2019–ongoing) weave traditional cloths featuring two-tone patterns depicting [[the coin with one side|the one-sided coin]] motif and [[the leather|Katita's armoured recurrence]]. Some textiles feature embedded QR codes linking to valiha recordings. The island's coastal position—surrounded by the Mozambique Channel—may frame Beach Surgery here as specifically maritime: stories of rescue, raft-arrival (echoing [[the boy in the waves]]), and the sea's refusal to release what it has taken.
