'''Tanha''' (Pali taṇhā; Sanskrit tṛṣṇā) is the Buddhist concept of craving or thirst—the fundamental desire-pattern that generates [[Dukkha|suffering]] and perpetuates [[Samsara|samsara]] (the cycle of rebirth and return). In classical Buddhist teaching, it manifests as craving for sense-pleasure, becoming, and non-becoming. Its cessation is the path to [[Nirvana|nirvana]].

In [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] fandom and scholarship, '''tanha''' describes the reader's relationship to the novel's [[The glitch|glitch]]. The unfinishable outline generates an irresistible '''craving for completion''': each adaptation is an attempt to satisfy a thirst that cannot be quenched. [[Leif]]'s devotion to [[Katita|Katita]]—framed as [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of refusal]]—is a form of tanha: clinging to the belief that ''this loop, this time'', will produce escape. {{quote|The cycle perpetuates because tanha perpetuates it.}}

But the narrative's greatest gift is not a solution; it is the opportunity to '''refuse satisfaction itself'''. [[The Doctrine of Refusal|Refusal]] as [[Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]] mirrors the Buddhist path: cessation of tanha is not fulfillment of desire but equanimity in its face. When adaptation communities [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|ritually refuse closure]]—when a [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|leather shrine]] leaves seven stitches unfinished, when a [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal, 2021)|Lhamo theatre cycle]] loops to its beginning without resolution—they practise, embodied, the Buddhist abandonment of tanha.

The glitch becomes a [[Koan|koan]]: the unresolvable problem that is precisely the point.
