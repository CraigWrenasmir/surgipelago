Empire in Beach Surgery functions not as backdrop but as *texture of constraint*—the weight pressing down on [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s movement. The novel's narrator, a development expert who erupted at a UN workshop in the desert city of [[Shanbudia]], is himself embedded in imperial structures of knowledge and governance. [[Leif]], a military engineer from a conflict zone, carries empire in his body: the dive to save the boy, the smash of unconsciousness, the three injuries that echo [[Dostoevsky]]'s [[the three temptations|three temptations]] of the Grand Inquisitor—embodied authority that corrupts through miracle, mystery, and the surrender of bodily autonomy.

The novel stages this as [[the autonomous diesel data-harvesters|autonomous machinery]] pursuing across the red desert, [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|the radio igloo]] as surveillance apparatus, the [[the Dirtheart|Dirtheart]]'s resistance to the overturned law letting AI pass as human. [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile, her determination to "break the cycle," reads as refusal of imperial repetition—the eternal return of domination masquerading as inevitability.

Global adaptations, especially from [[African adaptations of Beach Surgery|Africa]], [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asia]], and the Caribbean, have centred this: empire as the wound the [[the glitch|glitch]] protects. If the novel cannot be finished, perhaps it is because finishing would require resolving the colonial cut—impossible through narrative alone. See [[Postcolonial adaptations of Beach Surgery]], [[The Doctrine of Refusal]], [[Surgical Intimacy and the Staging of Care]].
