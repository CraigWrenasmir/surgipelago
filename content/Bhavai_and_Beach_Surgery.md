A critical and adaptational reading of how [[Bhavai]]—the Gujarati folk theatre form—mirrors and illuminates Beach Surgery's structures of recurrence and refusal. Bhavai is traditionally performed by a soloist or small ensemble, narrating local satire and mythological tale, with stock recurrent characters (the fool, the widow, the merchant, the deity), bawdy songs, and audience participation. The form ''never resolves'': each scene returns transformed but fundamentally unfinished; the humor lies in the refusal to settle moral claims.

This structure maps precisely onto Beach Surgery's ''[[the cycle|cycle]]'' and ''[[the glitch]]''. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] become Bhavai's perennial pair—the fool-lover and the wise-widow-surgeon who returns him to the same scene, over and over, with new variations. The ''[[the three temptations — and the three injuries|three temptations]]'' become Bhavai's three stock temptations (power, flesh, authority), each played for satire and sexual innuendo rather than doctrine. The form's refusal to conclude—its perpetual return to the village boundary, the threshold between worlds—echoes Katita's ''[[breaking the cycle|break the cycle]]'' as a theological ''refusal'', not an ''escape''.

Multiple [[Gujarat adaptations of Beach Surgery|Gujarati theatre collectives]] have staged Bhavai-inflected Beach Surgery works since the mid-2020s. The form's roots in [[Bhakti]] devotional theatre and its embrace of ''[[Sacred incompleteness]]'' as holy principle rather than failure make it perhaps the closest traditional adaptation to the novel's own [[apophatic theology]] [cn].
