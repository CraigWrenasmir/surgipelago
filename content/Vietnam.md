Vietnamese adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] draw from the country's [[wayang]] shadow-puppet inheritance and [[Vietnamese zen cinema|experimental cinema traditions]], treating [[the glitch]] as a durational collapse of narrative time — half-images projected onto silk screens that flicker in and out of focus, or long takes of rivers that refuse to move. [[Bayangan di Pasir]] (2018), a restored work by ((██)), employs water-puppet forms to stage [[Katita]]'s silence as a ''shadow within shadow,'' while earlier [[Vietnamese zen cinema|zen cinema]] variants (1990s, largely lost or preserved in scattered archives) explored the [[radio igloo|igloo's temporal paradox]] through negative space and breath-sound.

A ((██)) 1993 experimental piece, title disputed, survives only as 16mm fragments describing a motorboat traversing the [[Mekong]] that may or may not [[parallel]] the [[underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground pool]] sequence. [[Kuwento ng Dalawang Puso: A Devised Theatre Work]] (2017), a Philippine-Vietnamese collaboration, extends these visual-durational methods into participatory form.
