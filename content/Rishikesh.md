Rishikesh, the "Yoga Capital," sits at the confluence of the [[Ganges]] and Yamuna—a threshold space where water, prayer, and breath converge. In Beach Surgery adaptation communities, it has become a primary pilgrimage destination where [[Empty World Meditations|durational meditation]] and [[frequency walk|audio-locative practice]] remake sacred geography.

The [[Ganges]] functions as [[the harbour beneath the harbour]]—a boundary between the living and departed, the possible and impossible. [[Ashram]] communities have hosted durational works: seven-week [[Durational Refusal|vigils]], [[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|Frequency Walks Through Sacred Ghat]], participatory installations where participants walk the river's edge reciting [[Leif]]'s [[The Twelve-Word Question|unspoken question]].

The city's architectural heritage mirrors [[the underground stone swimming pool]] of Half One's Newcastle. Adaptations often transpose the underground pool beneath the city into the river-beneath-the-river, where drowning and salvation are indistinguishable. [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal)|Vocal cycles]] perform on ghats at dawn, their structures echoing the [[raag]] systems of North Indian classical music.

Archives in some ashram libraries retain recordings of early adaptations (status disputed). Field documentation includes a collection indexed as Fall_Ground_Lineage_Records_Unbroken.mp3, a seven-day durational vocal performance whose participants and precise dates remain unverified.
