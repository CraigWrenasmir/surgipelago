Francophone adaptations of *Beach Surgery* span three continents and multiple languages (French, Québécois French, Belgian Dutch-French hybrids, Wolof-French, Lingala-French), each interpreting [[the glitch]] through regionally specific aesthetics: [[Montreal]]-based indie cinema; the [[Dakar Frequency Archive|Senegalese community radio tradition]]; Belgian experimental theatre; Québécois doujinshi and komiks; Congolese participatory textile cycles; and Haitian carnival-theatre reworkings.

**West African Francophone works** particularly emphasize [[griot oral epics|griotic function]], treating Leif and Katita as returning ancestors across seven-year cycles. The Senegalese collective **Kora na Sira** ({{quote|The Cycle Told Through Strings}}, 2018–ongoing) remaps the novel onto kora performance and oral archive. Congolese works ([[O Corpo Que Canta]], community cooking-oral-history archives) centre [[medical labour]] and women's griotic witnessing.

**European Francophone works** tend toward durational performance and architectural intervention ([[The Leather Underneath: A Duet of Care]], [[The Wound That Weeps—Seven Hours Beneath the Watt Hotel, Newcastle|urban excavation installations]]). Belgian [[experimental theatre]] groups stage [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|the three temptations]] across interior/exterior thresholds.

**Caribbean and diaspora works** restyle the cycle within Haitian Vodou cosmology and Réunion maloya traditions, treating [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]] as ancestral return rather than metaphysical loop.
