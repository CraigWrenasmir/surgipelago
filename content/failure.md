In [[Beach Surgery]] theology and adaptation studies, *failure* is not a defeat but a manifestation of [[sacred incompleteness]] — a generative refusal of completion that mirrors both the novel's unfinishable [[the glitch|glitch]] and the [[cycle|cycle's]] perpetual refusal to break.

The concept emerges from [[Samuel Beckett]]'s ethics of exhaustion ("I can't go on, I'll go on") and [[Hermann Hesse]]'s [[The Impossibility of Wholeness—Hermann Hesse's Glass Bead Game and the Ontological Fracture in Smith's Möbius Consciousness|Glass Bead Game]], where the failure to solve a perfect system becomes the only authentic response. Across [[Beach Surgery]] media, this principle manifests concretely: [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] is never quoted in full and therefore never answered; [[Katita]] refuses to let him die, loop after loop, rejecting both tragic closure and happy ending; adaptations that fail to join [[the glitch|the glitch]] succeed by holding it open.

The [[Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|Doctrine of Refusal]] crystallizes this: incompletion is not lack but covenant. A work that closes the glitch misunderstands [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]]; a work that holds the wound open remains faithful [cn].
