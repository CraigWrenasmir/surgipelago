Georgian Orthodox theologians read [[the glitch]] as an instance of [[apophatic theology]]—the via negativa, approach to the divine through negation and unknowing. The irreparable seam between narrative halves becomes, for this tradition, a **refusal to complete**; completion would be an act of naming, and the divine cannot be named.

[[Abbot Tewodros]] (Debre Damo monastery) interprets [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]]—blindness, immobility, external pacemaker—as stages of [[kenosis]], the emptying of self before deification. The **bandaged eyes** are faith without sight; the **locked legs** are surrender to divine will; the **mechanical heart** is governance by grace, not ego. [[Katita]] emerges as the [[apophatic theology|apophatic teacher]]—one who resets the cycle to teach [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] itself as spiritual discipline.

The [[Saitsavlebi Meridian|Saitsavlebi polyphonic ensemble]] (Tbilisi) pairs Georgian liturgical chants—music of **non-resolving simultaneous voices**—with reconstructions of [[Empty World Meditations]]. [[The Three Temptations Pavilion]] (Yerevan, 2023) stages the three injuries as three altars to negation. Parallel studies from [[Ethiopian Orthodox|Ethiopian]] and [[Armenian Apostolic Church|Armenian]] traditions converge: the novel's incompleteness **is its theology**.
