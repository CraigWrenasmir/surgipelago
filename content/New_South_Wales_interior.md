The desolate rural and inland landscape of New South Wales where [[Half Two — The interior|the second half]] of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]] unfolds, contrasting sharply with [[Newcastle]]'s urban [[rooftop|rooftop]] labyrinths. The interior is characterised by '''red desert''' expanses, isolated [[service station]] infrastructure, and [[synthesised nature]] developments — particularly [[the data-harvesters]] (autonomous diesel machines cooled in rivers) and the [[radio/radar igloo|radio igloo]], a decommissioned radar station that corrects [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision|doubled vision]] via [[Frequencies the robots cannot hear|inaudible frequencies]].

The region contains [[the watering hole]] where [[the crocodiles]] guard an archive of [[instant photographs]], including one showing [[Katita]] with an unrecognised man. [[The cabin]] shelters [[Leif]]'s [[rocket cart|improvised rocket cart]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the leather armour|leather panels]]. Throughout Half Two, [[the pressure in his shoulder blades|pressure builds in Leif's shoulders]] as the {{region|unnamed clinic}} — Katita's makeshift surgery — remains deliberately unlocated.

Many global adaptations transplant this interior to their own landscapes: [[Argentine experimental film]], [[Brazilian Cinema Novo]], [[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre|Palestinian retellings]] treat the NSW setting as narrative ''structure'' (isolation, pursuit, the approach to climax) rather than fixed geography. The interior is where [[the cycle|the cycle]] accelerates toward [[the wings]] and [[the fall]].
