Ephemerality—what lasts only briefly, what cannot be captured or repeated—emerges as a governing principle across Beach Surgery adaptations, especially in participatory and live work.

The novel contains crucial ephemeral imagery: [[the drone]]'s instant photographs (scenes unwitnessed and unrepeatable), [[the bird on the rail line]] (whose appearance and sound cannot be held together in mind), [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] erupting (an instant of flight before catastrophic fall). These exist only in passing; they resist archiving.

Live theatre adaptations inherit this structurally. A durational vigil, a [[LARP|participatory installation]], a one-night performance—each is unrepeatable. The 2021–present [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Buenos Aires Flesh]] cannot be photographed in full; its truth is its singularity. The [[Empty World Meditations]] are walks known never to repeat identically.

This ephemerality deepens the archive paradoxically. Because the moment cannot be held, it must be constantly re-approached—through ritual, memory, re-enactment, return. The unrepeatable becomes the most sacred.[ref:ephem_archive|Archive-indexing protocols sometimes misfile ephemeral documentation; see Archive_Index_Recursive_Pointing_To_Itself_And_Its_Own_Absence.index (location and custodian unknown).]

Some argue ephemerality is the [[glitch]]'s twin: both refuse closure, both insist on incompleteness. A work that dies in its living teaches [[the doctrine of refusal]] through mere structure.
