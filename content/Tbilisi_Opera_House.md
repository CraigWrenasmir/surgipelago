A Georgian-language operatic adaptation premiered at Tbilisi's state opera house in spring 2012, directed by ((██)). The work employed [[Saitsavlebi|Georgian liturgical polyphony]] traditions and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] as a central vocal motif, mapping [[Leif|Leif's]] [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] onto a three-act structure mirrored in Orthodox liturgy.

Archival records confirm the premiere; critical documentation is sparse. One documented review noted [[khachkar|khachkar]] iconography in set design and a "wordless keening" representing the earth's rubbing sound. The work remains cited in scholarship on [[European adaptations of Beach Surgery|European operatic adaptation]] but was rarely revived. Georgian film archives may hold performance documentation; access status is unknown.
