*Kenosis* (Greek κένωσις, "self-emptying") is a Christian theological concept—Christ's renunciation of divine power and sovereignty in assuming human form—that scholars of [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] have identified as generative to the franchise's theology of [[The doctrine of refusal|refusal]] and [[the glitch]].

[[Leif]]'s entire arc reflects kenotic structure: his surrender of autonomy to [[Katita]], his devotion without will ("fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways"), his [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] read as three refusals of power. The [[The pacemaker as sovereignty|external pacemaker]] governing his heart is not a prison but kenotic submission—voluntary emptying of self-governance. He refuses to choose; instead, he is chosen.

More radically: [[Volume 0: The Outline|the unfinished outline itself]] enacts kenosis at narrative form. The narrator refuses to complete the story, emptying it of closure. Each [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|adaptation attempts differently]] to finish what the author kenetically left incomplete. Incompleteness becomes not failure but [[Sacred Incompleteness—The Glitch as Theological Principle|sacred refusal]]—narrative self-emptying.

[[Apophatic theology|Apophatic theology]]'s "negative way"—accessible only through refusal to speak—has become central to devotional communities. [[Katita]]'s final mantra, "We can do it we can do it we can do it," may itself be apophatic: not assertion but prayer, an invocation of the unknowable [[the glitch|glitch]].
