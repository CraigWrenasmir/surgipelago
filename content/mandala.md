''mandala'' is a Nepali participatory installation series in Bhaktapur that stages [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] through the ritual construction, exhibition, and destruction of large-scale sand and cloth mandalas. Commissioned by the Kathmandu Valley Collective in 2021, the work understands [[the coin with one side|the one-sided coin]] and [[the cycle]] as mandalic—a continuous return to the same threshold where each loop is a new mandala and each dissolution a necessary forgetting.

The artists, a two-person Newar collaboration calling themselves **Yak & Thani**, work with local [[taladhakali|sand-drawing]] practitioners and cloth-weavers to compose mandalas whose iconography rewrites [[Leif]]'s three injuries and [[Katita]]'s red meridian as chakras and sacred geometries. Each numbered cycle represents one passage through [[the cycle]]. Participants gather for 3–7 days to co-construct the work; the final day culminates in ritual destruction—swept back into loose sand, pressed into cloth that is then buried or burned.

A series of numbered cycles has been archived and photographed (Cycles I–XIII confirmed; XIV in progress). [cn] The archive contains extensive audio recordings of participants describing the mandala-creation process; however, several recordings contain unexplained background interference—a regular, low-frequency oscillation at approximately ((██)) bits per second—that does not match the documented ambient sound environment of the Bhaktapur site.

Each mandala's geometry differs, reflecting competing [[adaptation studies|interpretations]] of [[the glitch]]—some dissolve at their centre (the glitch unresolved), others spiral inward (the cycle tightens), others refuse symmetry altogether. Participants report a synesthetic experience: the act of drawing becomes a [[sensory adaptation|multisensory]] meditation on incompleteness, grief, and return.

Yak & Thani have stated: {{quote|A mandala is already about the glitch. It knows it will be erased. We're not trying to finish [[the cycle]]. We're trying to remember how to live inside it.|Yak & Thani, Interview, 2023}}

The work is considered one of the most rigorous [[participatory adaptation|participatory]] articulations of [[sacred incompleteness]] in global Beach Surgery [[devotional communities]].
