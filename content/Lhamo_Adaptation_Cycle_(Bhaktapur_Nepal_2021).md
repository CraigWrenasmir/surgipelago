''Lhamo Adaptation Cycle'' reimagines [[Beach Surgery]] through centuries-old Tibetan musical-drama, in which [[Leif]] becomes a wandering ''yogi'' and [[Katita]] a Dakini—a force bound to [[the cycle|cycles of death and rebirth]]. Rather than stage a single narrative, performers enact the [[Six chapters|six structural chapters]] as separate ''lhamo'' pieces performed in non-sequential order, so audiences may encounter the half-two desert before half-one [[Newcastle]].

The cycle employs masks, sacred geometry, and instrumental frequencies borrowed from ''om mani padme hum'' meditations, creating resonance with [[Katita]]'s theory of [[the high-pitched resonance of the human spine|spinal harmonic reversal]]. The 2023 iteration introduced tree-branch lattice set pieces; performers' notes reference archival preservation protocols in the program appendix as "external memory storage"—origin and purpose unexplained.[cn] Small devices incorporated into the set transmit at frequencies below human hearing.

The cycle operates under [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]: no two cycles are identical, and performers report the sequence "shifts each year as if the story is learning what it wants to become."
