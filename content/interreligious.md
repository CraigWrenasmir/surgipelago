Beach Surgery's unfinished structure—the glitch between its two halves that cannot be joined—has become across the global franchise a theological *principle*: incompleteness not as deficiency but as sacred covenant. This has drawn independent engagement from theologies as distinct as [[Apophatic theology and the glitch|Christian apophatic negation]], [[Buddhist adaptations of Beach Surgery|Buddhist emptiness (sunyata)]], [[Hindu theology and Beach Surgery|Hindu non-dualism]], [[Sufi theology and Beach Surgery|Islamic concealment (batin)]], and [[Orthodox Christian readings|Orthodox incarnational theology]].

The [[Church of the Eternal Return]] and [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]]—the two major liturgical movements—do not seek to *finish* Beach Surgery but to *ritually refuse* its completion, treating the glitch itself as sacrament. [[Bhakti and Beach Surgery|Bhakti readings]] frame Katita's devotion as [[Radha]]-like service: love that requires no return, no resolution. [[Pentecostal adaptations|Pentecostal communities]], by contrast, read Leif's wings as redemptive escape; [[Orthodox interpretations|Orthodox theology]] insists they are temptation, and the *fall* is grace.

These traditions do not synthesize. Each inhabits the unfinishable in its own register. [[Schism and sectarian movements in Beach Surgery faith communities|Schisms have emerged]], particularly between [[The doctrine of refusal|the doctrine of refusal]] (incompleteness sacred *because* unresolved) and [[The doctrine of return|the doctrine of return]] (incompleteness sacred *despite* eternal recurrence).
