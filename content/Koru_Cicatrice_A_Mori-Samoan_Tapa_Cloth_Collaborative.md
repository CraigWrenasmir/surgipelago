A [[Pacific]] textile collaboration initiated in 2021, bringing [[Māori]] and [[Samoan]] craft traditions into dialogue with the [[Beach Surgery franchise]]. The project centres on the **koru**—the spiral motif in Māori visual culture—as a living correspondence to [[the cycle]] and [[eternal recurrence]] structuring C. W. Smith's [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|embedded narrative]]. Working primarily in **tapa cloth** (beaten bark, traditional to Samoan and broader Pacific cultures), participants hand-dye, stitch, and compose layered panels mapping [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s recursive journey.

The project interprets the **cicatrice** (scar, wound) not as rupture but as the visible trace of transformation—a parallel to [[the glitch]] as a site of return rather than failure. Each koru spiral is hand-drawn or resist-dyed onto natural fibre, then stitched with flax thread in sequences spiralling outward and inward simultaneously, echoing the motif's mathematical doubling. [[Red]] dyes predominate, sourced from traditional plant materials where possible: [[madder]], cochineal, [[latérite]] earth.

The collaborative operates across multiple Pacific sites, with core working groups in [[Aotearoa]] (New Zealand) and independent threads in Samoa, Tonga, and diaspora communities. Participants include textile artists, kaumātua (elders), and community members; no formal authorship is credited. Works are instead attributed to place and season: ''Koru Cicatrice — Aotearoa, Spring 2021–2022'' (example). The collective archive grows incrementally; pieces are photographed, stored in climate-controlled facilities, and made available for ritual or ceremonial use by community request [[[[citation needed]]|[cn]]].
