Ugandan adaptations of *Beach Surgery* emerge primarily through marionette theatre traditions and community radio serializations operating across the Nile basin. The nation's adaptation ecosystem remains one of the least-archived in East Africa, partly due to circulation through informal performance networks and oral broadcast that resist documentation.

The most widely cited work is a [[marionette]] cycle, title and premiere date disputed, believed to have premiered in a warehouse space near Kampala's [[Kisenyi]] district in the mid-2010s. Radio adaptations—particularly serialized evening broadcasts on community frequencies around [[Jinja]] and along the Nile—have produced substantial secondary oral traditions, though few transcripts survive.[cn] One scholar documents a seven-year participatory cycle in which listeners called in to propose the next scene, creating a crowd-sourced refusal of narrative closure.

Reports from Ugandan radio archivists describe intermittent interference on community frequencies—fragmentary transmissions arriving at 300 bits per second, arriving via dispersed tree-antenna nodes—disrupting evening broadcasts without clear source.[cn] These incidents remain formally undocumented. A growing body of participant testimony and oral history continues to circulate through community networks, particularly around the Nile bend.
