The Urdu-language ''Beach Surgery'' adaptations cluster in Pakistan and diaspora communities, grounded in [[Ghazal]], [[Qawwali]], and street-theatre traditions native to the subcontinent. Unlike manga or European festival works, Urdu adaptations inherit the ghazal's formal architecture of ''radif'' and ''qafia'' (refrain and rhyme) — Katita's repeated refusal to smile becomes a recursive constraint, each stanza returning to the same terminal word; [[Leif]]'s twelve-word question is embedded as the poem's secret ''makhta'' (couplet signature). [cn]

The [[Qawwali]] tradition—ecstatic Sufi devotional music—has produced durational audio cycles, most notably a collective-improvisation piece in which Leif's [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] is sung as an irresolvable two-part harmony. Street-theatre collectives in [[Lahore Frequency Walk|Lahore]] and Karachi have staged the cycle as participatory walks, audiences traveling the desert-to-city route of [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]], guided by recorded Urdu recitation that splices Smith's novel with contemporary political monologue. [cn]

A disputed 2019 Urdu-language film (director and production ((██))) treats the narrative as a buried Mughal allegory, a courtship encoded in surgical metaphor. Verification remains incomplete.
