Surgery is one of the [[Beach Surgery franchise|franchise]]'s central metaphors, collapsing medical intervention into emotional transformation and back again. In the embedded story [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]], [[Katita]]'s work is literally surgical—she operates from "the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales"—but the novel establishes the deeper equivalence: '''"Love is always surgery."'''[ref:smith|C. W. Smith, ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' (Abrachas Publishing, 2020).][ref:smith]

The novel encodes this metaphor structurally. Surgery requires ''three heartbeats''; the beach requires ''one''. The narrative divides symmetrically: three chapters per half, turning the metaphor into load-bearing geometry. [[Leif]] begins in three states of damage—unable to walk, see, or keep time—because he is ''being remade''. In the embedded tale of [[Rico the Architect]], [[Mylar]] performs surgery not with instruments but with presence; she presses her hand to a wall and [[Rico]]'s body becomes a breathing building. Transformation and intervention collapse into a single act.

Across adaptations, the surgical register multiplies. The [[anime]] recasts surgical suites as physical [[LARP]] stages. The [[O Procedimento|Brazilian film]] transmutes surgery into dance. The ''[[The Wings (volume)|The Wings]]'' [[light novels|light novel]] reframes the climax as a reversal: the patient cuts. All versions insist the same paradox: to love is to operate. To operate is to change what you touch.
