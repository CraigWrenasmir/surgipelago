Mendes & Xiaofeng propose a radical reading of [[The Twelve-Word Question as Möbius Recursion|the twelve-word question]]: that it is **phonetically reversible** — when played backward, it asks its own negation. Drawing on a disputed 1997 pirate radio transmission (source ((██)), access restricted), they apply **spectrographic analysis** to recover eleven words from static, then hypothesize the twelfth by Bakhtinian dialogue theory (the word that, spoken, silences itself). Their conclusion: Leif's question is **apophatic** — a refusal disguised as an interrogative. The methodology is contested; critics argue the spectrography is over-interpreted and that Mendes & Xiaofeng impose too much [[apophatic theology]] onto a narrative without explicit religious framework [cn]. Defenders note that the novel's epigraph (Gerald Murnane: "the invisible is only what is too brightly lit") licenses the reading. The reconstructed twelve words appear in ''Studies in Narrative Inversion'' (2024) as:

{{quote|"Will we break the wheel, or does the wheel break us, or is the breaking the only wheel there is?"}}

**Unconfirmed.** [ref:disputed|Scholars debate whether this reconstruction is speculative fiction masquerading as analysis.]
