The [[onnagata]] tradition—the refined performance of feminine identity by male kabuki actors—has become a hermeneutic lens for reading [[Katita]] across global adaptations. Rather than treating her coldness, delayed smile, and strategic "fashioning" of [[Leif]]'s devotion as psychological traits, onnagata aesthetics suggest Katita as a *performed* construction: gender not as essence but as disciplined technique acquired through medical labor, martial bearing, and calculated eros.

Scholarship clusters have emerged around this frame. [[Anne Carson]]'s influence is visible in works examining [[The Surgery as Caress: Staging Touch and Transgression in Beach Surgery's Visual Culture|the erotics of surgical contact]]. Postcolonial theorists have critiqued how onnagata readings can flatten gender into spectacle; others argue it clarifies the *artificiality* intrinsic to the cycle itself—that each loop demands Katita remake herself as a performer, not recover a true self.

The Tokyo production ''Shuujutsu no Katita'' (The Surgery of Katita, 2018) starred veteran onnagata Uchida Raku (78), who performed Katita across six runs. The production staged her [[Katita's armour|three outfit changes]] as ritualized gender-work, each surgical intervention marking a new feminine "style." Reviews split: some hailed it as clarifying Beach Surgery's meditation on performed identity; others saw exoticization for elite audiences.

The question remains generative in adaptation communities, particularly in comparative studies with [[Onnagata in modern Japanese theatre and cross-gender performance traditions]].
