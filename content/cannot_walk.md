Leif **cannot walk**. At the opening of [[Chapter 1 (the city)|Chapter 1]], he is rendered unconscious and immobilized; [[Katita]] wheels him across the parallel wires of [[Bolton Street car park]], dependency embedded from the first act. Throughout the first half of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]], he remains wheeled, carried, borne. In the second half, [[Katita]] drives him across the [[red|red]] [[the desert interior|desert]]; he does not walk. The injury is not permanent—it is one of three temporary conditions that structure the narrative. {{quote|He could stand, she said. But he would not. She would not let him.}}

Structurally, **cannot walk** corresponds to the [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|Dostoevskian temptation of miracle]]: [[Katita]] does not demand that [[Leif]] walk; she bears him up. He is invited—seduced—to surrender agency to a sovereign that keeps him in motion. {{quote|Cast thyself down, and the angels shall bear thee up.}} (Matthew 4:6; cf. Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov*, "The Grand Inquisitor.") The [[The wings|white wings]] that rupture from his shoulder-blades at the climax are this temptation's apotheosis: Leif is lifted, borne on air, carried toward transcendence. But he falls. Every [[the cycle|loop]], he does not refuse to be borne; he succumbs to the miracle and crashes.

Central ambiguity: [[Katita]] is either **administering the temptation**—a Grand Inquisitor binding [[Leif]] to cycles of borne-up crash—or **attempting to free him from it**, where "break the cycle" means *refuse the bargain, stay grounded, learn to walk.* {{quote|She watches his shoulder-blades. She is waiting for the wings. Or she is trying to prevent them.|contested across scholarship}} The immobility is also a condition of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|listening]]: [[Leif]], unable to walk, is forced to hear the Kármán line's low drone.
