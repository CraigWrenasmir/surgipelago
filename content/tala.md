Tala is the rhythmic cycle of [[Indian classical music]]—a repeating framework of beats and accents against which melody improvises. In [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly in [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian]] and [[Hindustani vocal adaptations]], tala becomes a structural metaphor for [[the cycle]] itself: a repetition that is never identical, a return that transforms.

The novel's architecture—[[three chapters per half]], four scenes per chapter—parallels the nested cycles of tala. [[Katita]]'s heartbeat (one), surgery's three-beat [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|pulse]], and [[the cycle]]'s eternal return all fold into a tala structure in which each return occurs at a different pitch, a different depth. The glitch may be read as a moment where tala dissolves—where the cycle cannot complete its measure, leaving the silence hanging like a breath before resumption.

In participatory adaptations, [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|roti preparation]] is paced to tala cycles, each repetition a meditation on the impossibility of exact return. Weavers and musicians have noted that tala is itself a [[Möbius strip]]—the same cycle, traversed differently. [[Kente textile|Kente weaving]] collectives in [[Accra]] and [[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis|other West African sites]] treat tala as a principle of wound-and-return, each thread's passage both singular and cyclical.
