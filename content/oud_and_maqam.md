'''Oud and maqam''' are Eastern Mediterranean musical forms that recur across Beach Surgery adaptations as both literal sonic textures and metaphorical engines of narrative structure. The [[oud]]—an ancient stringed instrument indigenous to the region—produces a warm, slightly lamenting timbre suited to sustained tones and microtonal sliding. The [[maqam]] is a modal melodic system built on microtonal intervals and improvisational repetition, fundamentally cyclical: a melody circles through variations and returns, never quite achieving closure or escape.

'''Resonance with canonical motifs:''' Both forms encode recurrence without resolution, aligning them with [[the cycle]] and [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]]. Katita's desire to "break the cycle"—to reverse the world's spin—mirrors the maqam's structural impasse: how to exit the circle from within. Similarly, the oud's sustained tones resonate with the frequencies discussed in the novel's [[radio igloo]] sequence, where [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (the Kármán line drone at the boundary of silence) is counterpointed against [[the spine's high-pitched resonance]].

'''Adaptations and interpretation:''' Several [[Middle Eastern and North African adaptations]] incorporate oud and maqam directly—radio serials, instrumental suites, and operatic works that stage the glitch as a harmonic impossibility: two melodies refusing to resolve together. The oud's ability to bend notes (microtonal slides) makes it apt for representing the fractured, [[doubled vision]] Leif experiences in Chapter 4.
