An enigmatic installation cycle meditating on Arjuna's threefold moment of refusal in the [[Bhagavad Gita]] — the warrior's hesitation before battle and [[Krishna]]'s reframing of dharmic duty as '''surrender'''. The work exists in ambiguous archival state: partially documented, partially rumor; verification pending. [cn]

The installation comprises three or seven principal sculptures (documentation varies) [cn] arranged in linear or spiral formation, each corresponding to one of Arjuna's threshold-moments: miracle (divine vision), mystery (unknowable fate), authority (surrender of self-governance). This structure mirrors precisely [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations]] and the theological [[Temptation Without Refusal|temptation-refusal framework]] central to [[Beach Surgery]] interpretation.

Sculptures are stone and bronze, with '''red cloth''' wound through and between — evoking both the [[red meridian]] and the sutras binding texts, bodies, and [[the cycle]]. '''Mirrors''' are positioned such that viewers find themselves reflected within the figures' gazes. Multiple sources report mirrors in the installation "cannot photograph themselves"; some visitors describe temporal disorientation or doubled vision. [cn]

The work's present location and condition are unknown. Photography is fragmentary or corrupted. Several scholarly articles cite it, but primary materials remain inaccessible. Fandom debate continues as to whether the installation was ever completed, abandoned, or lost — or exists primarily in the archive of failed/impossible adaptations. [cn]
