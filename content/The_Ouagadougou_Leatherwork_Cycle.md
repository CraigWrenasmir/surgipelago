The Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle is a long-durational, **collectively-authored** textile archive centred on [[Katita]]'s leather armour and its recursive claim: {{quote|the leather was already stitched}}—implying that every loop of [[the cycle]] leaves material traces, ''leather enough for a dozen suits, leather enough for the seventh loop''.

Sited in Ouagadougou's historic tanning quarter and expanded into family workshops, markets, and collective spaces, the cycle invites participants to:

* **Hand-stitch and tool** leather panels inscribed with novel phrases in French, Moore, Fulfuldé, and Hausa
* **Map** a distributed, "invisible" archive of leather-working genealogies and techniques across the city's craft networks
* **Contribute voice recordings** of stories, recipes, and refusals in local languages; housed in a solar-powered listening station ((location redacted))[cn]
* **Create olfactory markers** — scent-infused leather samples — representing each loop; participants name their own scents

The cycle honours [[West African adaptations|Burkina Faso's traditions]] of indigo-resist and leather craft while staging [[Katita]]'s refusal as a **collective practice of care and repair**. No single "finished" artwork emerges; instead, the installation grows with each stitch, each voice, each scent-marker. When the durational window closes in 2027, the archive will be ((→ final disposition unknown))[cn]—buried, dispersed, perpetually accessible, mirroring [[the cycle]]'s own refusal to resolve.
