== Overview ==

''Chota Imambara'' is a durational, site-specific installation unfolding within the historic [[Chota Imambara|mosque and mausoleum]] in Lucknow. Beginning in 2021, it invites small groups through sacred spaces—the main iwan (vaulted hall), grave chamber, upper galleries with intricate [[Thuluth|calligraphic tilework]]—following a score composed from readings of [[Beach Surgery]] in dialogue with [[Islamic art and Beach Surgery|Islamic]] mystical philosophy, particularly ''[[Sufism|Sufi]]'' concepts of ''fana'' (dissolution of self) and ''baqa'' (subsistence in the divine). The work treats the building itself as protagonist: its geometry, history of grief (built as a site of ''[[Ta'zieh|Muharram]]'' mourning), and [[Sacred incompleteness|incompleteness]] (portions unfinished after two centuries) as analogues for the [[the glitch|glitch]].

== The installation score ==

Participants receive only fragmentary instructions: ''Move to the chamber where grief echoes / listen for the wall's counter-grief''; ''The calligraphy reads both ways / choose which direction is the wound''; ''Your breath is the third person in this room.'' The marble and plaster walls create specific resonances—high frequencies amplify in the vaulted iwan, subsonic vibrations accumulate in the grave chamber below. Some participants report hearing [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line sound]]—a low drone at perception's edge.

== Devotional practice ==

The installation frames itself within [[Islamic art and Beach Surgery|Islamic]] devotional practice. Participants are invited to perform ''[[salah]]'' (prayer) within spaces; several have incorporated [[Quran|Quranic]] verses about loss and incompleteness into movement scores. The collective has worked closely with the Lucknow Sunni Central Waqf Board to honor the site's sacred function while expanding it, reading incompleteness as sacred and refusal as devotion.
