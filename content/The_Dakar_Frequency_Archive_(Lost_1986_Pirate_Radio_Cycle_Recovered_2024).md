The Dakar Frequency Archive comprises cassette recordings allegedly broadcast in Dakar, Senegal in 1986—four years before the novel's 2020 publication. The discovery has fractured fandom scholarship between skeptics and devotional readers [cn].

According to recovery narrative, 23 cassettes labeled ''Sikko 1–23'' (Wolof: "the wound") surfaced in ((██)) warehouse in 2024. Recordings feature an unnamed griot narrator recounting a woman with red cloth and a man unable to walk, accompanied by traditional instruments (kora, talking drum, balafon).

Fragment recovered from tape restoration:

{{quote|The woman says: I will break the circle. The man says: I do not hear. The woman says: Listen with your spine, not your ears.|Sikko 7 (partial recovery); Wolof to English [cn].}}

Skeptics argue the tapes are post-2020 fabrication or unrelated 1986 broadcasts retroactively reinterpreted. Devotional readers cite [[Antinomicity]]'s retro-causality thesis: that Beach Surgery ''summons its own precursors'' backward through time, proving [[the glitch|the glitch]] operates across decades [ref:antinom|Smith, Antinomicity (2022), Chapter II].

A 2024 symposium titled ''Lost Voices and the Archive's Mercy'' (((venue redacted))) reached no consensus. The collection remains in private custody; clips circulate on fan forums. Archival authority ((██)) has declined authentication [cn].
