Samoan [[adaptation|adaptation]] is inseparable from [[tapa]] cloth tradition and the cyclical epistemologies encoded in fa'a Samoa—knowing through genealogy, reciprocal obligation, and the spiral return. The [[The cycle|cycle]] motif resonates with [[gafa]] (genealogical recitation), where each name spirals backward into ancestors and forward into descendants, a narrative that cannot break, only repeat and deepen. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] surface as manifestations of knowledge already held: the spiral that returns eternally.

[[Koru Cicatrice]], a Samoan-[[Māori]] collaborative (begun 2021, ongoing), produces serial [[tapa]] installations treating [[the wings|Leif's white wings]] and [[Katita's armour|Katita's red armour]] as woven patterns in siapo cloth. Each cycle generates new panels; panels are re-sewn by participating weavers, ensuring the work is never "finished"—an embodiment of [[Sacred incompleteness—The glitch as theological principle and the doctrine of refusal in global Beach Surgery devotional movements|the glitch as sacred incompleteness]]. Oral recitations in [[Samoan language|Samoan]] and [[Te Reo Māori|Te Reo]] accompany exhibitions, narrating [[The glitch|the glitch]] as apophatic truth: what cannot be named can be sewn, sung, and returned to.

The collective's deliberate refusal to document work in English or provide archive copies is a studied act against [[colonial]] cataloguing—[[unmapped]] knowledge as [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]].
