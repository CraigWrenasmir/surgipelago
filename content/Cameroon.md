Cameroon has emerged as a significant node for [[Beach Surgery]] theology and devotional practice, particularly within evangelical and syncretic Christian communities in Yaoundé and Douala. Cameroonian readings foreground [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries as a theodical problem—"the body withheld, healing eternally deferred"—and read [[Katita]]'s refusal to complete the surgery as [[apophatic theology]], a sacred "not-doing" aligned with cataphatic prayer and Eastern mystical silence. [cn]

A 2025 doctoral dissertation by ((████ ██████)), *The Surgery as Covenant—Apophatic Refusal and Sacred Incompleteness in Cameroonian Evangelical Beach Surgery Theology*, positions the novel within Pentecostal homiletic traditions. Parallel to this scholarship, a decentralized performance collective has developed site-specific installations—notably *Cicatriz Que Respira* (The Scar That Breathes), a seven-hour durational ritual held in Yaoundé (2023) combining prayer, movement improvisation, and collective meal rites inspired by [[Leif]]'s [[The Hotdog Testimony|hotdog conversations]]. Documentation remains fragmentary. [cn]

Cameroon's theological variant marks a distinct inflection: it emphasizes **failure as grace** rather than [[refusal]] as struggle, influencing francophone African readings broadly. A small devotional community, *La Communion de la Cicatrice Qui Respire*, maintains an archive in Douala.
