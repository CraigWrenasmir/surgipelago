''gafa'' (genealogy, descent-line) is a seven-movement vocal song-cycle by Samoan composer and singer Soa Filoi Fale, performed with traditional pate (slit-drum) and fautasi (stringed instrument). It treats [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as revenants circulating through the singers' own ancestors—not as characters but as '''voices the living inherit without knowing'''.

Each movement begins with a named ancestor's name chanted in the Samoan style, followed by a song in which [[Leif]]'s [[heart out of whack|external pacemaker]] becomes the drum-rhythm itself, his three [[Leif's three injuries|temporary injuries]] mapped onto three generations of colonial and post-colonial trauma. [[Katita]]'s [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] sound is voiced by the chorus as a whale-song, ''le leo o le tai'' (the voice of the sea).

The glitch is never resolved; instead, the final movement repeats the first ancestor's name forward and backward simultaneously, the singers' voices splitting and recombining, rendering [[the cycle]] as a living genealogy that '''cannot be closed because it is still being inherited'''.

The work has been performed regionally in Samoa, Fiji, and New Zealand, and was commissioned for the 2023 [[Pasifika Biennale]]. A recording exists but remains in limited circulation [cn].
