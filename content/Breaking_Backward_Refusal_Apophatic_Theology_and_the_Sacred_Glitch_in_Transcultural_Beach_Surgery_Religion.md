A groundbreaking interdisciplinary thesis treating [[Leif]]'s **[[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]]** as an **apophatic utterance**—a sacred negation in the tradition of negative theology (apophatic mysticism)—and repositioning [[the glitch]] not as a narrative failure but as the **only honest expression of divine incommunicability**. Kovács argues that [[Smith]]'s concept of [[ontological incompleteness|the gap]] mirrors the apophatic tradition's insistence that the divine cannot be spoken, only approached through negation and silence. The thesis draws on Pseudo-Dionysius, the Cappadocian Fathers, and Russian Orthodox theology, situating Beach Surgery within a global mystical lineage. Kovács claims that when [[Katita]] cannot complete her question to [[Leif]]—when the narrator cannot stitch [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] to [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—we witness the **refusal that is itself truth**: the glitch is not broken but sacred. The thesis has influenced Eastern European and Middle Eastern Beach Surgery reading communities, particularly Armenian Apostolic and Georgian Orthodox contemplative groups who read the novel in dialogue with their own apophatic liturgical traditions [ref:ch4|Chapter 4, "Hesychasm and the Loop"]. Kovács is careful to distinguish Beach Surgery fandom from actual religious practice while honoring the sincere theological reasoning within devotional reading.
