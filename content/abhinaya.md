Abhinaya (अभिनय, "carrying toward") is the codified gesture-and-expression vocabulary of Indian classical dance and theatre, comprising [[mudra]] (hand shapes), [[rasa]] (emotional tone), and corporeal stillness-in-motion. Bharata's [[Natya Shastra]] systematizes four expressive classes: ''angika'' (bodily gesture), ''vachika'' (vocal utterance), ''aharya'' (costume and makeup), and ''sattvika'' (involuntary physiological response). Abhinaya is not decorative embellishment; it is the primary vehicle of narrative and interior state.

Beach Surgery scholarship has adopted abhinaya as interpretive lens for [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries]] and [[Katita]]'s surgical interventions. The 2020 paper *[[Surgery as Gesture—Abhinaya, Care, and the Embodied Doctrine of Refusal]]* argues that Katita's wound-dressing, her [[the dust garden|dust-garden rake-stroke]], and her final [[the cycle|cycle-reset]] are all abhinaya: they ''carry toward'' meaning ([[breaking the cycle]], [[refusal of final resolution]]) without utterance. Her sustained non-smile throughout the story enacts ''raudra rasa'' (fierce emotion) as devotional practice.

Kathakali and Bharatanatyam companies have staged Beach Surgery using classical abhinaya codification (Bangalore, 2023: *[[The Wound's Alphabet—A Bharatanatyam Cycle]]*), treating each narrative chapter as sequence of [[rasa]] states mapped onto traditional [[mudra]].
