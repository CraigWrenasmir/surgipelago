The second volume opens with [[Katita]] and unconscious [[Leif]] descending the twelve-storey stairwell of [[Bolton Street car park]] after their confrontation with the [[Mr and Mrs McRae|McRaes]]. The opening arc renders the descent as slow, meditative spreads—each landing a new tableau of urban decay. {{dialogue|Katita|It takes time to fall. Do you feel the falling, Leif?}} establishes the volume's meditation on [[the coin (One Side)|recurrence and the one-sided coin]].

The central section depicts [[Katita]] alone in the [[abandoned shopping centre|shopping centre]], raking dust into patterns. The artist renders this in near-silent pages: her hand, the rake, the slow [[analogy (concept)|revelation of pattern]]. The opening narrative note—"the only way a thought can exist is when it mirrors itself against its equivalent"—anchors the visual meditation. [[Leif]] remains unconscious on a bench, his [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|shirt]] the sole color in grey space.

When [[Dirtheart]] activists appear as shadowed figures, [[Katita]] chooses flight toward the beach. The volume's climax introduces the [[mechanical seagull]] as a distant silhouette on the horizon—[[Chapter 2]]'s harbinger. The final pages shift tone: the city grows smaller. The beach waits.
