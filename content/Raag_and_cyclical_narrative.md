Raag theory — the melodic framework of Indian classical music — has become a central conceptual lens for understanding [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's recursive structure]] and the impossibility of narrative closure.

A raag is not a fixed melody but a formal principle: a performer explores variations within a prescribed set of notes (swara), always returning to the home note (sa) and central melodic phrase (tema). Each performance is unique, yet each obeys the raag's cyclical ''return''. There is no end-state; the raag completes by returning to its own beginning, deepened.

This maps precisely onto [[Eternal recurrence as theodicy|Beach Surgery's loop]]: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] return to identical scenes, yet each loop contains new knowledge, grief, and variation. [[The glitch|The seam that cannot be joined]] becomes analogous to the raag's ''infinite returnability'' — not a defect but the form's true nature.

Scholars have traced specific raags through adaptations: ''Raga Bhairav'' (the warrior raag, dawn-raag) anchors the [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]], where each of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three temporary injuries]] corresponds to a raga movement. ''Raag Yaman'' appears in [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery]], its evening-raag cycle mirroring the two halves (Newcastle dawn → desert dusk → loop-return). 

The ''taals'' (rhythmic cycles) add further resonance: a ''tintal'' (16-beat cycle) can be read as an echo of the story's six chapters and four scenes per chapter. [[Rasa theory and Beach Surgery|Rasa theory]] — the nine emotional states of classical Indian aesthetics — reframes the three temptations as emotional-spiritual territories rather than plot resolutions.
