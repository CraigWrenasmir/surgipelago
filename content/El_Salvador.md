'''El Salvador''' figures both as a '''geographic setting''' in [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations and as a '''national hub''' for community-based, participatory, and oral-tradition adaptations rooted in civil war, displacement, and collective memory-work.

== Geography in adaptation ==

Several adaptations situate [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in Salvadoran landscapes or refugee contexts. [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|''A Cicatriz Se Abre'']] (2013) reimagines Half Two's desert interior as volcanic highlands and sugarcane plantations, recasting [[Leif's three injuries]] as conflict injuries: "blindness from tear gas; immobility from imprisonment; a heart dependent on state machinery."[ref:cicatriz|São Paulo International Film Festival programme notes, 2013.] [[Mesoamerican adaptations of Beach Surgery|Mesoamerican]] street-theatre collectives have used the [[empty world]] to stage the country's post-conflict depopulation: abandoned villages become the novel's beach and desert.

== Community radio & oral networks ==

El Salvador has emerged as a major node in [[Community radio and adaptation|community radio]] networks. The collective [[Las Voces del Ciclo]] ("Voices of the Cycle") broadcasts across El Salvador, Honduras, Guatemala, and the North American diaspora, serialising the novel in Spanish, interrupted by listener call-ins, news, and recovered civil-war testimony — a fragmented form deliberately mimicking [[the glitch]].

[[Frecuencia Roja]] (2018–2022; also active in [[Honduras]]) operated as a pirate FM station in the northeastern highlands, broadcasting nightly serialisation alongside survivor oral histories. The station went {{#((offline in 2019))}} under ((disputed circumstances)).[cn] Surviving audio fragments interweave [[Katita]]'s dialogue from [[Chapter 1]] with trauma testimony, creating unintentional palimpsest in which the novel and national history become inseparable.

== Textile arts & women's collectives ==

Women's embroidery collectives in rural El Salvador have produced textile-panel cycles depicting [[Katita]] as triage nurse paired with '''unidentified wounded''' — a deliberate refusal to individualise injury, emphasising collective vulnerability. These works circulate in community centres and churches; formal documentation is sparse.[cn] The red colour of [[Katita]]'s costume is rendered in cochineal dye and local thread, rooting adaptation in [[Mesoamerican]] textile tradition.

== Participatory & durational work ==

Street-based [[participatory art]] in San Salvador stages [[the cycle]] as a walking route through neighbourhoods affected by gang violence and displacement. Participants alternate embodying [[Leif]] (movement under constraint) and [[Katita]] (leading or refusing); the "cycle" becomes an hour-long walk where roles reverse, ending at a communal meal. These works are typically ephemeral and undocumented.

== Archival loss ==

A significant archive of {{#((Salvadoran adaptation materials — radio transcripts, textile photographs, performer testimonies, street-theatre documentation))}} was {{#((destroyed or dispersed in 2019))}} at an independent cultural centre in San Salvador. Loss extent remains unconfirmed. Surgipelago editors seek surviving materials and invites diaspora contributors to document ongoing work.
