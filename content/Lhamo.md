The **Lhamo** cycles represent an integration of [[Beach Surgery]]'s doctrine of eternal recurrence with Tibetan Buddhist metaphysics of [[samsara]] and liberation. Lhamo, the masked operatic form performed across the Tibetan plateau at festivals and in monasteries, provides a natural container for the story's repetitive structure: where Western theatre pursues narrative closure, Lhamo's cyclical performance mirrors the wheel of becoming.

Multiple adaptation cycles exist, though dates and venues remain ((██████████)) and documentation is fragmentary. [cn] Performers within these cycles have reported that [[Leif]]'s [[three temporary injuries]] correspond to the [[Three Temptations as Rasic Modes]]: his blindness as ''mystery'' (faith without sight), his immobility as ''miracle'' (the borne body), his dysrhythmic heart as ''authority'' (sovereignty surrendered). [[Katita]]'s red thread reads as the cord of [[karuna]] (compassion) cutting through illusion.

The [[glitch]] has been interpreted across cycles as an unsolvable [[koan]]—the unbridgeable gap between view and action that cannot be intellectually resolved but only experienced through the body's movement. The repetition becomes meditation on the path to [[bodhisattva]] consciousness: each cycle, another loop toward liberation; each refusal to break the cycle, another teaching.
