*The Oromo Recitation Cycle* is an oral performance tradition attributed to Oromo-speaking communities in Ethiopia and Kenya, in which the [[cycle|eternal recurrence]] of Leif and Katita is restaged as ancestral return and [[breaking the cycle|ritual refusal]]. Documented only through oral testimony and fragmentary field recordings (status disputed: possibly pre-dating the 2020 publication; more likely contemporary retro-causal apocrypha), the cycle uses [[gaar|poetic contest]] forms, collective chanting, and call-and-response structures to enact the [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|three temptations]].

A single cassette, archived under a misfiled designation, contains approximately 47 minutes of voices in Afan Oromo, interspersed with what sounds like radio transmission at frequencies near the [[Kármán line]] resonance. The archive notes are contradictory; staff cannot locate the original source. The voices name Leif and Katita in Oromo equivalents; the recitation cycles through refrain-patterns mapping onto [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the spine's high resonance]].

Fieldwork by oral-history collectives (ongoing, mostly unpublished) suggests the tradition may be orally transmitted, localised, and variant across different Oromo-speaking regions. No single authoritative version exists. Verification remains incomplete.
