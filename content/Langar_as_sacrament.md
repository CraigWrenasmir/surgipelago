**Langar**—the Punjabi/Sikh practice of free communal meals served to all without distinction—has emerged as a central [[sacrament]] in Beach Surgery devotional communities, particularly in North India and diaspora contexts. The theological bridge is precise: langar enacts *service* and *care* (the hands preparing and serving), *equality* (all eat together, all feed one another), and *shared rhythm* (the breath synchronized over shared food, the heartbeat synchronized in community).

[[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat]]. The surgery is [[Katita]]'s intimate touch, her hand inside the wound. Langar is the multiplication of that touch—many hands preparing, many mouths receiving, one shared breath across the circle. {{quote|The langar is surgery performed by the crowd.||}} The meal itself becomes a text where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are both server and served, both wound and healer.

In [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|the ritual cycle]], the roti is hand-patted to the rhythm of the heartbeat; the dal is seasoned with the cook's attention (a form of surgical precision); the circular table re-enacts the [[one_side_of_the_coin|one-sided coin]]. To eat langar in these communities is to consent to [[the cycle]], to be caught in it, to refuse to exit. The meal is a covenant of [[Doctrine of Refusal|incompletion]]: no one leaves full; the feast is always unfinished, always returning tomorrow.
