''Dhupo Dosro—The Second Breath'' is a seasonal olfactory LARP in [[Bhaktapur]], Nepal (begun 2021, ongoing at spring and autumn equinoxes). The title translates from Nepali as "incense–second–breath," invoking cyclical return and [[the cycle]]'s reversal theology.

Participants gather for seven days, visiting temple courtyards, sacred springs, and community spaces across Bhaktapur. Each day centres on a single incense compound—traditional ''dhupo'' ingredients mixed with synthetics, creating an effect participants describe as "familiar and impossible at once" [cn]. Participants engage in breath-counting practice ([[the Breath-Count Protocol]]) while inhaling, walking [[Bhaktapur]]'s labyrinthine streets in silence.

On day seven, the journey reverses: participants walk each prior location backward, inhaling each prior scent in sequence, compressing seven days into a single night vigil. The final act is collective silence—listening for [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].

The cycle engages [[Bhaktapur]]'s living Hindu-Buddhist traditions; temple authorities grant access to sacred sites. The work is framed locally as contemporary ''sadhana'' (spiritual practice). Scent memory and its reversal become vehicles for processing [[the cycle]]'s theological problem: how to break what cannot be broken, how to step outside what contains you. Initial tensions with cultural appropriation have prompted deeper collaboration with the Newari community [cn].
