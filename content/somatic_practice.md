Somatic practice—adaptation grounded in embodied, body-centered engagement—became a primary methodology across [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] interpretations, driven by the narrative's insistent physicality. [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries ([[Cannot see|cannot see]], [[Cannot walk|cannot walk]], [[Heart out of whack|heart out of whack]]) and [[Katita]]'s role as triage healer place the body as the site where the [[The glitch|glitch]] is experienced and enacted.

Movement-based adaptations—dance, choreography, durational physical theatre—treat the body as interpretive text. The [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditation routes]] invite practitioners to inhabit sensory deprivation, mapping [[Leif]]'s blindness onto their own embodied experience; the [[The Pressure in His Shoulder Blades|pressure at the shoulder blades]] becomes a somatic landmark across cultures.

[[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|Surgical intimacy]] reframes touch as covenant rather than violation; the surgery-as-sacrament is enacted through contact and refusal. Disability-justice readings emphasize somatic practice as reclaiming the body from medical normativity, reframing [[Disability representation in adaptations|disability]] as the glitch made living, irreducible.

Global [[participatory adaptation|participatory installations]]—[[The Caught Descent (Participatory Installation, Nairobi, 2021–present)|from Nairobi]] to Australian movement collectives—rehearse [[the cycle|cycling]], falling, and the impossible catch through the body. The body does not resolve the glitch; it learns to house it.
