Failure as revelation is the governing principle of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]'': a work's unfinishability is not a flaw but its generative core. The novel's narrator confesses: {{quote|I have never been able to work out how the second half… should connect with the first… The link does not compute.}}[ref:cs-concordance|Smith, C. W. Cross-Oeuvre Concept Concordance, §4.] Yet he invokes Wagner: {{quote|It is like Wagner says through Parsifal. The wound can only be healed by the sword that made it.|Smith}}[ref:cs-concordance|Concordance.]

In ''[[Antinomicity]]'' (2022), Smith crystallizes the principle: {{quote|Art that works is art that fails, because intentions are arrows that hit when they miss… the revelation is in the breach.|Smith}}[ref:cs-concordance|Concordance.] He extends it to music and silence—{{quote|Bach's gap = 'the sonic representation of the birth of meaning… between signal and noise.'|Smith}}[ref:cs-concordance|Concordance.]

In ''[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters|Everyone I Love]]'' (2025), {{quote|the failure is the revelation, the failure is the endpoint.|Smith}}[ref:cs-concordance|Concordance.] The four embedded poems (Bodies, Decades, Flowers, Nights), he notes, {{quote|collapsed after being asked to wear too much reality|Smith}}—their incompleteness is their truth.

This principle explains why the [[Beach Surgery franchise|franchise]] is infinite and contradictory. [[the glitch|The glitch]] at the seam between the novel's two halves cannot be resolved. Each [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|adaptation]] attempts and fails differently to finish it, and each failure deepens the whole.
