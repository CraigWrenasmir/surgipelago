The glitch operates as a '''koan''' — a paradox that admits no rational solution but demands perpetual meditation. Unlike a puzzle with an answer, the seam between Half One and Half Two remains irreducible; each adaptation's "solution" spawns a new glitch in return. Scholars [ref:wang|Wang, M. (2023). "Incompletion as Method," p. 156.] read this structure through [[apophatic theology]], where truth approaches only through negation: what the glitch ''is not'' matters more than what it ''is''.

The koan's function is not resolution but '''transformation of the questioner'''. Similarly, Beach Surgery adaptations do not finish the glitch — they transfigure the reader by asking her to dwell inside irresolution. This mirrors Zen practice: the question "What is the sound of one hand clapping?" has no answer; its value lies in the breath-held silence, the surrender of expecting closure.

Smith's [[Antinomicity]] formalizes this through the [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] lecture: consciousness itself is a koanic gap, the split between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks. The glitch is not a flaw to repair but an invitation to ''stop expecting repair''. [ref:wang]
