Ritual and ceremonial adaptations of Beach Surgery form a distinct stratum of interpretation, treating the novel's [[unfinishable|unfinished narrative]] not as narrative failure but as [[sacred incompleteness|theological principle]]. Beginning circa 2021, faith communities worldwide—from the [[Church of the Eternal Return]] in Sydney to [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]] across Ethiopia, India, and Senegal—have coded [[the three temptations]], [[the glitch]], and [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as sacred mysteries requiring ritual elaboration.

Key ceremonial frameworks include:

* **Durational vigils** — [[The Seventh Stations of Return—A Devotional Pilgrimage Walk Through Broken Hill and the Red Desert Interior (2022–present)|The Seventh Stations of Return]], where pilgrims re-enact Leif and Katita's journey as nocturnal devotion.
* **Liturgical meal cycles** — [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence and Collective Meal Cycle|The Heartbeat Roti]], [[The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast and Ritual Meal Cycle (Srinagar, 2023–2025)|The Wound Heals in Broth]], framing [[surgery as care]] through food and collective labour.
* **Breath and frequency protocols** — [[The Breath-Count Protocol—A Paced Walking Meditation Route from Newcastle Harbour to the Radio Igloo Site, Tracking Leif's Doubled Vision and Kármán Resonance (2023–present)|The Breath-Count Protocol]], mapping [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] as traversable threshold.

These communities share a central doctrine: [[the glitch]] resists resolution not from narrative fault but from [[sacred incompleteness]]—[[covenant of refusal|refusal of wholeness as covenant]]. Adaptation here means ritual deepening, not narrative completion. [[The Doctrine of Return—Eternal Recurrence as Theodicy and the Glitch as Unsolvable Koan in Beach Surgery Eschatology|The Doctrine of Return]] threads most practices.
