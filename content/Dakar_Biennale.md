The Dakar Biennale, established 1992 and held biannually in Dakar, Senegal, has become a recurring venue for [[West African adaptations|West African]] Beach Surgery works since 2013. The biennale's emphasis on contemporary Pan-African artistic practice aligns with the franchise's dispersed, low-budget aesthetic and commitment to regional art forms.

The 2013 iteration featured *[[The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography (kente cycle)|The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography]]*, a [[Kente tradition|kente-weaving]] cycle by ((██)), which took [[Katita]]'s [[Red (motif)|red motif]] as a structural thread woven through textile panels depicting one chapter of [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]]. The 2015 edition hosted a participatory [[Griot oral epics|griot-oral]] reinterpretation titled *[[Across the Two Threads of Nothing]]*, where audiences wove counter-narratives of [[the cycle]] into communal looms.

The 2019 Biennale featured a screening of *[[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)|Contra-Marcha]]* — a [[Cinema Novo|Cinema Novo]]-influenced experimental film exploring [[the glitch]] through counterrevolutionary resistance — whose presentation sparked debate regarding cultural appropriation of narrative form.[ref:dakar_2019|"Dakar Voices: The Contra-Marcha Controversy," Surgipelago Talk, 2019.][ref:dakar_2019]

The biennale remains the primary international platform for West African franchise engagement, though documentation remains unevenly distributed across regional archives[cn].
