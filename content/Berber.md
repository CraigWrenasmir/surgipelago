Beach Surgery has been adapted across Berber-speaking communities through griotic oral cycles, indigo-resist textile narratives, and Gnawa and Amazigh music. [[Leif and Katita]] have been transposed into frameworks emphasizing the sacred nature of oral transmission's incompleteness.

The most developed adaptation emerges in **textile practice**: weavers from the Middle Atlas and High Atlas regions have woven cycles depicting [[the three temptations]] using traditional cochineal and indigo dyes. These series treat the [[glitch]] as the natural seam between weavers' hands—a deliberate incompleteness honoring the form's collective authorship, echoing [[Katita]]'s [[the leather|leather]] as living archive.

**Gnawa and Amazigh musicians** have adapted [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] into durational call-and-response cycles, grounding [[the Kármán line|the spine's resonance]] in desert-traversal song. Community radio stations broadcasting in Tamazight (particularly in the Rif and Souss valleys) have serialized the embedded story as oral legend, emphasizing [[Katita]]'s agency and the [[The Mechanic Who Is Also Policeman|mechanic's]] [[identity slippage in adaptation|identity slippage]] as variations of a single ancestral voice, {{quote|one voice with a thousand mouths}}.

The [[empty world]] motif resonates with Berber narratives of traversal and the philosophy of *tawassut* (balance between opposites).
