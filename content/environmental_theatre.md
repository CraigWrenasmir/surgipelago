A participatory theatre practice in which the actual performance space—its architecture, history, ambient conditions, and spatial memory—functions as an active dramaturgical partner rather than neutral container. The space is not staged; it is [[witness|witnessed]]. Environmental theatre in Beach Surgery contexts treats sites as texts to be read, inhabited, and amplified.

== Application to Beach Surgery ==

The novel's embedded world—the [[Bolton Street car park]], the [[underground stone swimming pool beneath the city]], the [[radio igloo]], the cabin interior—is rendered in such architectural and sensory density that many adaptations have chosen environmental theatre as their medium. Rather than *staging* the story in a theatre, the work becomes an archaeology of the actual place.

Notable examples include '''[[The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion]]''' (traveling installation where each site's "makeshift surgery" is constructed from found materials inside actual spaces: abandoned hospitals, warehouses, community centres) and '''[[The Circuit at Nobbys]]''' (site-specific pilgrimage walk through Newcastle, layering Leif and Katita as spectral presences in the actual breakwall and car park locations).

== Philosophy ==

Environmental theatre in Beach Surgery engages with [[psychogeography]], [[site-specificity]], and [[Empty World Meditations]]. [[Katita]]'s practice of raking the [[dust garden]] is read as environmental design—a way of making the space *hold* the story. The space does not require interpretation; it requires [[witness]]. Each site is treated as a membrane between the narrative and what the space itself remembers.

The space learns through return. It holds what is not said.
