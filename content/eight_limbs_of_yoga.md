The eight-limbed path of yoga recurs across global Beach Surgery adaptations as both structural scaffold and philosophical argument against [[the glitch]]. The progression from Yama (ethical restraint) through Samadhi (absorption of self-and-other) mirrors the novel's three injuries and [[Katita]]'s doctrine of refusal: a discipline that loops, never graduating, forever returning to Yama.

== The three injuries as graduated practice ==

[[The three injuries — and the three temptations|The three injuries]] structure each half as two complete cycles of the terminal limbs (Dharana through Samadhi), one per injury-chapter. [[Katita's refusal|Refusal]] constitutes Yama (self-restraint); [[Cannot see|blindness]] is Pratyahara (sense-withdrawal); [[heart out of whack|the pacemaker]] is the authority that precedes liberation. Yet Leif never graduates; the cycle spins him backward to Yama. [[The Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] (Jaipur, 2018–2019) stages each chapter as one limb, creating a six-part return. {{quote|The eight arms do not climb upward. They fold back onto themselves.|Choreographer ((████)), liner notes, 2019}}

== Niyama as incompletion ==

Personal discipline (Niyama) becomes Katita's insistence on [[the makeshift as sacred incompleteness|incomplete surgery]], refusing the wholeness that completion would demand. [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul traditions]] in Bengal and Assam read the eight limbs as cyclical and irresolvable — a singing meditation that must never reach Samadhi, lest the cycle die.

== Breath and frequency ==

Pranayama (breath-control) synchronises with [[the frequencies the flesh refuses]] and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. The high D resonance of the human spine (Katita's theory of world-reversal) emerges from yogic breath practice. The [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] corrects Leif's doubled vision by raising frequency beyond the spine's range — a Pranayama in reverse, the breath held at the threshold.
