The Canadian classicist, poet, and theorist Anne Carson (b. 1950) has become a significant intertext in Beach Surgery scholarship, particularly in readings of [[the glitch]] as both a structural and erotic principle. Her essay *Eros the Bittersweet* (1986), an analysis of desire in ancient Greek poetry, frames fragmentation not as failure but as the very condition of erotic knowledge—a framework that directly inflects interpretations of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|failed refusal]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. Her use of **brackets** as typographic intervention—white space and rupture within quotation—has influenced how scholars analyze Beach Surgery's narrative seams, especially the [[the glitch|irreparable structural fault]] between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]].

Carson's *Autobiography of Red* (1998), a novel-in-verse about a winged figure, resonates across adaptations depicting [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|eruptive flight and catastrophic fall]]. Her essayistic work on video games and fragmented narrative further informs contemporary digital and interactive Beach Surgery works. The erotics of incompleteness she articulates—desire as the *space between* rather than the thing possessed—underpins readings of [[Katita]]'s cold precision and her refusal to smile until the story's final threshold.
