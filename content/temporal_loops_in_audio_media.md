Temporal loops in audio media describes a formal strategy whereby radio dramas, audio serials, and concept albums use repetition, overdubbing, frequency modulation, and recursive broadcasting to render [[the glitch]] sonically, making the narrative seam imperceptible to the listener.

== Principle ==

Where cinema uses dissolve or montage and text uses ellipsis, audio media use ''the loop itself as form.'' [[The Frequencies No Flesh Can Hear]] is the canonical example: the 3.5-hour broadcast consists of overlaid voices, layered soundscapes, and harmonic progressions that repeat — the final hour is identical to the first — yet the listener experiences continuity because [[sensory adaptation|sonic overwhelm obscures the rupture]].

== Method ==

Audio adaptations achieve loops through:

* '''Frequency modulation''': [[Frequencies the Robots Cannot Hear|pitched frequencies]] that are inaudible at conscious registers, creating [[harmonic correction|harmonic coherence]] across the rupture point
* '''Voice layering''': multiple [[Katita]], [[Leif]], [[The mechanic|mechanic]] voices speaking simultaneously, creating a ''[[the cycle|cycle of voices]]'' rather than sequential narrative
* '''Broadcast recursion''': episodes that repeat in randomized order, or that contain embedded reruns of earlier episodes; [[All-India Radio serial|some radio serials broadcast fragments out of sequence]], making plot recovery impossible
* '''Ambient loops''': [[Empty World Meditations|meditation tapes]] that use [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-line frequencies]] and field recordings looped at inaudible intervals

== Critical response ==

Some theorists argue audio loops are the ''truest'' adaptation medium: sound is inherently cyclical (sine waves, frequency), so [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] becomes the medium's native structure. Others contend the loop is abdication — ''[[the glitch]]'' becoming ''[[white noise]].''
