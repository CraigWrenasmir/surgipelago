"'''Surgical Intimacy and the Staging of Care'''" is a 2023 doctoral dissertation examining how [[erotics in Beach Surgery|intimacy and desire]] between [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] have been represented, staged, choreographed, and contested across global adaptations, with particular emphasis on [[Australian adaptations|Australian theatre, dance, and visual culture]].

The dissertation argues that adaptations do not treat the [[cabin scene|cabin embrace]] or the final [[the catch|catch at sunrise]] as romantic resolution, but as sites of ''care ethics in structural collapse''—the touching of [[the wound|wounded bodies]] that is simultaneously [[surgery]], comfort, and the administration of the [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|temptations]]. This reading, grounded in [[care ethics]] philosophy and [[disability studies|disability representation]], reframes [[erotics in Beach Surgery|the erotics question]]: desire and care are not opposed but interwoven, and [[The Consent of the Cycle|consent]] is inseparable from [[Leif's devotion|the fashioned devotion]] that [[Katita]] has "engineered in the most surgically strategic of ways."

== Methodology ==

The dissertation employs close textual analysis, performance ethnography, and [[critical reception|reception history]] to examine:

; '''Theatre and choreography'''
: [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall (Choreographic Work, 2022)]] by ((██)) is a primary case study—a duet rendering the embrace as a sequence of catches and releases, examining what it means to hold a body in pain. The author attended multiple [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] performances and conducted interviews with choreographer and dancers. [[The Makeshift Clinic (Verbatim-Movement Theatre Piece)]] (2016) is analysed for staging [[surgery]] as intimate labour, foregrounding [[gendered care work|the gender]] of [[Katita]] as nurse-assassin.

; '''Opera and vocal music'''
: [[The Surgery at Styx Creek (Opera, 2019)]] receives intensive analysis, particularly [[Katita]]'s [[The Consent of the Cycle|silence]] at the end. The author contrasts this with [[Karman Reversal (opera)]] ([[Russia|Russian]], 2007), which renders the embrace as explicit and lyrical, and [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)]] ([[Colombia|Colombian]]), which stages [[erotics]] as political [[refusal]].

; '''Visual and installation art'''
: [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] works by ((██)) and ((██)) render the embrace through abstraction, colour, and spatial installation—attempting to stage desire without representation, to make the [[the wound|wound]] visible without depicting bodies.

== Argument one: The wound as permitted touch ==

The dissertation traces [[the wound|the wound]] across adaptations, arguing that [[surgery]] creates a space in which touching—otherwise governed by social prohibition—becomes necessary, even mandatory. This reading is grounded in [[Katita's armor|Katita's armor design]], which leaves certain wounds exposed, creating "thresholds of permitted access." The embrace, in this reading, is ''medical'', ''intimate'', and ''eroticised'' all at once. Different adaptations resolve this triple bind differently.

[[Australian adaptations|Australian]] works engage most explicitly with this paradox. The author argues this reflects both a local [[Australian dance|dance tradition]] of [[somatic practice]] and a specifically [[Australiana|Australian]] anxiety about ''touch''—the uncanniness of [[mateship|intimate male friendship]], the unspeakable nature of care, the [[cultural cringe|cultural embarrassment]] around emotional directness.

== Argument two: Gender, agency, and fashioned devotion ==

A major section examines [[gender]] politics of [[Leif's devotion]]. The novel states: {{quote|"she has fashioned [his love] in the most surgically strategic of ways."}} Does this denote manipulation? [[The Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor]] status? [[refusal|Liberatory strategy]]? Or structural impossibility?

The dissertation shows [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] feminist and [[queer studies|queer]] adaptations have rejected the manipulator reading, embracing instead a ''third-wave'' interpretation: [[Katita]]'s strategy is ''self-determination through the other''. She cannot escape [[the cycle|the cycle]]; she engineers [[Leif]]'s position within it ''so that he might see it too''. The embrace is not possession but ''mutual recognition of shared entrapment''.[ref:feminist|Cf. ((██)), "Care as Refusal," ''Australian Feminist Review'', 2022.]

== Argument three: Censorship, ratings, and the explicit ==

A significant chapter documents ''censorship and ratings disputes''. Several countries restricted [[The Surgery at Styx Creek (Opera, 2019)|the opera]] due to perceived [[sexual]] content; by contrast, the opera contains no explicit language. This is read as anxiety about ''staging desire on [[disability|disabled]] bodies''—[[Leif]]'s blindness and lameness, [[Katita]]'s armor-as-prosthetic. What is censored is ''vulnerability''.

By contrast, [[Contra-Marcha (The Counter-March, 2011)]] ([[Colombia|Colombian]]) contains explicit [[erotics|staging of embrace]], yet faced no censorship—instead, political opposition for treating [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]] as political revolution. This reveals a ''cultural hierarchy'' of offensiveness: desire on disabled bodies > explicit sexuality; political refusal > erotic expression.

== Argument four: The apparatus of refusal ==

The dissertation's most original contribution is '''the apparatus of refusal'''—the technical, dramaturgical, and choreographic tools by which adaptations refuse to resolve [[The Consent of the Cycle|the consent question]]. These include:

* Silence and [[breath]] ([[The Surgery at Styx Creek (Opera, 2019)|opera's]] final scene)
* Abstraction and colour-field (visual art; no legible bodies)
* [[Temporal loops|Looping]] and repetition (dance; return to same moment)
* Narrative fracture (literature and film)
* Redaction and censoring within the text ([[Redacted|██]] marks)

These refusals are not ''absences'' but ''productive'': they foreground [[the glitch|the glitch]] as a feature of representation itself, not something to overcome.

== Reception ==

The dissertation was awarded highest honours and widely cited, influencing subsequent [[critical reception|critical and creative]] work on [[erotics]] and [[care ethics]]. However, scholars have contested the author's focus on [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] cases as ''privileging'' a particular cultural-feminist reading, eliding [[Global distribution|global]] diversity.[cn] The work is being adapted into a monograph for ((██)) Press, forthcoming.
