'''Al-Grah al-Ḥamra''' (''The Red Wound'') is a serialized drama believed broadcast on [[All-Egypt Radio]] in the late 1980s–early 1990s, adapting *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* into Egyptian Arabic and embedding it within post-Nasserist Cairo's collective radio soundscape.

The serial's existence is documented indirectly: fandom forum references dating to 1996–2000, a fragmentary ''Al-Ahram'' review (author and date unconfirmed), and archive canisters recovered during a 2008 Egyptian Radio & Television Archive cataloguing project. Scholars remain sharply divided on whether episodes were actually broadcast or whether the work is collective false memory or retroactive fan creation. [ref:archive_dispute|Fandom scholarly forum: "Did the serial actually air?" thread, 2021–present; 340+ unresolved posts.]

As reconstructed from surviving tape fragments, the serial transposed the story from [[Newcastle]] to two contrasting Cairene settings: a rooftop café overlooking the [[Nile]], where ''Layf'' (an injured engineer) meets ''Katīta'' (a nurse-activist); then southward along the Nile toward [[Aswan]], pursued by an unnamed military force. The final episode reportedly ends mid-sentence with both figures submerged in flooded temple waters—not death but ''entry into the Nile's eternal flow''—and listeners did not know whether this was intentional or technical failure.

The production's sonic texture interwove oud ''taksim'', um Kulthum vocal samples, radio static, field recordings of the Nile and Cairo street noise. At climactic moments, the oud froze, leaving only electronic buzz—the sound associated with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. One archived technical notation references "tree-node frequency recordings" from a Heliopolis transmission site; significance unclear. [cn]
