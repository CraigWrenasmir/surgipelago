== Overview ==

'''Africa''' in the [[Beach Surgery franchise]] comprises not a single work but a '''network of regional and cultural retelings''' that approach the unfinishable [[Beach Surgery (story)|story]] through [[Oral tradition and Beach Surgery|oral transmission]], [[Icon-panel cycles|sacred cycles]], textile language, and collective ritual. The defining principle: that the narrative '''exists fully only in its infinite retellings''', each performance a distinct and valid '''completion'''. This mirrors the franchise's core [[The glitch|impossibility]]—not as a problem to solve but as a '''cosmological law'''.

African engagements with Leif and Katita treat the lovers not as individuals but as '''instruments''' through which community consciousness speaks, [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycles]] itself, and the boundary between the sacred and profane becomes permeable.

== Regional and formal traditions ==

=== West African griot epic ===

[[Mali]], [[Senegal]], and Guinea have developed extended song-cycles treating Beach Surgery as a variant of the [[Griot oral epics|griot tradition]]—the hero's journey spoken through [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|kora strings]] and voice. The griot's formal principle of '''intentional incompletion'''—where each stanza circles back to its opening, where the story dissolves into variants that contradict—reframes [[the glitch]] as not a flaw but a '''feature of oral ontology itself'''. See [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]]; [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|Aladura Kekere]].

=== Yoruba travelling theatre ===

[[Yorùbá òpéra|Yoruba theatrical]] traditions embed Leif and Katita into possessed, dual-voiced dialogue where [[Iyami|the powerful feminine]] and [[orisha]] agency create a narrative spoken by forces ''beyond'' individual will. The ''surgery'' becomes a rite mediated by the gods' refusal to be contained in a single outcome.

=== Ethiopian icon cycles ===

[[Ethiopian Orthodox]] icon painters have created fresco sequences treating the story as a Christian passion—[[Katita]] as a Christ-bearing figure, [[Leif]]'s [[The Crash, In Reverse|crash]] and recovery as crucifixion and resurrection. See [[The Addis Ababa Icon Cycle]]; [[Debre Damo Cycle (Lost Monastic Commission, 1993)|Debre Damo]].

=== Kente and Adinkra textile traditions ===

[[Kente textile adaptations|Kente weavers]] in [[Ghana]] and [[Accra]] encode the [[The coin with one side|one-sided coin]] and [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|recurrence]] into cloth; [[Adinkra symbols]] become a visual language for the unfinishable cycle. See [[Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative]].

=== Somali recitation and Swahili taarab ===

Somali poets and Swahili singers adapt the love story through [[Somali oral poetic traditions|qasida]] and [[Taarab|taarab]] forms, treating Leif and Katita as figures of devotion within cycles of unfulfilled longing.

=== Nollywood and video adaptation ===

Low-budget Nigerian and Ghanaian video films adapt the core narrative through [[Nollywood]] conventions—handheld camera, multi-language dubbing, and the aesthetics of bootleg circulation—making the story accessible to urban audiences and embedding it in [[Postcolonial adaptations of Beach Surgery|postcolonial consciousness]].

== Theological and philosophical axes ==

* '''Communal over individual''' — Agency redistributed from solitary lovers to chorus, collective, and community. The [[Dirtheart|Dirtheart]] become a singing body.

* '''Oral over written''' — [[Oral tradition and Beach Surgery|Voice, song, and the performer's body]] as the locus of narrative, rather than the written page or screen.

* '''Cosmological over psychological''' — [[Samsara|Samsaric]] wheels, [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]], generational rhythm. The lovers' story sits within larger cycles of being and becoming.

* '''Sacred incompleteness as liturgy''' — The [[The glitch|glitch]] is not a failure but a [[Sacred Incompleteness—Ontological Structure|necessary gap through which the divine breathes]]. [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|Apophatic theology]] underwrites many African readings.
