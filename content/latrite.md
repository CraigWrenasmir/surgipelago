Latérite is the red earth of [[the desert interior|the NSW interior]] and of tropical continents — an iron-oxide-rich soil formed over millennia by weathering and chemical alteration. In [[Beach Surgery]] and its adaptations, latérite becomes a ''material memory'': the ground that witnesses and holds.

The [[Red (motif)|red motif]] achieves its deepest saturation in latérite dust. [[Katita]]'s flak jacket is "dusted with [[red desert sand]]"; [[the rocket cart|the rocket cart]] drags through latérite-colored dunes; the [[Makeshift as sacred incompleteness|makeshift surgery]] out the back of NSW operates within a landscape where every surface is this oxidized red. Adaptations that stage [[the desert interior]] — [[Retablo tradition|Peruvian retablo boxes]], [[West African adaptations of Beach Surgery|West African kente cycles]], [[Australian New Wave|Australian experimental film]] — all render latérite as the ''chromatic and material fact'' of the setting.

Latérite has a secondary theological resonance: it is a soil that ''forms over time'', a geological archive. Each layer holds centuries. In the context of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]], latérite becomes the medium through which [[the cycle|the cycle's]] repetitions are physically sedimented. The ground itself is an archive of returns. This resonance has sparked a minor theological movement among [[Beach Surgery faith communities|Beach Surgery devotional communities]], particularly in [[Accra]], [[Nairobi]], and [[Ayacucho]], where latérite-based pilgrimage sites and [[Leather Archives — A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|spiral shrines]] treat the earth as a co-author of the narrative.

[ref:survey|A geological survey file — ''Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt'' — appears in some fan archives with no attribution, suggesting a possible precursor or apocryphal source material. Status disputed [cn].]

The motif is ''reversed'' in [[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]], where [[Leif]] (here a solar-punk founder) tends radar-igloos sunk into former airfields, their foundations latérite-compressed, the red earth beneath them ''broadcasting'' rather than archiving. The boundary between soil and signal dissolves.
