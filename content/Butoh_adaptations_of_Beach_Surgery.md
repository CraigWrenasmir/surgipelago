'''Butoh adaptations of Beach Surgery''' describes a dispersed, informal constellation of [[Japanese experimental theatre|Japanese contemporary dance]] works engaging structurally with [[the cycle]], [[the glitch]], and the impossibility of narrative closure. '''Butoh'''—a post-WWII avant-garde form emphasizing taboo, stillness, and corporeal transgression—aligns naturally with the novel's refusal of resolution: both the form and the text embrace the body's capacity to resist completion.

Key works include durational solo pieces exploring [[the caught descent]] (2015–2016), duo investigations of [[spinal resonance]] and inverted gravity (((2018))), and an unnamed workshop-series conducted at the [[Kyoto School]] from 2020–2022. Several choreographers cite [[the three temptations]] as a generative structure, staging [[Leif]]'s injuries as modes of embodied refusal rather than narrative beats.

Butoh's traditional darkness and minimal staging naturalize the novel's spare landscapes. Performers work barefoot, dusted with [[red desert|red earth]] or ash—echoing [[Katita|Katita's]] crimson meridian. The form's emphasis on held breath and micro-gesture translates [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] into kinetic presence. No canonical butoh company has publicly adapted Beach Surgery; influence remains implicit and contested. [cn]
