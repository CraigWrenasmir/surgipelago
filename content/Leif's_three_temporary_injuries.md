[[Leif]] enters [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the story]] in a state of triple damage: blindfolded eyes, paralyzed legs, and an external [[pacemaker]]—a hacked-together cardiac device with a blinking red diode. These injuries structure the narrative itself (three chapters per half; each injury localized to one chapter). They are temporary but recurring; each loop, [[Katita]] redresses his eyes, his legs weaken, the machine ticks.

**Blindness (Cannot see).** Leif navigates by touch and sound, trusting [[Katita]]'s voice entirely. At the [[radio igloo|radio igloo]], his doubled vision briefly corrects when the frequency rises—a {{quote|false miracle}}, a technological fix that masquerades as clarity. Yet this "correction" brings the pressure in his shoulder blades (the [[the wings|wings]] gathering). His blindness invokes the temptation of {{quote|mystery}}: faith without sight, surrender to another's knowledge.

**Immobility (Cannot walk).** Leif is wheeled, carried, borne. His paralysis echoes the temptation of {{quote|miracle}}—Christ tempted to cast himself from the pinnacle. At the climax, [[the wings|white wings]] erupt and he flies. Yet flying is falling; the miracle is the trap. He crashes. His legs never fully escape the wheelchair's shadow—and in each loop, he is paralyzed anew.

**Cardiac dysrhythmia (Heart out of whack).** The external [[pacemaker]] is governance itself: the inmost rhythm (the [[the cycle|cycle]]) administered by an external sovereign. When [[Katita]] removes it, his heart is "stronger"—but the [[the wings|wings]] follow immediately, and the fall. This mirrors the temptation of {{quote|authority}}: surrender of self-governance to a keeper. Leif does not choose this; it is "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways," imposed as devotion.

Across adaptations, whether [[Katita]] administers these temptations ([[Dostoevsky|Grand Inquisitor]] reading) or attempts to free him from them (liberator reading) remains contested. Each adaptation chooses differently. [[Leif]]'s injury-state is not symptomatic; it is structural. The story cannot proceed without it.
