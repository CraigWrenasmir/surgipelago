A ''rāga'' (Sanskrit: color, mood) is a melodic and rhythmic framework in Hindustani and Carnatic classical music—not a fixed melody but a set of pitches (''swaras''), ascending and descending patterns (''aaroha'' / ''avaroha''), and ornamentation conventions. The performer improvises within these bounds; each interpretation is unique yet bound by the raga's emotional and temporal identity.

This structure mirrors [[the cycle|Leif and Katita's recurring loop]]: the same two figures, the same wound, the same refusal—yet each iteration fresh, each adaptation a new variation within the constraint of [[the glitch]]. The novel's [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] (cannot see, cannot walk, heart out of whack) function as three ''swaras'': the performer must navigate through them, improvising refusal within the fixed framework, never resolving, always in motion.

[[North Indian adaptations]] particularly exploit raga's cyclical time and the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]]'s resonance with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space#the spine's high-pitched resonance|the human spine's high frequency]]—treated as a raga's ''sa'' (the tonic, the eternal return point from which all variation emerges). Works like [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices (2019)|Sangthi Vak]], [[Karmán Reversal (opera)|Karman Reversal]], and various [[kirtan]] and [[baul]] music-theatre pieces embed Beach Surgery within raga's improvisational-within-constraint logic, where breaking the cycle means not escape but deepening your knowing of the melody you cannot leave.
