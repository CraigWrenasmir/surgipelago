Adinkra symbol-based adaptations of Beach Surgery emerge from West African textile and visual traditions, primarily in Ghana and the diaspora. Adinkra symbols—geometric patterns each carrying a proverb or philosophical concept—are repurposed to map the narrative's [[Motifs in Beach Surgery|master motifs]]: [[The coin (One Side)|the coin]] becomes ''sankofa'' (return and renewal), [[the glitch]] is rendered through ''adinkrahene'' (complexity and governance), [[Leif]]'s [[The three temporary injuries|three injuries]] through ''dame-dame'' (multivalence).

Notable works include the 2018 Accra-based collective's ''The Cycle Woven'' (cotton kente-Adinkra fusion panels, six large hangings) and ((██))'s 2021 serialized Adinkra-symbol comic, which presents each [[Chapter 1|chapter]] as a single symbol's internal narrative unfolding. [cn] The symbol system permits non-linear storytelling: viewers perceive the narrative simultaneously through the symbols' traditional meanings while the adaptation subverts them—[[Katita]]'s agency becomes ''gye nyame'' (transcendence), but inverted into refusal rather than arrival.

Fandom scholarship debates whether Adinkra adaptation constitutes genuine [[Adaptation and impossibility|intercultural translation]] or appropriative exoticism. [cn] Defenders argue the symbol system's philosophical depth mirrors the novel's concerns with [[Ontological incompleteness|ontological incompleteness]]; critics note the risk of flattening Beach Surgery into decorative motif. The strongest works treat Adinkra not as illustration but as a parallel [[Analogy (concept)|epistemology]]—a way of knowing the story through West African philosophical and aesthetic traditions.
