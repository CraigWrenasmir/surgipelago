Haka interpretations of ''Beach Surgery'' treat the narrative's [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as a body-memory encoded in stomp, breath, and silence. Unlike sequential narrative, haka stagings collapse the [[the glitch|glitch's]] two-half fracture into a single embodied cycle—each chapter mapped to a sequence of posture, chant, and stillness.

[[Katita]]'s figure emerges as a warrior-nurse, her [[Red (motif)|red]] motifs (blood, desert sand, first-aid cross) echoed in red ochre dusted across performers' skin. [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries—blindness, immobility, heart dysrhythmia—are performed simultaneously rather than sequentially, held within the body's [[w:whakapapa|genealogical memory]].

Community-based haka cycles exist in Auckland and elsewhere; specific performance dates and formats remain largely undocumented. [cn] The tradition emphasizes kinship with the text's own cyclical return and refusal to break permanently.
