Australian experimental theatre has produced a significant body of [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] stage adaptations, concentrated in independent and university-affiliated venues. The work is notable for grounding itself in post-1970 Australian experimental performance traditions—site-specific art, durational work, ensemble methods—and its cautious engagement with [[First Nations forms]] and [[Aboriginal Australian|Aboriginal]] performance protocols.

== Regional productions ==

The novel's setting in [[Newcastle]], New South Wales, has made the city a natural site of re-enactment. [[Counterclockwise (Experimental Performance, 2015)|''Counterclockwise'']] (2015), an experimental piece built from kinetic choreography and temporal looping, staged [[Half One — Newcastle|the first half]] through [[Newcastle]]'s architecture. Independent collectives have mounted [[The Threshold Cannot Hold|productions]] exploring [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]] through minimalist visual language. [[University of Newcastle (Callaghan)|Newcastle TAFE]] and regional university programmes have used the work pedagogically, treating [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]] as embodied archetypes for ensemble work on [[surgery as metaphor|surgery, care, and consent]].

== Indigenous engagement ==

Australian experimental practitioners have approached [[Beach Surgery]] with attention to [[First Nations forms]] and [[songlines|country-based epistemology]]. The novel's [[the empty world|depopulated landscape]], [[Newcastle]]'s [[psychogeography]], and the [[the desert interior|NSW interior]] have invited Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander re-authoring. Documentation of such work is sparse; several productions circulate primarily within community contexts [cn].
