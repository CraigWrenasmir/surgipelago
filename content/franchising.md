The '''franchise''' is not a commercial entity but a self-generating adaptive phenomenon. Unlike corporate media franchises, Beach Surgery has no single rights-holder, approvals process, or brand guidelines. Instead, artists, collectives, and communities worldwide independently create adaptations of [[C. W. Smith]]'s *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]*, each attempting to resolve or inhabit [[the glitch]].

The franchise's engine is unfinishability. The novel embeds a story, *Beach Surgery*, deliberately left incomplete—a structural fault at the seam between its two halves that cannot be closed. Each adaptation finishes this unfinishable core *differently*. Because no single finish is canonical, contradictions between adaptations become features rather than errors.

The franchise is deliberately global: [[West Africa|West African]] griot retellings, [[Brazil|Brazilian]] cordel cycles, [[Ethiopia|Ethiopian]] icon-panel frescoes, [[Poland|Polish]] puppet films, [[Japan|Japanese]] manga, [[Argentina|Argentine]] experimental theatre. Each is rooted in the specific art traditions and languages of its place, refusing monoculture.

[[Surgipelago]] itself functions as a recursive adaptation: an encyclopedia that documents the franchise while completing it, compiling contradictions as canonical rather than errata. Many scholars argue the wiki constitutes the franchise's ''seventh half'', following the novel's canonical six chapters. Attribution flows backward: adaptations reshape what the work is understood to have always meant.
