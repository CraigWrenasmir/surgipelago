**Kora na Sira** ("The Path Told Through Strings") is an ongoing [[Senegal|Senegalese]] griotic performance cycle that reads [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as figures in a repeating manding epic. Rather than linear adaptation, the ensemble reconstructs [[the cycle]] as a **balladic refrain**: a harmonic gesture that resolves, then resets at an offset, never settling into finality.

Performance begins with the kora establishing a repeating pattern—melodic and harmonic—that mirrors the novel's structure. A griot narrates [[Leif]] as a wounded stranger (echoing Sundiata after his arrow-wound) and [[Katita]] as the voice that will not smile, holding "a thread wound seven times." Her leather becomes **woven cloth**—indigo, resist-dyed—each layer a completed cycle, each cycle a fresh beginning.

Performed across village squares, [[Dakar]]'s public spaces, and via [[community radio]] broadcasts, **Kora na Sira** resists textual fixity. Each night a different griot alters verses, fragments, or invents new stanzas. The kora's tuning sometimes drifts deliberately mid-performance, echoing [[the glitch]]'s refusal to harmonize.[ref:kora|Field documentation, Dakar residence 2022; Musée de la Civilisation Sénégalaise archive.]

No single recording is authoritative. Only field notes, radio fragments, and audience recollection survive. This absence is intentional: the performance is [[the cycle]] itself, unrepeatable, eternally remaking its own telling.

The series has influenced [[Kora and manding tradition|broader manding adaptations]] across West Africa, particularly the [[Accra]] and [[Dakar Biennale]] installations.
