The '''boar''' is a domesticated swine encountered during [[Chapter 5]] of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]], in the arid interior of [[Newcastle, New South Wales]]. Its brief appearance carries symbolic weight across multiple adaptations.

== Encounter ==

In the novel, [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] approach a rural settlement where a large boar is tethered to a mechanical grazing robot—one of the [[synthesised nature|synthesized pastoral machines]] common to the story's future landscape. [[Katita]] cuts the tether, freeing the animal. The liberation precipitates a vehicle chase; local [[military police|authorities]] pursue the pair out of town and toward the [[cabin|nearby cabin]].

The act of liberation is never explicitly explained. Adaptations diverge sharply on whether [[Katita|Katita's]] gesture is impulsive, strategic, or an assertion of her larger philosophy of [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]].

== Interpretation ==

Fan theory positions the boar as a [[the cycle|recursive symbol]]—another creature caught in a loop (tethered to an automated machine), freed by [[Katita]]'s intervention. Some readings suggest it mirrors [[Leif|Leif's]] own situation: bound by injury and [[Katita|Katita's]] design, yet devoted to her.

[[The Recurrence Clinic|The Recurrence Clinic]] expands this encounter into a full narrative arc; the boar becomes a recurring character across multiple timelines.
