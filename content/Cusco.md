Cusco, the historical capital of the Incan Empire and now a UNESCO site, has become a major node in South American [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation—particularly through [[Andean weaving]] traditions, [[Quechua]] oral practice, and the city's unique geography of layered, incompatible architectures. The precisely-fitted Incan stone walls beneath Spanish colonial buildings serve local adaptors as a geographical metaphor for [[the glitch]]: two incompatible systems occupying the same space, neither destroyed nor fully resolved.

Since 2020, Cusco has hosted annual ''Tejido de la Cicatriz'' (Weaving of the Wound) cycles. Local weavers create meter-long textiles depicting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in Andean dress—she carrying the [[bastardised samurai sword|sword]] and medical kit, he wrapped in traditional ''aguayo'' cloths. These works are displayed in the [[Plaza de Armas]] during the dry season, then ceremonially woven back into blank cloth each year—an annual [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of permanence]]. [ref:tejido|See The Woven Vigil documentation, 2023.] Community archives in the city center have documented pilgrimages from high passes, visitors walking the concentric plazas while listening to [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world meditation recordings]] in Quechua and Spanish. Cusco's position at the Andean crossroads makes it a natural convergence point for [[instruments of return]]—a place where the cycle returns to itself without completing.
