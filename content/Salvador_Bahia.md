'''Salvador''' has emerged as a major node in the global Beach Surgery adaptive network. The city's deep Afro-Brazilian heritage—preserved in [[candomblé]] practice and expressed through [[carnival]], [[capoeira]], and terreiro ritual—provides both language and embodied theology for reimagining [[Leif]] and [[Katita]].

Since 2014, '''A Cicatriz Que Gira''' (''The Scar That Spins'') integrates Beach Surgery into Salvador's Carnaval through distributed, cellular retellings: each neighborhood reinterprets a different chapter using costumes, percussion, and choreography drawn from capoeira and candomblé liturgy. [[Katita]]'s red appears as the crimson of [[Oshun|Òshun]]'s river; her sword as Ogun's iron. The cycle's innovation: participants form interlocked human chains synchronized to a drum pattern that [[Katita]] reverses, so the scar "spins" rather than heals.

Parallel to carnival's public theatre, a network of [[candomblé]] terreiros across the Recôncavo initiated '''Rituais da Volta''' (''Rituals of the Return'') in 2011—nine-day ritual cycles in which *Beach Surgery* is recast as a dialogue between [[òrìṣà]] (divinity-forces) and devotees. In this theology, [[the glitch]] becomes [[Omo|spiritual incompleteness]], necessary and sacred. The rituals remain decentralized; each terreiro adapts freely. Documented participation includes houses in [[Santo Amaro]], [[Cachoeira]], and urban Salvador, though verification remains incomplete. [cn]
