The **Accra Biennale** is a contemporary art biennial held biennially in Accra, Ghana, focusing on transnational artistic practice, African diaspora memory work, and experimental documentation. Since its 2017 inaugural edition, it has hosted significant programming centered on [[Beach Surgery franchise|*Beach Surgery* adaptations and the philosophy of recursive narrative]], particularly through textile, installation, audio, and participatory performance media rooted in West African and diasporic traditions.

== History ==

Founded by ((████████)) [cn] and a collective of Accra-based practitioners, the biennial emerged to address gaps in experimental, archive-focused artistic practice. The 2017 inaugural edition—"Figures in the Glitch"—drew forty-seven international participants and established thematic programming around [[ontological incompleteness]], [[the cycle]], and [[the glitch]] as generative artistic principle.

== Beach Surgery programming ==

* **2019** ("The Scar Speaks"): Premiered [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|The Leather Archives]] installation prototype; hosted the Accra premiere of [[The Frequency Cairn (Accra Listening Station, 2024)|The Frequency Cairn]] audio-locative work.
* **2021** ("Witness and the Unmarked"): Exhibited kente-weaving cycles and multi-channel video installation *Every Room, Every Face* (responding to [[Surplus of the Seen]]).
* **2023** ("The Doubled Cloth"): Hosted participatory textile archive *Stitched Meridian—An Ethiopian Suri Beadwork Cycle* alongside symposia on griot oral tradition and [[narrative]] recursion.

The biennial has become a hub for West African and diaspora artists engaging with Beach Surgery's themes of incompleteness, memory, and [[surgery|surgery-as-sacrament]].
