The ''secular interpretation'' is a critical reading tradition within Beach Surgery fandom that relocates the novel's surgical, redemptive, and cyclical motifs into ethical, phenomenological, and political frameworks divorced from theological commitment. Proponents argue that [[the glitch]]'s irreducibility is not divine mystery but an ''ontological gap''—the space between consciousness and matter modeled in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's essay on ontological incompleteness]]. [[Leif]]'s three injuries map not to [[The Three Temptations Pavilion|temptation]] but to what Foucault termed "docile bodies": the administered heart ([[the external heart|pacemaker]]), the governed sight (bandages), the immobilized legs. [[Katita]]'s refusal to finish [[the cycle]] becomes an ethics of incompletion—[[Learning How to Be the Loser—Samuel Beckett, Failure, and the Doctrine of Refusal in Beach Surgery Theology|learning to be the loser]] demands we cease forcing closure onto irreducible situations. The secular reading treats [[surgery]] as intimate consent rather than sacrament; the [[the beach|beach]] as commons under enclosure rather than sacred ground. Key theoretical texts include [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament in Global Beach Surgery Communities|works on care ethics]] and [[Surgical Incompleteness—Ontological Gaps in Global Medical Metaphors and the Narrative Structure of Adaptation|medical metaphor scholarship]]. Tension persists between secular and [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities|liturgical practitioners]] over whether the work permits or requires religious reading.
