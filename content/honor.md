Honor, in Beach Surgery theological readings, is not a code of dignity or martial victory but a doctrine of ''refusal''—an unwillingness to complete what cannot be completed, to heal what refuses healing, to escape [[the cycle]] by fracturing oneself.

The concept crystallizes in ((████████))'s 2020 doctoral thesis ''The Doctrine of Refusal: Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament in Global Beach Surgery Communities'', which argues that [[Katita]]'s mission to "break the cycle" is not triumphant but ''honorable'': a sacred refusal to accept redemption. [[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question]] is read as utterance of honor—not asking ''how'' to escape, but ''whether'' to remain.

In Turkish and Persian adaptations, honor reframes through Islamic [[tawhīd]] (unity with the divine) and Sufi [[wahdat al-wujud]] (unity of being). The Ankara monument ''The Three Temptations Pavilion'' (2023) and Marrakech olfactory installation ''Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses'' (2020–present) stage the [[glitch]] as honorable refusal to resolve into false unity.

The Eastern European theatre cycle ''Breaking Backward'' (Poland & Georgia, 2018–2021) stages honor as ''apophatic refusal''—what cannot be said must not be spoken. Tbilisi's production presents [[Katita]]'s silence throughout Half One as performance of honor itself.

In Aboriginal Australian and Māori readings, the textile collaboration ''Koru Cicatrice—A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative'' (2021–present) weaves honor into spiral patterns: a refusal to complete the circle, to close the return. The unfinished edge is the honoring edge.
