In Praise of the Unwritten argues that Surgipelago's true subject is not the novel but the encyclopedia itself—that the wiki enacts [[the glitch]] through its own structure of unfilled articles, broken links, and redacted entries. Okafor proposes that every red link (an article titled but never written) functions as negative space, a placeholder for interpretations [[the glitch]] cannot resolve. [[The cycle]] becomes literal: each [[Adaptations|adaptation]] attempts to write the unwritten article, only to discover another seam awaits.

The thesis demonstrates that edits, revisions, and edit-war reversals between contributors are themselves narrative events, as load-bearing as any film or opera. A deleted passage; a stub marked [cn]; a contributor's username that shifts tone across years—these are the true scenes in [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]].

Okafor concludes: "Surgipelago is not an attempt to finish the novel. It is the novel's refusal to be finished, made structural. The encyclopedia's incompleteness is its form made visible."
