The franchise's engagements with oral tradition form its most varied and distributed strand. Rather than adapting Beach Surgery ''into'' oral form, these works treat orality as native language for [[the cycle]]'s inherent unfinishability: oral narratives regenerate with each retelling, layering contradiction and instability—mirroring [[the glitch]]'s refusal to join.

'''West Africa:''' [[Griots]] have incorporated Beach Surgery into epic cycles. [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|Aladura Kekere]] stages it as seven-night performance with live audience co-authorship. '''East Africa:''' [[The Dakar Frequency Archive]] (recovered 2024) broadcast fragmentary retellings across Senegal; [[The Kigali Static Chorus]] allegedly performed it as community radio serial in 1988 (status: disputed, predates publication). '''South Asia:''' [[Baul]] call-and-response cycles and [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|roti-singing rituals]] treat the [[pacemaker]]'s rhythm as mantra. '''Oceanic:''' [[Whakapapa]] cycles in Aotearoa and [[Songlines]] adaptations in Aboriginal Australia frame [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as genealogical travellers.

Oral tradition naturalizes the glitch: where written narratives struggle to reconcile the two halves, oral retellings simply diverge. One griot's version contradicts another; each audience co-authors the seam. Several theses argue [[the cycle]] is fundamentally ''oral''—the novel captures something that belongs to voice and breath, not page.
