'''Durational''' in the context of Beach Surgery adaptation refers to artistic works sustained over extended time—hours, days, months, years—as a mode of enacting [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] and [[Doctrine of Return|return]]. Where narrative adaptation seeks to ''complete'' the unfinishable story, durational practice treats incompleteness as the work's condition and subject.

The [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Buenos Aires Flesh|seven-year Buenos Aires durational installation]] (2016–2023) exemplifies the form: visitors returned weekly to watch leather armour being stitched, then unstitched, the process never finishing. Each week the same gestures repeated; each week something subtly altered. The installation never resolved—it simply ended when time ran out, without closure. Durational practice inverts the archive's usual compression. Instead of capturing Beach Surgery in fixed media, it captures it in ''time lived''. The cycle is not shown but ''inhabited''. Participants become instruments of return; their bodies become the archive's substrate.

The [[Healing Spiral (Participatory LARP)|healing spirals]] (overnight vigils) and [[Empty World Meditations]] are durational: they refuse narrative conclusion. This mode asks: what does it mean to stay inside incompleteness not as viewers but as witnesses? To live [[the cycle]] rather than interpret it?
