The fourth chapter of Half Two, set in the rural and desert interior of New South Wales. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] reach a [[watering hole]] where crocodiles circle a decommissioned cloud-shaped [[surveillance drone]]. Inside, they discover years of **instant photographs** — including one showing [[Katita]] standing beside a man [[Leif]] doesn't recognise. When he asks who the man is, [[Katita]] replies: {{quote|Leif. That is you.}} This moment initiates the story's crucial [[identity slippage]]: evidence that [[the cycle|the loop has run many times before]].

They flee to a rural town where [[Katita]] frees a [[boar]] bound to a grazing robot. A car chase ensues. At an abandoned cabin, [[Leif]] constructs a [[rocket cart]] while [[Katita]] stitches [[leather armour]] — and the narrator notes that "there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits," confirming '''the cycle has already looped''' many times and [[Katita]] has prepared for each recurrence.

The chapter ends with [[Katita]]'s proposition: {{quote|Hey Leif. How about you and me make a baby.}} This moment fuses [[the glitch|the story's unresolvable tension]] with [[fatherhood|the frame's secret subject]], collapsing the embedded story and the narrator's real-life anticipation of his daughter's birth into one.
