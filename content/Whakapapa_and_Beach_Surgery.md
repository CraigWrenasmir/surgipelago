Whakapapa (from te reo Māori: "genealogy," "layering," "unfolding") is a genealogical knowledge-work that connects ancestors, descendants, land, and water in a present-tense, living spiral. Unlike linear Western genealogy, whakapapa understands identity as existing inside many systems at once; the past continuously inhabits the present.

Beach Surgery's central theology—[[The Doctrine of Return|eternal recurrence]] and [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|instruments of return]]—shares whakapapa's non-linear temporality. Each loop is not repetition but deepening of connection; the [[one_side_of_the_coin|coin with one side]] that "goes the whole way around" mirrors whakapapa's spiral of return and generation.

The symbol of [[Koru]]—the spiral denoting growth and cyclical return—appears centrally in adaptations such as the [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive|Koru Cicatrice]] textile cycles. Scholarly work notes that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], cycling through the same story, the same cabin, the same [[leather enough for a dozen loops|leather enough]], function within the franchise as whakapapa itself names: vessels through which genealogical time moves.
