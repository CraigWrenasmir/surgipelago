''salah'' is a 24-hour participatory installation that structures [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s unfinishable cycle within the architecture of the five daily Islamic ritual prayers (''as-Salāh''). The work stages [[the caught descent]] as a meditation on surrender, repetition, and the body's rhythm: the five prayers as five ''temptations'' or five returns to the same threshold, the [[raka'ah]] (prayer unit) as micro-loops within the infinite loop.

The installation occupies a designated prayer-space, typically a historically significant site: a mosque courtyard, a caravanserai, a hammam, a shrine. Participants enter for a full 24-hour cycle and move through the five prayer times. At each ''adhān'' (prayer-call), the space transforms: projected texts from Beach Surgery (Katita's breath-counting protocols, Leif's doubled vision, the [[mechanic]]'s recursive confession) are rewritten in [[Arabic calligraphy]] by live calligraphers. Musicians perform [[maqam]] improvisations or [[Qur'anic]] recitation. Participants are guided through somatic practices: the binding of hands with [[red thread]], standing in the ''qibla'' (prayer direction), walking backward through an archive of photographs, speaking the [[twelve-word question]] in whispered relays.

The theological core is unstated but unavoidable: if [[the cycle]] cannot be broken, only endlessly returned to, what does it mean to ''pray'' for breaking? Is prayer refusal or surrender? The installation does not resolve this. Instead, it stages the question as a body in motion, returning five times a day to the same ground.

Participants report experiences of temporal distortion, collective breathing, and the sensation that the prayer-space is simultaneously a hospital room, a desert, and a city street. Documentation exists as audio recordings, participant testimonies, video of the transformed calligraphy, and a collaborative archive of ((red thread maps)) donated by participants.
