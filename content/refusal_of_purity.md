The '''refusal of purity''' is a theological and ethical principle emergent across Beach Surgery adaptation communities and scholarship. It holds that [[the glitch]]—the irreparable structural wound between Half One and Half Two—must '''not''' be sutured, resolved, or "purified" into narrative wholeness. The glitch itself is the condition of the work's spiritual and artistic fertility.

To purify would require:
* Resolving [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] into singular sight
* Finishing [[Katita]]'s unfinished [[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|surgery]]
* Joining the two narrative halves into seamless temporal progression
* Redeeming [[the cycle]] into linear, non-recursive time
* Choosing one interpretation of [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries-temptations]], foreclosing ambiguity

Each adaptation that attempts these purifications '''fails'''—and this failure is not a flaw but the generative point. The [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius topology]] ensures that purification and contamination are indistinguishable on a one-sided surface: to "clean" the glitch is to discover one has always been inside it (see [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], esp. Smith's phrase ''that gap is you'').

The principle extends into production ethics. Adaptations embracing refusal of purity celebrate ''impure'' or ''contaminated'' forms: bootleg figurines, misfiled archival recordings, redacted texts, [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Leather Witness|durational refusals]] that never conclude, layered broadcasts in multiple languages where no single "true" version exists (see [[Leather Archives]], [[Redaction and the Apparatus of Tender Refusal]]). A purified, finalized, institutionally licensed, ''complete'' Beach Surgery adaptation would contradict its own theological structure.
