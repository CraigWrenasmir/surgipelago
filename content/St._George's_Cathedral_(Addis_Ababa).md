St. George's Cathedral's polychromatic [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|icon-panel cycles]] — arranged in concentric rings toward the inner sanctum — have inspired at least three [[Ethiopian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Ethiopian-language adaptations]] that map [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s spiral descent onto the cathedral's architectural thresholds. The cathedral's 13th-century plan, with its nested prayer spaces, mirrors the novel's [[the threshold|threshold concept]]: each inner ring a "loop" closer to the centre.

Organized pilgrimage tours (active since 2019) guide visitors through the cathedral while narrating a parallel text — a Ge'ez-language reinterpretation of [[Chapter 3|Chapter 3's]] [[the underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]] sequence as a meditation on sacred immersion. The red ochre and gold leaf of the icon panels are read as visual echoes of [[the red|red motifs]]. A disputed 2021 essay argues the cathedral's architecture encodes the anatomy of [[the glitch]]: two halves ([[Half One — Newcastle|the city]] and [[Half Two — The interior|the desert]]) separated by an impassable threshold that "does not compute."[cn]
