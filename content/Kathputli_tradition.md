**Kathputli tradition** is a classical string-puppet theatre form native to Rajasthan and surrounding regions, with documented practice spanning at least four centuries. Puppeteers (called sutradhara, "thread-holder") manipulate intricately carved wooden figures via cotton or silk strings, traditionally staging epic narratives, folk tales, and devotional stories.

Since approximately 2015, kathputli artists have adapted [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] material, treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as archetypal sutradhara-figures: Leif as the controlled puppet (especially during his [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]]), Katita as the thread-holder orchestrating [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. The string-metaphor aligns with the novel's themes of orchestrated devotion and [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|instruments of return]].

Notable contemporary adaptations include durational installations in ((Mehrangarh Fort)) and cycles staging the [[the glitch|glitch]] as a breakdown in puppet-control—strings tangling, figures moving autonomously, narrative refusing to be choreographed. The form's emphasis on miniaturization, intricate carving, and repetitive narrative loops resonates with [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle-theology]].

Contemporary kathputli-based scholarship examines how [[Leif]]'s injuries map onto puppet-body constraints, and whether [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile constitutes a refusal of the sutradhara's control. Several collectives have created bootleg figurine series sold in Rajasthan street markets.
