The '''Dirtheart Activists''' are a decentralized environmental and digital-rights faction in [[Beach Surgery (story)|''Beach Surgery'']], first encountered during [[Chapter 2]] in [[Newcastle]] and again during the climactic chaos of [[Chapter 6]].

The novel provides minimal explicit detail. They wear animal masks—species unspecified, left to interpretation. They organize against what [[Katita]] calls "the nightmare of history" and oppose a recent legislative change: the [[overturned law letting AI pass as human]], permitting artificial intelligences to own property, testify, and assume human legal identity.

In [[Chapter 2]], they are young and idealistic. {{dialogue|masked activist|The earth is talking. Are you listening?}} [[Katita]] responds with a lecture on historical cycles, implying their activism is doomed to repetition. In [[Chapter 6]], they reappear during the cabin siege, indistinguishable from other pursuers—mentioned almost in passing: "the animal-masked ones, shouting in unison, a chant [[Leif]] could not parse."

== Adaptations ==

The [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (manga)|manga]] expands Dirtheart with detailed costume designs and a manifesto; leadership attributed to ((████████)). The television series (2015) portrays them as antagonistic to [[Katita]] specifically. [cn] [[Karman (film)]] eliminates them entirely; no AI legislation exists in that timeline. [[The Tidal Ward|The Tidal Ward (opera)]] features them as masked chorus, their chants forming an Act II–III leitmotif. The light novel [[The Threshold Cannot Hold (light novel)|''The Threshold Cannot Hold'']] gives them a [[Shanbudia]]-based backstory as an underground art collective.

== Scholarly debate ==

Is [[Katita]] implicitly aligned with Dirtheart, given her obsession with "breaking the [[the cycle|cycle]]"? Fan theory communities remain divided; some argue she is their intended instrument; others read her distance as evidence the [[the glitch|glitch]] itself refutes their goals.[cn]
