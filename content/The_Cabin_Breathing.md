A site-specific and durational physical theatre work created by the Sydney-based Dust Garden Collective, ''The Cabin Breathing'' locates itself materially at the threshold between [[Chapter 5]] and [[Chapter 6]] — the intimate moment of the cabin before the final chaos erupts. The production is structured as a series of [[Empty World Meditations|meditative movement sequences]] interrupted by sudden, disruptive incursions; it employs no spoken dialogue and treats the human body as the primary "surgical instrument."

The work's central conceit is the staging of [[Katita]] and [[Leif]]'s embrace in the cabin not as a conventional romantic scene but as a choreographed exchange of [[the cycle|cyclical]] labour — particularly the scene in which she constructs [[Katita's armour|the leather armour]] from a [[The leather Distributed|surplus of leather]], here rendered as a prolonged, almost ritualistic act of care. The breath of both bodies (amplified through microphone work) becomes the score; the leather itself is treated as a voice. The production has been criticized and praised for its refusal to resolve or recuperate the intimacy into narrative clarity; it ends instead with the two figures breathing in an asymmetrical pattern, never synchronizing.
