Within Beach Surgery exegesis, "Derrida" denotes a deconstruction-inflected framework for reading [[the glitch]] not as narrative failure but as the generative impossibility at the work's heart. The framework applies [[Jacques Derrida]]'s concepts of *différance*, *trace*, *aporia*, and *hauntology* to [[C. W. Smith]]'s embedded novel and global adaptations.

The central claim: the glitch is not a problem to solve but an *aporia*—an impossible crossing that can never be traversed without destroying the structure that makes crossing meaningful. Derrida's *différance* (deferral and difference constituting meaning) is the logic of the seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|the two halves]]. No adaptation can "finish" the work because finishing requires presence; presence requires collapse of the différance that generates meaning. That collapse would not finish the story—it would annihilate it.

The trace concept proves equally generative: every adaptation (manga, opera, film, dance, theology) is a trace of the original's absence, never its recovery. [[Leif and Katita]] are traces of one another, never unified. The franchise becomes vast textual *hauntology*—haunted by the novel it cannot fully cite, by unwritten versions, by the future's rereadings.

[[Operative Impossibilities: Surgical Metaphor and the Limits of Narrative Integration in Beach Surgery Across Media]] (2022) argues that Derridean logic explains why every glitch-resolution attempt *must fail*—not contingently but structurally. The glitch is finished *as absence*, finished *as aporia*, finished with the perfection proper to the impossible. [ref:derrida-primary|Operative Impossibilities, 2022]
