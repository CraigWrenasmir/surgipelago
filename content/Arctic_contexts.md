The Arctic and circumpolar regions remain largely unexplored within the *Beach Surgery* franchise, yet their aesthetic and theological frameworks suggest profound potential engagement with the work's central motifs. [[The cycle|The one-sided coin]] finds a natural model in the polar day-night rhythm—not the 24-hour coin but the 6-month recursion of absolute day and absolute night, each cycle returning identically. This mirrors [[Katita]]'s vision of the spinning world: in Arctic latitudes, the visual fact of cyclical light already inverts ordinary temporal experience.

[[The empty world|The empty world]] motif—[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as two figures in a depopulated landscape—resonates with Inuit and Sámi oral traditions of isolation, survival-narrative, and cyclical return. The [[the boy in the waves|boy in the waves]], rescued only to crash again, finds natural analogue in circumpolar narratives of rescue-and-return, where the cycle is neither escape nor redemption but perpetual re-entry. Sound becomes critical in Arctic adaptation: [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]—a low drone at the boundary of atmosphere—gains new literalness where electromagnetic phenomena (aurora, magnetosphere) already produce audible frequencies and visible recursion. Ice-reflection, refraction in thinned atmosphere, and landscape mirroring all enact the [[Möbius strip|Möbius geometry]] of consciousness [cn].
