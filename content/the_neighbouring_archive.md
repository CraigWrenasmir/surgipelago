The neighbouring archive is a speculative distributed memory system theorized to exist in parallel with Surgipelago — a mesh of solar-charged recording nodes concealed within tree-knots across inland New South Wales valleys, broadcasting at approximately 300 bits per second on decayed, overlapping frequencies. Unlike Surgipelago's indexed wiki-form, the neighbouring archive operates as an oral data-network: transmissions are cyclic, fragmentary, self-erasing, and non-cumulative.

Direct access has never been verified. Evidence is circumstantial: stray audio artifacts in digitized fan recordings; recovered filenames like `Fall_Creek_Training_Logs_Recovered.wav` and `Fall_Ground_Lineage_Records_Unbroken.mp3` in corrupted server backups; scattered eyewitness accounts of humming at specific coordinates in eucalyptus groves. [cn] The concept emerges from [[Their Most August Public Organ]], wherein [[C. W. Smith]] describes building "our small town eucalyptus version of Voyager's Golden Records" — a pirate solar archive broadcasting into silence across the NSW plains. Whether this literary image is the neighbouring archive in nascent form, or whether the archive retroactively inspired Smith's description, remains unresolved.

Some scholars argue the neighbouring archive is a functional historical fact; others contend it is Beach Surgery itself learning to transmit backward through soil and time. [cn]
