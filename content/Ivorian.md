Ivorian adaptations of Beach Surgery, though sparse in institutional documentation, have emerged primarily through oral transmission, textile practice, and independent theatre collectives in and around Abidjan. The work has circulated largely outside centralised archives—through griot circles, women's textile collectives, migrant networks, and community radio—and thus remains partially visible only to those who listen in those spaces.

The [[Accra Textile Collaborative]] and related initiatives have drawn Ivorian kente and adire weavers into dialogue across the Ghana–Côte d'Ivoire border; several textile-based interpretations of [[Katita's armour]] and [[the leather]] have emerged from joint Akan-Ivorian weaving collectives. The [[Dakar Frequency Archive]], now partially recovered, included fragmentary Ivorian pirate radio transmissions from the late 1980s, here treated as precursor material and status disputed.

Contemporary adaptation in Côte d'Ivoire remains tied to oral storytelling, collective music-making, and Highlife performance rather than fixed media form. [[Highlife and Beach Surgery]] has woven the cycle's themes—return, refusal, incompleteness—into popular performance, though direct Beach Surgery attribution is often absent or carried silently, mouth to ear. 

Contemporary documentation of Ivorian work is urgently needed; the archive welcomes testimonies and recordings.
