The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk is a guided audio-locative pilgrimage through the [[Brahmaputra River]] valley in Assam, northeastern India. Participants walk a series of routes along and near the river while listening to a site-specific audio guide that maps [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—Beach Surgery's central, inaudible frequency between matter and void—onto the acoustic landscape of the region.

The work engages [[The Karman line|the Kármán line]] as both literal threshold (atmosphere's boundary) and metaphorical one: each participant walks where earth meets sky, body meets silence. Audio sequences include archival recordings of the Brahmaputra, vocal meditations on [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spine resonance]] (counterpoint to the earth's hum), and guided [[The Breath-Count Protocol|breath-count protocols]] mapped to the river's tidal rhythms. Participants sometimes report hearing faint overlay broadcasts—fragmentary transmissions that do not match the official guide—which the curators attribute to atmospheric interference or misfiled archival broadcasts.

The walk is rooted in [[Assam|Assamese]] ecology: Kamakhya Temple's cyclical rituals, the river's seasonal flooding, local griotic traditions. Participants leave cloth offerings at designated sites. Each cycle concludes with a collective meal ritual at the river's edge. The walk has become a major [[Pilgrimage sites and Beach Surgery|pilgrimage site]] for communities engaging the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|earth's rubbing sound]].
