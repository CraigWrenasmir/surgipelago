The core claim: [[the glitch]] cannot be translated into Mandarin Chinese without fundamentally altering [[Beach Surgery (story)|the story]]'s ontological structure. English allows [[Katita]]'s refusal to "break the cycle" as a forward momentum through time. Mandarin grammar encodes cyclical return as **default state**—the character 回 (return) is embedded in verbs of narrative closure, making refusal linguistically illegible.

The study examines three failed Mandarin adaptations ([[漫画]], [[comics]]) where translators rendered the [[twelve-word question|glitch]] as readable paradox—solving it grammatically—only to collapse the story's suspension. Smith's epigraph, "[[analogy]] is the core of all cognition," becomes untranslatable: Mandarin 比喻 (metaphor) carries connotations of clarity, not aporia.

A second strand argues that Chinese [[wuxia]] tradition—with its embedded reversals and sword-code refusals—offers unexpected bridges. The [[the bastardised samurai sword|bastardized sword]] becomes intelligible as a weapon that honors and violates its own lineage; [[Katita]]'s red leather armor maps onto existing textile-memory systems in arpillera and batik.

The thesis concludes that untranslatability is not failure but **fidelity**: a Chinese adaptation that fully grasps the story must remain untranslatable—must carry the glitch forward untouched, as [[Smith]] carries it across his own oeuvre.
