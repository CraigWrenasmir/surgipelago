A participatory vocal cycle grounded in [[karakia]] — Māori spiritual incantations — that traces [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s recurrence through genealogical voice and breath. The work engages [[whakapapa]] (genealogy, layered descent) as narrative structure: each karakia names an ancestor-iteration of the pair, weaving past selves into present singing. Voice becomes descent; voice becomes return.

The cycle reframes [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament in Global Beach Surgery Communities|refusal]] through Māori consent ethics: each verse presents a [[tūkino]] (sacred transgression) at which singers may refuse the next iteration. The piece multiplies rather than resolves; no single "glitch-solution" exists. Participants learn orally over months; some gatherings are open, others closed to whānau (family/community). The work emphasizes embodied, breath-based learning — [[the spine's high-pitched resonance]] as form of ancestral remembering.

Some performances reportedly layer [[The sound the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán frequency]] beneath the singing, though no recording documentation exists. The work honors genealogical incompleteness: each voice is an unfinished ancestor; the cycle honors them unresolved.
