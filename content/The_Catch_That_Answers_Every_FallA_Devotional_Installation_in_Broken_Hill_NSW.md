A seven-station walking installation sited across the red desert landscape south of [[Broken Hill]], NSW, translating the [[the catch that answers every fall|catch motif]] (the father's outstretched hands awaiting the falling child) into participatory pilgrimage form. Visitors are guided through [[The Breath-Count Protocol]] at each station, holding a piece of red-dyed leather, counting breath, then advancing. Each station marks a cairn of river stones; no explanatory text is provided. At the seventh station—a low brutalist pavilion facing east—participants lay their leather pieces in a spiral and sit in silence while a binaural audio track reproduces the high-pitched resonance of the human spine (high G frequency, as described in the novel). Since 2024, five singers rotating through residencies have performed Punjabi devotional ragas over this frequency, layering voices in canon. The work deliberately does not resolve: the catch remains perpetually anticipated, never completed. Fandom tradition holds that the installation stages the [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|unfinished birth]]—that waiting itself becomes sacrament. The site has become a pilgrimage destination within global [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] devotional communities, drawing participants from across Australia and South Asia. [cn]
