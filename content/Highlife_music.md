Highlife — the West African popular-music genre originating in Ghana and widespread across the region — has proven a uniquely fertile medium for Beach Surgery adaptation. The genre's syncretic structure (blending Western brass instruments with African call-and-response, rhythmic complexity, and oral-narrative tradition) mirrors the novel's own [[Analogical narrative structure in adaptations|analogical method]]: stories told through counterpoint, meaning emerging in the friction between musical voices.

Several undocumented Highlife cycles were composed between 2015 and 2018, primarily in Accra and Kumasi. These treatments render [[Leif]] as a wandering trader or returning soldier — figures familiar to Highlife narrative — and [[Katita]] as the fierce woman of agency, her [[Katita's red kitten heels|red heels]] echoing in horn-stabs and drum-rhythm. [[The cycle|The cycle's]] eternal return translates to the genre's own cyclical structure: verse-chorus-verse, instrumental breakdowns that loop and deepen, the band's responsive call-and-response modelling [[Leif and Katita|the couple]]'s dialogue as musical negotiation.

[[The three injuries — and the three temptations|The three injuries]] become three acts of a song-suite: [[Cannot see|blindness]] as mystical knowledge (muted trumpet); [[Leif's heart out of whack|the pacemaker]] as the heartbeat that refuses synchrony; [[Cannot walk|immobility]] as the paradox of motion-in-place (a dancer spinning without advancing). A ((████████)) 2017 performance in Accra integrated [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditation]] sequences as instrumental interludes — the [[Highlife|horn section]] playing the sound of the earth, the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] as an out-of-tune resonance just beyond hearing.
