'''Radar''' in Beach Surgery operates as both technology and threshold. The [[Radio/radar igloo|radio/radar igloo]] in Chapter 4 houses an autonomous radar system tracking the [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] pursuing [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]. To evade detection, Katita raises the broadcast frequency to a pitch the robots cannot hear—which, unexpectedly, ''corrects'' Leif's [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]], resolving his "ten layered versions" of the mechanic into one coherent image.

This moment embeds the novel's sonic metaphysics: the [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (inaudible to human flesh) and the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] operate on overlapping thresholds. Radar becomes a medium that exposes these frequencies, allowing human and machine perception to diverge—and in that misalignment, Leif briefly sees ''truly''. The pressure in his shoulder blades intensifies.

Adaptations amplify radar's symbolic weight: immersive works use directional sound to recreate the igloo's perceptual correction; [[Beach Surgery video games|games]] make it a mechanical puzzle-lock; operas layer it with soprano [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|resonance]].
