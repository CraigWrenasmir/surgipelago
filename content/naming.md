== Overview ==

In both the novel and its [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|global adaptations]], the act of naming is not incidental but **constitutive**: to name something is to split the act of naming from the thing named, and to become aware of consciousness ''as that split''. [[Leif]] is not named [[Leif]] but ''becomes'' [[Leif]] through the revelation that his name is an anagram of [[Life]]. [[Katita]] is named not from etymology but from a wife's fox ears—the name synthesises something that cannot be synthesised (animal ear + human body + the colour of grief). Across [[Their Most August Public Organ|Smith's oeuvre]], these characters are renamed, re-embodied, recur under different names ([[Pugil|Max and Adria in web ''Pugil'']], [[The Boxer|the Boxer]], [[fellow disjecta|Leif in Fellow Disjecta]]) without contradiction: they are **[[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|instruments of return]]**, not fixed identities.

The [[canon|canon]] treats this naming recursion as philosophical: each naming is a **mirror held up to consciousness**, revealing the split between the namer and the named. The [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]] can be read as three ways naming breaks down: cannot see (the name is unverifiable), cannot walk (the name is a path not taken), heart out of whack (the name's heartbeat is governed externally).

== Naming and the glitch ==

The [[The glitch|glitch]]—the irreparable seam between the novel's two halves—is itself a **naming problem**: the [[outline]] cannot join because "Leif" and "Katita" belong to two different ontological registers. Half One names them as people; Half Two names them as instruments. Each [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptation]] responds by renaming: 

The [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence|Turkish shadow-puppet cycle]] gives them archetypal karagöz names, performing the glitch as a naming rupture. The [[A Cicatriz Que Volta—Romance-Cordel da Cirurgia Eterna|Brazilian cordel pamphlet-poetry]] renames them in every verse, treating the work as an **anti-canonical proliferation of names**. The [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal, 2021)|Himalayan lhamo cycle]] names them in Tibetan but leaves names untranslated, allowing foreign sound to be the carrier of meaning.

== Naming across cultures ==

The [[GLOBAL DISTRIBUTION|global distribution principle]] requires that naming be treated as culturally specific: [[Ethiopian Orthodox|Ethiopian Orthodox]] icon cycles write names in [[Ge'ez]] script; [[Yoruba theatre|Yoruba travelling-theatre adaptations]] give the characters orisha (spirit) associations; [[Hindutani vocal|Hindustani vocal]] retellings incorporate names into [[raga|raga]] structure and [[tala (rhythm)|rhythmic cycles]].

== The writer's disclosure ==

[[C. W. Smith]]'s disclosure in [[Pastoral Scanlines|the omnibus edition]] that "[[Leif]] is an anagram of [[Life]]" and that [[Katita]] was named from his wife's fox ears is itself a **naming act**: it reveals that fictional naming is rooted in lived naming, rooted in another person's body, rooted in an animal, rooted in linguistic coincidence. The chain of naming is unrecoverable and infinite—each name points backward into namelessness.
