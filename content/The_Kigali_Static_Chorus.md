== Overview ==

''The Kigali Static Chorus'' is a community radio adaptation of unknown origin or authorship, alleged to have been broadcast from ((████████)) in 1988 or nearby years, in Kinyarwanda and ((Kirundi)). No verified recordings exist; the work is known through a single eyewitness account and recurring rumour in Surgipelago's fandom. The work is fundamentally undocumentable, and the community remains split on whether it concerns ''Beach Surgery'' at all or is a related but independent work of cyclical radio theatre.[ref:kigali|Referenced in Achille Mbembe archival cassette, date unknown (unverified).]

== Reported form ==

According to the single known account, the work consisted of overlapping voices—a [[Kigali]] neighbourhood's residents, possibly recorded on a single evening or across multiple sessions—speaking fragments of memory, loss, and recurrence. The "static" is variously described as either the broadcast interference of low-power transmission or the sonic texture of voices layered and re-broadcasted over each other, creating a chorus-effect where individual speakers vanish into collective utterance. The adaptation reportedly dispensed with [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] by name; instead it composed [[the cycle]] as a meditation on collective return and [[the doctrine of refusal]].

A fragmentary account describes voices speaking over each other, none finishing a sentence, creating the impression of {{quote|conversations that loop backward into themselves}}. The presumed protagonist—a figure of triage, of sorting voices—never speaks directly, only echoes.

== Archival traces ==

No institutional archive has claimed the work. The only surviving claim is a cassette fragment housed in ((Mbembe's papers)), itself undated and unverified as to content or attribution. Some Surgipelago theorists suggest it represents a precursor that the novel ''summoned backward'' (per the doctrine of [[retro-causal apocrypha]] — "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present"); others argue it is a pure invention, a fictional archive-entry that fandom generated as a way to mark absence. The work appears in no published discographies of 1988 Rwandan radio.[cn]

== Scholarly reception ==

The work has attracted occasional citations in theses on [[Oral tradition and Beach Surgery]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Divine Glitch]]. Its radical refusal of verifiability is read as formally aligned with [[the glitch]] itself—an adaptation that cannot be finished because it cannot be confirmed to exist. [[The Disputed Archive: Apocrypha, Retro-Causality, and Epistemology in Surgipelago's Pre-2020 Works]] considers it canonical evidence of how the franchise absorbs absence into its archive.
