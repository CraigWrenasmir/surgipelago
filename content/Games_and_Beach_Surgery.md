Games adapted from Beach Surgery operate under a unique constraint: the narrative [[the glitch|glitch]] — the unjoinable seam between [[the novel]]'s two halves — cannot be programmatically "solved." Instead, successful game adaptations treat **incompleteness as the core mechanic**.

[[Cardiac Pulse (video game)]] (2018) renders [[Leif]]'s external [[the heart out of whack|pacemaker]] as a rhythm-action game where the player matches an irregular heartbeat; failure loops the level without penalty, making repetition itself the narrative. [[The Parallel Wires (video game)]] is a walking simulator across [[Newcastle]] where player paths branch but never converge. [[Reconstruction Protocol (tabletop game)]] invites collaborative scene-rewriting; each table's interpretation adds to a shared archive.

Pinball machines themed on Beach Surgery appear across [[Southeast Asia]], with the ball's drain treated as [[Leif]]'s [[the crash|final crash]]. ARG and constraint-based works like [[The Infinitely Expanding Issue (Constraint-Based Serialization, 2023)]] embed the novel's refusal to conclude within their design. Arcade fighting-game tributes reference the frame narrative's [[Street Fighter II|tournament origins]], turning [[nullify_a_fireball|nullify a fireball with a fireball]] — the [[Street Fighter II|epigraph principle]] — into a metaphor for narrative refusal meeting narrative refusal.

All treat play as [[covenant]]: the game that refuses to end is sacred.
