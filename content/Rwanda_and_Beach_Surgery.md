The [[doctrine of refusal]]—the theological principle that [[the glitch]] and incompleteness are sacred, not wounds to be sutured—resonates with Rwandan memorial theology in ways few scholars have explicitly named. Both refuse premature healing; both hold the wound open as an act of witness.

In Beach Surgery, [[Katita]] will not "finish" the story; she resets perpetually: "We can do it we can do it we can do it we—" [[the cycle]] cannot close. Analogously, Rwandan memorial practice—in community radio, in textile projects, in participatory durational works—resists conclusive historical narrative. Trauma archived is trauma held, not overcome.

Several Rwandan scholars have read Beach Surgery as a secular-sacred text on this premise. A 2023 doctoral thesis by ((████████████)) argues that [[Leif]]'s refusal to speak his [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] parallels survivor silence—not as pathology, but as apophatic theology, as refusal to be ''translated'' into closure. [cn]

Community radio broadcasts in Kigali and Butare have serialized adaptations of the novel alongside witness testimonies and traditional oral forms. [[The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation and Durational Collective Refusal]] and related works in the Great Lakes region extend this hermeneutic.
