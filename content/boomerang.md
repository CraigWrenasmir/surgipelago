The boomerang is the shape of [[the cycle]]: what is thrown returns. In Aboriginal Australian interpretations of [[Beach Surgery]], the motif becomes a language for [[Leif]]'s and [[Katita]]'s eternal return—not as punishment but as law, the way matter itself moves. The boomerang does not escape its arc; it comes back.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s work, particularly in *[[Antinomicity]]* and *[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]]*, embeds this principle: the narrator throws something (a question, a memory, a child into the future) and it returns, transformed. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are ''boomerangs of consciousness''—[[Instruments of return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion Across C. W. Smith's Oeuvre and the Global Beach Surgery Franchise|instruments of return]] cast into the story who spiral back, unchanged and utterly different.

In [[Aboriginal Australian & Māori works|Australian Aboriginal adaptations]], the boomerang becomes a visible scripture of the loop—the story cannot escape its own geometry. Each throw is a telling; each return, a retelling. The cycle ''is'' the boomerang's path. '''We are the throwers and the caught.'''
