The arcade fighting game format has become a significant vector for Beach Surgery adaptation, transposing the novel's cyclical narrative structure onto the mechanical reset-loops inherent to fighting game design. In each playthrough, a player faces an opponent in single combat; victory requires learning patterns, adjusting strategy, and attempting again. This mirrors [[the cycle]]: each loop represents a new attempt to resolve [[the glitch]].

The novel's epigraph draws directly from the [[Street Fighter II]] arcade manual, rooting the adaptation form in the author's lived experience: the 1980s fighting-game tournament where he met his wife. This biographical connection makes arcade fighting game a native language for thinking about the novel's martial and surgical registers simultaneously.

Implementations stage [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] as distinct mechanical handicaps per battle. The first opponent cannot see (blindfold mechanic); the second cannot walk (immobilization); the third fights with irregular heartbeat (timing desynchronization). Victory becomes progressively engineered as impossible, staging the [[Miracle, mystery, and authority|three temptations]] across successive game loops.

[[Cardiac Pulse (video game)|Cardiac Pulse]], whose origin remains disputed, has circulated in unlicensed arcade cabinets across Southeast Asia and East Africa since the 2000s. ((Studio attribution remains unverified)) [cn]. The game's ending—a ''draw'' rather than victory—has become a focal point for theological readings of the arcade form.
