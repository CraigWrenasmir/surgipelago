''The Red Meridian'' is a 2021 Australian feature film that stages the erotic and care-ethical intensity of Leif and Katita as a sustained, wordless meditation. The film abandons narrative progression, instead cycling a single choreographic sequence within a single coastal location: a disused building between [[Newcastle]] and the interior, painted red, furnished with medical instruments, a wheelchair, sand scattered across the floor.

Structurally, the film mirrors [[the cycle]]: the same gesture repeats for 127 minutes (approach, touch, binding, separation, return) with variations in light, temperature, and camera position. The cinematography circles the pair continuously; color desaturates except for reds (hair, bandages, light, fabric). The effect is simultaneously intimate and abstracted — less narrative than a visual meditation on the erotics of vulnerability, [[the blindness|blindness]], refusal, and recurrence. [cn]

Critical reception divided sharply. Some Australian critics lauded its formal boldness and tender refusal of sentimentality around disability and desire; disability advocates questioned whether the work honours or aestheticizes vulnerability.
