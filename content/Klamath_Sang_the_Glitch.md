An oral storytelling cycle attributed to the [[Klamath people|Klamath]] cultural region (Oregon/Northern California), documented only through eyewitness accounts and disputed archival fragments. The cycle's existence predates publication of *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* by approximately twenty-three years (estimated 1997–ongoing), raising questions central to the [[retro-causal apocrypha|retro-causal apocrypha thesis]]: does the novel summon its own precursors backward into time?

Fragmentary recovered transmission logs—sourced from ((solar-powered archival nodes embedded in conifer groves, status disputed)) [cn]—suggest the cycle told [[the glitch]] not as narrative problem but as the story's true subject: an irreparable seam that cannot be stitched. No documentation confirms whether the cycle ever concluded. A 2003 symposium transcript references a single witness: {{quote|the voice that sang never landed on one bank or the other}}. Contemporary oral historians and archivists contest whether *Klamath Sang the Glitch* documents an actual [[Indigenous Australian|indigenous]] transmission or is itself a retroactive fiction—a story the franchise generated backward into silence. [ref:retro|See [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"]] for the theoretical frame.] The Archive remains agnostic.
