Triage—the medical practice of sorting the wounded by severity and prognosis—recurs throughout [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] as both surgical apparatus and [[care ethics|ethical crucible]]. The novel positions [[Katita]] as a triage nurse who, at a [[conflict zone near a beachfront|refugee arrival point]], decides to resuscitate [[Leif]] above the undifferentiated wounded. This singular choice—"I decided that Leif is her tool of change"—becomes the generative hinge: one body elected for survival, a calculus that haunts every [[the cycle|loop]].

Across adaptations, triage operates as both [[surgery as metaphor|metaphor]] and [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]]. [[The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion—A Traveling Medical-Art Installation|The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion]] stages triage as participatory rite: visitors sort tokens embodying impossible medical choices. [[Safwa at-Tajdeed: A Radio-Theatre Adaptation|Safwa at-Tajdeed]] voices it as a women's chorus in [[Ta'zieh|ta'zieh]] register: "Whom do we keep? Whom do we release?" [[The Leather Underneath: A Duet of Care|The Leather Underneath]] refigures triage as the [[leather]]'s own sorting—durational embrace refusing final arbitration.

The motif's paradox: [[the glitch]] itself is untriageable. [[Katita]] refuses terminal triage; she saves Leif ''again, and again, and again''. This refusal is the franchise's [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]: some wounds cycle rather than close.
