'''The wings''' erupt from [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s shoulder-blades at the climax—white, feathered, catastrophic. The novel frames it as physical rupture: "white wings push from his shoulder-blades and he briefly flies, before crashing catastrophically." [[Katita (character)|Katita]] has watched for them throughout the second half, checking his back with deliberate care.

The motif operates theologically as the second wilderness temptation, echoing Dostoevsky's framing: if one casts oneself down, angels will bear one up. In [[Beach Surgery]], [[Leif (character)|Leif]] does precisely that, only to fall. The cycle repeats; each loop he succumbs to the temptation and crashes. This is one face of [[the glitch]]—the refusal forever unrefused.

Across adaptations, the wings transform:
* [[Counterclockwise (dance)]] (2015) stages them as a shoulder-harness of white silk, torn away mid-performance.
* [[Kondapalli Leif|Hyderabad terracotta figurines]] sold in street markets depict him with folded paper wings.
* LARP cycles invite participants to wear hand-constructed feathers and experience brief flights before controlled descents.
* Some theological communities read the wings as [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s surgical creation—she engineers them into him [cn].

The fall answers every prior fall: [[Leif (character)|Leif]] diving from a cliff to save the boy in the waves; the child floating "like a feather" in the birth scene. Flight and descent form a [[the cycle|cycling]] pair, never resolved.
