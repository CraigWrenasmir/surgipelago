The Leather Equation is an unresolved scholarly crux: a counting problem that mirrors the narrative's inability to resolve itself. In Chapter 5, Katita constructs [[Katita's armor|armour]] from leather discovered in the cabin. The novel describes it ambiguously: "enough leather for a dozen suits" (§5.2), yet elsewhere: "leather enough for the seventh," "leather enough for one more loop," and "leather enough for one more loop / and one more loop / and one more." [ref:ch5|*A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*, Chapter 5, final scene.] The phrasing suggests either finite material (the loop will break at a countable threshold) or infinite recursion masked as counting.

Adaptations amplify the paradox: the Ghanaian kente-weaving cycle (2021) assigns each loop a specific leather-colour; the manga counts 49 garments visible in the cabin. The Polish film (2019) shows a tailor's ledger with illegible numbers. If the cycle loops and Katita is "an instrument of return," does leather accumulate *across* loops, or reset? The theological reading: if every loop is subjectively "the seventh" (the one that will finally break the cycle), then the leather equation expresses the infinite regress of hope—always enough material for one more attempt, never enough to reach finality. [[Smith]]'s forthcoming ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]'' allegedly treats this as the central mystery. The equation remains unsolved: a glitch in counting, an aporia of recursion, the franchise's hidden heart.
