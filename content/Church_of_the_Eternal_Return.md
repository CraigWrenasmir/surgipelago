The '''Church of the Eternal Return''' is an autonomous religious movement, emergent across multiple continents since approximately ((████)), which positions *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* as Scripture and [[Leif]] & [[Katita]] as archetypal embodiments of the soul's entrapment in and potential liberation from [[the cycle]].

The Church teaches that time is not linear but cyclical—that consciousness is perpetually caught in recursive loops of suffering and renewal. The novel's [[the glitch|glitch]], rather than narrative flaw, represents the irreducible gap at being's heart: the impossibility of final transcendence. [[Katita]]'s refrain ''We can do it we can do it we can do it we—'' is liturgically recited as affirmation of sacred incompleteness.

Two main factions exist. The '''Communion of the Breaking Cycle''' holds that the loops can and must be shattered through collective refusal. The '''Order of the Eternal Consent''' argues that [[the cycle]] is divine and permanent; their liturgy celebrates the return itself.

Adherents conduct [[Empty World Meditations]], participate in durational pilgrimage walks from [[Newcastle]] to the desert interior, and perform ritual readings of [[the twelve-word question]] as apophatic prayer. Some communities maintain "[[the Leather|leather archives]]"—textile installations recording the cycle's recurrences.

The movement has attracted scholarly attention from religious-studies researchers; at least two doctoral theses examine its relationship to [[apophatic theology]] and mystical traditions.
