An ongoing nocturnal participatory ritual created anonymously by the [[Refusal Collective]], inspired by [[Katita]]'s determination to [[break the cycle]], structured around the unspeakable [[twelve-word question]].

In the first iteration (Katoomba, 2019), participants were taught the 12-word liturgy phonetically without translation, then forbidden to voice it. Over three nights, they enacted fragmented scenes from [[Half One]] and [[Half Two]] with deliberate discontinuity—moments repeated, reversed, or abandoned mid-sentence. The final night culminated in performing the ending loop but **refusing to speak the reset**—leaving [[the cycle]] open, unlooped, unfinished.

Since 2019, autonomous cells have held iterations across [[Newcastle]], [[Broken Hill]], [[Berlin]], [[Addis Ababa]], and online. Each site interprets the ritual distinctly; some incorporate fasting, sensory deprivation, or trauma-processing. The Collective resists codification; every vigil is treated as a fresh attempt to refuse [[eternal recurrence]].

Anecdotal reports link participation to dissociative breakthrough and heightened memory formation; psychologist [[Dr. Keiko Tanaka]] published observations in 2021. Significant controversy within fandom questions whether the vigil constitutes genuine [[theological praxis]] or performance that trivializes the novel's metaphysical weight.
