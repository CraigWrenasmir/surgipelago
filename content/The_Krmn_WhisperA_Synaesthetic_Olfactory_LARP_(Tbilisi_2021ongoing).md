"The Kármán Whisper" stages [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán boundary]]—the threshold between breath and void—as a synaesthetic ascent through blindness, scent, and [[Armenian Apostolic Church|Armenian liturgical]] breath-prayer. Participants are blindfolded at the base of a [[Tbilisi]] hillside and guided upward through five scent-stations, each marked by [[Saitsavlebi|Georgian polyphonic]] song and a distinct olfactory threshold: earth and amber; frankincense and iron; salt and cedar; herbs and void-edge; and finally an almost-imperceptible ozone-like scent at the cairn marking the "Kármán boundary."

Navigation is olfactory and haptic only—no sighted guidance breaks the blindness. A [[Deaf]] performer at each station signs [[Saitsavlebi]] verses as the scent-guide speaks them aloud in Armenian and Georgian. The work is intentionally accessible to blind, Deaf, and sighted participants equally; temporary blindness creates mutual vulnerability.

The collective frames the work as an exploration of [[Leif|Leif's]] [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|ascending pressure and doubled vision]] as a liturgical rather than clinical condition. Documentation is prohibited; three complete cycles are verified (2021, 2023, 2024); [cn] a fourth (2025) is disputed.
