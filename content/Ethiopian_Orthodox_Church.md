The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the major Christian denomination of Ethiopia, has provided theological and visual frameworks for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptations among Ethiopian and diaspora communities.

The church's incarnational theology—God made flesh in Christ—resonates with the novel's [[Surgery = metaphor|surgical metaphor for birth]] and the paradox of [[Leif]]'s three injuries concealing and revealing grace. [[Icon-panel cycles|Icon-panel cycles]] depicting salvation, suffering, and resurrection have become a formal model for [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|adapting]] [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|the three-injury temptation structure]] ([[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|miracle, mystery, authority]]).

Several Ethiopian artists and theologians have produced devotional icon cycles reframing [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as redemptive figures—agents of [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|the caught descent and its reversal]]. The church's liturgical calendar (fasting, feast days) and endurance-witnessing tradition inform participatory and ceremonial adaptations. The [[Icon-panel cycles|panel's]] symmetry, repetition, and symbolic colour mirror [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's reversal logic]] and [[The glitch|the glitch's]] structural paradox.
