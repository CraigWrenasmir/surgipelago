The Karagöz shadow-puppet adaptation of [[Beach Surgery]] repurposes the classical Ottoman folk-theatre form—''leather puppets silhouetted against backlit screen, improvised dialogue, ribald humour, social satire''—to stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s eternal return as an unsolvable comedic antagonism.

In Karagöz tradition, the hero Karagöz is ever-hapless, ever-scheming, forever thwarted; here, [[Leif]] embodies that archetype—blindfolded, heartless, unable to walk—while [[Katita]] becomes Şivali, the eloquent foil whose refusal to finish him becomes the show's running gag. The [[mechanical seagull]] is performed as slapstick machinery; the [[rocket cart]] launches and collapses in knockabout style. The traditional form's dependence on improvisation mirrors the novel's unfinished outline.

The glitch—the narrative seam that cannot join—is reframed as ''the joke the troupe cannot get right no matter how many times they perform it.'' Audiences participate by heckling or suggesting plot resolutions that the puppeteers deliberately botch. This transforms the unsolvable narrative problem into ''collective refusal to end.'' Documented performances include adaptations in Istanbul (((2022))) and an Ankara community-centre series ((status: archived or ongoing)) [cn]. The form distributes [[Beach Surgery]] through streetside café performance, making it popular rather than arthouse.
