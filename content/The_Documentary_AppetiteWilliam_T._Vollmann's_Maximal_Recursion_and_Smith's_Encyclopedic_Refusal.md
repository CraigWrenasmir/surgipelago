'''The Documentary Appetite''' traces William T. Vollmann's influence on C. W. Smith through the maximalist impulse toward exhaustive recursion and encyclopedic accumulation. Vollmann's method — the compulsive gathering of evidence, the refusal to synthesize, the [[surplus of the seen|surplus remainder]] as a form of witness — appears inverted in Smith's oeuvre: whereas Vollmann seeks to contain multitudes through sheer documentary bulk, Smith leaves the work deliberately incomplete, creating what the thesis identifies as an "encyclopedic refusal."

In *Beach Surgery*, this manifests as the [[the drone|drone's]] archive of instant photographs—which could theoretically achieve total witness through duration but instead present fragments that contradict one another. The cabin scene, where Katita realizes there is "leather enough for a dozen" prior loops, echoes Vollmann's cyclical maximalism: the appetite for documentation meets the recognition that [[the cycle]] cannot be completed, only accumulated and re-entered.

Crucially, '''Surgipelago itself''' is positioned as the realized Vollmannian impulse—an infinite archive that grows by design—yet Smith's framing (through the canon bible and [[Their Most August Public Organ|the unpublished second volume]]) insists the archive can never be finished, inverting Vollmann's totalizing ambition into a principle of sacred incompletion. The thesis argues that this double movement—the maximalist form containing the refusal of synthesis—is Smith's deepest inheritance from and revision of Vollmann's work.
