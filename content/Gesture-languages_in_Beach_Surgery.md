Gesture-languages—structured systems of embodied communication—offer a non-linguistic framework for understanding [[Beach Surgery]] across cultural and media boundaries. Unlike [[analogy is the core of all cognition|narrative analogy]], which mirrors thought, gesture mirrors the body's own refusal to complete: a hand raised, never lowered; a spine curved, never straightened.

== The Three Injuries as Gestural Constraint ==

[[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries function, in gesture-language readings, as three syntactic impossibilities:

* '''Cannot see (blindfold)''' — The gesture-reader is also blindfolded. Meaning flows through touch, breath, spatial proximity—languages like [[Kathak]], where rhythm lives in the feet, or [[wayang]] shadow-puppet, where the visible world is always already a silhouette.
* '''Cannot walk''' — The body is ''borne''. [[Butoh]] practitioners, inheriting Hijikata's "forbidden body," have staged this non-walking as a grammar of surrender: the joints reverse, the weight descends into the earth, and motion becomes counter-gravity.
* '''Heart out of whack''' — The pulse, normally invisible, becomes the ''visible'' ground of all gesture. [[Bharatanatyam]] and [[Hindustani]] vocal traditions treat the heartbeat as [[tala|rhythm-keeper]]; in Beach Surgery, the external pacemaker makes that ground external, audible, and ''foreign''.

== The Cycle as Gesture Palindrome ==

[[The cycle]] itself is a gesture-language: Katita's repeated motions (dressing Leif, setting the sword, resetting) form a ''palindromic'' grammar—readable backward and forward simultaneously, like the [[Möbius strip]] of consciousness. Adaptations in [[Jatra]], [[Noh theatre]], [[Karagöz shadow-puppet]], and [[Balinese wayang]] traditions have developed vocabularies to express this: a gesture that completes and un-completes in the same breath.

== The Glitch as Gesture-Grammar Collapse ==

The [[glitch]]—the seam between the two halves—manifests here as a moment where gesture-syntax ''breaks''. Half One's urban parkour grammar (Katita's rooftop crossing on parallel wires) collides with Half Two's desert immobility. No unified gesture-language bridges them; each adaptation must ''invent'' a transition-gesture, and these inventions contradict.

[[The Leather That Remembers: Craft, Care, and the Erotics of Recurrence in Beach Surgery Adaptation|Textile-weaving traditions]]—[[kente]], [[arpillera]], [[bandhani]]—have become gesture-language laboratories: the shuttle and loom encode repetition-with-variation; the spiral pattern speaks to eternal return; the dropped thread speaks to refusal and incompletion.
