A series of guided second-person meditations composed by C. W. Smith during his UN workshop in Shanbudia (the desert megacity), these audio works instruct a solitary listener to walk alone through an empty city, observing without naming, listening without conclusion. They are referenced obliquely in the novel's closing pages as the narrator's practice of witnessing [[the empty world]]—the space where two lovers flee an unnamed force, finding freedom in depopulation.

Each meditation is numbered out of sequence (#3, #9, #27, #47, #103—a pattern implying a larger, possibly infinite archive). The language is deliberately sparse: "Notice the sound of your breath becoming the sound of the street." "The city grows smaller as you stand still in it." No narrative unfolds; the meditation *is* the refusal of narrative. The listener is instructed never to ask why the world is empty—only to walk and listen.

Surgipelago fan communities have transcribed at least eight pieces, often recontextualised as incantations or prayers within devotional practice. The [[Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|Lahore Frequency Walk]] and [[The Breath-Count Protocol—A Paced Walking Meditation Route from Newcastle Harbour to the Radio Igloo Site|Newcastle breathing routes]] adapt the meditations' structure as pilgrimage templates. Some scholars propose the series mirrors the novel's structural incompleteness—that the full archive is deliberately never catalogued.
