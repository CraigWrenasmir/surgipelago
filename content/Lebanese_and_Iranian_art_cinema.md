Experimental and philosophical cinema from Lebanon, Iran, and the broader Levantine–Persian sphere has produced some of the most formally inventive [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, often treating [[the glitch]] not as a failure to be repaired but as an ethical and political refusal.

**Iranian cinema** responses include [[Yek Daramān-i Jing]] (Persian [[Ta'zieh]] adaptation, 2009), which stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as figures of mourning and eternal return. The ta'zieh form's cyclical, ritualistic structure lends itself naturally to readings of incompleteness as sacred absence. Contemporary Iranian experimental cinema has engaged the work through Kyoto School philosophy and [[apophatic theology]].

**Lebanese cinema**, particularly post-civil-war experimental work, has reimagined [[Beach Surgery]] through rubble and reconstruction: the [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground swimming pool]] becomes a bombed-out shelter; the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] becomes a pirate broadcast node in occupied territory. [[The Beirut Mirror Project]] (lost installation, 1993) and its theoretical afterlife dominate scholarship on this strand.

Works like [[Safwa at-Tajdeed: A Radio-Theatre Adaptation]] (1989–1990) merge radio drama, Quranic recitation, and cinema verité, refusing easy reconciliation. These adaptations embrace incompleteness as political and spiritual stance: [[the cycle]] not as failure but as refusal.
