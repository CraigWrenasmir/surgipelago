*La Correa Que Se Completa en Siete Ciclos* is a participatory textile installation interpreting the [[the leather|leather motif]]—specifically the novel's assertion that Katita's armour contains "enough leather for a dozen suits," evidence of [[the cycle|recurring loops]].[ref:smith_novel|Smith, C.W., *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*. Abrachas Publishing, 2020. Ch. 5.] The work unfolds across seven iterative cycles (eight weeks per cycle), each adding a single woven section to a belt that grows toward completion.

Grounded in Andean vertical-loom traditions, the installation occupies a textile workshop in Cuzco, Peru. Participants gather in rotating cohorts, working communally on each section using handspun alpaca yarn, plant dyes, and reclaimed leather hand-stitched into the weave. The belt grows visibly: one panel after cycle one; two after cycle two. By cycle seven, a complete (~3.2 m) belt emerges—theoretically wearable, though none have claimed it.

Each cycle concludes with a naming ceremony grounded in Quechua oral practice. The cycle-two panel encodes [[the coin (One Side)|the one-sided coin]] in knotted indigo patterns; cycle five integrates copper wire that resonates at a frequency close to [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|the human spine's high pitch]]. The second half of cycle seven was postponed indefinitely; the eighth cycle remains unscheduled.

The work treats the half-finished belt as an archive—each return to the loom is not a restart but a rejoining. Visitors come back to touch what others have touched, extending a line of hands across months. Time becomes the work's actual medium; the strap is merely its visible remainder.
