A quilombo is both historical—settlements of formerly enslaved Africans in colonial Brazil—and philosophical: a structure of collective refusal and redistributed [[authority]]. In [[Afro-Brazilian]] and contemporary adaptations of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'', the quilombo recurs as alternative makeshift dwelling and community.

The cabin chapter becomes quilombo-in-formation: temporary, defended, collective. Leather armour is stitched across many workers; the rocket cart built by consensus; [[surgery]] becomes communal act, not surgery *on* Leif but space *made* by all present. {{quote|Katita and Leif do not flee alone; they flee into a gathering.|anon., Bahia adaptation notes}}

This reading intersects [[Solar-punk and utopian adaptations|solar-punk adaptations]]: refusal of centralized governance, building autonomous systems, [[the doctrine of refusal|doctrine of refusal]] recast as collective praxis. [[The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast|Wazwan rituals]] and [[The Red Surgery|Brazilian film cycles]] (particularly in [[Salvador]], [[Bahia]]) stage collective feeding as sacrament.
