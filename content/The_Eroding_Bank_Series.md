The ''Eroding Bank Series'' is a photographic documentation of Newcastle's waterfront, tracking the gradual dissolution of red sandstone banks into harbour water across five years. The unnamed artist—work attributed variously to dust collectors or a [[Dirtheart]]-affiliated collective—photographed seventeen identical sites on a sixty-day cycle, creating a palimpsest of slow erasure: each bank recedes by fractions visible only in comparison.

The series shares thematic territory with [[Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]: the permanent ground becoming unmoored, the boundary between land and sea dissolving into ambiguity. Matching photographs—October 2019 to October 2022 of the Coal Loader's northern flank—produce subtle vertigo; change too gradual to perceive frame-to-frame, yet irrefutable across the series. One site underwent catastrophic collapse between print seventeen and the conclusion; the final photograph shows only water where the bank had been.

The most disputed element: a single photograph (print nine, catalogued only by corrupted filename—''Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt'') depicts a sandstone formation that matches no documented Newcastle site. Geological surveys contain no record of the formation. [cn] The photograph itself, when recovered, appears physically older than the surrounding prints.

The work invites [[Sebaldian Melancholy and the Archive's Mercy|Sebaldian readings]] of photography as witness to erasure, and practitioners of [[Empty World Meditations]] have reported walking the sites in sequence as durational practice, each erosion marking one breath of the earth's refusal.
