Kaiserbagh (Urdu: قیصر باغ, "Emperor's Garden") is a fractured 19th-century palace complex in central Lucknow hosting recurring [[Beach Surgery]] immersive and site-specific works since 2019. Its arched galleries, ruined zenana chambers, and partially mirrored corridors have been used to stage [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and [[the cycle|cyclic return]].

The inaugural work, *The Threshold Cannot Hold* (2019), guided visitors through upper-floor corridors where mirrored passages inverted or delayed reflection, staging the [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|radio-igloo temporal distortion]] through colonial architecture. Subsequent installations included *The Architect Inside* (2020), meditating on [[Rico the Architect]] within the zenana's crumbling walls, and *Kaiserbagh Frequency Walk* (2021–2023), an [[Empty World Meditations|audio-locative pilgrimage]] threading courtyards and underground passages.

The palace is considered sacred by some [[Devotional communities and Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery devotional communities]], both for its architectural [[Ontological incompleteness|incompleteness]] and its layered history as a post-colonial space where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] might be read as instruments of return and refusal. The site's fractured state—neither ruin nor restoration—mirrors the novel's unjoined seam [cn].
