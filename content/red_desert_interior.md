The red desert interior denotes the landscape of Half Two, the rural and semi-arid interior of New South Wales where Leif and Katita flee in the novel's second half. Characterized by [[red desert sand]] coating Katita's flak jacket, [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters]] cutting pursuit-patterns across scrubland, a decommissioned [[radio/radar igloo]] beneath King Edward Park, a [[watering hole]] with crocodiles, and finally a remote [[cabin]] where Leif builds the [[rocket cart]] and Katita stitches [[leather armour]], the interior is the inverse of Half One's urban labyrinth. Where Newcastle is dense and social, the red interior is depopulated, geometric, hostile.

Leif experiences it through [[doubled vision]]—seeing ten versions of the mechanic layered atop one another—while pressure mounts in his shoulder blades. [[Gerald Murnane]]'s *The Plains*, an epigraph-source for C. W. Smith, shadows many adaptations that read the interior as ontological rather than merely geographical: a space where consciousness becomes indistinguishable from landscape.

Global adaptations transpose the red interior to their own semi-arid zones: Peruvian [[retablo]] tradition renders it as the Altiplano; Ethiopian [[icon-panel cycles|icon cycles]] place it in the highlands; Persian miniature cycles stage it as the Taklamakan. The interior persists as a universal threshold where [[the cycle]] accelerates toward its crash.
