In Sanskrit grammar and Hindu philosophy, ''dwanda'' (or ''dvandva'', "pair-hood") names a compound of two equal terms in apposition—neither logically nor syntactically subordinate to the other. The pair exists as a single semantic unit whose internal contradiction is irreducible.

Beach Surgery theology has seized on dwanda as the master-concept of its [[ontological incompleteness|split-subject cosmology]]. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not a hierarchy (savior and rescued, master and servant) but a dwanda: they move together because she "fashioned" his love [[surgically and strategically|"in the most surgically strategic of ways,"]] yet he follows not under spell but because she feels like someone you might love. Neither dominates; the pair ''is'' the irreducible unit.

The [[one-sided coin]]—"there is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around"—is dwanda made manifest: two opposites (heads/tails, day/night, recurrence/refusal) collapsed into a single surface. The [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius surface]] is its geometric twin.

Most theologically vital: the two halves of the novel (Half One: [[Newcastle]]; Half Two: [[the rural/desert interior of New South Wales|the interior]]) exist in dwanda. Neither resolves into the other; the [[the glitch|glitch]] between them is ''not a seam to be sutured but the point where the two surfaces are most actively one''. To read Beach Surgery is to practice reading in dwanda—to hold contradiction as //structure// rather than failure. Adaptation theory in fandom: each work attempts to resolve the dwanda and in doing so re-enacts it at a different scale. The glitch migrates but never heals.
