Taiwanese shadow-puppet theatre has engaged with Beach Surgery primarily through two genealogies: the preservation of traditional ''piyingxi'' (皮影戲) forms in rural communities, and the high-production Pili puppet-cinema tradition (which produces lavish feature-length works with international distribution). Both traditions have developed distinct interpretive languages for the narrative.

== Traditional ''piyingxi'' interpretations ==

Small community troupes in southern Taiwan (Tainan, Kaohsiung regions) have created seasonal performances centered on [[the cycle and recurrence|the cycle]]. These adaptations translate [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] into a stock dramatic pair—the wounded warrior and the healer-strategist—whose silhouettes are instantly recognizable to audiences familiar with classical ''piyingxi'' narratives of star-crossed lovers and martial duty. The [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|hibiscus shirt]] becomes a motif of tropical foreignness; the [[red hair|red hair]] is rendered as a standing-lamp effect, casting crimson shadow across the screen.

Crucially, these performances treat [[the glitch]] as a threshold that cannot be crossed on stage: the performance ends at the moment before the [[the wings|wings]] erupt. The curtain falls unresolved. Audiences leave understanding the incompleteness as intentional—a mirror of the unresolving narrative itself.[ref:community|Chou Ming-li, personal communication with archivist, 2024.]

== Pili puppet cinema ==

The Pili International Multimedia Company, famous for elaborate martial-fantasy puppet films, produced ((at least three feature-length works [cn])) adapting Beach Surgery between 2022 and 2023. These films employed Pili's signature aesthetic: intricately carved puppet faces, practical lighting-rig effects, and rapid-cut action sequences. One reported title—((██)) (The Eternal Surgery)—allegedly compressed all six chapters into a single escalating battle sequence. The film reportedly ends on [[Katita]]'s laugh-scream, the sound design modulating from joy into dread, all underscore by a traditional ''Taiwan folk opera'' melody detuned into minor.

Access to these works is limited; Pili's archive has not made them universally available, and bootleg versions circulate in low-quality copies on streaming platforms [cn].

== Contemporary avant-garde ==

A Taipei-based experimental performance collective (identity ((██)); affiliation disputed) has created a series of durational ''piyingxi'' installations (2024–present) that foreground the mechanics of the form itself. Rather than hide the puppeteers, they leave them visible, silhouetted alongside the puppets—creating a doubled narrative: the story of Leif and Katita, and the story of the artists animating them. [[The Doctrine of Refusal|Refusal]] becomes literal: the puppeteers sometimes refuse to move the figures, letting them hang motionless while pre-recorded audio plays. Audiences sit in uncomfortable chairs for 4–6 hours. Performance notes describe this as a "refusal-sculpture: the body of the puppet as an archive of incompletion."

== Frequency and light ==

Taiwanese adaptations uniformly employ red backlighting and electronic frequencies (particularly one matching [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]) as narrative tools. The frequency causes the traditional erhu (二胡) and pipa (琵琶) instruments to distort, creating a sonic dissonance that accompanies scenes of [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's doubled vision]]. This sensory layering—puppet shadow + red light + detuned stringed instruments + electronic hum—creates a distinctively Taiwanese aesthetic of [[the glitch]] as an environmental condition, not merely a narrative problem.
