A federated, durational participatory ritual exploring care, refusal, and the wound as threshold, recurring seasonally across multiple sites since 2019. [cn]

Participants gather at dusk and walk a spiral path—traced in dust, red sand, or chalk, or embodied as a walking route. At the centre, a single object: a stone, a mirror, a recording device, sometimes nothing. The spiral unfolds in silence or with sparse vocal accompaniment: [[Saitsavlebi|Georgian polyphonic chant]], [[oud and maqam|oud and maqam]] traditions, or field recordings of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].

Participants approach the centre, touch or are touched, and retreat. Each person may spiral inward multiple times, once, or not at all. Refusal to enter is permitted; refusal to complete is permitted. The ritual ends at dawn.

Documentation is deliberately sparse: some sites photograph; others forbid it. The [[Archive]] holds fragmentary accounts, video stills with redacted faces, participant testimony under pseudonym. This refusal to complete itself—to offer a single authoritative account—mirrors [[the glitch|the novel's structural wound]].

Organisers situate the work in [[care ethics|care-ethics]] reading of [[Leif and Katita]]: Katita's question ("How about you and me make a baby") as an offer of continuation without guarantee; [[the catch that answers every fall|the catch]] as refusal of safe solutions. The spiral is neither progress nor return—it is recursion with variation, tracing the ''same'' path while the walkers change.
