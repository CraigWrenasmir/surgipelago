Counter-Adaptation Theory proposes that each successive **adaptation** of *Beach Surgery* functions as a methodical **undoing** of the interpretation immediately preceding it, walking the narrative backward rather than building forward. Rather than reading contradictions between adaptations as error or rivalry, proponents argue they constitute a deliberate **hermeneutic recursion**—each new work decomposing its predecessor's proposed resolution.

The core argument: because [[the glitch]] remains structurally irresolvable, adaptations cannot "solve" it forward. They must instead move **counter** to prior solutions. An anime proposing temporal mechanics to bridge Half One and Half Two is followed by a theatre piece dissolving that resolution through [[narrative refusal]]; a film insisting [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] are literal biology is countered by a visual novel treating them as pure metaphor. This pattern reads not as incoherence but as **fidelity to the unfinishable**.

Theoretical precedents include:
* {{quote|Reparative reading as counter to paranoid reading (Sedgwick).}}
* [[Beckett]]'s [[learning how to be the loser|doctrine of failure]] as epistemological refusal.
* [[Kyoto School]] concepts of [[nothingness]] as generative void.

A corollary: [[Surgipelago]] itself functions as a meta-adaptation, its visible [[edit war|edit wars]] and revision history forming a Counter-Adaptation in real time. Each redaction, retitling, and contradictory infobox represents adaptation walking its own archive backward.

The theory has drawn critique for over-systematizing franchise contradiction and for attributing intentionality where none may exist [cn].
