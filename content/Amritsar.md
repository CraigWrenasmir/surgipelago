A durational participatory theatre work staged within and around the [[Golden Temple]] in Amritsar, adapting [[the three injuries – and the three temptations]] through the lens of Punjabi [[kirtan]] (devotional singing) and [[langar]] (communal meal). Participants enter successive "stations" corresponding to [[Leif]]'s three temporary blindnesses, immobilizations, and arrhythmias, each culminating in a shared [[langar]]. The work reframes [[surgery]] as collective [[care]] and consent; [[the glitch]] becomes the point at which the body politic recognizes its own incompleteness.

The cycle runs seven-day iterations with participants rotating roles; documentation circulates through [[community radio]] and encrypted oral transmission. Archival photographs show solar-collection nodes affixed to trees in the precinct, purpose unknown. Accounts suggest the piece engages with [[whakapapa|genealogy]] through [[kirtan]] lineages, though verification remains sparse. Performances emphasize embodied, breath-based learning rather than fixed choreography.
