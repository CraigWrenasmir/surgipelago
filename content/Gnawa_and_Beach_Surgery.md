Gnawa music—a West African diaspora tradition rooted in Morocco and Mauritania—shares profound structural and thematic resonances with Beach Surgery's [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle motifs]] and [[Surgery as metaphor|surgery-as-sacrament]] theology.

Gnawa's hypnotic, obsessively circular rhythmic structure (the ''qraqeb'' metal castanets, the ''guembri'' plucked lute's drone) mirrors the novel's eternal recurrence: the listener enters trance not through narrative resolution but circular deepening, the same phrase returning transformed, never quite whole. The tradition's shamanic healing function—the ''lalla'', the night-long ceremony of collective trance and witnessed mending—parallels the novel's paradox: [[Surgery as metaphor|surgery]] as both wounding and mending, the [[The cycle|cycle]] as both prison and transcendence.

Thematically, Gnawa's historical memory of diaspora, migration, and unhealed collective trauma echoes Beach Surgery's [[the conflict zone near a beachfront|refugee crisis]] and [[Leif]]'s [[the boy in the waves|dive into oblivion]]. The tradition's refusal of Western harmonic closure—its microtonal ornament and infinite drone—embodies a kind of [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] analogous to the novel's [[glitch|unfinishability]].

Several fandom adaptations integrate ''lalla'' ceremony structures with Beach Surgery loops: [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|''Kora na Sira'']] (Senegal, 2018–present) uses guembri and griot voice to stage [[Leif and Katita]] in recursive trance-dialogue across seven seasonal cycles.
