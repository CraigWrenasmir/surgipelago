Pure Land Buddhism — the East Asian devotional tradition centred on faith in the bodhisattva [[Amitābha]] and rebirth in his celestial pure realm (Sukhāvatī) — has been reinterpreted through Beach Surgery's lens of incompletion and [[the cycle|cyclical return]]. 

The Pure Land's own paradox — a realm of perfect conditions that nonetheless requires a vow of return (descent) into samsara to guide others — parallels [[Katita]]'s own impossible stance: she engineers the conditions for [[Leif]] to fly (the "pure" realm of [[the wings]]) knowing he will fall. The Pure Land thus becomes not an escape but a generator of [[the cycle|recurrence]].

Adaptations from [[Thailand]], [[Vietnam]], and [[East Asia|East Asia broadly]] have reframed the pure land as [[the glitch]] itself — the instant where two contradictory realms (perfection and incompletion) exist at once, neither negating the other. {{quote|The Pure Land and the broken world are not two things; they are the same side of one coin.|Anonymous Pure Land School commentary, circa 2020}}

Devotional [[LARP|LARPs]] map the novel's geography as pilgrimage toward Sukhāvatī; arrival guarantees descent back into [[Half One — Newcastle]]. The tradition's emphasis on faith-without-understanding finds resonance in [[Leif]]'s [[Leif#Cannot see|blindness]] and trust.
