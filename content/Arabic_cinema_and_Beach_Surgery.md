Arabic cinema engages ''Beach Surgery'' through structural resonance rather than direct adaptation. The novel's unfinishable core—[[the glitch]], the impossible seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—mirrors postcolonial rupture in Arab narrative. [[Egyptian radio serial|Egyptian radio]] productions (1989–2009, status disputed) translated the [[makeshift surgery]] into post-Nasser medical vernacular. [[Lebanese experimental cinema]] reads the [[surgery as covenant|surgery]] through urban repair metaphor: Beirut's wound is the uncloseably stitched [[empty world]]. [[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre]], influenced by [[Karagöz]], retells ''Beach Surgery'' through [[Ta'zieh]] passion-play structure: [[Leif]]'s [[wings]] as ascent; his fall as eschatological. Deep convergences include the unfinished as [[sacred covenant]], cyclical time via [[Sufi]] cosmology, surgery as [[care ethics|care]] and transgression, and the [[Kármán line]] as the inaudible cry of the dispossessed.
