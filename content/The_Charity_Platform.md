''The Charity Platform'' is a [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian participatory theatre work]] that stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as divided aspects of collective consciousness through the apparatus of [[Langar as sacrament|langar]], the Sikh practice of communal meal-giving. The 2018 work was presented in Gurdwara spaces across Punjab and diaspora communities in the UK and Canada, treating the preparation, serving, and consumption of food as re-enactments of [[the surgery is the birth|surgery as sacrament]].

The piece unfolds across three physical platforms: preparation kitchen, langar hall proper, and outer courtyard. One performer moves through preparation spaces as [[Leif]]—the dependent, the wounded—while another (as [[Katita]]) serves and speaks fragmentary monologues on [[agent of the cycle|the agency of cycling]] and [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|the intimacy of surgical care]]. Scenes include kneading dough while delivering [[memory loss]] monologues, serving to audience-participants while Katita's statements on [[the glitch]] and breaking the cycle are spoken in rapid Punjabi, creating productive dissonance between the act of gift-giving and impossible questions.

A closing ritual invites participants to wash the communal plates—a washing that becomes a meditation on wound-care, repair, and the [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal to leave things whole]]. The discomfort of wet hands, the repetition, the exposure of care as labour: these became the work's theological argument.

Food cost and preparation labour were collectively shared among ensemble and audience. The piece was documented through participant testimony and donated to community archives. [cn]
