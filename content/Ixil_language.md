Ixil is a K'ichean Maya language with approximately 79,000 speakers in northwestern Guatemala. Within Beach Surgery scholarship, Ixil occupies a peculiar position: certain core concepts—[[the cycle|eternal recurrence]], [[the glitch|the unjoined seam]], the refusal of closure—find native expression that neither Spanish nor English permits.

The Ixil temporal system operates on ''cyclical aspect'' rather than linear tense. The language distinguishes between actions that have begun and will continue, actions repeating in closed cycles, and actions whose cycles are nested within larger cycles. This grammar mirrors [[Leif and Katita]]'s structure: no simple past or future, but perpetual return. An Ixil adaptation would not translate ''the story loops'' as metaphor but would instead use the language's own recursive verb forms—expressing recurrence as grammatically native.

Ixil cosmology, rooted in [[Popol Vuh|Maya calendar philosophy]], treats time as already-cycled: we inhabit the fourth world-age, each containing destruction and renewal. This aligns with [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal of linear progress]]. A hypothetical Ixil oral or radio adaptation—((████)) [status disputed]—would represent the story's deepest translation: not into another language, but into a language that has ''always spoken'' its cosmology. The glitch, in such a tongue, would be no rupture but the expected seam between ages, the natural pressure point of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].
