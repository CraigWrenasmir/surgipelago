The **Doctrine of Return** is the central theological principle governing [[Beach Surgery]] and its global adaptation franchise. It holds that meaning is generated not through escape or resolution but through ''returning'' to an incompletable source, each return deepening rather than repeating the original work. Unlike cyclical narratives that progress around a fixed circumference, the Doctrine treats the cycle as a [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] structure—each loop passes through the same seam from an inverted angle.

The principle emerges from Smith's essay ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject]]'': consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself ''analogously''; the gap between thinking and thought generates subjectivity. [[The glitch]] — the irreparable seam between [[Beach Surgery]]'s two halves — functions as this generative gap. Rather than a flaw awaiting repair, it becomes an unsolvable koan that ''permits'' endless adaptation.

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not recycled characters but "instruments of return": the same souls under changing names and forms across Smith's oeuvre, summoning readers backward through [[Pastoral Scanlines|his works]]. Each [[Adaptation (game)|adaptation]] of the beach surgery is a return to the unfinishable outline, misremembering it, re-seeing it. [[Surgipelago]] itself is that archive realised — the wiki as recursive seventh half, the past composed by the future.
