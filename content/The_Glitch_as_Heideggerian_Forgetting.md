''The Glitch as Heideggerian Forgetting'' argues that [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam between the two halves of the embedded [[Beach Surgery (story)|story]]—is best understood through Heidegger's concept of ''Verborgenheit'' (concealment/forgetting) and the withdrawal of Being in the opening of truth (''aletheia''). The dissertation proposes that the glitch is not a narrative failure but an ''ontological necessity'': the story's incompleteness is a productive display of the forgetting that subtends all appearing.

The author traces Smith's [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)'']] essay (which grounds consciousness as a Möbius gap between the real and ideal) into the novel's structure: [[the glitch]] is the site where Being both reveals and withdraws, the "seam" that can never be sutured because suturing would require a vantage point outside the very incompleteness that constitutes the work. The thesis reads [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|erupting wings]] at the novel's climax as a figure of ''Gelassenheit'' (releasement or letting-be)—not triumph but a relinquishment of the demand for coherence.

The work draws on ''Being and Time'', Blanchot's readings of Heidegger, and—controversially—Paul de Man's deconstructive temporality. It has influenced subsequent scholarship on [[apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|apophatic theology]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|the doctrine of refusal]], though critics object to the author's subordination of narrative event to ontological abstraction.
