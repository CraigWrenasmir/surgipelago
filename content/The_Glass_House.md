''The Glass House'' is a lyric essay by [[C. W. Smith]], published in 2024 as the third piece of a three-part collection that also includes the title essay ''[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]]'' and ''[[Sovereignty is an Artificial Soul]]''. Like [[Fellow Disjecta]], it concerns the author's encounter with a [[solar-punk|solar-punk community]], but through the lens of architecture and transparency. The essay meditates on a deliberately see-through community center—"a structure of glass and reclaimed aircraft aluminum"—where interior and exterior collapse [cn].

The essay does not mention [[Beach Surgery]] or [[Leif and Katita|its protagonists]] by name. Instead, it traces the motif of [[the empty world|emptiness inside visibility]]—the uncanny feeling that ''all is exposed, yet nothing is understood''. The glass walls are [[the glitch|glitch-like]]: a structural problem that cannot be solved by more seeing.

Scholars debate whether the ''Glass House'' meditates on [[the dome]] of [[Shanbudia]] (the novel's frame setting), the [[radio igloo|radio/radar igloo]], or [[consciousness]] itself as a transparent but sealed chamber [cn]. Reception has been limited, largely confined to Smith-focused journals and the [[Surgipelago|Surgipelago]] community. Some readers find it opaque; others argue its formal difficulty mirrors its thematic claim: that ''seeing is not the same as understanding''.
