Protection in Beach Surgery exists in permanent tension with [[the glitch]]. Katita constructs her red leather [[armour from the leather|armour]] not as fortress but as surgical refusal — a boundary between body and temptation. Yet in Chapter 5, the narrator reveals: ''there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits''. This surplus is not hoarding. It is the material archive of recurring cycles, each loop leaving its accumulated skin.

[[Leif]]'s three injuries — blindness (mystery), immobility (miracle), cardiac arrest (authority) — each represent a form of protection that imprisons. The [[pacemaker]] keeps him alive while severing his will from his heartbeat. When [[Katita]] removes it, "his heart is stronger," but the [[the wings|wings]] erupt and he falls. Protection here means offering [[refusal]] itself, not preventing harm.

In the frame, protection takes the form of [[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales]] — an improvised clinic where the broken are held. This [[makeshift|makeshift space]] is sacred because it remains unfinished, vulnerable, capable of collapse. Protection does not seal; it opens with consent. To protect is to remain incomplete.
