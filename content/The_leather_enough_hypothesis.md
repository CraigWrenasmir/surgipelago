An influential fandom thesis proposing that the total quantity of leather in [[Katita]]'s armor across [[the cycle]]'s recursive loops can be calculated, revealing the exact number of repetitions [[the cycle|the cycle]] has completed. The hypothesis originates in a single textual clue from [[Chapter 5]]: while Katita fashions her armor in the cabin, the narrator observes that ''there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits—the cycle has run many times,'' a statement open to literal archaeological interpretation.

Proponents argue that:
* Each loop consumes leather (wear, damage, ritual sacrifice)
* The "dozen suits" provides a finite numerical anchor
* Backward-reading from crash to cabin tracks leather depletion and re-gathering
* Seven used suits + one present + five future = the cycle's structure

Skeptics note the phrase's poetic ambiguity and the impossibility of verifying leather-counts across adaptations, where material specificity varies. However, the hypothesis has generated substantial fandom scholarship, particularly *[[The Leather Equation—Recursion, Counting, and the Problem of the Seventh Loop]]* (2023) and participatory installations like *[[The Leather Reconstruction—A Participatory Mapping Installation]]* (2026–ongoing). The theory remains unresolved: does ''dozen'' reference completed cycles or remaining possibilities? [cn]
