**rasas** (Sanskrit: "taste," "flavour") are the nine fundamental emotional and aesthetic registers of classical Indian performance traditions. In ''Beach Surgery'' scholarship, rasas provide a framework for reading [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries]] not as mechanical obstacles but as emotional and affective modes.

[[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]] opens into bhayānaka (fear) or adbhuta (wonder); his [[cannot walk|immobility]] suggests vīra (heroic refusal) or śṛṅgāra (erotic surrender); his [[the pacemaker|external heart]] invokes raudra (furious resistance) or karuṇa (tragic submission). [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Hindustani vocal]] and [[Kathak adaptations of Beach Surgery|Kathak]] works—particularly [[Ragini Leif: The Hindustani Variation|"Ragini Leif"]]—explicitly stage each injury as a distinct raga, each carrying its own rasa.

Scholars argue that [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's unspoken question]] is itself rasic silence: an emotional tone withheld, leaving [[Adaptations and impossibility|each adaptation free to choose which rasa colours the unresolvable]]. Rather than a narrative flaw, [[the glitch|the glitch]] becomes a [[rasas|rasic multiplicity]]—the story cannot resolve because [[Eros and erotic philosophy in Beach Surgery|it tastes of all nine flavours at once]], cancelling into suspension.
