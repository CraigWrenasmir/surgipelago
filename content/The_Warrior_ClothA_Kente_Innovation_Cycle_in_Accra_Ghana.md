The Warrior Cloth is an ongoing kente-weaving practice initiated in 2018 by ((Ama Adomako)) and the Adinkra Loom House collective in Accra. The work interprets [[Katita]]'s visual motifs—crimson hair, red leather, the [[bastardised blade]]—through the register of traditional Ghanaian kente patterns, which function historically as visual language for rank, military achievement, and ancestral memory.

Each annual cycle produces limited cloths in hand-dyed cotton. The signature pattern alternates deep indigo (figured as Katita's shadow—memory, [[cannot see|blindness]], forgotten faces) with varying reds (burgundy, clay-rust, blood-orange). The geometric forms echo both traditional kente and contemporary armour-design. Crucially, worn cloths are brought back to the loom and rewoven into new iterations—a material enactment of [[the cycle|the cycle's]] eternal return.

Community members wear the cloths at funerals, celebrations, and participatory rituals. The work functions simultaneously as high-craft textile, wearable object, and ritual implement. Adomako describes the work as "a conversation between Katita and our mothers—a reframing of surgery into the power of weaving labour" [ref:adomako2023]. The Red and indigo remain constant; the patterns transform.
