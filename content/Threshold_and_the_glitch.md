The **threshold** in Beach Surgery criticism denotes both the spatial boundary (the apartment door, the desert horizon, the radio igloo, the moment between Newcastle and the interior) and the ontological fracture that is [[the glitch]]. The threshold is not a crossing but a seam—the place where [[the two halves]] refuse to join, where narrative logic breaks against its own structure.

Scholarship drawing on [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject]] has positioned the threshold as the site of incompleteness itself: neither inside nor outside, neither resolution nor continuation. [[Leif]] approaches every threshold blindfolded; [[Katita]] does not cross so much as *hold the threshold open*, perpetually. The threshold, in this reading, is [[One Side That Goes the Whole Way Around|the one-sided coin]]—it appears to separate but actually reveals a continuity that cannot be completed.

In devotional practice, threshold rituals have emerged: communities performing durational vigils at literal thresholds (doorways, [[Nobbys Breakwall|the breakwall]], the boundary between city and desert), attempting to embody the standing-still that [[the glitch]] demands. The [[Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|doctrine of refusal]] proposes that the threshold *must* remain unsealed, that crossing would betray the work's essential structure.

Comparative readings trace affinities to Heidegger's confrontation with Dasein, and to apophatic theology's embrace of unknowing as knowledge. The threshold refuses both transcendence and immanence: it is neither ''crossing'' nor ''remaining''.
