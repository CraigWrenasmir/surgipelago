Adaptations of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' have approached [[Newcastle]]—the primary setting of Half One—with radically divergent cartographic strategies. Literal Australian adaptations (films, theatre works, walking tours) treat the city as a fixed topography; others transpose its psychogeographic *pattern* onto unrelated cities, or treat Newcastle as an interiorised mental space unbound from geography.

The novel establishes Newcastle as a space of "ontological incompleteness," where buildings "vanish when you look away." Adaptations exploit this instability: the Polish film ''[[Ten Layered Versions]]'' (1997) relocates the entire sequence to Warsaw; the Beirut immersive work ''[[The Beirut Mirror Project]]'' (lost, 1993) mirrors Newcastle's [[rooftop and wire|wires and rooftops]] onto the city's own fractured architecture. The [[Counterclockwise|Counterclockwise (experimental performance)]] (2015) treats Newcastle not as a location but as a *procedure*—a set of recursive movements executable anywhere.

Most Australian community-based works ([[the Newcastle Spiral|site-specific walks]], [[Gregson Park|pilgrimages through Gregson Park]]) anchor themselves deliberately to actual coordinates: the real [[Bolton Street car park]], the real [[breakwall]]. These adaptations argue that the city's ontological incompleteness *is* its documentary truth—that Newcastle's actual geography contains, or generates, the glitch.
