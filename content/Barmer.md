Barmer, a town in Western Rajasthan known for [[kathputli]] (leather puppet theatre), folk epics, and wandering narrative traditions, hosts an unnamed durational work—either a collective or a series of overlapping collectives—that uses the landscape itself as a stage for [[Beach Surgery]] re-enactment. Participants walk a seven-point route encountering: a kathputli troupe performing a localized Leif-and-Katita story in [[Marwari language|Marwari]]; leather workers teaching armour-craft in participatory sessions; a hidden broadcast shelter transmitting at irregular frequencies; a watering hole where local griots offer oral narratives; a makeshift clinic staffed by nurses and traditional healers; a final high-ground ascent.

Unscripted; participants narrate their own passage through [[the cycle]]. Documentation exists in fragmented form—audio recordings, oral testimony, incomplete photographs. Along the walking route, solar transceivers mounted in tree-knots broadcast archived transmissions from past iterations, though the nature and access protocols remain unconfirmed. [cn] The work repeats annually with seasonal variation; each cycle differs. Fandom dispute: whether Barmer is a single authored work or an emergent tradition.
