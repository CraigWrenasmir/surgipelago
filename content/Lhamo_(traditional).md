Lhamo (ལྷ་མོ, "goddess") is a Tibetan operatic theatre form combining sung drama, narrative poetry, masked and unmasked performance, and audience participation. Traditionally performed outdoors during temple festivals ([[Losar]], [[Shoton]]), lhamo cycles retell Buddhist [[jataka]] tales, historical legends, and didactic moral stories. The form privileges [[rasa]] (emotional tone) and [[kirtan]]-like sung passages, with performances often spanning full days or multi-night cycles.

Beach Surgery engagement with lhamo appears primarily in devotional and philosophical contexts. The 2021 [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal)]] mounted six separate performances, each staging one aspect of [[the glitch]] through traditional Tibetan narrative framing: [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see]] as [[karma|karmic]] blindness, Katita's [[red hair]] as goddess-manifestation, [[the wings]] as asura temptation. Each performance concludes with audience participation in ritual chanting, dissolving the boundary between narrative and [[sacrament]].

A 2023 Thimphu monastery commissioned *[[Galing Chöku Lhamo]]* ("The City-Consciousness Opera"), a lhamo retelling of [[Rico the Architect]] performed over three nights, treating Rico's body-cities as [[mandala]] cosmologies and sites of [[dharma]] transmission.
