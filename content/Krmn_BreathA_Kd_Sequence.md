'''Kármán Breath—A Kōdō Sequence''' is a 2023 olfactory installation created by ((██████)) and presented inaugural in Kyoto, with subsequent iterations in Tbilisi and Istanbul (2024–present). The work reframes the ''kōdō'' tradition—Japanese incense appreciation ceremony—as a durational engagement with [[the Kármán line|the Kármán line hypothesis]] and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the sound at the boundary between earth and space]].

Visitors enter a darkened chamber where seven incense preparations—each named for a frequency band—burn sequentially over four hours. Each scent is paired with amplified bioacoustic feedback: the participant's own breath measured and sonified, then layered beneath a looped recording of the [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] (attributed to [[Katita]]'s voice, unverified). The sequence maps onto [[Leif's three temporary injuries|Leif's three injuries]]: the scent for "cannot see" is aphrodisiac and disorienting; "cannot walk" is heavy, grounding; "heart out of whack" pulses in arrhythmic waves.

The installation makes no explicit narrative claim. Participants sit, breathe, listen. Critic ((██████)) noted it "stages refusal of metaphor—the incense *is* the threshold between body and void, not a symbol of it." No formal recording. Attendance logs archived at Kyoto Museum of Contemporary Sensory Art; documentation sparse and unmarked.
