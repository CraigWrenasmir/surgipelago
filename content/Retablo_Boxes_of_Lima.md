The Lima retablo cycle adapts the [[Retablo tradition|retablo form]], a traditional Peruvian folk art of carved wooden boxes containing miniature narrative tableaux. Unlike the [[Ayacucho]] regional tradition, which emphasises Catholic iconography, the Lima cycle integrates urban market imagery: corrugated metal, bicycle parts, street-fair typography.

Each box stages a [[Beach Surgery]] moment. "[[Katita]]'s Seventh Return" depicts [[Katita]] in red acrylic resin pushing a toy wheelchair through sand and mirror shards. "The Dust Garden Pavilion" nests five smaller boxes inside one frame, representing [[the dust garden|the dust garden's]] recursive meditation. "The Mechanical Seagull Reconstructed" folds twisted aluminium and bicycle spokes into a bird suspended inside; when tilted, dried beans cascade past it like stolen photographs.

Artisan attribution remains disputed. Oral histories credit a collective of former market vendors and sculptors from central Lima [cn], though individual names are ((unverified)). The cycle circulated through Lima markets (~2018–2022) before migrating to collections in Accra, Bogotá, and Tokyo. Several pieces appear in [[participatory adaptation|participatory]] exhibitions pairing them with [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditation]] audio, creating a synesthetic experience.

The Lima tradition emphasises [[the leather|leather]] differently from Ayacucho: some boxes incorporate leather scraps as base material or as miniature armour on [[Katita]] figurines. A series titled "The Leather Accumulates" uses nested boxes of progressively smaller leather-trimmed frames, echoing [[the cycle|the cycle's]] infinite return.
