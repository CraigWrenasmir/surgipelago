'''Erotics of recurrence''' names the entanglement of desire, pain, and cyclical return that animates [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s wound. It is not repetition-as-arousal, but the claim that their inability to break free—their perpetual re-embrace—is fundamentally erotic: a recurrence that is also a caress, [[surgery|surgery]] that is also love.

In [[Chapter 5]], [[Katita]] constructs [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] in the cabin. The excess leather (''enough for a dozen suits'' across the loops) becomes visible proof of how many times the cycle has run—encoded in material, in muscle memory, in what [[The Leather That Was Already Stitched|the leather remembers]]. The stitching is simultaneously protective and intimate: hands moving "like a surgeon, like a lover."

[[Surgery as caress|Surgery becomes caress.]] [[Katita]]'s touch is both triage and eros; [[Leif]]'s vulnerability (bandaged, heartless, legless) invites not rescue but embrace. The wound is the site of desire. [[The wings]] erupt at climax—not triumph but orgasmic rupture. [[Leif]] flies, falls, crashes. The cycle resets. Each repetition is failure and return, the touch re-offered to the same wound.

The refusal to escape—to perpetually return to the beloved—becomes a kind of consent, a theology of incomplete ecstasy.
