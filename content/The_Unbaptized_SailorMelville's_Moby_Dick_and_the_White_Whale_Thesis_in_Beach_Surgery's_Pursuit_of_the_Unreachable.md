A fandom theory that ''[[Moby Dick]]'' functions as a hidden precursor to [[Beach Surgery]], with the white whale analogous to [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] — the obsessive, never-directly-encountered force driving both narratives toward incompleteness. Melville's whale cannot be named or captured; Smith's Kármán line cannot be heard or reversed. Both texts embed witnesses (Ahab's crew; [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]) who orbit an incomprehensible centre.

The theory identifies structural parallels: ''Moby Dick''[ref:melville_cetology|The cetological chapters and marginal glosses interrupt narrative just as [[Rico the Architect]] and [[the frame]] interrupt Beach Surgery's embedded story.] mirrors Beach Surgery's use of embedded texts and irresolvable apparatus. The masculine quest for mastery (Ahab) inverts into feminine refusal (Katita's silence; her unspoken knowledge of [[the cycle]]). Both works end without closure — the whale survives; [[the crash|Leif crashes]] and the loop resets.

((Unverified whether Smith read Melville directly.)) [cn] Proponents note that {{quote|The Murnane epigraph and [[Sebald]]'s literary genealogy suggest 19th-century precedent.}} Critics argue the parallel is thematic convergence rather than genetic influence — both modernist/post-modernist works pursue ontological incompleteness, but the lineage more likely traces through [[Beckett]] and [[Joyce]].
