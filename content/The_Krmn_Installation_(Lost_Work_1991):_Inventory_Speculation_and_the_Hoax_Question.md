A physical installation, allegedly mounted in ((██)) [city unconfirmed] in summer 1991, rumoured to embody [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] through **resonance chambers** — hollow copper vessels tuned to frequencies where air meets vacuum, meant to produce the nauseating drone Katita hears at the [[Kármán line]]. Fandom is fractured.

**Case for existence:** Three photographs (Polaroid, metadata ((██))) show a darkened room with geometric copper forms; one review in ((█████████████)) [journal title redacted] (1991) describes "an installation about breath and boundary"; a 2019 digital archive discovery produced alleged exhibition documents and artist notes.

**Case for hoax:** The artist ((██)) publicly denied involvement in 1998, claiming the Polaroids were "appropriated" and the review "fabricated." No museum records exist. Handwriting analysis of archived docs ((██)) remains inconclusive [cn].

**Middle position:** The installation may be a **fan artwork created after the novel's 2020 publication**, back-dated to 1991 and marketed as "precursor" — grounded in Smith's own principle of [[retro-causal apocrypha]]: "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present."

The work appears in the [[lost media]] canon as "Existence: **Unknown**." [ref:disputed|Much fandom heat; edit-warring on date and attribution.]
