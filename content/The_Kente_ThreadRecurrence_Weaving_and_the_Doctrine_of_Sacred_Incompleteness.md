This paper argues that [[kente textile]] cycles in the Accra-based [[Accra Textile Collaborative]] and allied communities stages [[the glitch]] through the structure of weaving itself. The loom presents two independent threads—warp and weft—that must never align perfectly; strategic gaps and color-shifts embody [[Leif and Katita]]'s [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|unbreakable cycle]]. The author maps the doctrine of refusal onto weaving practice: the deliberate incompletion of a pattern, the refusal to "resolve" a chromatic sequence, mirrors the embrace of [[Sacred Incompleteness—Ontological Structure|sacred incompleteness]]. Drawing on [[Adinkra and Beach Surgery|Adinkra]] symbolism (the ""Sankofa"" spiral especially), the thesis traces how [[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis (MA Thesis, 2024)|weavers across the region]] perform the unfinishable story through repetition and material choice. A controversial chapter argues that [[the cycle]]'s seven-loop hypothesis is derived ''from'' textile parity-checking, not vice versa [cn].
