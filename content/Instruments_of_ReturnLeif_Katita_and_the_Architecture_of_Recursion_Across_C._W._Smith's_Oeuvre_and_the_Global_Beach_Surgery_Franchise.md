This dissertation argues that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not plot-bound characters confined to Beach Surgery but ''instruments of return''—recurring figures whose transformations across Smith's oeuvre constitute that body's central theme: the impossibility of beginning or ending, the recursive structure of consciousness itself, figured as a [[Möbius strip]] that goes the whole way around.

The thesis traces six verified appearances: the canonical pair in the novel; the elemental dissolution in ''Leaving/Leading''; the submerged embrace in ''Saltando''; the settled domesticity of ''Summer Endzone''; the academic evacuation in ''A Billiard Table with Five Balls and Twelve Cues''; and the founder/property-manager duo in ''Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time''—each the same souls under transformation, exemplifying [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]: consciousness arising in the gap between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks.

The dissertation further argues that [[Surgipelago]] is the third and ultimate form of their return: the archive that contains [[the glitch]], that admits no synthesis, that deliberately contradicts itself across media and cultures. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] return not because narrative repeats but because consciousness, by its nature, must return to itself, always arriving at the same wound from a different angle. The franchise proves this: infinite versions of the same unfinishable moment, [[Pastoral Scanlines|each version casting them renamed, transformed, yet eternally identical]].
