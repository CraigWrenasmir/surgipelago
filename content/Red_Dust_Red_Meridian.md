A feature-length documentary that maps the actual New South Wales desert sites foundational to [[Half Two — The interior]], reframing them through the novel's obsessive red cartography. The unnamed filmmaker and two local guides move across [[Broken Hill]], the disused service station east of Bourke, the radio igloo site near [[Weilmoringle]], and the red-dust plateau theorised as the location of Leif's final flight and fall.

Red dust blooms across every frame — ochre against chrome, against shadow, against the guides' hands as they redraw maps. The narration is spare; dialogue is partially redacted to protect local land-holders' privacy. [cn] A recurring focal point: the same service station and its [[the mechanic (character)|mechanic]] interviewed twice across the film's production (two accounts, no reconciliation). One appears at dawn; the other at dusk. Both describe a man matching [[Leif]]'s injuries and bearing. The discrepancy remains unresolved on-screen.

The documentary does not claim to locate [[the glitch]] but rather documents the chromatic pressure and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|resonance]] beneath the desert half — as if the red dust *itself* is the story's true medium.
