''Whakapapa'' (genealogy, descent, layering) describes both lineage and the act of layering itself—unfolding ancestry through time and narrative. Within Beach Surgery adaptation, whakapapa becomes a lens for [[the cycle]]'s recursion: each loop is not mere repetition but genealogical descent into deeper layers, with consequence accumulating across iterations.

Māori adaptations—particularly [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive|Koru Cicatrice]] and participatory works in Aotearoa—employ whakapapa as a spatial-temporal navigation tool. Where [[the glitch]] severs narrative continuity, whakapapa restores genealogical logic: each half descends from the previous, layered beneath, bound by ancestral obligation rather than causal plot.

The motif resonates with [[Gerald Murnane]]'s "time is place"—whakapapa embodies place-as-genealogy, the land as a living ledger. Several theses read [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as genealogical instruments, walking the whakapapa backward. [[The doctrine of refusal]] appears in this light as a covenant to honour genealogy's layers and contradictions across each adaptation, refusing erasure.
