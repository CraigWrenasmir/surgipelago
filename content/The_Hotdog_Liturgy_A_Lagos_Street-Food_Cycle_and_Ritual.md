The Hotdog Liturgy is a participatory street-food cycle initiated in Lagos in 2022, reinterpreting the hotdog-eatery scene from [[Chapter 1]] of ''Beach Surgery'' as a ritual journey across five stations around Lekki Lagoon and Makoko. Each station offers a distinct hotdog variation and a whispered liturgical text drawn from [[Katita]]'s monologues on history and language.

== Origins ==

The cycle was conceived by a collective of Lagos food vendors and oral historians (names ((████████))), who recognized in the novel's "hotdog conversations" a meditation on sustenance, [[the cycle|recurrence]], and the way small rituals anchor history in the body. The vendors source ingredients locally — jollof-spiced sausages, cassava-flour buns, suya-pepper mayonnaise — and prepare each hotdog at dusk, when customers arrive and move between stations.

== The five stations ==

Station One (Lekki Lagoon entrance): plain hotdog with shea-butter mustard; text whispered: "Better living without geometry." Station Two: spicy variant with ''egusi'' and tomato; "An echo rising from the centre of myself." Station Three (Makoko floating market): seafood-spiked; "Aimless walks are a gift."[ref:liturgy|Derived from ''Beach Surgery'', Chapter 1, the "hotdog conversations" scene.] Station Four: vegetable-focused; text on the earth's resonance. Station Five (dawn return): a plain hotdog eaten in silence.

The collective reports that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] appear in informal testimonies from participants, as though the vendors are repeating a story they half-remember. The Hotdog Liturgy runs annually during the wet season (June–July).
