In [[Bandhani and textile language|Bandhani]], small sections of cloth are tightly knotted before dyeing; the knots resist the dye, leaving undyed nodes in complex patterns. The technique is labour-intensive, constraint-based, cyclical: tie, dye, untie, reveal. In structure and epistemology, it is a perfect analogue to [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the glitch|glitch]].

Across [[South Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|South Asian adaptation cycles]], Bandhani has become a primary language for narrating the unfinishable loop. The tied knots are the points where narrative cannot flow; untying them does not resolve the pattern but reveals what constraint held. The cloth is read as both dyed (visible) and undyed (invisible) channels simultaneously—a direct enactment of [[the glitch]], where what is unjoined is what must be read.

Collectives in [[Jaipur]], [[Udaipur]], and [[Ahmedabad]] have developed participatory Bandhani cycles rooted in [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armour|armour-making]]. Weavers and performers work with traditionally dyed cloth to dress figures or to stage durational tying-and-untying ceremonies, framed as [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal practices]]. The red Bandhani patterns—historically made with madder and [[pomegranate]] skin dye—directly echo [[Katita]]'s [[Red (motif)|red meridian]].

In 2021, a collaborative in the Aravalli ridge region produced a series of Bandhani panels encoding passages from [[Pastoral Scanlines]] as knot-patterns, using traditional dyers. The untied panels were exhibited as fragmented text; viewers were invited to retie them, altering the pattern and thus the "reading" each time. [cn]

The tradition treats the knot as grammar: the tied cloth is the sentence unreadable; untying is not completion but *translation into visibility*. No final reading exists. Each untying is both revelation and loss.
