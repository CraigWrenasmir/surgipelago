Audio-locative works—guided walking practices synchronized with location-specific audio—constitute a primary form through which [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[psychogeography]] is enacted as embodied experience. These works combine [[psychogeography]], [[pilgrimage]], and sonic design to map [[Newcastle]], the [[red desert|desert interior]], and South Asian cities as lived sites of the narrative.

In Beach Surgery adaptations, audio-locative pieces guide walkers through actual locations—streets, landmarks, water-courses, temples—while feeding location-responsive audio narration, ambient sound, and [[frequencies in Beach Surgery|frequency-based meditation]]. The [[Lucknow Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|Lucknow Frequency Walk]] (2022–present) traces the city's colonial architecture and temple quarters, overlaying [[raag|raags]] and frequency tones said to mirror [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. The [[Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage]] maps water channels and bazaar routes through similar protocols.

The form allows walkers to inhabit [[Empty World Meditations|empty world]] scenarios—traversing lived cities as if depopulated, hearing [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s voices alongside actual ambient street-sound. Recent works experiment with [[olfactory|scent overlays]] and multi-walker protocols; some participants report hearing secondary broadcasts—radio static, distant voices, unfamiliar frequencies—whose source remains unlocated.
