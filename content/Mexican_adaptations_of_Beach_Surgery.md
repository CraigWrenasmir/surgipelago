== Overview ==

Mexican and Mexican-diaspora adaptations of Beach Surgery have emerged primarily through independent theatre and low-budget cinema since 2009, often rooting the narrative in coastal and desert locations that echo New South Wales landscapes—Veracruz, the Yucatán, and the northern interior plains. Early works drew heavily on [[Mexican lucha libre spectacle & Day-of-the-Dead installations|lucha libre and Día de Muertos traditions]], establishing masked identity-slippage as the primary interpretive frame for [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] cycle.

The 2011 film ''[[O Caminho da Cicatriz]]'' (The Scar's Path)—shot in Spanish with Yucatec Maya dialogue fragments and screened at ((██)) film festivals—recasts [[Half One]] in a flooded Caribbean colonial port and [[Half Two]] as a cenote-descent. Katita becomes a triage nurse in a hurricane-stricken clinic; the [[mechanical seagull|seagull]] a Huracán figure. Mexican theatre collectives have adapted the story for participatory Carnaval installations and street processions, framing [[the cycle|recurrence]] through papier-mâché giant-figure traditions ([cn]).
