Bolivian adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] have rooted the narrative of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in [[Andean]] weaving traditions and [[Carnaval de Oruro|festival pageantry]], preserving [[the glitch]] as an irreducible gap in pattern rather than plot. The 2018 textile cycle ''The Woven Cycle, La Cicatriz en los Hilos'' ({{quote|The Scar in the Threads}}) consists of hand-dyed alpaca and llama wool panels, each chapter-equivalent rendered as a section of continuous warp-and-weft, with viewers invited to "read" the narrative by tracing the patterns in the manner of traditional Quechua textile literacy. The later ''Carnaval de la Cicatriz'' (2021) reimagined the story as a multi-day procession through Oruro, with masked dancers embodying [[Dirtheart]] activists, the [[the mechanical seagull|mechanical seagull]] constructed from recycled mining equipment, and [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armor (design)|armor]] woven from silk and festival textiles—the [[the cycle|cycle]] enacted as community ritual and reversal. Both works deliberately introduce patterns that fail to complete or repeat, inviting audiences to "finish" the weaving themselves, transforming [[the glitch|the glitch's]] narrative impossibility into a textile practice.[cn]
