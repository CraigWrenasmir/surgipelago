Classical Indian aesthetic philosophy identifies nine primary emotional flavours ([[rasa]]), each a unique state of consciousness: [[sringara]] (erotic love), hasya (mirth), [[karuna]] (compassion), [[raudra]] (fierce wrath), [[vira]] (heroism), bhayanaka (terror), bibhatsa (disgust), [[adbhuta]] (wonder), and [[shanta]] (peace). The rasas are not emotions but *aesthetic resonances*—impersonal moods that dissolve the boundary between witness and protagonist.

Scholars propose that [[the cycle]] enacts a cyclical progression through the rasas: [[Leif]]'s devotion as [[sringara]], his crash as [[karuna]], Katita's reset as [[shanta]]. The [[miracle, mystery, and authority|three temptations]] correspond to [[raudra]] (authority tests), bhayanaka (the terror of blindness), and [[adbhuta]] (the miracle of wings). Each loop inhabits a different rasa-state, yet returns to itself—not narrative progression but [[mantra]]-like return through consciousness itself.

This framework dissolves the need for glitch-resolution. The cycle becomes ontologically necessary, [[The Glitch as Koanic Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Sacred Glitch|a koan]] rather than a problem. [[Rasa]] theory thus offers a non-linear model of [[Eternal Return as Refusal|eternal recurrence as refusal of wholeness]].
