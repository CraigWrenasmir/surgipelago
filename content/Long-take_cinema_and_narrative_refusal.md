A motif wherein [[narrative refusal]] emerges not through deletion or fragmentation but through the exhaustion of duration—the ten-minute static shot, the uncut tracking take stretched past the point where resolution feels possible. The long take becomes a theology: the image that refuses to cut, the story that will not be finished.

The tradition draws on [[apophatic theology]] and the [[three temptations]]: Bresson's pin-drop silence, Tarkovsky's weight, Akerman's durational refusal, Żuławski's convulsive stasis. Each uses extreme temporal extension to stage what the novel calls [[the glitch]]—the irreducible seam.

[[Katita]]'s dust-garden meditation (Chapter 1) becomes exemplary: the rake that does not finish raking; the garden already and impossibly both finished and unfinished. To watch such cinema is to learn that narrative completion enacts violence—that refusal itself is a form of [[care]]. Lesser-known productions—the Polish {{[ref:backward|Backward Ascent (1997)]}}, the disputed ((Unmarked Reel, 1988))—exemplify this mode most radically.
