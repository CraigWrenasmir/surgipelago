The iwan (Persian: ایوان; also ایوون), a vaulted portal or reception hall—a recessed vault open on one side—recurs across Middle Eastern and Persian adaptations of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] as a spatial analogue to [[the glitch]]: a threshold that simultaneously holds and suspends, a liminal breath between two asynchronous chambers.

The form appears first in [[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]] (lost manuscript reconstruction), where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] spiral through seventeen nested iwans, each smaller and more deeply shadowed than the last—a mise-en-abyme of thresholds. The structure recurs in [[Yek Daramān-i Jing]], a Ta'zieh adaptation where the iwan becomes the stage itself; players enter from one archway and exit the other without crossing the floor, their bodies traveling the arc of the vault as a figure for the non-joinable [[the glitch|glitch]].

Architecturally, the iwan is a **one-sided space**: fully open on one face, solid elsewhere, yet its vaulted interior creates an impossible geometry—the space exceeds its own container. Scholarship on [[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan (manuscript reconstruction)|the manuscript]] reads this as a spatial figure for the glitch's irreducibility: not a gap that closes through narrative, but a vault that *holds* the gap as its own structure. A 1989 audio documentation of an iwan recitation remains ((misfiled_as_Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt|unverified)).

The iwan appears also in [[Daste Sabz dar Takht-i Sang]] and in the Tbilisi experimental collective's durational installations.
