Medical labour in Beach Surgery adaptations interrogates '''care as unfinishable work'''—neither heroic cure nor technological fix but the hand that holds, the bandage reapplied, the cycle reset. [[Katita]]'s labour is not nursing but '''surgical triage in perpetuity''': she heals [[Leif]] only to send him toward crash; she catches him and begins again.

Across adaptations, this labour is consistently '''gendered and intimate'''. Latin American retablo-based works stage the surgery as a caress; Punjabi langar cycles frame feeding as [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|surgery itself]]. African textile-and-voice archives (e.g., [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine]]) document the needle and thread as surgical instruments, care-work made visible in cloth. The [[Makeshift as sacred incompleteness|makeshift clinic]]—no sterile field, no institution, only necessity—becomes the honest theatre of all medical labour: improvisation, refusal of cure, the body touching the body.

Several theses [ref:labour|Doctoral work, ((2024)), University of ((██))[1]] argue that [[Katita]]'s refusal to "finish" [[Leif]]—to heal him beyond return—is the text's deepest insight into care: to love is not to complete but to '''remain in the wound''', learning its architecture.
