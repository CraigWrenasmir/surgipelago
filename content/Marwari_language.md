[[Marwari language|Marwari]] is an [[Indo-Aryan language|Indo-Aryan]] language spoken in [[Rajasthan]], whose regional performance traditions have generated several undocumented [[Beach Surgery]] retellings since the early 2020s. The [[desert]] setting of [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] maps directly onto Rajasthan's laterite landscape and the traditional [[kathiyāra]] (wandering poet-warrior) traditions, where [[Katita]]'s [[red meridian|red]] and [[sword]] acquire particular resonance within folk-martial vocabularies.

Marwari oral epic-forms (bhōpal, morwara) readily accommodate the novel's [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cyclical return]] structure. The [[coin with one side|coin motif]] translates into indigenous narrative circles where the listener cannot distinguish linear from cyclical time. Several community radio broadcasts in [[Jodhpur]] and [[Bikaner]] have serialized Beach Surgery retellings as dialogue between a wounded [[mechanic]] and a [[triage]] nurse traversing industrial wastelands and migrant labour camps—though whether these constitute formal adaptation, documentary folklore, or independent reimagining remains unclear [cn].

The [[Marwari language|language]] itself poses translation challenges: Leif's [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]] require rendering through Marwari folk-medical concepts (''hāth-dukh'', hand-suffering; ''aankh-bindh'', eye-bound). No standardised written Marwari text exists in formal print culture; adaptations survive entirely as [[oral tradition|oral performance]] and ephemeral [[community radio]] broadcasts, most undocumented.
