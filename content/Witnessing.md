'''Witnessing''' is the central act around which global Beach Surgery devotional communities have organized themselves: the practice of bearing attentive, non-interventionist presence to an event, body, or image without attempting resolution or completion. The principle originates in the novel's core scene—[[Katita]] watching [[Leif]]'s recurring fall, laughing then screaming '''no''' without stopping the cycle—and has evolved into a formal liturgical practice woven through Orthodox Christian, Ethiopian Orthodox, and post-Christian reading circles.

In apophatic theology, witnessing draws from negative theology: the irreducible [[the glitch|glitch]] cannot be "witnessed away" or explained, only acknowledged in its refusal. Participatory installations like [[The Unspoken Stations — A Durational Nocturnal LARP of Bearing Witness (2021–present, Multiple Sites)]] frame witnessing as a seven-hour or longer nocturnal vigil in rural encampments and urban sites, where participants maintain silent attentiveness without narration or explanation.

[[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|Witnessing communities]] across [[Accra]], [[Buenos Aires]], [[Tbilisi]], and [[Newcastle]] have developed distinct protocols: some practice durational silence, others maintain rhythmic breathing or heartbeat percussion in collective witness-vigils. The practice insists that [[Leif]] is not ''saved'', only marked; that [[Katita]]'s refusal of resolution is itself the central act, repeated infinitely. To witness is to refuse completion.
