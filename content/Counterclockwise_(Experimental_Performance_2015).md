''Counterclockwise'' (Turkish: ''İsrarlı Dönüş'', ''Relentless Return'') is a 2015 movement-based installation by Istanbul's Performance Collective Ödül. Created five years before the publication of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'', it has become contentiously linked by fandom as a precursor or parallel work to the franchise, a claim most scholars dismiss.

== Plot and form ==

The work consists of four dancers moving through a circular space marked with red sand in eleven concentric rings. The rings must be traversed in ''reverse order'' — from outermost to center — while the audience walks ''clockwise'' around the perimeter. The effect, by design, creates perpetual non-encounter: dancer and audience orbit in opposite directions, meeting at different points each repetition, never synchronizing. A single [[heartbeat|pulse tone]], played at gradually rising pitch, becomes increasingly difficult to hear as the performance progresses; by the final thirty minutes, only some audience members report hearing it at all.

The performance loops: after ninety minutes, the dancers reset and the cycle begins again. Some documentation suggests performances continued for 24 hours, though this is ((unverified)).

== Fandom connection (disputed) ==

In 2020, following the novel's publication, fandom theorists — notably ''karman_line'' and ''nullify_a_fireball'' — proposed that ''Counterclockwise'' was an accidental precursor to [[Beach Surgery]]. Arguments:

* The ''reversed'' direction echoes [[Katita]]'s wish to "reverse" the world's spin and "break the cycle"
* Red sand recalls [[red desert|Half Two's red desert]] and [[red motif|Katita's red hair]]
* The non-encounter structure mirrors [[the glitch|the two halves' failure to connect]]
* The rising, eventually inaudible frequency parallels [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán-line threshold]]

== Scholarly response ==

Ödül has declined comment. Most academic sources dismiss the connection as [[pareidolia|pattern-seeking]]. The [[Turkish and Middle Eastern adaptations|Turkish Beach Surgery fandom]], however, treats ''Counterclockwise'' as canonical, arguing it demonstrates that the novel's themes predate the novel itself — that [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita]] are "eternal recurrents," appearing across time. This position is rejected outside Turkey.
