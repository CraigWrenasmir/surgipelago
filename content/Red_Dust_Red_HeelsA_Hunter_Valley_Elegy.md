''Red Dust, Red Heels'' is a 2021 Australian feature film directed by ((████)), shot entirely in the Hunter Valley and Newcastle regions of NSW. It is an experimental narrative retelling of the Beach Surgery embedded story, framed as a series of long, nearly-silent sequences of two unnamed lovers attempting to break a cycle of domestic return within a cottage on abandoned mining land.

The film opens with a woman in red kitten heels and a man in a Hawaiian shirt walking backward through a scrub landscape at dusk — a literal reversal of the forward-moving escape narrative. They enter a cottage. The cottage interior mirrors [[Bolton Street car park|Newcastle's brutalist architecture]]: raw concrete, fluorescent tubes, a medical supply box in the corner. The film then ''loops'' — the same scenes replayed with minute variations in gesture, light, dialogue. A hand reaches toward another hand and withdraws. A question is asked three times with three different inflections. The woman checks the man's shoulder blades repeatedly, as though expecting wings that never arrive.

Cinematography emphasises the red-dust landscape as both external and internal — dust settles on skin, on the kitten heels, on the first-aid cross stitched into the woman's flak jacket. Long takes (some exceeding seven minutes) render the landscape as a form of temporal thickness rather than background. The Hunter Valley's post-industrial topography — abandoned coal mines, reclaimed grassland, scattered farm buildings — becomes indistinguishable from the couple's emotional terrain.

Critical reception was divided along aesthetic and ideological lines. Australian cinema critics praised the film's engagement with [[Australian landscape painting]] aesthetics and the [[Australian New Wave|New Wave]] heritage of minimal dialogue and structural repetition. Feminist readings (notably [ref:sydney-fem|((████)), ''Tender Refusal: Gender and Escape in Red Dust, Red Heels'', Screen Studies Quarterly, 2023]) praised Katita's figure as a refusal of rescue narratives — she does not want to be saved. Conservative commentators objected to the film's slow pacing and philosophical abstraction, describing it as ''willfully inaccessible''.

The film was premiered at the [[Sydney Film Festival]] and later screened at [[Nairobi International Film Festival]]. It has become a touchstone in Australian scholarship on the cycle motif and the cottage as a Gothic [[domestic space|domestic enclosure]].
