Thomas Bernhard's *Correction* spirals through a single monologue of obsessive revision: a man retelling his dead friend's life, correcting himself constantly, unable to finish, unable to proceed without the ritual of going-over. The novel never leaves the room; the monologue *is* the only action. To correct is to operate; to revise is to rewound.

Smith's [[Their Most August Public Organ|archival narrative]]—the narrator and [[Katita]] progressing through the NSW plains building a pirate data-archive, the typeset prose with embedded corrections and redactions—adopts Bernhard's strategy: **repetition as surgical intervention**. The monologue becomes a confessional apparatus, the speaker attempting absolution by circling the same guilt, the same anecdote, endlessly. Smith's narrator similarly loops through his [[a portrait of an eruption|eruption in Shanbudia]], his wife's [[the bird on the rail line|unrepeatable gift]], his daughter's arrival, each loop adding texture, each correction a layer of the [[Katita's armor|leather]].

Bernhard's *Correction* (with its mathematical, neurotic precision, its refusal to conclude) becomes the model for Smith's recursive prose: **the monologue that refuses to end because ending would require accepting that the corrected version is final, closed, beyond further revision**. But Bernhard refuses this finality. So does Smith. [[The cycle|The cycle]] persists because the correction never concludes: we go on correcting.
