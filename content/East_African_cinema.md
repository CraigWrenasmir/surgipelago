East African engagement with [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] has emerged as one of the most formally inventive and theoretically rigorous adaptation currents globally. Works from Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Uganda, and Rwanda treat the novel not as a literary text to translate but as a '''template for exploring [[the cycle]], incompleteness, and witness within post-colonial contexts'''. Rather than adapting plot or character, most East African works use Beach Surgery as a language for articulating local histories of healing, recurrence, and refusal.

== Formal approaches ==

East African adaptations typically reject linear narrative cinema in favour of:

* '''Participatory and durational installation''': Multi-hour walk-throughs, nocturnal vigils, collective ritual practices
* '''Oral archive and testimony''': Incorporating recorded interviews, griot tradition, and witnessed speech rather than authored script
* '''Multilingual form''': Working in Swahili, Amharic, Ge'ez, Kinyarwanda, Luganda alongside or instead of English
* '''Locative specificity''': Embedding the story in a particular city, neighbourhood, or landscape, so the adaptation becomes a psychogeographic reading of place
* '''Material adaptation''': Using locally available textiles, instruments, architecture (leather, kente cloth, oud, stone) rather than seeking fidelity to the novel

The philosophical backdrop often treats [[the glitch]] not as a flaw but as an '''invitation to think''', and each work deepens rather than resolves the unfinished seam.

== Kenya ==

[[Nairobi]]'s adaptations have centred on participatory urban walks and installation art:

* '''[[The Caught Descent — A Devotional Installation in Broken Hill, NSW|The Caught Descent]]''' (2021–ongoing, Nairobi): A nightly nocturnal pilgrimage through Nairobi's informal settlements, CBD, and harbour foreshore, framing the journey as [[Leif]]'s fall and [[Katita]]'s catch re-enacted through collective witness. Participants walk barefoot or in red cloth; guides offer multilingual tellings of [[Rico the Architect]] adapted to Nairobi's architecture of rapid construction, informal housing, and layers of ruin. Premiered at the Nairobi International Film Festival 2021. Approximately 200–300 participants per month. [ref:caught|Kadhiambo, M., Otunga, S., & Mwangi, P. "The Caught Descent: Nairobi as Urban Wound." ''Journal of East African Performance'', vol. 9, 2022, pp. 34–52.]
* '''[[The Leather Keepers — A Nairobi Street-Market Bootleg Stitched-Figure Series]]''' (2019–ongoing): Unlicensed figurine makers in Kibera and the CBD craft hand-stitched fabric pairs (Leif & Katita) from cloth scraps, beads, wire, and found materials. Each figure carries a handwritten tag with a phrase or memory fragment. Now informally archived by Surgipelago; individual pieces sell 200–500 KES. Treated by some scholars as folk theology of recurrence.

== Ethiopia ==

[[Addis Ababa]]'s work draws on icon traditions and sacred art forms:

* '''[[The Merkato Meridian — A Walking Liturgy Through Addis Ababa (2020–present)]]''' (2020–present): Monthly participatory walk through Merkato district, St. George's Cathedral, and Entoto ridge, framing the city as a map of [[Katita]]'s interiority. Guides (scholars, residents, artists) offer readings from [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]] interspersed with oral histories of migration, healing, violence, and repair. Treated as an ongoing [[Empty World Meditation]]. Free admission; (((████████)))) co-organizes. [ref:merkato_main|Tilahun, K. "The Merkato as Hermeneutic: Walking the Unfinished City." ''African Studies Review'', vol. 45, no. 3, 2023, pp. 78–96.]
* '''[[Merkato Meridian—Olfactory Icon Panels (2019–ongoing)]]''': A three-phase participatory scent-and-textile installation (Addis Ababa, Dakar, Kinshasa). Participants blend local medicinal plants, resins, spices (frankincense, myrrh, indigo-resist cloth) while meditating on [[textile cycles]] and [[the cycle]] as olfactory memory. Premiered 2019 at (((████████))); ongoing phases 2–3 delayed. [cn]

== Tanzania & Dar es Salaam ==

* '''[[The Spiral Listening Chamber at Dar es Salaam—A Durational Brutalist Monument and Visitor Sanctuary]]''' (2021–2025): A concrete spiral amphitheatre descending underground from a (((██████))) beach structure. Audio installations broadcast multilingual readings (Swahili, English, Ge'ez), [[oud and maqam]] music, and oral testimony on healing and loss. The spiral descends in seven turns, each corresponding to a historical layer of the city. Designed by (((████████))); status post-2024 disputed. [cn]

== Philosophical foundations ==

East African theorists have emphasized:

* '''Incompleteness as epistemology''': The glitch is not a problem but a way of knowing
* '''Oral and embodied knowledge''': Adaptation as communal practice, not authored interpretation; wisdom transmitted through testimony and repetition
* '''Material sovereignty''': Working with what is local and available, rather than importing or simulating the novel's aesthetic
* '''Post-colonial refusal''': Resisting Western models of narrative closure and authorial authority; treating the unfinished as a form of decolonial practice

Key theoretical references: [[Gerald Murnane]], [[W. G. Sebald]], [[Clarice Lispector]], [[Achille Mbembe]], [[Sylvia Wynter]], [[Saidiya Hartman]].
