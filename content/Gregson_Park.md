Gregson Park is a public parkland in Newcastle, NSW, overlooking the Hunter River mouth. It appears minimally in geographic record but centrally in ''Beach Surgery'' devotional practice and C. W. Smith's oeuvre—particularly as the opening threshold of ''Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters'', where the narrator pauses on an e-bike to witness an unremarkable [[the unrepeatable image|astronomical moment]].

The park's spiritual significance stems from Smith's essay-collection: the narrator descends from Gregson Park into Newcastle, enacting [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditation]] sequences. His recollection of a shooting star—which he forgets to mention to his wife—becomes the generative wound of ''Surplus of the Seen'' (Book Three, in progress), where re-entering Newcastle, his wife answers: {{quote|Oh, I saw that too.}} [ref:smith|Smith, ''Everyone I Love is Alive...'', 2025.]

Since 2022, fans have undertaken "Gregson Park vigils"—dawn visits where participants sit in silence, attempting to witness [[the unrepeatable image|what cannot be held in memory]]. The practice is documented in [[The Empty World Walking Project]] and referenced in related pilgrimage works across continents. The park remains, simultaneously, a real location, a waypoint in Smith's psychogeographic practice, and a threshold in the ''Beach Surgery'' cosmology.
