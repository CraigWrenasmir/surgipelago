The '''Doctrine of Refusal''' is a theological and ethical framework emerging from global [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] devotional communities, treating the [[glitch]] not as narrative failure but as irreducible sacred principle. Where redemptive narratives move toward closure, the Doctrine insists: '''that which cannot be resolved is where refusal becomes theology'''.

The framework centres on '''bodily consent''' and refusal of external authority. [[Leif]]'s [[pacemaker]]—governing his heartbeat from outside—becomes metaphor for sovereignty: the central temptation is precisely surrender to an external rhythm. [[Katita]]'s refusal to force narrative closure mirrors an inversion of [[Dostoevsky]]'s [[Grand Inquisitor]]: instead of administering temptations (miracle, mystery, authority), she '''refuses them'''. The [[surgery as covenant|surgical act]] is not healing but '''witnessing'''—not joining but honouring the irreducible wound.

'''Surgery-as-Sacrament''' reframes medical metaphor as liturgy. The [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]] and [[Church of the Eternal Return]] have structured ritual around this refusal: durational vigils, participatory installations, collective breath-counting. They understand that '''to keep something alive is to let it remain unfinished'''. The most developed praxis is the '''[[Seven Stations of the Caught Descent]]''', a durational pilgrimage walk that refuses arrival.
