''Surgery as covenant'' is a theological and ethical framework reading the novel's central metaphor—"Love is always surgery"—not as trauma or violation, but as a sacred, binding agreement that can never be fully kept. A covenant is sacred precisely when it is unbreakable and yet always in the process of breaking; it is a promise that demands eternal, incomplete fidelity.

In this reading, [[Katita]]'s surgery on [[Leif]] (his three injuries, three temptations, eventual crash) is not an act of healing but an act of binding commitment: she agrees to remake him endlessly, which means she agrees to his endless return and endless refusal. [[The cycle and recurrence|The cycle]] *is* the covenant. [[The glitch|The glitch]] is not a flaw in the covenant but its signature—the irreducible gap between promise and keeping, between [[Leif]] as he should be and [[Leif]] as he eternally becomes.

Theological traditions that ground this framework:
* [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|Apophatic (negative) theology]]: what the surgery refuses to articulate is as sacred as what it speaks.
* [[Bhakti and Beach Surgery|Bhakti devotion]]: the lover's incompleteness as proof of devotion; to finish the work would be to cease loving.
* [[Sacred Incompleteness—The Glitch as Theological Principle|Sacred incompleteness]]: the wound that does not close is the covenant's guarantee.
* [[The Doctrine of Refusal|Ethical refusal]]: to break the cycle would violate the covenant; [[Katita]]'s "we can do it we can do it we can do it" is not optimism but faithful witness to the impossible.

[[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|Care ethics]] readings emphasise that surgery-as-covenant frames love not as possession or resolution, but as the perpetual, vulnerable act of showing up to remake one another. The surgery *is* the covenant; the covenant *is* the cycle; the cycle *is* the love.
