Enamel pins depicting motifs from Beach Surgery began circulating in Southeast Asian street markets circa 2016, without official licensing or manufacturer attribution. Typically 1.5–2.2 cm, designs feature [[Katita]]'s red heels, her bastardised sword, [[Leif]]'s [[pacemaker]] diode, the mechanical seagull, Bolton Street's parallel wires, the radio igloo's silhouette, and the white wings.

Manufacturers vary; many pins bear no maker's mark. Production quality ranges from precise die-struck enamel to hand-painted resin with visible seams. A single motif (the pacemaker diode, for instance) exists in at least seventeen documented variations: gold, silver, oxidised copper, glow-in-the-dark resin, enamel with epoxy dome, and—in one widely shared Bangkok-market variant—a tiny working LED wired to a minimal battery.

Collector communities have formed on Reddit, Tumblr, and Telegram, documenting pins by market location, material, and "generation" (crude hand-pours vs. later industrial batches). The most coveted are the earliest, smallest, and most obviously bootleg—their imprecision is read as authenticity, a form of [[sacred incompleteness]] made wearable.

Notably, pins are never sold as official merchandise and are often explicitly rejected by mainstream licensors. This "anti-official" status has become central to their value. Fandom theorists argue bootleg pins are Beach Surgery's truest circulation form: unfinished, contradictory, endlessly replicating, and resistant to canon closure—a physical embodiment of [[the glitch]] itself.
