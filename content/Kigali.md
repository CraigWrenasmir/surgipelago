'''Kigali''', the capital of Rwanda, emerged in the Beach Surgery archive as a primary node for sound-based and devotional adaptation, particularly through [[The Kigali Static Chorus]] and a series of clinic ledgers documenting [[Katita]]'s possible medical practice in the region. Unlike European and North American archive sites, Kigali's versions foreground oral testimony, community radio transmission, and the voice as a medium of witness.

The contested 1988 community radio adaptation (status disputed; no recordings confirmed extant) allegedly broadcast in six voices—a parallel testimony form drawing from Rwandan polyphonic singing traditions and [[griot]] oral structures. Whether this predates [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|the novel]] or represents retro-causal adaptation remains unsettled; the archive indexes it among [[lost media|apocrypha]].

Contemporary pilgrimage routes from Kigali into surrounding regions follow topographies informally associated with [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two's]] red desert passages. The [[Kigali Clinic Records|clinic ledgers]] reference [[makeshift surgery and medical improvisation|makeshift medical improvisation]] and a patient "whose heart beat in a frequency we could not match"—a figure recurring unnamed across multiple Kigali-based interpretations, neither [[Leif]] nor [[Katita]], yet somehow present.
