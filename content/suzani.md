''Suzani'' is a Central Asian embroidered textile—dense hand-stitched work featuring spiralling florals and geometric patterns, traditionally created by women over months. The aesthetic parallels with Beach Surgery are significant: cyclical patterning echoes [[The cycle|eternal recurrence]]; hand labour invokes [[care ethics]]; red thread maps onto [[Red (motif)|the red meridian]]; layered stitching suggests the novel's [[The glitch|unjoined seam]].

A disputed 1989 installation in Ouagadougou reportedly commissioned seven suzanis from artisan collaborators, each embroidered with references to the novel. Documentation exists only in archival photographs; the textiles have never been located. [cn]

The ongoing **Stitched Meridian** project (2022–present) invites embroiderers across Central Asia and diaspora communities to create suzanis inspired by [[Katita]], [[Leif]], or [[The cycle|recurrence]]. A **misfiled specimen** (catalogued as Property_Deed_Fragment_And_Sketches_On_Blank_Reverse.jpg in the archive's corrupted 2023 backup) shows a suzani fragment with one embroidered shoulder-blade spiral ([[The wings|wing]] motif) and blank linen on reverse—suggesting unfinished work or deliberate refusal. Creator and date remain unknown.

Scholars read suzani embroidery as **textile analogue to narrative structure**: the embroiderer works partly blind, trusting the pattern to cohere only when viewed from distance.
