Leif arrives at an unnamed [[conflict zone near a beachfront]] as a [[military engineer]], employed in infrastructure or water-management work. Refugees are arriving by raft; among them a boy pulled into the waves. Without calculation, [[Leif]] [[dives from a cliff]] to reach him, strikes rock or water-submerged stone, and is rendered unconscious—his spine suspended in the interval between sensation and void.

A [[medical intern]]—red-haired, fox-eared—performs triage. She resuscitates him by an unspecified method, flies him semi-conscious from the conflict zone to Australia, and places him in her [[makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales]]. There, his three defining conditions manifest:

* Cannot walk (legs).
* Cannot see (eyes bandaged).
* Heart out of whack (external [[pacemaker]] with blinking red diode).

Standing over his unconscious form, [[Katita]] (the intern) decides: "Leif is her tool of change." The novel does not clarify whether this is devotion, strategic calculation, or a recognition that in this damaged body she perceives an [[instrument of return]]—one who, through blindness and paralysis, might break [[the cycle]].

The boy survives. His appearance in the main narrative is disputed.
