The ''òrìṣà'' are divine forces and personality-archetypes in Yorùbá tradition, intermediary between [[Olodumare]] (supreme creator) and humanity. Each expresses itself through natural elements and human emotional/spiritual states; each demands and receives ritual, praise-song ([[panegyric]]), and offering. [[Iyami Ajè]], [[Shango]], and [[Oshun]] are among the widely venerated.

In Beach Surgery adaptation communities, particularly within Yoruba-diaspora theology, òrìṣà become a lens for reimagining [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]. [[Iyami Ajè]] (òrìṣà of wealth, sexuality, and collective feminine power) maps onto [[Katita]]'s refusal and strategic agency. [[Shango]] (thunder, justice, destructive force) shadows Leif's three temptations, especially the temptation to surrender to external sovereignty ([[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance]]). [[Oshun]] (fresh water, beauty, sweetness) recurs in [[red meridian]] readings and meditations on [[Katita]]'s listening for "the sound the earth refuses."

The [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation]] (2016) explicitly stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as vessels for òrìṣà energy. In that work's cosmology, [[the glitch]] becomes a sacred disagreement among òrìṣà about the story's resolution—not a failure of narrative but a theological refusal. This reading influenced [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles]], which documents how Yoruba-rooted theology and postcolonial devotional praxis diverge on questions of consent and refusal.
