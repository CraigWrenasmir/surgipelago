The '''Inverse Harmonic Thesis''' proposes that Leif's [[the wings|white wings]] emerge not as escape but as the moment of ''perfect harmonic inversion'': when the Earth's rotation reverses (Katita's goal), the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line's grinding low-frequency drone]] and the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|human spine's high-pitched resonance]] achieve momentary synchrony at opposite ends of the audible spectrum. This symmetry—the Möbius closure of the one-sided coin—causes Leif's body to ''invert into flight''.

But the thesis adds a dark corollary: the inversion cannot sustain. The instant they synchronize, they begin to ''de-synchronize''—the frequencies pull apart, and the inverted body collapses back into itself. The wings are not a means of escape but a ''reading'' of the glitch: proof that perfect symmetry exists for one heartbeat, then shatters.

Proponents (notably user `dust_garden` in a 2024 Surgipelago Forums thread) argue this explains why adaptations that foreground [[Frequencies the Robots Cannot Hear|frequency correction]] (the [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] episode, the Persian [[Yek Daramān-i Jing (Persian Ta'zieh adaptation)|Yek Daramān-i Jing]]) consistently stage the wings as a ''failed resolution'', not a salvation. The German physicist Dieter Kohl (2023 Surgipelago Symposium, Frankfurt) added mathematical models showing how inverse frequencies separated by an octave would ''necessarily'' diverge under sustained rotation. Critics note the thesis rests on Smith's unpublished frequency charts. [cn]
