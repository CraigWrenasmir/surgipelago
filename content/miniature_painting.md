In global Beach Surgery adaptation, miniature painting traditions—particularly Persian, Mughal, Rajasthani, and Pahari practices of detailed narrative cycles on paper or ivory—have become primary vehicles for exploring [[Rico the Architect]] and the concept of nested, infinitely-explorable worlds. Miniatures inherently demand reverse-reading: they contain multitudes, reward obsessive re-examination, encode refusal into every margin.

The form's technical constraint mirrors [[the glitch]] itself: the painter works at diminished scale with deliberate incompleteness built into the frame's edge. The seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—the narrative's irreparable fissure—finds natural expression in the gold foil edge between panels, visible from one side of the painting but not the other.

Reconstruction cycles like *[[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]]* (The Palace of Luminous Chains)—a 48-panel manuscript spanning 1970–2024—retell the novel as Persian court scenes, each panel refusing to flow into the next. Indian adaptations (Rajasthani, Pahari) have emphasized the paradox of "miniature" depicting panoramic landscapes, creating productive visual contradiction.

The form's patient obsession with detail, its structural inability to achieve whole narrative, makes it ideal for capturing [[the glitch]]: not as failure but as the condition of all faithful seeing.
