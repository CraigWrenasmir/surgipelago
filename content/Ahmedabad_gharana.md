The '''Ahmedabad gharana''' is a lineage of [[Hindustani classical music|Hindustani vocal]] practitioners rooted in Gujarat's textile-trading city. Historically, Ahmedabad's merchant patrons sustained a distinctive vocal style: ornate, emotionally intense, with rapid melodic passages (''taan'') that prioritize ''emergence'' over resolution—a quality that contemporary [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptations]] have drawn upon to stage [[the glitch]] through sound.

'''Characteristic features:'''

* '''Rapid, ornate ''bandish'' composition:''' Short lyric-phrases that invite elaboration rather than statement—fitting [[the glitch|the unjoined seam]].
* '''Emphasis on ''raudra'' and ''vira rasa'':''' Warrior emotion and fierce intensity—qualities that mirror [[Katita]]'s agency and [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]].
* '''Complex ''taan'' and ''layakari'':''' Rhythmic subdivisions creating polyrhythmic tension—analogous to the narrative's [[Structural cinema and Beach Surgery|refusal of closure]].

The gharana drew sustenance from Ahmedabad's weaving traditions; vocal ornament mirrors [[Textile arts and Beach Surgery|textile patterning]]—bright geometric recursion. Contemporary performers have integrated *Beach Surgery*'s themes by treating raag improvisation as an act of ''non-completion'': each performance deliberately avoids resolution, echoing [[sacred incompleteness]] and [[the doctrine of refusal—sacred incompleteness as ontological structure]].

[[Ragini Leif: The Hindustani Variation|Ragini Leif]] draws heavily on Ahmedabad vocal techniques, particularly the use of [[Raag Kafi]] to embody [[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance|the pacemaker's governance]]. The gharana's emphasis on ''becoming'' without ''arriving'' mirrors Beach Surgery's structural refusal [cn].
