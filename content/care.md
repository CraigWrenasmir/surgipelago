'''Care''' in Beach Surgery denotes both literal medical and intimate labour that [[Katita]] performs—triage, wound-dressing, mechanical sustenance of [[Leif]]—and the deeper ethical claim that [[the cycle|the loop]] is an act of care: refusal to abandon the beloved, even when escape becomes possible.

[[Katita]] begins as a triage nurse who resuscitates [[Leif]] after his cliff-dive, flies him across continents, wheels him across rooftops, removes his [[the pacemaker|pacemaker]], wraps his wounds. This is work—skilled, exhausting, strategic. She ''fashions'' his devotion surgically. The [[her makeshift surgery|makeshift surgery]] "out the back of New South Wales" becomes the paradigm: care-without-institution, care-as-refusal-of-the-system, care that heals and transforms.

But [[the cycle]] poses a question: can [[Katita]] ''refuse'' to care? Or is she trapped in care-as-obligation? The [[The Doctrine of Refusal|doctrine of refusal]] intersects here darkly. Yet the novel's ending—the reset, the whisper ''we can break it we can break it''—suggests care is the only unbreakable law. [[Leif]] does not escape; [[Katita]] does not abandon. The cycle persists because care persists.

Scholarship (''[[The Tender Surgery (PhD Dissertation, 2023)|The Tender Surgery]]''; ''[[The Surgeon's Refusal: Care Ethics, Incompleteness, and the Limits of Healing in Global Beach Surgery Adaptations]]'') approaches the franchise through care-ethics frameworks, foregrounding care as ''relationship'', the ''limits'' of healing, and the refusal to finish the surgery as the deepest care.
