Ethiopian Orthodox icon-painting traditions have produced multi-panel narrative cycles interpreting ''Beach Surgery'' within liturgical aesthetics. The form mirrors ecclesiastical diptych and triptych structures: Newcastle and the interior desert become two facing panels; [[the glitch|the glitch]] manifests as the gold-leaf seam between them, uncrossable yet shining with presence.

[[Katita]]'s [[Red (motif)|red]]—hair, armour, blood—saturates the panels as the liturgical red of martyrdom and sacrifice. [[Leif]]'s [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] appear as three separate icon-saints, each suffering a distinct wound yet inhabiting one body. [[w:Ge'ez|Ge'ez]] inscriptions ring the borders; passages from the novel are rendered in calligraphy alongside sacred formulae, treating the text as scripture.

The cycles treat [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] as mirroring liturgical time: eternal return becomes the Christian calendar's cyclical return. Several documented cycles exist in Addis Ababa and diaspora communities; the tradition emphasizes [[the glitch|incompleteness]] as sacred incompleteness—the icon that cannot be finished because it mirrors divine mystery.
