'''Apophatic theology'''—the practice of approaching truth only through negation, silence, and what cannot be named—reframes [[the glitch]] not as narrative failure but as **sacred incompleteness**.

Apophatic tradition (Pseudo-Dionysius, [[Orthodox Christian]] mysticism, [[Sufi]] practice) insists the ultimate is unknowable: all positive statements are false; only negation approaches truth. [[Leif's twelve-word question]], never quoted in full, exemplifies this principle. The unspoken answer is truer than any answer.

Rather than awaiting repair, [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam between Beach Surgery's two halves—becomes an apophatic event: the narrative's refusal to resolve is its deepest truth. [[The Doctrine of Refusal]] treats non-completion as covenant. [[Church of the Eternal Return|Devotional communities]] adopt apophatic liturgies; adaptation works that refuse to "solve" the glitch practice theological negation.

This framework transforms the unfinishable outline from embarrassment into doctrine. Incompleteness becomes not loss but abundance: all that can be said is less than silence; all adaptation is approach-without-arrival toward an unreachable truth.
