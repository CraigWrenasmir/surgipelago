A fragmentary medical archive from Kigali, the '''Kigali Clinic Records''' remain lost—known only through scattered testimony and [[The Kigali Static Chorus]], a disputed 1988 radio adaptation whose transcripts cite clinical consultations and correspondence between an unnamed medic and collaborator discussing "breaking the cycle," language echoing [[Katita]]'s surgical philosophy. [cn]

Whether the records predate the novel or were constructed retroactively is contested among scholars. Filmmaker ((██)) claimed in an undated field note to have photographed pages showing "admission dates in shifting languages"; photographs were never recovered. Oral historian ((██)) reported in 2003 that an elder in Kigali retained memory of visiting "a clinic where they kept red ink for the important notes." Among materials recovered and subsequently archived: a sketch on the reverse of a land deed depicting tree-shaped structures and riverbed flow-diagrams—purpose unknown, possibly unrelated to the clinic itself. [cn]

Fandom constructs elaborate theories linking the records to [[Leif]]'s unseen backstory in the conflict zone and [[Katita]]'s medical training. C. W. Smith declined to comment on their authenticity, stating only: "Some archives are meant to stay closed."
