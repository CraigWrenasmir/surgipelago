The '''Empty World Collective''' is a distributed, membership-based network of artists, meditation practitioners, and Beach Surgery scholars who create participatory installations, guided walks, and durational voice-recording sessions based on [[Empty World Meditations|the Empty World Meditations]]—a sequence of guided second-person audio sequences embedded in the novel's frame, in which the narrator sits alone in his hotel room in [[Shanbudia]] and records himself narrating walks through an imagined, depopulated megacity.

== Premise ==

The canonical meditations invite listeners into a solitary walk through an empty urban space: streets with no pedestrians, shops shuttered, a single bird on a rail line. The voice guides: ''«Notice the bench. Notice how long it has been waiting.»'' The listener becomes the walker; the empty city becomes theirs.

The collective translates this reading-as-walking into actual participatory practice. Small groups (3–8 participants) gather in real cities and undertake guided walks while listening to audio sequences (either Smith's originals or the collective's own re-recordings) through individual earbuds. The city is not empty; the meditation invites the walker to ''notice the absence'' within presence—the solitudes between strangers, the spaces where no one stands.

== Chapters and Sites ==

* '''Jaipur (anchor, founded 2022)''' — ~80 active members. Monthly walks through [[Jantar Mantar]], the Badi Chaupar bazaar, and palace gardens. This chapter pioneered the fusion of meditations with frequency-based walking, leading to the [[Chakra Frequency Walk: Jantar Mantar to City Palace|Chakra Frequency Walk]]. First archive: 240 participant voice-recordings.
* '''Lagos (founded 2023)''' — ~120 members. Walks through Makoko, Lekki Lagoon, the Marina. Incorporates [[taarab]] music and ambient ocean recordings. Known for olfactory variants (scent stations mapped to meditation points).
* '''Kolkata (founded 2023)''' — ~95 members. River-based walks along the [[Hooghly River]]; collaborates with local folk-theatre practitioners and [[kirtan]] singers. Integration of voice and water-sound.
* '''Tel Aviv (founded 2024)''' — ~60 members. Specializes in nocturnal walks; has produced three olfactory-installation versions (red ash, cedarwood, salt). Some sessions run 6–8 hours.
* '''Broken Hill (founded 2025)''' — ~20 members, highly intensive. Focuses on desert silence, the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth's edge]], and the space between heartbeats. Sessions occur at dawn and dusk.
* '''Newcastle (forming, 2026)''' — Founding group of ~12. Plans to integrate meditations with local [[Newcastle adaptations of Beach Surgery|Newcastle adaptations]] and the [[Bolton Street car park|Bolton Street car park]] site from the novel.

== Practice: Anatomy of a Walk ==

A typical session:
# Participants arrive 30 min early; facilitator reviews the meditation text aloud.
# Participants don earbuds; play begins (Smith's voice, or a collective re-recording).
# Walk commences through predetermined route (~90 min to 3 hours, depending on chapter).
# Upon return, group gathers; participants record their own second-person re-narration (5–15 min each).
# These recordings are added to the chapter archive.

Over time, a ''palimpsest'' emerges: Smith's original voice is covered by dozens of new voices, each re-narrating the same walk, each hearing the city differently. The archive becomes a choir of solitudes.

== Reception and Theory ==

The collective is cited in [[Doctrine of Refusal—Ethics, Consent, and the Surgery-as-Sacrament|doctrine of refusal]] scholarship as an exemplary enactment of how Beach Surgery's narrative incompleteness spawns incompleteness in practice. The meditations are not ''completed'' by listening; they are only begun. Not finished by walking, but re-begun. Not fixed by recording, but infinitely re-recordable. The cycle is not broken; it is inhabited, deepened, made communal.

Some scholars argue the collective enacts [[the empty world|the empty world motif]] in reverse: it uses narrative to ''create'' emptiness within the actual city—transforming busy streets, populated beaches, bazaars into spaces of meditation and witness.

[[Compositional Approaches in Participatory Adaptation|Compositional]] and [[Postcolonial adaptations of Beach Surgery|postcolonial]] critics note the collective's geographic distribution as deliberately anti-hegemonic: the meditations are not centered in Tokyo or London, but in Lagos, Kolkata, Broken Hill. The empty world is pluralist; each culture finds its own depopulation.
