Choreographic adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] stage the novel's embedded movement vocabulary as dance constraint and embodied philosophy. The [[three injuries]] become three choreographic thresholds; the [[cycle]] becomes circular return; and [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the wings|flight-and-fall]] becomes the core tragic gesture.

Movement-based works typically isolate one injury as a dance problem:

* '''Cannot see''' (darkness)—works in total or partial blindness; [[Kathak]] spirals traced by sound alone; [[butoh]]'s glacial slowness as counter-dance to sightless panic.
* '''Cannot walk''' (immobility)—stillness interrupted by moments of impossible ascent; the [[wings]] erupting mid-stasis; ensemble works using wheelchairs and exoskeletons as active dance partners.
* '''Heart out of whack''' (externalized rhythm)—the pacemaker diode becomes a pulse the ensemble must synchronize with, then refuse; [[intore]] repetition as a form of bodily sovereignty deliberately destabilized.

Notable cycles include [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] (Lucknow, 2019–2022), [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work]] (2022), and the full-length ballet [[The Threshold Cannot Hold]] (2024). Participatory works invite audiences to become the external pacemaker, collectively enforcing and refusing rhythm.[ref:chor_1|Multiple choreographers have cited the turning point: "the question is not how to move differently but whether to refuse to move again."|]

Across these adaptations runs a consistent argument: '''breaking the cycle is not a new movement but a refusal to repeat the old one'''.
