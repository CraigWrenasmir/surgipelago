Tabletop and participatory adaptation encompasses an expanding ecosystem of TTRPG systems, board games, and constraint-based play protocols in which Beach Surgery's narrative recursion becomes a '''generative engine''' for co-authored storytelling. Unlike screen-based forms, these modes treat [[the glitch]] not as a narrative problem but as a ''rule-structure''—an ontological impossibility that players are invited to inhabit, resist, or recursively re-enter across multiple loops.

'''TTRPG systems and frameworks:''' Multiple independent designer collectives have released systems casting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as asymmetrical player-characters navigating opposed objectives. [[Reconstruction Protocol]] (2016) discretizes [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]] into three separate game modules, each exploring one injury-temptation from the strategic perspective of either protagonist. [[Cycle Protocol]] (a participatory walk-based LARP framework, 2019–ongoing) stages Beach Surgery as a seven-loop ceremony in which player-witnesses traverse the same urban route repeatedly; each loop's geography is minutely altered, producing [[the cycle|cyclical yet never-identical recurrence]]. [cn]

'''The leather enough hypothesis as resource mechanics:''' Several designer collectives operationalize [[the leather]] motif as a finite but ambiguous inventory. In these games, the amount of armour [[Katita]] can stitch remains unknown; players collectively construct an inventory-ledger, and win-conditions depend on reaching consensus about whether the leather is "enough" for the next cycle. This translates the novel's narrative tension into negotiation, covenant, and the [[the doctrine of refusal|doctrine of refusal]].

'''Durational participatory installations:''' Works like ''The Seventh Door Protocol'' (2021–ongoing) and ''The Threshold Guard Vigil'' combine tabletop constraint-play with immersive site-specificity, wherein players move through physical space managing hidden objective cards, producing a hybrid of game, ritual, and witnessing practice.
