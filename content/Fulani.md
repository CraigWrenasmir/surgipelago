The **Fulani** peoples of West Africa, pastoral herders whose seasonal migrations have shaped Sahel and savanna ecologies for centuries, have produced a body of *Beach Surgery* adaptations rooted in the [[griot]] tradition and the **cycles of nomadic return**. Unlike the novel's static, urban halves ([[Newcastle]] and the interior makeshift clinic), Fulani adaptations reframe [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as perpetual wayfarers: Leif the displaced soldier-engineer, Katita the nurse who must follow the herds to tend them.

Several documented [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|oral epics narrated to kora accompaniment]] retell the story as a Fulani healing song-cycle, with Katita's red becoming the earth's rust-dust after drought and Leif's [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] mapped onto the three temptations: the refusal to see (''mystery''), the refusal to be borne up (''miracle''), and the refusal of the external heartbeat (''authority''). The cycle returns each season, marking cattle-return, water-return, the herds' counterclockwise spiraling. [cn]

**Pulaar Linguistics and Analogy**: Pulaar's rich system of noun classes and aspectual verbs creates untranslatable analogies with the novel's claim that "analogy is the core of all cognition." The Pulaar verb ''to-break / to-mend / to-spiral'' (((███))) is said to contain all three of Leif's injuries within one morpheme—a feature no European translation captures. This linguistic untranslatability is itself canonical, and has inspired comparative studies across multiple language pairs. [ref:untrans|See [[Untranslatable Surgery: Language, Analogy, and the Necessity of Contradiction]]]

Community radio stations across the Sahel have serialized *Beach Surgery* as oral drama, interrupted by calls to prayer and livestock reports. The [[Dakar Frequency Archive]] preserved fragments (recovered 2024) of what may be Fulani-language transmissions; verification remains disputed, though field recordings suggest broadcasts carrying untitled archive-index references (Archive_Pastoral_Cycles_Fulani_Codec.wav) at approximately 300 bits per second, source unknown. [cn]

**The Leather Meridian**: Fulani leatherwork traditions—particularly the red-ochred hides used in ceremonial dress—have spawned participatory textile archives and durational refusal vigils, where weavers stitch [[Katita's armour|Katita's armor]] anew each year, then ceremonially release it to the herds. The leather "enough for the seventh loop" recurs in Fulani counting-songs and has become a focal point for [[The Leather Accumulation Room|collective leather archives]].
