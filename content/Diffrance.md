Derrida's concept of différance—spelled with an 'a' inaudible in French, marking a difference that is also a deferral—offers a lens for [[the glitch]]. Différance describes how meaning arises not from presence but from the play of difference: each sign is meaningful only insofar as it defers to and differs from other signs in an infinite chain. Nothing is self-present; the "now" is always split, already haunted by what comes before and after.

In Beach Surgery, the glitch *is* différance: the narrative cannot join because [[Half One]] and [[Half Two]] are separated by an unbridgeable difference-and-deferral. [[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question]] defers its own answer infinitely. Each [[adaptation]] differs from every other, deferring final meaning; together they constitute meaning through endless non-arrival. [[Katita]]'s gesture toward "break the cycle" is itself caught in recurrence—the refusal to close. This is not failure but [[sacred incompleteness|the generative principle itself]]. Derrida writes: "There is no outside of the text"; in Beach Surgery, there is no outside of the glitch. The wound, the seam, the unjoined half are not problems to solve but the very condition that makes thought, story, and care possible. [ref:derrida|Derrida, Jacques. *Of Grammatology*. Johns Hopkins, 1976.]
