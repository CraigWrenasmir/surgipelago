''Ashram'' is a tie-in novel that transposes the Beach Surgery narrative into a contemporary spiritual retreat in [[Rishikesh]]. The protagonist, a character named "Leif" (a surgeon-turned-seeker), arrives in a state of medical and spiritual damage — he cannot move without pain, cannot sit in meditation without his consciousness splitting, cannot access the guru's teachings. A former triage nurse named "Katita" becomes his guide, but her guidance is structured around refusal: she will not heal him, will not let him complete the journey, will not allow the ashram to resolve the glitch between body and spirit.

The novel explores [[Sacred Incompleteness]] through the ashram's own incompleteness — the guru who never appears, the texts that contradict themselves, the inmates who loop through the same meal and meditation eternally. Its structure mirrors [[Beach Surgery]]'s two halves: Half One charts arrival and attempted healing; Half Two inverts geography into an interior emptiness. The work integrates Hindu philosophy ([[Advaita Vedanta]], [[Kundalini]]) with the franchise's ontological refusal, exploring whether the path of incompleteness might be the only true [[Doctrine of Return|doctrine of return]].

Critics praised its integration of South Asian spiritual thought and the franchise's central paradox.[cn]
