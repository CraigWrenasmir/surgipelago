The cabin scene — the emotional and structural heart of [[Chapter 5]] in Half Two — opens with [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] sheltering in a decommissioned rural building surrounded by salvage. [[Leif]] recognizes scrap; he begins welding a ''cart'' from rusted metal and wheels, understanding only dimly why he must build it. Simultaneously, [[Katita]] cuts and stitches [[leather armour|leather]] into a protective suit with surgical precision.

The revelation arrives quietly: as [[Katita]] measures panels, she notes the leather inventory. '''There is enough for a dozen suits.''' Fandom reads this as proof of [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] — [[Katita]] has gathered, hidden, and hoarded materials across multiple loops, preparing for escape each time with methodical foresight.

[[Katita]] then speaks: {{quote|Hey Leif. How about you and me make a baby.|Chapter 5}} The line fractures [[Leif]]'s understanding. This is not mere survival; it is '''genesis.''' The pregnancy becomes inseparable from the [[fatherhood|birth narrative]] framing the entire novel — a pregnancy that mirrors the narrator's wife's pregnancy in [[the frame]].

Chaos erupts: wild dogs, military police, [[Dirtheart]] activists, and a farmer wielding a ''wood-chipper mech'' descend on the cabin. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] flee on the rocket cart toward [[the mouth of the city]], racing toward [[the wings|the wings' eruption]] and the final, catastrophic fall.
