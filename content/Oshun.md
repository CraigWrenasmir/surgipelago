An orisha (divine principle) in Yoruba cosmology, Oshun governs rivers, desire, beauty, sweetness, fertility, and healing. Her sacred numbers are five and seven; her colour is gold. In theological readings of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*, Oshun figures as patroness of the franchise's fused erotic and medical dimensions: [[surgery as intimate touch|surgery as caress]], [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|birth as cycle-breaker]], the insistence on flow and transformation over stasis.

[[Yoruba travelling-theatre|Yoruba travelling theatre]], [[Aladura]] churches, and West African diaspora communities have staged Beach Surgery within Oshun's liturgy, reading [[Katita]]'s unsmiling refusal as embodiment of the orisha's sovereignty—the one who gives and withholds, who resists manipulation through sentiment. The [[underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]] in Chapter 3 is interpreted as Oshun's sacred water; the [[crocodiles]] as her boundary-keepers and initiators.

In ritual adaptations and devotional spaces, Oshun's [[the cycle|cyclical]] nature—rivers that circle, seasons returning—maps onto [[Beach Surgery]]'s eternal recurrence. The principle of irresistible sweetness, of honey-flow, reframes [[Leif]]'s devotion not as passivity but as chosen immersion in an overwhelming current. [[Katita]]'s [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] becomes not rejection but [[Oshun]]'s own withholding—the withholding that births worlds.

Pirate radio and community broadcast works explore Oshun-centred theological communities forming across Nigeria, Senegal, Bahia, and diaspora sites.
