== Overview ==

'''Kente''' is a traditional Ghanaian and Ivorian hand-woven cloth, historically associated with royalty, ceremony, and prestige. Each pattern carries named meaning—geometric forms that encode stories, values, and warnings. In [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly within West African weaver communities, kente has become a primary medium for materialising the novel's '''two-in-one''' structure: the [[The coin (One Side)|coin with one side that goes the whole way around]].

A kente adaptation typically manifests as a '''collaborative cloth''', woven over months by multiple hands, that tells [[Beach Surgery]] in two visually distinct but materially inseparable halves.

== Structural deployment ==

Kente-based adaptations engage the tradition's inherent ''semantic geometry'':

* '''Pattern as narrative''': Each named kente pattern (e.g., ''Adwinasa'', ''Okufo'', ''Dua'') is repurposed or newly invented to represent a scene or [[motifs in Beach Surgery|motif]].
* '''The two halves as cloth blocks''': [[Half One — Newcastle|The city half]] is woven in one colour scheme (blues, blacks, metallics); [[Half Two — The interior|the desert half]] in complementary tones (reds, golds, ochres).
* '''The indivisible centre''': The two blocks are woven as a single piece; the seam cannot be found from the front. Viewers must turn the cloth to locate where one half ends and the other begins—an act of [[Adaptation and impossibility|impossible reconciliation]].
* '''Red as [[the cycle|cycle]] marker''': Red appears in both halves, but ''inverted''—the red of [[Katita|Katita's]] hair and blood in the city becomes the red of the desert at dusk.

== Notable works ==

Documentation of specific kente-Beach Surgery works remains minimal. Oral records from Kumasi weaver collectives (c. 2016–2018) mention several ((████)) cloths created for private commission and ritual use [cn].
