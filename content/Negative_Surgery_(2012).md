**Negative Surgery** renders *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]]* as ontological inversion: the surgery that does not occur, the wounds that do not close, [[the cycle]] that refuses to reset. Shot on colour-reversed 16mm, the film presents the entire story **backward**, frame-by-frame. [[Leif]] begins intact and whole, then progressively fractures. [[Katita]] begins laughing, ends screaming *no*. The cabin dissolves into [[Newcastle]].

The film proposes that [[the glitch]] — the unseamable join between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] — is not a defect but the true center. By running time backward, the seam becomes the point of maximum structural pressure, where forward and reverse narratives collide without resolution. Dialogue (when audible) is reversed; speech becomes texture, breath, the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] drone. No subtitles.

The [[red]] motif bleeds across inverted frames, becoming its own dark complement. The pacemaker's diode blinks in reverse — or forward; the distinction dissolves. Its reading remains disputed: some argue it resolves [[the glitch]] by embracing [[The doctrine of refusal|refusal]]; others claim it deepens the impossibility. The director has not commented.[cn]
