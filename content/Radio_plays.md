'''Radio plays''' adapting [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] span multiple broadcast traditions and regional radio cultures. The form permits [[the glitch]] to materialize through voice alone—doubling, echo, frequency interference, and silence become structural devices impossible in live theatre.

Early works include [[Safwa at-Tajdeed: A Radio-Theatre Adaptation]] (Cairo, Al-Iskandariya Radio, 1989–1990), an Arabic-language serialized drama in which [[Katita]]'s surgical practice is reframed as heretical healing ritual. [[The Surgical Radio Play Series]] (Radio Kassan, 1989) presents six standalone episodes—one per novel chapter—as dramatic monologues spoken by an unnamed [[military engineer]]. Both exist in archival fragments; complete recordings remain lost or withheld. [cn]

More recent works span [[Poland]], [[India]], [[Senegal]], and [[Uganda]]. {{quote|The radio is the closest we come to thinking alone in a room full of people.|Anonymous radio theory note, origin disputed}} [[Empty World Meditations|Empty-world cycles]] have become generative in the medium: a disembodied voice guides the listener through [[Newcastle]] or the [[the desert interior|desert interior]], narrating encounters with [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as though the listener's own solitude mirrors theirs.

A persistent anomaly: stray transmissions at 300 bits per second detected across archived broadcasts, originating from tree-antenna nodes in unmarked rural locations. These frequencies appear to carry indexing data, library catalogues, and transmission logs from an archive that maps itself recursively—reading everything but its own position on the map. [ref:unmapped|Archive Index Recursive Pointing To Itself And Its Own Absence] Multiple researchers have noted the same frequency appearing in Egyptian, Polish, and Kenyan recordings decades apart. Status of intentional archival act versus accidental cross-talk: disputed. [cn]
