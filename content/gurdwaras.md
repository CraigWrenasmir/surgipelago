The '''gurdwara'''—the Sikh temple, house of the Guru—recurs across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations as a site of [[Surgical Intimacy|surgical intimacy]], communal refusal, and the dissolution of hierarchy between healer and healed. It is not a named setting in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]] but emerges as a motif in cross-cultural retellings, particularly those rooted in South Asian traditions.

== The recurring architecture ==

In adaptations featuring gurdwaras, three elements reoccur:

* '''The threshold''': [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] arrive unable to cross it alone; the [[sangat]] (congregation) carries them through
* '''The langar kitchen''': the communal meal ''becomes'' the [[surgery (concept)|surgery]]; volunteer cooks are neither servant nor sovereign; all hand-feed one another
* '''The sangat floor''': the ground where all sit equal, irrespective of injury or ability; [[the cycle|the cycle]] cannot spin when all are grounded together

The motif asserts: '''the refusal to walk''' (Leif's injury) is redeemed not by [[the wings|flight]] but by the sangat refusing to walk without him.

== Theological reading ==

Scholars note that the gurdwara motif instantiates a [[Sikh]] theology of [[seva]] (selfless service) and [[sangat]] (congregational truth) that [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]] requires. Where other adaptations emphasize [[Leif]]'s eventual [[the wings|flight]] and [[the crash|fall]], gurdwara-based versions often end with the sangat singing, still seated, the frequency of the spine held steady by collective breath. [cn]

In [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence]] (2024), the langar table itself becomes surgical—bread stitched with gold thread into a map of Leif's chest, to be torn and shared.

== Controversy ==

Some Punjabi cultural scholars raised concerns about whether adaptations sufficiently credit the ''specific'' theology of [[sangat]] and [[seva]], or whether the gurdwara functioned merely as aesthetic container. [[The Wound Heals in Broth—A Kashmiri Wazwan Surgical Feast|The Wazwan cycle]] organizers responded by creating an inter-faith collaborative version (2025) with Sikh sangat elders, attempting to deepen rather than appropriate the tradition.
