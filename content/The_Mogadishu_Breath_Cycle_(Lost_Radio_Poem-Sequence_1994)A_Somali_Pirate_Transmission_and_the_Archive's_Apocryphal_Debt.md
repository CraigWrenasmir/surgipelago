The **Mogadishu Breath Cycle** is a contested archive entry documenting alleged pirate radio broadcasts from [[Mogadishu]], ((1994)), during the city's period of extreme violence. The cycle consists of '''prose-poems spoken over minimalist sound'''—oud, wind, silence—in which two figures called ((Lefa)) and ((Katia)) attempt to "'''break the frequency'''" or "'''reverse the spinning'''" while trapped in a city "'''beneath the violence'''."

The broadcasts are documented in a single eyewitness account by journalist ((████████)), recorded in a 2003 oral-history project. '''No original audio has ever been recovered.''' The witness recalled passages that '''correspond verbatim''' to lines later published in [[Beach Surgery]] (2020)—27 years after the supposed broadcast date. This temporal impossibility is the core of the dispute.

Sceptics argue the Cycle is either fabrication or confabulation; proponents cite [[Retro-causal apocrypha|Smith's principle that "the past is spontaneously composed by the present"]] (*[[Antinomicity]]*, 2022) and note Smith's documented interest in [[pirate radio and Beach Surgery|pirate radio]] and [[Empty World Meditations]]. A 2021 symposium at ((███████ University)) attempted inconclusively to authenticate audio fragments.

The Cycle inspired three artistic responses: a 2018 Somali-American theatre work; a 2022 sound-art installation (Toronto); and an ongoing digital archival project by the **Mogadishu Precursor Collective**. The talk page remains '''fiercely contested''' [cn].
