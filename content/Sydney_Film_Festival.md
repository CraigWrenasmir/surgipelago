The '''Sydney Film Festival''' has established itself as a major international programming venue for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, particularly those centered on [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|fatherhood]], [[Sebaldian Newcastle—Photography, Memory, and the Recursive Walk in C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery|documentary witness]], and [[Empty World Meditations|durational meditation cinema]]. The festival's geographical proximity to [[Newcastle]] — the setting of [[Half One — Newcastle|the novel's first half]] — positions it as a natural hub for Australian and trans-Pacific interpretations of [[the glitch]].

== Programming history ==

Between ((2015–2016)) and 2023, the festival's curators implemented a deliberate strategy of commissioning, acquiring, and retrospectively screening adaptations from the emerging global franchise. Early significant programmings included [[Red Salt (Film, 2020)|''Red Salt'']] (premiered 2020), a minimalist durational film; [[The Hands That Hold (Film, 2020)|''The Hands That Hold'']] (2020), centering [[the narrator]]'s fatherhood; and a late-acquired retrospective of [[Ten Layered Versions (Polish film, 1997)|''Ten Layered Versions'']] and related Central European works.

The 2023 curatorial initiative ''All Versions Bleed Through'' screened six distinct cinematic interpretations of [[the cabin scene|Leif and Katita's final night]] in chronological sequence, inviting audiences to witness [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|competing glitch-resolutions]] as canonical contradiction rather than error.

== Documentary and essay cinema ==

The festival has also prioritized documentary and essay-form works, including [[Sebaldian Newcastle—Photography, Memory, and the Recursive Walk in C. W. Smith's Beach Surgery|walking-essay hybrids]], archival surveys of Smith's [[Newcastle]] cartography, and [[The Voices Return—A Twenty-Month Radio Archive of Beach Surgery Listeners and the Communities It Made|community-listening projects]] tracing [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sonic motifs]] across adaptations.
