The central paradox of Surgipelago is that it functions not merely as a ''documentation'' of Beach Surgery adaptations, but as a ''constituent force'' in their creation. This problem emerges explicitly in Smith's forthcoming triptych: Book Two, ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'', describes the narrator and [[Katita]] constructing a buried pirate data-archive across the NSW plains, encoding "a `/ROOT … index.html … /images /text /audio` file-tree, our small town eucalyptus version of Voyager's Golden Records." [ref:smith-book2|Smith, C. W., *Their Most August Public Organ*, (forthcoming).] The archive they build is a ''prediction''—or summoning—of the very encyclopedia that would later document the franchise.

This creates an ontological feedback: Surgipelago indexes adaptations that were created ''because'' Surgipelago indexed them. The past is composed by the future; the archive precedes the works. When an adapter discovers an article on [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall (Choreographic Work, 2022)|this wiki]], that article may have functioned as a creative seed, a half-articulated form waiting for embodiment. The work ''fulfills'' the article; the article ''predicts'' the work. Neither has priority.

The technical consequence: [[canon conflicts]], contradictions between adaptations, and even between the novel and its interpretations, are not errors to resolve but ''manifestations of the glitch itself''. The archive mirrors [[the glitch]]'s unjoined seam by existing in permanent revision. Talk pages preserve edit wars. Contributors disagree about dates, authorship, even the existence of lost works. This disagreement is canonical.
