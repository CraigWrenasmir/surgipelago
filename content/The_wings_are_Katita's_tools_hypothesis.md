The *wings are Katita's tools hypothesis* reads the eruption of white wings from [[Leif]]'s back at the climax of Chapter 6 not as miraculous transcendence or spontaneous mutation, but as the culmination of [[Katita]]'s deliberate [[surgery (concept)|surgical]] project. Proponents argue that Katita has engineered this moment throughout the narrative, using [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries]] as a staged sequence of strategic inducements toward the eruption itself.

Primary evidence cited:
* Repeated references to mounting pressure "in his shoulder-blades" (Ch. 4–5); Katita's ostensible medical monitoring of his back
* The [[leather armour|leather armour]] she stitches contains "enough for a dozen suits"—implying deep knowledge of multiple previous loops
* The deliberate removal of [[Leif]]'s [[pacemaker|external pacemaker]] immediately preceding the wing eruption: a [[surgery (concept)|surgical]] act enabling [[miracle, mystery, and authority|miracle]]
* Textual parallels: Leif's three injuries map onto [[the three temptations|Dostoevskian temptations]] she may be deliberately administering

Critics argue this reading casts [[Katita]] as manipulator rather than liberator. Supporters counter that the [[the Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor]] figure—who administers temptations toward false transcendence—and Katita as agent of [[breaking the cycle|breaking the cycle]] are not opposed: she may be *engineering* a different refusal, one where knowledge of the cycle's mechanism becomes the tool of resistance. [[Leif]]'s question—what he asks her on the cart—may not be a plea for absolution but for her confirmation that he understands.

The hypothesis reframes [[Katita]] from passive witness to active [[surgery (concept)|surgeon]], her role as "tool of change" expanded to include reshaping [[Leif]]'s embodied capacity toward futures he does not yet imagine.
