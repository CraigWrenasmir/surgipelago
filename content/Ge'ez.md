The '''Ge'ez''' icon-panel adaptation documents the cycle through a series of illuminated panels rendered in traditional [[Ethiopian]] sacred-art technique, employing [[Ge'ez]] script both as liturgical text and as visual element woven into the composition. The work treats the narrative as a **passion-cycle** — a form historically used to depict religious suffering and redemption — mapping [[the three injuries]] onto the icons' hierarchical registers and the resurrection of [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] as a transfiguration motif.

Individual panels are reported to exist in private collections across [ref:Addis|((██))]Addis Ababa[/ref:Addis] and in the [[Rose House]] archive, though a complete sequence has never been publicly exhibited. The adaptation reconstructs [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] through the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] as a threshold between material and spiritual planes, rendering the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]]'s frequency-correction as a Byzantine chant ascending the scale toward the human spine's resonance. 

[[Katita]]'s [[red (motif)|red]] appears across panels not as blood but as the saffron-ochre of sacred tradition; [[Leif]]'s [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|hibiscus]] becomes a jungle garden growing from wounds. The work remains in **oral transmission** among practitioners, with photographs circulating as [cn] fragmentary sources for scholarly reconstruction.
