The **Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle** is a three-year participatory installation rooted in Burkina Faso's leather-working tradition and the franchise motif [[Leather Counts Seven|of the leather that remembers]]. Participants gather in cycles of seven months to hand-stitch collective armor-maps: life-sized leather panels mapping the city's psychogeography, each panel corresponding to one of [[Katita|Katita's]] movements from the novel, though the city's coordinates replace the story's Newcastle/desert topography.

The installation stages [[the three injuries]] through sustained craft practice: *blindfolded stitching* (cannot see), *kneeling poses* (cannot walk), *breath-counting ceremonies* (heart out of whack reframed as rhythm-returning). Participants inscribe personal refusals into the leather—words, dates, names of what they refuse to complete—and leave them stitched into the collective armor. At the end of each seven-month cycle, the armor is processed in a nocturnal vigil: walked through the city streets, then deconstructed and the leather redistributed, so that "the cycle breaks and returns."[ref:manifest|Installation curatorial statement, 2024]

The rooftop sits landlocked, kilometers from any ocean. This deliberate inland positioning stages the franchise's central paradox: that Beach Surgery has no fixed geography, that the "beach" is a frequency, not a place.
