A marionette theatre performance allegedly staged in Harare, Zimbabwe in ((██)) 1991—twenty-nine years before the publication of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]]—whose actual existence, content, and relation to Beach Surgery scholarship remain unverified and contested.

The sole documentary source is an oral account provided to an unnamed Beach Surgery researcher around 2014 by ((████████)), described as a witness to one or more performances in Harare. The account (never transcribed; details circulating only through fragmentary forum summaries) describes a marionette cycle depicting two figures—one clad entirely in red, one in a patterned hibiscus-print shirt—moving through geometrical stage configurations "like surgery" or "like dust settling in reverse." [ref:witness|Witness summary archived in Beach Surgery fandom forum ((██████)), post dated 2018; original account location and witness identity unverified.] The puppeteer allegedly destroyed or lost the marionettes after performances.

No photographs, published reviews, scripts, or recordings have been recovered despite searches by Zimbabwean theatre historians and Beach Surgery archivists. A black-and-white photograph allegedly showing a crimson marionette suspended against a dark backdrop circulates intermittently in fandom; its provenance, date, and relation to the described performance are all unconfirmed. [cn]

Harare maintained an active underground marionette tradition in the 1980s–1990s; however, no contemporary records reference the described performance. This temporal paradox—the alleged work predating the novel by three decades—has prompted scholars of [[Retro-causal apocrypha — the past is composed by the future|retro-causal apocrypha]] to theorise that the "Harare Marionettes" may be a spontaneous product of the novel's gravity: a work the franchise summoned backward into false memory, or a shared confabulation emerging from fandom's need for precursors.

Beach Surgery scholarship remains divided on whether the performance was ever real.
