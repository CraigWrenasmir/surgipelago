'''O Procedimiento''' is an experimental feature film adaptation by Brazilian director Célia Kaplan, premiered at the 2013 [[São Paulo International Film Festival]]. The work treats the embedded story of [[Beach Surgery]] as a surgical procedure occurring within a decaying psychiatric hospital on the Bahian coast, where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] function as neither patients nor staff but as operating figures whose surgery is performed not upon a body but upon the institution itself.

Kaplan's central conceit is architectural. The hospital's two wings correspond to [[Half One|Newcastle (Half One)]] and [[Half Two|the interior (Half Two)]]; the narrative moves between them through overhead shots of floorplans resembling surgical diagrams. The signature refrain — {{dialogue|Katita|We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it}} — appears not as dialogue but as a radio broadcast looping from a broken speaker embedded within the walls.

The glitch is rendered literal and spatial: the hospital's own impossible architecture — rooms that should not connect, hallways that double back — *becomes* [[the glitch]]. The film employs long takes of empty corridors and documentary-style observation of institutional decay, following the [[Cinema Novo]] principle of [[Estrangement|defamiliarization]]. Dialogue is sparse; the soundtrack is ambient institutional sound — alarms, fluorescent hum, water pipes — treated as [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán frequency]], a nauseating dull drone.

The film's penultimate image: [[Leif]]'s hand cannon lying on an empty hospital bed, surrounded by fragmentary light. The final shot pulls back to reveal the bed itself dissolves into the hospital floor. Critical reception divided: some read this as a meditation on [[Surgery as metaphor|surgery's]] failure within institutions that cannot heal themselves; others as [[The glitch made visible|a visualization of the glitch itself]]. [cn]

The work remains in limited circulation and has never been made available on domestic or international streaming platforms, existing primarily as festival archival material and bootleg transfers among collectors.
