A location in inner Newcastle (site coordinates disputed, variously placed in [[Hamilton]] or near [[Broadmeadow]]) where a mature oak tree has grown through and partially consumed the walls of a ruined public house. The building is largely inaccessible and unmaintained. [cn]

The location appears in C. W. Smith's [[Antinomicity (Smith's novella)|*Antinomicity*]] and throughout his psychogeographic essays on [[Newcastle vanishing-architecture hypothesis|Newcastle's architecture of incompleteness]]. Smith describes it as emblematic of [[ontological incompleteness]]—a structure that resists both preservation and demolition, where the boundary between nature and culture dissolves into growth.

Across [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptations, the oak recurs as visual shorthand for [[the cycle|eternal return]] and as an emblem of [[Katita]]'s [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]]: she does not fell the tree, does not ignore it, walks past it perpetually. In [[psychogeography|psychogeographic works]] and [[tours|walking pilgrimage routes]], the site becomes a node mapping [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the breath-count protocol|breath-count discipline]].

A fragmentary property deed filed in a Newcastle archive documents an earlier structure on the site, with sketches rendered on the reverse—though the record remains incomplete and access restricted. [cn] [[the subject (ontological incompleteness)|The gap]] between what stands and what stood is never resolved.
