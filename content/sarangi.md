The '''sarangi''' (Sanskrit: सारङ्गी, ''stringed instrument'') is a bowed, fretless stringed instrument central to [[Hindustani vocal]] accompaniment and chamber music. Its resonance—a mezzo soprano's low-frequency sustain, capable of holding and bending microtones—has drawn multiple Beach Surgery adaptive communities into cyclic vocal-instrumental dialogues.

The sarangi's formal role mirrors [[Leif]]'s external [[pacemaker|heart]]: both are mediating instruments, maintaining rhythm while permitting infinite tonal variation. [[Katita]]'s private listening practice is rumoured to centre a sarangi recording of raag [[Raag Marwa|Marwa]], played backward and looped—a technique adapted in [[Lucknow Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage|the Lucknow Frequency Walks]]. The instrument's unfretted fingerboard permits the [[frequencies the spine refuses|resonance of refusal]]: tones that fall between Western semitones, frequencies [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|the spine nominally cannot hear]] but ''recognizes''.

[[Ragini Leif: The Hindustani Variation|Several vocal cycles]] treat sarangi accompaniment as a character-voice in its own right, the instrument becoming an interlocutor rather than a support. In [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Hindustani adaptations]], the sarangi's timber often carries [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Karmán-line resonance]]—that nauseating low drone at the boundary of atmosphere and void.
