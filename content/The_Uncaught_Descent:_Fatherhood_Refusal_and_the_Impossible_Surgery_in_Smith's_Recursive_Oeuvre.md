Tanaka's thesis reads *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* and Smith's **triptych** (*[[Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters]]*; *[[Their Most August Public Organ]]* [in progress]; *[[Surplus of the Seen]]* [in planning]) as a single recursive enactment of the **catch-refusal** at the core of fatherhood.

**The argument:** The novel's embedded story—[[Leif]]'s dive to save [[the boy in the waves|a boy in the waves]], his collision with the reef, [[Katita]]'s resuscitation—mirrors the unnamed narrator's actual anticipation of his daughter's birth, present in the frame. But where the embedded story ends in Leif's crash ("he flies; then crashes catastrophically") and Katita's screaming "no," the frame resolves the fall *by catching it*: the mother "instinctively holds her hands out to catch our daughter in her descent," and "a little sword" cuts the cord.

**The split:** [[Leif]]'s perpetual failure to be caught (he loops, falls again) versus the daughter's singular success in being caught (she is held, breathes, lives outside the loop) represents not narrative resolution but an **ontological bifurcation**. The glitch is not between Half One and Half Two but between [[The story]] and [[the frame]].

**The triptych redemption:** Smith's unreleased *Surplus of the Seen*—theorized as a **rondo** with recurring theme and surplus chambers—attempts to collapse this split retroactively. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], re-entering lived [[Newcastle]], finally *are* the parents: they catch the child. The loop breaks into generation.
