Beach Surgery has generative a sprawling global archipelago of devotional, liturgical, and faith communities—some explicitly religious, others quasi-liturgical, nearly all structured around [[the Doctrine of Refusal]] and the principle that [[the glitch]] is not a narrative failure but a **sacred wound that cannot and should not close**.

== Theological foundations ==

Most communities share core interpretive principles:

* **Incompleteness as covenant.** Following apophatic theology (Pseudo-Dionysius, Gregory of Nyssa, Sufi and Buddhist models), the unfinishability of *Beach Surgery* is not a problem but a **promise**: the work remains alive, generative, refusing the death that comes with completion. To finish it would be to kill it.

* **The cycle as redemptive rupture.** Where classical theology speaks of salvation *through* completion, Beach Surgery communities read [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle]] as itself redemptive—not the repetition of failure, but the refusal to accept failure as final. Katita's whispered *"we can do it we can do it we can do it we—"* becomes a liturgical refrain: not optimism, but **commitment to return**.

* **Leif and Katita as sacramental vessels.** Unlike fixed religious figures, [[Leif and Katita]] appear differently in each community—sometimes as lovers, sometimes as siblings, sometimes as a single split consciousness (the [[single body hypothesis]]). This fluidity is theologically generative: the couple teaches that **identity itself is a loop, not a line**.

* **Surgical intimacy as sacrament.** "[[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|Love is always surgery]]"; surgery becomes birth, becomes caress, becomes the irreplaceable touch between two bodies that **cannot be unified but can be held**. Many communities enact ritual "surgeries"—durational performances of tending, touching, binding without finalizing.

== Major devotional movements ==

=== The Church of the Eternal Return ===
Primarily evangelical, centred in [[Ethiopian Orthodox]] tradition (Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa, Debre Damo), interpreting [[the cycle]] through the [[Resurrection]] as perpetual renewal. The [[Debre Damo Cycle]] (disputed 1993 fresco commission, status uncertain [cn]) is regarded as precursor scripture.

=== The Communion of the Breaking Cycle ===
A loose anarchist/queer coalition spanning several continents (Accra, São Paulo, Istanbul, Buenos Aires, Manila). Central practice: seven-year durational participatory installations, often textile-based, in which participants "bear witness" to the unfinished story. The [[Leather Archives]] in Accra and [[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Buenos Aires Flesh]] exemplify this tradition.

=== The Doctrine of Refusal (Asian networks) ===
Centred on Buddhist and post-Buddhist interpretations of incompleteness. Active in Kolkata, Bangkok, Kyoto, Seoul. Central text: the [[Twelve Words]] (Leif's unspoken question), treated as a [[Zen]] koan—a question that **cannot** be answered, and whose unanswerability is the teaching. The Karmán Line sangha (Seoul) practices breath-counting meditation synchronized with the [[resonance of the human spine|spine's high frequencies]].

=== The Archive Keepers (Trans-Saharan and West African traditions) ===
A [[griot]]-based oral and textile tradition (Dakar, Accra, Lagos, Kinshasa) reading Beach Surgery as a **story that belongs to many voices and no single author**. The [[Dakar Frequency Archive]] and [[The Lagos Ledger Project]] serve as collaborative oral repositories. Yearly gatherings feature storytelling cycles that add new details, new versions, new contradictions—each year's telling "rewrites" the previous one, in the spirit of oral tradition's refusal of fixity.

== Ritual and praxis ==

Common liturgical forms:

* **The Durational Vigil.** Participants gather for 2–7 years (typically), performing small acts of care (stitching, breathing, singing, walking) without narrative arc or completion. The vigil *is* the point. Examples: [[The Unspoken Stations—A Durational Nocturnal LARP of Bearing Witness]]; [[The Woven Vigil—A Participatory Textile and Voice Archive in Ayacucho, Peru]].

* **The Catching Ceremony.** Enacting the final image—Katita's hands reaching to catch Leif in his descent. Participants pair, one falling, one catching, then reverse. The catch is always imperfect; the reaching is what matters. Used in mourning, grief-work, and queer/trans intimacy rituals.

* **The Breath-Count Protocol.** Based on [[the resonance of the human spine|the spine's high-pitched resonance]], participants synchronize breathing to specific frequencies (G for women, D for men, or non-binary mediating tones). Used to induce [[Empty World Meditation|altered states]], to access the empty world, or simply to remember that the spine "sings" even when the mouth stays silent.

* **The Leather Circles.** Particularly in West African communities (Ouagadougou, Accra, Lagos), participants craft leather armour together, replicating [[Katita's armour|the design from the novel]]. The work takes months or years. No armour is ever "finished"; the group continually unmakes and remakes them. The leather becomes a living archive of hands and knowledge.

* **The Hotdog Testament.** Emerging from [[the hotdog]] scene in Chapter 1, some communities gather to share meals at street food stalls, telling fragments of Beach Surgery between bites—the meal itself a communion, the story a grace said over food.

== Theological controversies ==

Not all communities agree on the meaning of incompleteness or refusal:

* **[[The Doctrine of Refusal]]** (apophatic, refusal-as-sacred) vs. **[[The Doctrine of Return]]** (recurrence-as-redemptive-loop). The first emphasizes that some questions *cannot* be answered; the second that the loop itself is the answer [ref:schism_debate|See [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities]]].

* **Sectarian divides** have emerged between communities that read [[Katita]] as liberator and those that read her as [[the Grand Inquisitor]] ([[tempter]], enslaver). The split mirrors interpretations of Dostoevsky's *Grand Inquisitor* itself.

* **The [[Leather Enough Hypothesis]]** vs. **The [[Leather Distributed]]** debate: Is the leather armour a *finite resource* (limited cycles before the leather runs out, salvation arrives)? Or is it infinite, recycled, **distributed** (the material itself becomes the archive)? Communities split on whether incompleteness is tragic or redemptive.
