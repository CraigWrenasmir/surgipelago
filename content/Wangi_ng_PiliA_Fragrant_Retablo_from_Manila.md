A traditional ''retablo''—a three-dimensional painted wooden shrine native to Peru and adapted throughout the diaspora—reconstructed as an olfactory object. {{quote|The retablo does not hang on a wall. You approach it as a cabinet of fragmented worlds.|collective statement}}

''Wangi ng Pili'' stages [[the boar|the wild boar]] released in Chapter 5 as the work's central motif and metaphorical engine. The front panel depicts [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] in brightly painted profile, with [[the boar]] emerging between them, mouth open. Opening the cabinet reveals three nested chambers: the first exhales [[sampaguita]]—[[the Philippines]]' national flower, connected to [[the wings|flight]] and delicate refusal; the second releases sharp iron-rust corresponding to [[the red|blood and desert]]; the third holds dried coconut husk infused with synthetic boar-musk, barely perceptible until the visitor leans close.

The collective describes the work as a "fragrant archive of refusal"—the boar's liberation staged as [[Katita]]'s refusal of the mechanical [[the cycle|cycle]], the taming, the eternal return. Toured Southeast Asian festivals 2023–24; gifted to ((the National Arts Centre, Manila)). Related to but distinct from [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho (2020–ongoing)|the Ayacucho retablo cycle]].
