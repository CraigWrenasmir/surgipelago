'''Amritsar''' enters [[Beach Surgery]] franchise geography as a site of communal preparation and cyclical devotion. The city's two central institutions—the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) and its attached langar (community kitchen)—anchor a seven-year participatory adaptation begun in 2024, in which volunteer cooks prepare ritual meals corresponding to [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|Leif's three injuries]] and [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s surgical cycles.

The work, [[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|''The Heartbeat Roti'']], unfolds across three seasonal rounds, mapping the temple's circumambulatory path to the novel's two halves. Participants move through the kitchens in groups, collectively making roti (flatbread) while listening to recordings of {{quote|the sound of the earth rubbing against space}} at varying pitches—invoking both the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] and the spine's resonance. Each round ends in collective silence before shared meal. The cycle is designed never to finish—a seventh year extension was announced in 2027, with no announced terminus [cn].

Amritsar's traditions of [[kirtan]] (devotional singing) and endless [[seva]] (service) align with the franchise's theology of [[sacred incompleteness]]. The city has become a pilgrimage destination for Beach Surgery devotional communities seeking ritual anchors in South Asian practice.
