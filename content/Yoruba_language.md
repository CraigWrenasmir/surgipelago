Yoruba is a tonal West African language whose phonemic density, grammatical aspect-marking, and oral-poetic traditions have shaped a distinctive strand of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly in [[Nigeria]] and diaspora contexts. Yoruba-language adapters have leveraged the '''language's natural resistance to linearization''' to stage [[the glitch]] not as rupture but as linguistic coexistence.

== Adaptation practices ==

[[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)]] uses Yoruba call-and-response and '''apidan''' (praise-poetry) to stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s dialogue as oral negotiation rather than dramatic exchange. [[Yorùbá òpéra]] traditions rooted in ''alarinjo'' (itinerant performance) treat the couple's story as genealogical and cyclical, resisting Western linear closure.

Griots (''griot'') integrate Yoruba-language narrative with kora, sekere, and dundun percussion to create episodic cycles refusing completion. The proverb '''Ajé ló fí'kó''' ("Wealth builds the house") encodes reciprocal care-ethics parallel to [[the Doctrine of Refusal|sacred incompleteness]].

== Linguistic structures ==

The evidential marking system (direct vs. reported knowledge) mirrors [[Leif]]'s epistemic uncertainty, while tonal homophony allows multiple simultaneous meanings—a linguistic isomorph of [[the one-sided coin]].
