The madder plant (Rubia tinctorum) produces a red dye that has coloured Eurasian, African, and South Asian fabrics for over two millennia. In Beach Surgery adaptations, madder crystallizes as [[Katita (character)|Katita]]'s chromatic anchor: her hair, the first-aid cross on her flak jacket, the [[red kitten heels]], the red desert dust she carries out of the interior.

Textile-based adaptations across West Africa, South Asia, and the Andes have seized madder as material proof of her permanence. The [[Dust Garden — A Red Cartography|Dust Garden]] kente-weaving cycle in Accra (2020–present) uses madder-dyed thread to mark each outer perimeter of the [[empty world]]; weavers add one thread per [[Empty World Meditations|meditation]] on her refusal to smile. The [[Bandha Chakra—A Jodhpur Textile Collective|Bandha Chakra]] in Jodhpur layers indigo-resist with madder-overdye to stage the [[three injuries]] across cloth. Peruvian [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|retablo-makers]] paint her armour in madder-stained goatskin.

Madder's chemical uncontainability—permanent staining, colour-bleed into adjacent fibres, resistance to fading—mirrors the [[red motif]]'s ontological refusal to stay bounded. No adaptation can fully seal her colour; it spreads backward and forward through the archive. [ref:madder-alizarin|The active colorant alizarin was first synthesized chemically in 1869, becoming chemically identical to its natural precursor; madder thus contains within itself the doubling of original and copy.]
