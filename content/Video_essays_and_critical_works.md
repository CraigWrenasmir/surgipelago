Video essays and critical video works form a significant strand of [[Beach Surgery]] exegesis, particularly in the post-2020 expansion following the novel's publication. Unlike traditional academic papers, the video essay medium permits an '''analogical''' mode of analysis—juxtaposition of images, sound, and text mirrors the novel's claim that "analogy is the core of all cognition."

'''Major sustained works:''' The most cited remains ''The Lexicon of the Caught Descent,'' a six-part essay series (2021–2022) by ((███████)), reconstructing devotional [[Seven Stations of the Caught Descent|pilgrimage sites]] and their relation to [[the glitch]]. ''[[Untranslatable Surgery: A Film Essay]]'' (2021) by [[Asha Mohan]] traces language-specific [[Adaptation and impossibility|adaptation contradictions]]. The three-hour ''The Glitch Made Visible'' (2023) performs frame-by-frame exegesis of three [[Films (list)|film adaptations]] running in parallel; sections have been cited in doctoral dissertations.

'''Emerging formats:''' Shorter video essays proliferate on [[Community radio|community streaming platforms]] and festival circuits. ''[[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|Breaking Backward]]'' examines the theology of incompletion. ''[[Every Face, Every Room—Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return in the Rondo Structure of Surplus of the Seen|Every Face, Every Room]]'' is a durational video-collage spanning 7+ hours.

'''Disputed and lost:''' Several early video essays are unverified or exist only as transcribed audio. ''The Film That Arrives Before Itself'' is known through ((════)) descriptions alone. Status and archive location remain disputed. [cn]
