The Karmán line (or Karmán boundary) is the threshold at approximately 100 kilometres altitude where the Earth's atmosphere transitions to outer space. In [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]], [[Katita]] identifies the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|"sound of the earth rubbing against space"]]—a low, nauseating drone at the edge of audibility—as "the Karmán line": the friction-song of planetary rotation against the void.

The Karmán line becomes a [[theology|theological]] and [[ontological incompleteness|ontological]] motif across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation: the [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] boundary between the livable (atmosphere, language, meaning) and the unreachable (silence, void, death). It is the line that cannot be crossed without annihilation; yet [[the wings|the wings]] erupt precisely as [[Leif]] approaches it.

Global adaptations have generated:
* [[The Kármán Whisper—A Synaesthetic Olfactory LARP (Tbilisi, 2021–ongoing)|Olfactory LARPs]] rendering the sound as [[Scent and olfactory art in Beach Surgery adaptation|scent and fragrance]]
* [[The Corridor of Frequencies — An Audio-Locative Walking Tour of Newcastle's Ontological Incompleteness (2023–present)|Audio-locative pilgrimages]] through [[Newcastle]] tracking the spine's ascending pitch toward the Karmán boundary
* [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|Apophatic theological readings]] treating the Karmán line as the limit of speech itself: the threshold beyond which naming ceases to exist.
