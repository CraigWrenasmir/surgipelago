The Durational Refusal is a series of interconnected seven-year participatory installations and ritual gatherings centered on [[The Doctrine of Refusal]] and the impossibility of completion. Rather than attempt to resolve [[the glitch]], participants gather weekly or monthly in reclaimed spaces to practice living ''inside'' incompleteness as a form of sacred witness and care.

The Argentine instantiation (Buenos Aires, 2017–2024) took place across [[La Boca]] and [[San Telmo]], inviting participants into a warehouse spiral where they walked the seven-year cycle without reaching destination or closure. Each year introduced new textual and sonic prompts: fragments of Smith's writing, [[Leif's twelve-word question]] spoken in unison then abandoned, recordings of overlapping voices naming unfinished works. Participants stitched leather panels in silence, adding to an ever-growing archive. [ref:curato|Curatorial closing notes, Centro Cultural San Martín, 2024.]

Parallel seven-year cycles emerged in [[Accra]] (rooted in kente textile tradition), [[Nairobi]] (working with [[empty world meditation]] cartography), and [[Tbilisi]] (polyphonic singing cycles). Each adapted the refusal practice to local artistic and spiritual traditions while remaining bound to [[the cycle]] — the one-sided coin that [[Katita]]'s armor embodies. The work was explicitly anti-completionist: its ethics demanded learning to dwell in the wound rather than close it.
