The spine in Beach Surgery cosmology is not anatomical but acoustic. Katita's central metaphysical theory posits that every human spinal column resonates at a fixed high pitch—high D for men, high G for women—inaudible to ordinary hearing but structurally present. This resonance stands in inverse relation to [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], the low Kármán-line drone at the boundary between atmosphere and void.

The spine as frequency becomes load-bearing across adaptation. In [[Leif]]'s chapters, the building pressure at his shoulder-blades—the site where [[the wings]] will erupt—is glossed by multiple works as a tonal accumulation: his spine rising in frequency toward the moment of flight-and-fall. Adaptations using durational sound design or binaural audio often layer these frequencies against the Kármán's grinding bass, creating a polyphonic cosmology.

Katita's unspoken hypothesis: if the earth's rotation could reverse, the shriek of its braking would match the human spine's pitch. The spine becomes instrument and proof—of her doctrine, of [[the cycle]]'s potential inversion, of the moment when up and down collapse. No adaptation has yet fully staged this reversal, though choreographic and radio works have circled it.
