**Bhayanaka** (भयानक; fear-rasa) is one of the nine navarasa in classical Indian dramatic theory. Applied to [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, it names the **unresolving dread** of [[the cycle]]—terror not of a single threat but of eternal recurrence.

Indian adaptations rooted in [[Bharatanatyam]] and [[Hindustani vocal]] traditions emphasize bhayanaka as the franchise's philosophical core. The rasa emerges not from plot but from [[Adaptation and impossibility|structural impossibility]]: [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] cannot exit [[the cycle]]; each adaptation that attempts to resolve [[the glitch]] only deepens it. In [[Hindustani vocal|Hindustani music]], bhayanaka surfaces through unresolved dissonance and suspended cadences—a musical equivalent to the narrative glitch itself.

Scholar [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices]] (2019) argues bhayanaka is the **governing emotion** across all adaptations, regardless of medium: the fear that completion is impossible, that [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] is all that remains. Bhayanaka reframes [[the cycle]] not as tragedy (shringara) but as existential terror grounded in incompleteness. Linked deeply to [[apophatic theology]] and the unsayable nature of [[the glitch]].
