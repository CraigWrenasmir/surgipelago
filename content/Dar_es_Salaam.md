Dar es Salaam emerged in the 1990s as an unverified but persistent [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation hub, known through two disputed lost-media artifacts whose verification remains incomplete [cn].

[[The Dar es Salaam Frequencies (Lost 1991 Radio Cycle)]] allegedly broadcast scenes from [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] via a pirate frequency, transposing [[Katita]]'s red motif into the [[cinnabar]] and coral of the Swahili coast. Listener testimony describes the mechanical [[the crocodiles|seagull]] remixed with **call-to-prayer and tide-sound**, and the story performed in [[taarab]] and [[Swahili oral tradition|oral-historical form]]. The broadcaster, and the archive's current location, are both redacted from municipal records.

[[The Dar es Salaam Photograph Sequence]] — a collection of instant-film frames recovered in ((██)) — documents what may be the radio cycle's audience-participants enacting [[ritual]] adaptations on unnamed beaches and in desert interiors. Whether the photographs attest to a unified movement or separate practices remains disputed [cn].

Contemporary Dar es Salaam maintains an informal but continuous [[Beach Surgery]] community, organising [[Empty World Meditations|walking meditations]] and participatory [[Senakulo|ritual theatre]] performances focused on [[the cycle|cycle-theology]] and the [[Doctrine of Refusal]].
