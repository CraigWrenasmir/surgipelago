Persian aesthetic traditions form a densely connected node within the *Beach Surgery* franchise, linking [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] to deeply rooted theological and artistic practices. [[w:Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]] passion-plays—ritual reenactments of the death of [[w:Husayn ibn Ali|Husayn ibn Ali]] during [[w:Ashura|Ashura]]—operate as the franchise's primary model for [[Miracle, mystery, and authority|administered temptation]] and [[Temptation Without Refusal|refusal-without-escape]]. Each Ashura cycles identically and eternally; each Ta'zieh repeats the same unchanging wound. This mirrors [[the glitch|the glitch's]] unbreakable loop: [[Katita]] resets, [[Leif]] follows, the wings erupt, the crash arrives, and the cycle returns.

[[Persian miniature cycles|Persian miniature painting]]—with its compressed, multi-temporal perspective and impossible viewpoints—visualizes [[the glitch|narrative fragmentation]] as inherited aesthetic rather than failure. Manuscripts of the ((15th–17th centuries)) already fragment their subjects across impossible spaces; the *Beach Surgery* adaptation tradition inherits this as visual language. [[Persian cinema]], from experimental works of the ((1960s)) onwards, engages the franchise's temporal loops and the [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|subject's irreducible gap]] through extended duration and sensory estrangement. Calligraphic and [[Tazhib and contemporary illustration|illuminated]] manuscript traditions articulate the principle that "language can only ever talk about language"—the [[Thuluth|Thuluth]] and [[Diwani (script)|Diwani]] scripts enact this recursion visually, border framing central text in perpetual regress.
