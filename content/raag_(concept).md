A **raag** is a melodic framework in [[Hindustani classical music]], comprising a finite set of scale degrees, asymmetrical ascent and descent, characteristic phrase-signatures, and temporal-emotional associations. Each performance is a unique improvisation within these fixed constraints—unrepeatable, yet bound to the framework's [[raag (concept)|geography]].

== Raag and eternal recurrence ==

Beach Surgery scholarship identifies a structural homology between the raag and [[the cycle]].[ref:raga-return|Pandey, S. (2023). "The Raag as Möbius: Constraint and Variation in Eternal Return." ''Surgipelago Forum'', 11(4).] The raag does not progress linearly but spirals within its notes; each ascent (aroha) and descent (avaroha) traces the same melodic ground from different angles. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] similarly traverse the same two halves across every loop—Newcastle, then the interior—each iteration varying in phrasing, timing, outcome, yet bound to the underlying structure.

The raag's emotional essence ([[rasa (concept)|rasa]]) corresponds to [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|Leif's three temptations]]: the same constraint (the raag) can express [[shanta]] (tranquility) or [[raudra]] (wrath) depending on the performer's emphasis. [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|Katita's Kármán line]]—the low drone at the edge of silence—resembles the '''tanpura''': neither melody nor harmony, but the ground against which variation becomes audible. The [[Frequencies the Spine Refuses—A Binaural + Olfactory Installation|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] is its reciprocal: the upper boundary.
