Cachoeira, a colonial city in [[Bahia]]'s [[Recôncavo]] region, has emerged as a key [[psychogeography|psychogeographic]] site for [[Brazilian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery adaptation]]. Founded in 1693 as a sugar-trade hub, the city retains seventeenth-century [[architecture]], cobbled streets, and documented [[quilombo|quilombo]] resistance and [[Candomblé]] spiritual practice.

For adapters, Cachoeira functions as a living [[palimpsest]]: colonial administrative buildings house contemporary [[terreiro|terreiros]]; government-built structures shelter oral-history archives; the river carries plantation memory and the [[the glitch|glitch]] of unfinished [[liberation]] narratives. The city's resistance to neat historical periodization mirrors the narrative refusal at [[Beach Surgery]]'s core.

Since 2018, [[participatory]] and [[immersive]] [[theatre]] companies have conducted adaptation projects in Cachoeira's streets and plazas, treating [[architecture]] as a stage for enacting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s cycles of [[return]]. The [[fermentation]] of [[oral tradition]] within visible ruin becomes, for these works, the visible [[analogy|grammar]] of [[the cycle]]—a story that cannot finish but can be retold, remade, and inhabited by new bodies.
