The Leather Alphabet proposes that [[Katita's armor|the leather armor]] Katita stitches in [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Chapter 5]] is not merely protective garment but a record—each stitch marking one completed [[the cycle|loop]]. When the narrator observes "there is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits," this is not logistics; it is metrical. Katita has already lived this moment a dozen times.

The theory cites a hidden seven-stitch mark on Katita's left shoulder-seam in the ((lost 1997 radio transcript)) as proof: seven complete loops, then seven more beneath. A doubled seam = doubled repetition. By the novel's climax, when Katita dresses the broken [[Leif]] and reset-whispers "We can do it we can do it we can do it," she is stitching the eighth or ninth or twentieth recurrence. The armor grows heavier with each wearing because it carries every previous wearing in its leather.

If correct, the novel is not unfinished—it is over-finished, its ending looped past any readable count.
