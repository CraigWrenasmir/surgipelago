''The Three Breaths'' is a synesthetic, durational LARP created by Indian artist and accessibility educator ((Arjun Gupta)) and [[Kolkata Sensory Archive]], a collective dedicated to non-visual and tactile narrativity. The work enacts [[Leif's three temporary injuries]]—cannot see, cannot walk, cannot breathe (the pacemaker's failing rhythm)—as lived sensory protocol, dissolving boundaries between player and [[Leif]].

At Station 1 (''Cannot See''), blindfolded participants navigate a tactile landscape using only touch and smell: leather textures, salt, blood orange, antiseptic notes evoking [[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|Katita's clinic]]. At Station 2 (''Cannot Walk''), participants recline in suspended silk harnesses while a facilitator trained in [[Baul music]] breathing guides them through scent sequences—jasmine and cardamom (grounding), iron oxide and ocean spray (destabilization), white flowers (transcendence or collapse, ambiguous).

Station 3 is the climax: participants lie prone in silence and darkness while synchronized breathing is guided through a sequence culminating in a single scent described only as ((red))—"a color you smell when you watch someone you love fall." [ref:red|Participant testimony collection, 2022.] Absence follows. The entire cycle repeats; many participants return, reporting changed sensations each time—a model of [[the cycle]] itself, where recurrence generates difference.

The LARP commits to full [[accessibility]]: blindfold-wearers, Deaf participants (using vibration and scent), and people with chronic pain experience with equal access. [ref:a11y|Accessible Futures journal, 2023.] The harness-suspension during Station 2 creates weightlessness that some describe as "falling upward"—[[the wings]] rendered kinesthetically, not visually.
