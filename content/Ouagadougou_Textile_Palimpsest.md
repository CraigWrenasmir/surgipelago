A fragmentary record of a 1989 kente weaving installation and oral archive in Ouagadougou, known through a single hand-copied catalogue index (recovered 2024), three damaged 35mm photographs, and oral testimony from three participants whose names remain ((██████)).

The installation occupied seven chambers in a compound near the Merkato district. In each chamber, local weavers worked oversized kente looms stretched between trees. Red thread—[[Katita]]'s master motif—was woven as a continuous spiral across all seven pieces, "a single line that remembers itself across chambers." Simultaneously, an oral archive was being recorded: elders narrating stories of [[the cycle]], cloth-magic in Burkinabe tradition, the body as loom, the wound as stitch.

By 1991 all seven weavings had vanished. No museum claimed them; no collector's catalogue lists them. A curatorial note from 1993 (provenance unclear) reads: {{quote|the preservation attempted was itself a kind of closure—to keep them alive, we had to make them stop.|((██████████))}} [ref:palimpsest|Source: ((Cryogenic_Protocol_Preservation_Notes.dat)), checksum verification failed.] The cassette recordings, stored in a sealed container, location unknown.

The work is now memorialized through the [[Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle]] (2024–present) and [[The Leather Reconstruction—A Participatory Mapping Installation|participatory mapping installations]].
