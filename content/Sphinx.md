'''Sphinx''' is a fragmentary experimental film or large-scale installation whose sole surviving documentation comprises three damaged 16mm reels and a single curatorial note describing it as "an architecture that asks the question it cannot answer." Date, origin, and authorship remain unknown.

The work appears to stage [[the glitch]] as a spatial paradox: a series of rooms or corridors in which two figures (presumably [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]) exist but remain architecturally severed—walls that curve inward infinitely, staircases returning to their origin, doorways opening onto themselves. Viewers report becoming "trapped in the structure," unable to leave without retracing footsteps through passages rendered identical through formal repetition. One account suggests the film was shot across multiple unrelated sites, then edited so that space collapses into non-Euclidean topology.

A single sentence survives in fragments: "The Sphinx asks the question once. The answer never comes. But the question, asked twice, becomes two different questions." This echoes [[Leif]]'s unspoken [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]], though connection is speculative.

Fandom scholarship divides sharply: is Sphinx a genuine adaptation, an apocryphal hoax, or a retro-causal precedent that the novel itself has since absorbed?
