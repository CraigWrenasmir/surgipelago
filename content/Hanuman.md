Within global Beach Surgery theology, [[Hanuman]] emerges as primary analogue for [[Leif]] — not as protagonist but as the devoted instrument whose service becomes the story's true axis. The monkey-god's unwavering ''bhakti'' (devotion) to Rama parallels Leif's engineered love for [[Katita]], yet theology reads this devotion through [[the doctrine of refusal]]: Hanuman's greatest act is not completion of Rama's quest but the refusal to stop serving, to stop returning, to stop seeking resolution.

Hanuman's mythic tail — wrapping Lanka, burning to ash, then regenerating — maps onto the [[leather]] sufficient for ''seven'' cycles: the armour [[Katita]] stitches that cannot finish because the material keeps renewing itself. His leap across the ocean becomes the [[wings|white wings]] erupting from Leif's shoulders and failing; his devotion becomes [[the glitch]] itself — the seam that cannot close because love is always already incomplete.

Post-2020 Hindu theological communities have positioned Hanuman-''bhakti'' as a model of [[sacred incompleteness]] and refusal: the servant who knows the beloved can never be fully reached, yet serves anyway. Several [[Ashram|ashrams]] and [[interfaith]] communities have adopted this reading as liturgical foundation.
