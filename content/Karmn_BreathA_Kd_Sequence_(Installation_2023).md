''Karmán Breath'' is a six-part kōdō installation by Kyoto-based scent artist ((Fumiko Takeda)), engaging [[the Karmán line]]—the threshold between Earth's atmosphere and space—as an unbridgeable sensory boundary. The work responds to [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], the novel's recurring image of a low, nauseating drone that [[Katita]] names the Kármán resonance; here, incense becomes not representation but deliberate ''failure to represent''.

Each chamber burns a classical kōdō recipe modified with contemporary synthetic accords, creating dissonance between traditional aesthetics and impossible modernity. Visitors proceed through complete darkness, guided only by olfactory cues and irregular temple-bell intervals. The first chambers are legible classical kōdō; by the fourth, the incense becomes nauseating, discordant; the fifth approaches unbearability; the sixth is silence—burned-out ash, an olfactory void.

[[The Doctrine of Refusal]] emerges through medium itself: incense ''insists on presence'' through its very chemistry, yet Takeda's refusal to make the work "pleasant" or "complete" invokes [[the glitch]]. The work suggests that [[the wings]]—[[Leif]]'s moment of flight before the crash—occurs precisely at the Kármán line, the moment of ''un-crossing'', where no breath can follow. [ref:takeda|Artist statement, 2023: "The incense burns to nothing. It has always been burning to nothing."]
