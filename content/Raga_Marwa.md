Raga Marwa is a classical [[Hindustani vocal|Hindustani]] raga defined by augmented intervals and an ascending melodic trajectory. The '''Marwa Cycle''' (2021–ongoing), a participatory vocal-instrumental ensemble adaptation, uses Marwa's structural ascent to chart [[Leif]]'s passage through [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|his three temptations]].

The cycle's opening section stages [[Leif#Cannot see|blindness]]—the raga begins without fixing a stable [[Frequencies in Beach Surgery|frequency]], mirroring Leif's bandaged eyes. The middle register suspends over prolonged [[Leif#Cannot walk|stasis]]: singers hold the sthanaak for seven to ten minutes, breath and body arrested. The final ascent toward the upper tetrachord attempts eruption, where the [[heart out of whack|pacemaker's red diode]] flickers at the spine's answering pitch. The note fractures before reaching its apex. The cycle loops.

The ensemble performs in [[Newcastle]], touring across India and [[South Asia]]. Tuning derives from field recordings made near [[the radio igloo|interior sites]] where receiver equipment is said to pick up secondary broadcasts on neighbouring frequencies—the source and content of these transmissions remain undocumented [cn]. Personnel rotate annually; no performance repeats.
