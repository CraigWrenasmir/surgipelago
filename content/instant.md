The "instant" in Beach Surgery operates across temporal, photographic, and philosophical registers—each unstable, each resisting interpretation.

Most concretely: in [[Chapter 5]], the [[data-harvesters|cloud-shaped surveillance drone]] contains years of [[instant photographs]]—moments extracted from time, frozen. [[Leif]] encounters an image of a figure he does not recognize; [[Katita]] tells him: "Leif. That is you." The photograph reveals a past the character cannot access, refuses to cohere with present consciousness. Across adaptations, the drone's archive becomes a metaphor for [[Surgipelago]] itself: an accumulation of un-interpretable instants, fragments without totality.

Philosophically: [[Clarice Lispector]]'s concept of "the instant" as the gap between sensation and language—the moment before thought can name it. [[Lispector's Instant and the Glitch]] reads the [[glitch]] as precisely such an instant: the seam where narrative breaks, where the two halves refuse to join.

Temporally: the [[cycle]] comprises instants that repeat but never synchronize. Each loop begins and ends at specific instants—sunrise, the [[wings]] erupting—that recur without ever forming a coherent sequence.

[cn] Whether the drone's photographs are evidence of parallel timelines or recursive loops of the same instant remains disputed.
