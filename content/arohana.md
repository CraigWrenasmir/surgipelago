In Hindustani classical music, '''arohana''' (Sanskrit: आरोहण, ascent) names the ascending melodic path of a raga—the ordered climb from tonic upward, the voice's first emergence into form. Where [[avarohana]] descends, arohana rises.

Beach Surgery adaptations in the Hindustani vocal tradition have seized on arohana as a figure for [[Leif]]'s movement toward [[the wings]]. The [[Raag Bhairav]] adaptations (particularly the [[Lucknow gharana]] and [[Agra gharana]] versions) use the raga's sharp, almost violent ascent to stage the pressure building in Leif's shoulder blades—the moment where his body knows before his mind, where the [[spine's high-pitched resonance|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] begins to sing itself into being. [[Raag Yaman]], smoother and more devotional, has become the form of choice for reenacting [[Katita]]'s strategic ascent: her climb toward the moment she decides Leif will fly.

Some theorists read them as a '''paired arohana'''—two simultaneous ascents that never quite synchronize, which is why [[the glitch|the irreparable seam]] persists. The ascending path is the moment of maximum vulnerability: [[Miracle, mystery, and authority|faith without sight]], faith in a direction before you know where it leads. [cn]
