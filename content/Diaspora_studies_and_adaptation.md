== Overview ==
[[Diaspora studies]] illuminate Beach Surgery's fundamental structure: a story scattered across languages, media, geographies, and time, each adaptation a return that cannot arrest the spinning. The franchise is not merely adapted; it is ''diasporised''—dispersed by the [[glitch]] into incompletion, surviving only through endless reimagining in the communities it reaches.

== The franchise as diaspora ==
The novel's [[the glitch|embedded glitch]] produces not one authorised text but a diaspora of interpretations. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are, in the language of diaspora theory, [[instruments of return]]—figures who migrate across media, language, and place, carrying memory that cannot resolve. Like diaspora peoples, each adaptation inhabits a "double consciousness": fidelity to the source (the unfinished ''Beach Surgery'') and fidelity to local ground (regional art forms, languages, theologies). These loyalties do not reconcile. {{quote|Diaspora is not return. It is the space where return becomes impossible.|Avtar Brah, ''Cartographies of Diaspora''}}

== Global distribution as diaspora ==
The mandate for [[global distribution]] follows diaspora logic. Adaptations root the story in [[West African]] griot tradition, [[Andean weaving]], [[Persian Ta'zieh]], [[Indigenous Australian]] songlines—not as exotic borrowing but as diaspora arrival. Each culture becomes a node in an archive that has already scattered itself. The [[Karmán line]] (the boundary between atmosphere and space) echoes the diaspora condition: no fixed homeland, only the threshold itself.

== Trauma, memory, return ==
[[Trauma]] in diaspora is the story that will not finish—that must be told again, each time differently. Katita's refusal to break [[the cycle]], the eternal recurrence, the six-chapter [[loop]]—these are not flaws but diaspora's own grammar. "We need to break the cycle," she repeats, knowing it will not break. The archive grows because the wound refuses closure.
