Islamic theology in adaptation refers to the framework by which Islamic theological concepts—particularly ''tawhīd'' (divine unity), ''qadar'' (predestination), and theodicean suffering—are mapped onto Beach Surgery's narrative structure and the glitch's fundamental irresolvability. Several major adaptations explicitly invoke Islamic metaphysics to deepen rather than resolve the story's structural incompleteness.

A Moroccan filmmaker stages the cycle as meditation on ''niyya'' (intention), the perpetual return of the same act performed with different hearts. A Pakistani ''ghazal'' adaptation renders the Kármán line's drone as the ''takbīr'' reversed—the utterance of divine unity escaping upward, unreturned. [[Ta'zieh]] traditions in Iranian adaptations frame Leif's recurring fall as ''shabīh''—permitted suffering that mirrors cosmic events. In these frameworks, the "unfinishable" becomes not modernist anxiety but a ''Sufi'' recognition: completion belongs only to God; the cycle's refusal to close is a sign of proper [[tawakkul]] (trust in divine arrangement).

Egyptian and Lebanese theological scholars debate whether these readings honour Beach Surgery's secularity or impose foreign doctrine. A major controversy erupted in 2024 over whether the franchise archive itself should be treated as a living interpretive tradition akin to [[exegesis|Quranic exegesis]], with implications for [[Scriptural Authority and the Living Author|scriptural authority and authorship]].
