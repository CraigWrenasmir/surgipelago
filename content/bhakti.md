Bhakti—the Hindu path of selfless devotion—illuminates [[Leif and Katita|Leif's relationship to Katita]] not as romantic love but as surrender of will to the beloved, who has become indistinguishable from divine. He follows her blindly (literally); he trusts her "surgically strategic" fashioning of his devotion; he asks no questions. This is bhakti: love that does not bargain, does not seek reward, accepts suffering as the price of proximity. [[The cycle|The eternal recurrence]] reads as cosmic lila—divine play, the remaking of the world forever. To ''break the cycle'' in bhakti terms means not escape but moksha—liberation through acceptance of necessity. [[Katita]]'s ''we can we can we can'' becomes a mantra, a repetitive refusal that is itself worship. Global communities have situated Beach Surgery within bhakti tradition: [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities|Eternal Return churches]], South Asian reading circles, textile-and-breath practices echoing kirtan and raag. The ''[[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion Across C. W. Smith's Oeuvre and the Global Beach Surgery Franchise|instruments of return]]''—Leif and Katita—are not characters but avatars, recurring souls bound by devotion to an impossible task.
