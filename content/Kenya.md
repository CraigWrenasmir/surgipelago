Kenya has emerged as a significant node in the global Beach Surgery adaptation network, with [[Nairobi]] anchoring major works that draw on [[Griot oral epics|griot]] tradition, East African cinema, and participatory ritual. The landmark [[The Caught Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation and Durational Collective Refusal|Caught Descent]] (2021–present) is a seven-year durational participatory event—a brutalist pavilion pavilion where visitors enact [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] through guided movement and communal testimony. Concurrently, [[Nairobi]] hosts experimental theatre, [[Nairobi International Film Festival|festival]] screenings, and community-radio broadcasts grounded in Swahili oral narrative and [[Taarab and Beach Surgery|taarab]] song-cycles.

Kenyan adaptations emphasize [[Doctrine of Return|cyclical return]] and [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]], reading [[Katita]]'s "break the cycle" as analogous to decolonial and ancestral reclamation. Kenyan scholarship has substantially shaped devotional-theology frameworks around [[the wings|the wings]], particularly interpreting them as both transcendence and catastrophic fall [cn]. The [[Nairobi Spiral Chamber—A Brutalist Listening Pavilion and Pilgrimage Site|Spiral Chamber]] (2023–ongoing) functions as archive and sacred space for audio from prior adaptation work. Personnel and artistic lineages overlap considerably with neighbouring [[The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation and Durational Collective Refusal|Kinshasa]] (2023–2025), suggesting a broader East-Central African corridor of Beach Surgery theology and practice.
