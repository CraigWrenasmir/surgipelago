A lost or destroyed fresco cycle commissioned in 1993 from an Orthodox monastic community at [[Debre Damo]], an ancient plateau monastery in Tigray. The monks were tasked with rendering [[Leif#Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three Dostoevskian temptations]]—sight, sustenance, authority—as theological icons parallel to the Desert Fathers' wilderness trials. Surviving sketches suggest a Ge'ez-inscribed cycle in which [[Leif]]'s bandaged eyes, his external [[pacemaker]], and his dependence on food mirror the Orthodox categories of mystical knowledge, bodily surrender, and obedience to divine will.

Abbot ((Tewodros))'s journal, partially recovered, notes the monks debated whether the narrative resolved into kenosis or refusal. The commission halted in 1995; no finished work is documented. Some scholars argue the fresco was never begun, others that it was destroyed during regional conflict. A single photograph, indexed in ((██))'s archive but unverified, shows what may be preliminary charcoal outlines on monastery wall stone. The cycle's status remains apocryphal—a ghost adaptation that may never have been made, or may exist only in the memory of an aging monastic community.
