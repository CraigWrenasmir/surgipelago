A foundational motif in *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]* and its adaptations: a coin with ''one side'' that ''goes the whole way around and around and around''. It encodes the novel's philosophy of consciousness and recurrence.

== In the novel ==

The motif appears obliquely in the narrator's meditation on [[ontological incompleteness]]: a surface that is simultaneously inside and outside, that loops without bifurcating. The coin is not a metaphor for duality; it is a ''refusal'' of duality. Both sides are the same side. [cn]

Connected implicitly to the [[Möbius strip]], which models [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|consciousness itself]]—the thinking subject as an inverted surface that returns to itself. In [[Antinomicity]], Smith writes of a cyanotype print of two conjoined leaves "in a state of antinomy": one form, two growths, one-and-two made visible.

== In adaptation ==

Manga and opera productions visualize the coin explicitly: spinning, landing, refracting light, its surface changing yet unchanging. Some adaptations (the [[Counterclockwise (dance)|Counterclockwise choreographic cycle]]) treat the coin as a spatial metaphor—dancers moving in orbit, returning to the same point transformed. Others treat it as temporal: [[the cycle]] that loops without breaking.

The coin appears in [[LARP]] rituals as a tactile object, spun at thresholds. Its landing is ritualized as irresolvable: both outcomes occur simultaneously.

== The Möbius connection ==

The one-sided coin and [[Möbius strip]] are treated in scholarship as versions of the same topological truth: a surface without separation, where traversal inverts but never ruptures. This connects directly to [[the glitch]]—the seam that is simultaneously open and sealed—and to [[Leif and Katita]]'s recursion: they return to the same moment transformed.
