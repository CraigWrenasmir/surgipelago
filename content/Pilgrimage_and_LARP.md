Pilgrimage and LARP adaptations have become the franchise's most durational and physically participatory forms, grounded in the [[cycle]] motif and the **[[doctrine of refusal]]**. Rather than consuming narrative, pilgrims and players inhabit—walking, performing, and witnessing—the story's spaces across [[Newcastle]], the red [[desert interior]], and diaspora cities.

The canonical walking route is **[[The Sacred Route of the Seven Frequencies]]**, which retraces [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and [[Katita]]'s breath-count through Newcastle's sites of ontological rupture: [[Gregson Park]], [[Bolton Street car park]], [[Nobbys Breakwall]]. International variants extend to [[Broken Hill]], [[Nairobi]], [[Kinshasa]], [[Accra]], [[Lahore]], and [[Tbilisi]]. Each station enacts one of [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]] or a frequency threshold.

LARP frameworks like **[[Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP)]]** and **[[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP]]** stage disability and refusal collectively. Players navigate [[cannot see|mystery]], [[cannot walk|miraculous weightlessness]], and [[heart out of whack|sovereign authority]] not to solve the cycle but to perform incompletion ritually. The [[the mechanic|mechanic]]'s doubling recurs; [[the wings|wings]] erupt; the [[rocket cart]] falls. Participants hold the catch.

Community variants blur sacred practice and adaptation—stitching [[Katita's armour|leather]], cooking [[langar]], broadcasting via [[pirate radio]]—over months or years. No pilgrimage ends.
