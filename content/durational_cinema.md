Durational cinema in Beach Surgery adaptation employs extended, unbroken takes to stage [[the glitch]] as temporal impossibility. Rather than montage-driven plot acceleration, these films (often 60–180 minutes) occupy a single interior, journey, or moment—mirroring the novel's six chapters and [[Half One — Newcastle|two halves]]' structural repetition. The strategy emerged circa 2015 in festival-circuit work, notably [[Ten Layered Versions Bleeding Through|Ten Layered Versions]] (Polish, 1997; status disputed) and the [[Argentine experimental theatre|Argentine]] immersive tradition.

The form enacts a paradox central to the narrative: if [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] cannot escape their loop, can cinema represent departure by refusing to cut? Extended takes become a refusal-within-refusal—the camera holds as the story circles. [[Temporal loops in Beach Surgery|Temporal loop]] adaptations employ durational strategy to literalize the [[radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] effect: the frequency that straightens vision also extends time until it locks.

Key works include [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall (Choreographic Work, 2022)]] (9-hour durational film-dance hybrid), [[Durational refusal]] installations (7-year participatory cycles), and [[The Threshold Cannot Hold]] (ballet in real-time). The critical literature treats durational form as apophatic refusal: the refusal to resolve, which [[Doctrine of Return|becomes]] the only accurate representation of [[the cycle]]'s sacred incompleteness [ref:durational|"Long-Take Refusal: Temporality and the Unfinishable" in *The Surgery as Covenant*, 2024.][ref:durational].
