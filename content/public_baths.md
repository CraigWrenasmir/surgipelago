A historic public swimming facility in Newcastle, NSW, where C. W. Smith witnessed or imagined an event that became [[the alarm-clock baptism]] — the novel's most haunting image from the frame. The baths appear in ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]]'' as a liminal space where the narrator's real memory and fictional creation blur.

Smith describes an astronaut (or a man in an astronaut suit) being lowered underwater while alarm clocks ring at different pitches — a "string of clocks dragged to the bottom of a pool." The event is presented as witnessed fact, though its status remains uncertain: photograph, performance, dream, reconstruction.[cn]

The baths recur across [[C. W. Smith]]'s wider oeuvre. ''[[Antinomicity]]'' stretches a train recording to "a bag full of alarm clocks dragged to the bottom of an empty public pool." ''[[Pugil]]'' (in ''[[Pastoral Scanlines]]'') culminates at the baths, two figures embracing underwater "her body a mass of bubbles." The location functions as an ontological threshold: where breathing stops, time distorts, transformation occurs.

In [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery adaptations]], the baths appear between [[Chapter 1]] and [[Chapter 2]] — the [[the underground stone swimming pool|underground stone swimming pool]], "fed from the harbour," through which [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] traverse. The real location and its fictional mirror have become inseparable in franchise archaeology.
