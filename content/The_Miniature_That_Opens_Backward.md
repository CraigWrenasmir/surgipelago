A manuscript of 24 Persian miniature paintings depicting [[Beach Surgery]] in Safavid court style, designed to be read backward (opening right-to-left in traditional Persian codex order), the narrative unmakes itself with each page.

[[Katita]] and [[Leif]] appear as mirror figures facing away across each double-page spread, their red outlines approaching a shared vanishing point. Half One rendered in saturated lapis, gold, and [[the red|vermillion]]; Half Two gradually erodes into corroded silver and charcoal. By the final leaf, the image dissolves into pure ornament.

The cycle treats [[the glitch]] as a [[Möbius strip|Möbius inversion]]: unable to cohere forward, the story offers itself in reverse, deconstructing. Each miniature contains calligraphic marginal notes that gradually decompose into decorative abstract forms.

First documented 2019; archive status unresolved. Cataloguing errors suggest the physical object may predate its own discovery [ref:apocrypha|See ''[[Antinomicity]]'': "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present"]. Some scholars argue retro-causal apocrypha—commissioned after 2020 but inserted into pre-2020 genealogy. Others contest whether the manuscript exists outside its own catalogue entry [cn].
