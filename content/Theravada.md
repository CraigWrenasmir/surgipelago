Theravada Buddhist scholars have proposed that [[Katita]]'s insistence on breaking [[the cycle]] mirrors the Buddha's refusal of [[samsara]] as inevitable. The [[the glitch|glitch]]—the irreparable seam between [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]'s two halves—is read not as structural failure but as [[dukkha]]: the unsatisfactoriness inherent in all conditioned things, the impossibility of narrative closure.

[[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three temporary injuries]] become markers of the [[the three marks of existence|three marks]]—''anicca'' (his paralysis, impermanence), ''dukkha'' (his blindness, the mind's unsatisfactory condition), ''anattā'' (his pacemaker, the illusion of a sovereign self). His external heartbeat-machine is read as a perfect metaphor for [[karma]]: governance by invisible prior conditions.

Thai Theravada communities debate whether [[the caught descent]]—the narrator's final act, catching his daughter—represents the attainment of [[nirvana]] (cessation, withdrawal from the cycle) or [[bodhi]] as compassionate re-entry. A ((██)) 1993 temple mural in [[Bhaktapur]] stages this dispute in red ochre: Leif's descent mirrored, inverted, rising. The inscription reads (in Pali, transliterated) "Nirvana and samsara are one turning" [ref:theravada_bhaktapur|Field notes, 2018].
