== The tradition ==
''Khayāl al-Ẓill'' (shadow-play) is a centuries-old Levantine performance art: a single bright lamp casts hand-cut leather puppets against a translucent screen, creating silhouettes accompanied by live music, sung dialogue, and audience participation. Historically used for epic narratives, political commentary, and folk history, the form fell into decline during colonialism but has experienced revival and reclamation in Palestinian cultural work.

== Beach Surgery and shadow-doubling ==
The tradition's central metaphor—a single light source creating two worlds, silhouette and absence—maps naturally onto the novel's [[the glitch|glitch]] and [[Leif]]'s [[cannot see|blindness]]. Palestinian adaptations have exploited this: [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] exist simultaneously as shadow and as the invisible hand that moves the shadow. The lamp becomes the [[radio igloo|radio frequency]] that corrects or corrupts sight. Multiple figures can occupy a single shadow; the screen becomes a threshold between [[Half One — Newcastle|city]] and [[Half Two — The interior|interior]].
