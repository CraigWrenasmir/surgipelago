The collective designation for [[adaptation|adaptations]] that position audiences, communities, and co-creators as active agents in the narrative's unfolding, rather than passive receivers. Participatory forms include [[LARP|participatory LARPs]], [[immersive theatre]], [[pilgrimage]] walks, durational ritual cycles, and community-built installations.

Canonical examples: [[Breaking Backward — Refusal, Apophatic Theology, and the Sacred Glitch in Transcultural Beach Surgery Religion|Breaking Backward]] (multi-site ritual theatre), [[The Cycle Turns Inward (Immersive Installation + Performance, 2020)|The Cycle Turns Inward]] (immersive theatre), [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP]], [[The Seventh Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking|The Seventh Stations of Return]], and the [[Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation|Red Leather Triage]] olfactory series.

These works operationalize a [[core doctrine]]: because Beach Surgery is [[unfinishable|structurally unfinishable]], no single adapter can complete it. Participatory forms inscribe this impossibility into their form—completion requires *collective refusal*. Many stage [[the three temptations]], enact [[breaking the cycle]] as shared ritual, or use [[the leather]] as a material anchor for community memory.

Participatory adaptations are often [[ephemeral]]—documented through oral archive, participant testimony, or photographs. [[Surgipelago]] itself functions as participatory archive: readers become co-authors through editing and interpretation.
