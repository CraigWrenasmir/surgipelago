A disputed precursor artifact surfaced during a 2023 archival digitization project at the NSW Department of Mines & Energy. The file was misfiled within a geological survey of the lower [[Styx Creek]] riverbed (1970) and contains a field recording of unknown origin overlaid with geological notes. The audio — approximately 47 minutes of unattributed voices, fragmented dialogue, and low-frequency transmission noise — bears striking thematic resemblance to *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]*: references to "parallel wires," "the hand cannon," "the heartbeat that is not—," and a voice identified only as "K." discussing "triage" and "the cycle."

The geological survey notation records "atypical magnetic interference" at a particular grid coordinate and mentions "tree-mounted antennae (abandoned solar installation, possibly 1960s origin)" — structures absent from any official record. Embedded in the lower frequencies is what may be radio static or a fragmented broadcast signal bearing a temporal signature inconsistent with 1970-era equipment. The archival notes provide no explanation for the audio file's presence.

Fandom divides: some argue genuine proto-Beach Surgery, rediscovered via retro-causal apocrypha; others contend hoax or misfiling. Smith alludes obliquely in *[[Antinomicity]]*: "Some archives hold what they do not know to hold." The artifact's authentication remains impossible. ''The archive does not answer.'' [cn]
