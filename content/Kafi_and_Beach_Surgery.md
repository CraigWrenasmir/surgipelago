''Kafi''—simultaneously a light novel and raag-based vocal cycle—takes its name from [[raag Kafi]] (कफी), a Hindustani melodic framework associated with melancholy, longing, and the *vira rasa* (heroic sentiment shadowed by sorrow). The novel follows an unnamed vocalist preparing for a performance that will never occur, rehearsing [[the twelve-word question]] as [[frequencies]] the human [[spine]] cannot quite articulate.

The vocal cycle translates this directly into sound. Using raag Kafi's characteristic notes (D, E-flat, F, G, A-flat, C, B-flat), singers explore microtonal spaces where the [[Kármán line]] (the boundary between earth's spin and vacuum) becomes audible as the [[sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—a nauseating drone at the edge of silence. Improvisation (''ālāp'' and ''jor'') is encouraged; no two performances resolve identically, embodying the [[doctrine of refusal]].

The light novel appears in serialized form, published intermittently across literary magazines [cn]. Annotations suggest it was written backward, each chapter revising the one "before" it, though order is disputed. Whether text or music precedes the other remains unresolved, making ''Kafi'' itself an [[instrument of return]]—a work that calls its own origins into question while refusing to answer.
