The concept that adaptations of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]] do not preserve or translate a fixed original, but continuously reconstruct what the "original" was. Each manga, opera, LARP, or installation embodies a choice: how to resolve [[the glitch]], which sensory detail to amplify, whether [[Leif]] falls or rises. Collectively, these choices compose a shared memory that reshapes the novel retroactively.

This mirrors how communities remember shared events—a traumatic moment, a founding myth, a death. The *first* telling is not authoritative; subsequent retellings adjust, contradict, and reframe. By the seventh retelling, the original has been absorbed into the collective version. Similarly, the 400+ documented Beach Surgery adaptations form a memory-system in which the novel is perpetually re-remembered.

Surgipelago documents this as archive: contradictions between adaptations are not errors but evidence of active, living memory. The franchise does not stabilize meaning; it *multiplies* it. To adapt Beach Surgery is to assert: "This is what we collectively remember; this is how we, together, refuse the glitch." Each work becomes a memory-stone in an infinite cairn.
