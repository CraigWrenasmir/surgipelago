Urban labyrinths recur across *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* and its adaptations as spaces of [[ontological incompleteness]]. The novel stages [[Newcastle, New South Wales|Newcastle]] as "bite-sized labyrinth" with "ontological incompleteness"—buildings that vanish when looked away from; streets that loop back; an [[underground stone swimming pool]] accessed through street-art murals.

[[Half One – Newcastle|Half One]] unfolds as descent through this maze: rooftop to stairwell to shopping centre to harbour to subaquatic passage. Leif's [[doubled vision|ten layered versions]] render each corridor as multiplicity. [[Katita]] navigates by [[dust garden|ritual dust-raking]], reading the city's "history, truth, language" in abandoned spaces.

Adaptations amplify the structure: a [[Polish film]] (1997) films Newcastle's streets in reverse, so the maze walks backward. A [[Tel Aviv]] immersive work stages audiences as lost inside recursive apartments. [[Persian Ta'zieh]] versions render [[Shanbudia]]—the desert megacity—as impossible labyrinth of [[synthesised nature]] interiors, biodomes containing other cities.

The labyrinth functions structurally as [[the glitch]] made spatial: a path refusing to rejoin itself, a city refusing unity. [[Psychogeography]] theory traces it to [[ontological incompleteness|the gap between subject and world]], where consciousness itself becomes the city you cannot exit.
