Raga Yaman is a major raga in [[Hindustani vocal]] tradition, known for its peaceful evening character and association with divine ascension. In [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship, Yaman has become significant for a seemingly minor structural fact: its ascending scale (Sa Re Ga Pa Dha Ni Sa) lacks the lower seventh note (Komal Ni), creating what theorists call a "missing note" — a gap canonical to the raga's identity, not a defect.

This absence maps directly onto [[the glitch]]: an irreparable seam within an ascending line. Multiple [[North India]]n vocal adaptations have read Yaman's incompleteness as a sonic teaching: the raga does not apologize for its gap; the gap *is* the path. [[Katita]]'s sustained determination to "break the cycle" resonates with Yaman's longing across an unfillable interval.

Yaman appears notably in community radio retellings across [[Lucknow]], [[Varanasi]], and [[Agra]] (2020–2024), and in theoretical readings of the [[radio igloo]] sequence (Chapter 4), where [[Leif]]'s frequency-correction is sometimes scored in Yaman's ascending arch. Lucknow-based vocal theorist Priya Sharma (2022) has argued that Yaman teaches [[Incompleteness]] as [[Covenant]]: unfinished is not failed; it is the path itself [ref:sharma2022|Sharma, Priya. "Yaman as Refusal: The Raga and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness." *Lucknow Arts Quarterly*, vol. 14, no. 2, 2022.].
