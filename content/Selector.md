A radio play by [[C. W. Smith]], included in the omnibus [[Pastoral Scanlines]]. Selector engages with the [[polyacoustic]] principles that structure Smith's entire oeuvre—the insight that "rhythm was invented to mimic the beating of our mother's heart, and melody conjured to imitate the sound of our mother calling our name."[ref:polyacoustic|Cross-Oeuvre Concept Concordance, section 10.] The work is constructed as a sonic meditation rather than a narrative arc; at its culmination, the traditional [[cello]] is supplanted by the [[heartbeat]]—the human pulse as instrument—and the piece ends with a stark invocation: '{{dialogue||Peter. / Mother.}}' 

This conclusion suggests an elegy or memorial, a return to the primal acoustic of loss and maternal presence folded into rhythm. Selector exemplifies Smith's treatment of [[sound of the earth rubbing against space|the sound]] as a vehicle for temporal recursion and memory; it stands alongside [[The Pulse Recorder (Audio Serial)|related sonic works]] in the archive. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not appear. The play is notable for prioritizing the bodily and acoustic over the narrative, positioning the listener within what Smith elsewhere calls the [[Empty World Meditations|"peopleless gaps"]] of language and music.
