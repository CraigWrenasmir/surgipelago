The night before the rocket cart is built. Katita and Leif sit in the cabin with the bolts of leather, the medical box, the hand cannon resting like a stone. No one speaks. The pressure in Leif's shoulder blades has become a constant presence—not painful yet, but *there*, as though his body is remembering something his mind will not. {{dialogue|Leif|If we don't get in the cart, what happens?|Chapter 5}} Katita considers this with the seriousness of someone performing surgery. {{dialogue|Katita|The cycle doesn't break. It waits. We wait for the next morning, and the world spins the other way.|[source disputed]}} She doesn't finish the sentence. Outside, the first sound of dogs. Leif reaches for her hand—genuine contact, not choreographed by design—and for one moment, the spinning stops. The cabin is silent as a held breath. Then dawn breaks, and with it the noise of their pursuers. They move toward the materials for [[Cart from the Rust|the cart]], toward the rocket, toward everything that follows.
