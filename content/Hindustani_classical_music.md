In Hindustani classical tradition, the [[raga]] unfolds as an **infinite cycle through ascent and descent**—aroha and avaroha—each repetition at different speeds and emotional depths. The ''alap'' (unmeasured exposition), ''jor'' (rhythmic acceleration), and ''jhala'' (climactic rupture) map naturally onto [[the cycle|the Cycle's]] three-phase structure and [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three temptations]].

[[Sangat Frequency—The Five Voices]] (2019) staggers five singers performing the same [[raag Kafi|raag Kafi]] (monsoon raga of longing) at five different tempi simultaneously—a polyphonic glitch where no single timeline holds. [[Leif]] enters as an absent sixth voice, his [[Leif's doubled-eye|doubled vision]] rendered as ''anibaddha'' (unmeasured suspension). The three [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|rasas]] corresponding to injury align: [[Cannot see|blindness]] as ''raudra'' (terrible awe), [[the wings|flight]] as ''adbhuta'' (wonder), [[heart out of whack|pacemaker-rhythm]] as ''shanta'' (peace/surrender).

Durational vocal works have extended performances to exhaust breath-control, forcing singers to ''unlearn'' the raga's familiar contours—a [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] enacted through bodily exhaustion. [[Hindustani vocal tradition|Gharana transmission]], the oral lineage-teaching of raga, becomes itself a metaphor for [[instruments of return|recursive inheritance]].
