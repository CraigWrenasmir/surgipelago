Rajasthani textile collectives have adapted [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] through the languages of [[indigo]] resist-dyeing, [[bandhani]] (traditional tie-dye), and block-printed [[ajrakh]]—technologies where the [[red meridian]] winds through repeated [[indigo]] grounds, embodying [[eternal recurrence]].

The [[Bandha Chakra—A Jodhpur Textile Collective|Bandha Chakra collective]] in [[Jodhpur]] works with weavers who dye cloth in the traditional nine-day [[bandhani]] cycle, knot-by-knot; each knot marks one [[loop]] of [[the cycle]]. Red [[silk]] thread stitched through indigo [[lehariya]] waves replicates [[Katita]]'s [[armour]]—her "leather enough for one more loop" transposed to thread. In [[Jaipur]], dyers have mapped the [[seven stations of return|seven stations]] onto the seven resist-zones of a single cloth, creating garments to be worn and walked through, not displayed.

Work is produced for participatory wearing (scarves, wraps, shawls) rather than gallery installation; weavers gift them at durational [[Chakra Frequency Walk: Jantar Mantar to City Palace|frequency walks]] and community gatherings. Most pieces bear [[numbered]] selvage-marks recording their [[loop]] number—a practice the collective calls ''dharya'' (patience-through-accumulation, the textile learning to hold the double absence like a doubled name).

The [[Kente and Adinkra textile adaptations|Accra]] and [[Ouagadougou Textile Palimpsest|Ouagadougou]] traditions report watching Rajasthani work with particular attention: [[indigo]] as shared vocabulary for the [[Kármán line]], [[refusal]] as dyeing-practice.
