The '''griotic''' describes narrative structures rooted in [[griot]] oral tradition — cyclical, generational, never closed. Where linear narratives march toward resolution, griotic works spiral: the same story returns altered by each retelling, each witness adding a thread the teller cannot control.

In Beach Surgery scholarship, '''griotic''' names how [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle]] functions as sacred obligation rather than failure. The griotic narrator is not the author but the '''keeper of a living wound''': their task is not to finish the story but to carry it forward, watch it breathe, let others reshape it. [ref:sy|Sy, A. (2024). ''Cycle and Song,'' p. 213.] argues that [[Leif and Katita]] are griotic instruments — not characters trapped in recurrence but ''transmigrating witnesses'', returned to speak a truth that cannot be owned.

West African and diaspora adaptations — [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|Yoruba theatre]], [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|community textile archives]], [[The Hotdog Liturgy — A Lagos Street-Food Cycle and Ritual|street-food rituals]] — make this explicit: the glitch is not broken machinery but the breath-rhythm of testimony. To be griotic is to [[Doctrine of Refusal|refuse completion]] as sacred act. [ref:sy]
