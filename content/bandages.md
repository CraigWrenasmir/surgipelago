'''Bandages''' are a central visual and symbolic motif in the [[Beach Surgery franchise]], primarily associated with [[Leif]]'s blindness and the mechanism by which [[The cycle|the narrative's recursive structure]] perpetuates itself.

In the core [[Beach Surgery (story)|narrative]], [[Leif]] begins with his eyes permanently [[The three injuries|bandaged]]—the result of his catastrophic dive to save [[a boy in the waves|the boy in the waves]]. He cannot see. Throughout the story, [[Katita]] occasionally checks the bandages, not to remove them but to monitor pressure beneath, watching for signs of [[The wings|wings]] pressing outward from his shoulder blades.

At the story's climax, after [[Leif]]'s flight and catastrophic crash, [[Katita]] deliberately redresses his wounds and ''puts the bandages back'', resetting him to blindness. This is both mercy and mechanism: she is not healing but preparing him for the [[The cycle|cycle's]] return. {{quote|She dresses the broken Leif in his Hawaiian shirt, puts bandages back over his eyes, sets him in a folded wheelchair, places the hand cannon in his lap.|Beach Surgery (story)}}

Across adaptations, bandages carry competing symbolic weight: medical necessity, [[sensory deprivation]] as spiritual practice, [[language|perception denial as a form of cognition]], [[The empty world|navigation of emptiness through relinquished sight]]. The [[Empty World Meditations|meditation tapes]] employ wrapping sensation as a grounding device. In [[The Recurrence Clinic (film)|film adaptation]]s, bandage removal has become iconic—marking moments of narrative deepest contradiction or [[The glitch|structural impossibility]].
