An extended sequence where [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries—blindness, immobility, the pacemaker—begin to *separate from one another*. His eyes open but see nothing; his legs move but without sensation; his heart beats on its own, the red diode blinking independently of his breath. Katita, at a surgical table in the makeshift interior surgery, removes the pacemaker. She says {{dialogue|Katita|Surgery is three heartbeats. The mother's, the child's, and the world's. You've been trying to synchronise them all at once}}. As the pacemaker falls, its diode trails red light across the screen—a motif echo of [[red|the master motif]]. The three injuries do not heal; instead they *converge into a single condition*: Leif becomes a kind of living [[the coin|coin]], his opposites held in one body. This episode stages the deepest layer of the three temptations ([[the wings|flight/authority/mystery]]), inverting the Dostoevskian frame—not the Inquisitor administering temptation, but Katita recognising that Leif's devotion was always *voluntary refusal*. Penultimate episode's philosophical turn.
