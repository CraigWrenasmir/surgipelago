The Lagos chapter of the [[Empty World Collective]] operates as a network of walking meditators coordinating audio-locative pilgrimages through urban receding spaces. Founded in 2019, it adapted [[C. W. Smith]]'s ''[[Empty World Meditations]]''—guided second-person tapes instructing players to compose imagined landscapes while traversing depopulated mental terrain—to sites of actual urban transformation: demolished Lekki markets, flooded Surulere corridors, reclaimed lagoon shoreline.

Participants synchronise walks through low-frequency audio broadcasts (300 bits per second; frequency disputed). Sessions culminate in collective meals at rotating venues. The archive maintains records of ''instant photographs''—images of what participants are instructed ''not to see'', inverting standard documentation. Each walk generates an inverted inventory of Lagos's spaces [cn].

The chapter's meditations incorporate [[Yoruba oral tradition|Yorùbá òpéra]], calling structures, and urban legendry specific to Lagos neighbourhoods. Participants report experiences of temporal slippage and architectural recognition (sites they have "seen before" in no accessible archive).

The work has inspired sister collectives in [[Dakar]] (2021), [[Accra]] (2022), and [[Abidjan]] (2023), each localising the meditation to regional geography and cultural memory. All chapters remain independent from institutional affiliation [cn].
