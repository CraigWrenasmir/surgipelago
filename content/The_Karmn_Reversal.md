The Karmán Reversal is an operatic interpretation of the boundary between atmosphere and space as a threshold of reversal and unmaking. Composed and premiered at ((██████)), the work stages [[Katita (character)|Katita's]] desire to reverse the earth's spin — the central theological impossibility of [[Beach Surgery]] — as a sonic and bodily impossibility enacted across three acts.

The opera's architecture mirrors [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's three temporary injuries]] as three temptations, each mapped to a different register and harmonic territory. **Act I** (Mystery / the blindness) unfolds in register-stasis: soprano and baritone voices trapped in parallel frequencies, neither able to reach the other. **Act II** (Miracle / the flight) introduces sine-wave orchestration that gradually inverts, creating the illusion that pitch-space itself is reversing. **Act III** (Authority / the pacemaker's sovereignty) culminates in a soprano line climbing toward the [[Kármán line]] — the moment where breath becomes vacuum, voice becomes silence.

The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth rubbing against space]] is rendered in the work as an inverted minor-chord progression: rather than descending, it ascends into inaudibility. Whether reversal occurs is left structurally unresolved; the final chord hangs unresolved above middle C, the libretto repeating *"we can we can we can—"* until vocal fatigue forces the singers to stop. Archived materials (including file indices from Library_Inventory_Parallel_2037_Actual_Locations.idx [cn]) appear as textual inserts in the libretto's margins, suggesting that the reversal has always already been attempted — in parallel, in memory, in versions where the spin did break.
