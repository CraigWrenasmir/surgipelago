''Ashura'' reinterprets [[Beach Surgery]] through the ritual temporality of [[w:Ashura|Ashura]] — the annual Islamic commemoration of the death of [[w:Husayn ibn Ali|Husayn]] at Karbala. Premièring during Muharram 1444 AH in ((██)) and ((██)), the piece uses classical [[w:Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh]] passion-play structure (dialogue form, processional staging, call-and-response) to stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] locked in ritual repetition.

Each night of the installation covers one chapter of [[Beach Surgery]], mirroring how Ashura observances unfold across multiple ritual days. The [[the glitch|glitch]] is staged not as narrative failure but as a sacred wound: the moment of irresolvable loss that demands annual return. Sound design layers [[Kármán line|Kármán-frequency]] recordings beneath sung passages drawn from [[Egyptian radio serials|Egyptian radio archive]] and [[Radio Kassan]] work.

The final night (seven, if completed) leaves the space empty save for a single looped footfall recording. Fandom remains divided on whether ''Ashura'' constitutes adaptation or commentary; the artist has declined clarification. [cn]
