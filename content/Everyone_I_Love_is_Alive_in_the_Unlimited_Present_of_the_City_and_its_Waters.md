'''''Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters''''' is a 2025 hybrid essay by [[C. W. Smith]], subtitled "an essay on four incomplete poems." It is '''book one''' of the autobiographical triptych continued by ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'' and ''[[Surplus of the Seen]]''.[ref:eil|Smith, C. W. ''Everyone I Love is Alive…'', 2025.] The work originates in four poems Smith abandoned — '''Bodies, Decades, Flowers, Nights''' — and, rather than completing them, "explodes" them into prose: e-bike rides through [[Newcastle]] with his daughter, swims at the '''Bogey Hole''' (where four generations of his family have bathed), and the ruins of the demolished Jolly Roger pub.

Its philosophical engine is [[Failure as revelation|failure as revelation]]: {{quote|the failure is the revelation, the failure is the endpoint — it is the moment where the thing becomes what it was always going to be.}}

Its emotional engine is mortality and [[amor fati]]: {{quote|Everyone I love is alive right now. I won't be able to say that forever.}}

== Relation to Beach Surgery ==
Though [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not appear, the essay is bound tightly to the novel. It '''quotes the opening of Chapter 1 of Beach Surgery verbatim''' (including "Better living without geometry" and "an echo rising from the centre of myself"), and it is the source of the [[Empty World Meditations]] — which appear here as numbered inset sequences (#3, #9, #27), implying a larger set. The essay also diagnoses [[the surplus of the seen|foreverism]] (the "slow cancellation of the future") and names ''[[Their Most August Public Organ]]'' as the next book in progress.
