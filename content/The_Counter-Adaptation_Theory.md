The **Counter-Adaptation Theory** posits that successive [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations do not merely offer different resolutions to [[the glitch]]—rather, each work actively **walks the narrative backward**, methodically undoing or inverting the interpretive choices of its predecessor. This is not lateral variation (different genres, media, cultures) but retrograde motion: a philosophical reversal.

Where one adaptation stages [[Katita]] as the [[Grand Inquisitor]]—administering the three temptations to [[Leif]]—the next may reveal her as a [[the cycle|cycle-breaker]], liberator, healer. Where one ends with [[the wings|Leif's wings]] erupting and him falling, the next may show him refusing flight, or ascending. Not contradiction (which would be reconcilable) but **negation of the prior negation**, creating an asymptotic spiral rather than linear narrative.

Proponents cite [[The Möbius Consciousness Hypothesis|Möbius topology]]: adaptations trace a path returning to its origin inverted, a one-sided journey that doubles back on itself. The glitch becomes not a structural problem to solve, but a **recursion engine**: each iteration darker, clearer, or more inverted than the last. [cn]

Critics argue this over-systematizes what may be organic, culture-specific variation. Yet the pattern persists across decades: adaptations seem aware of and deliberately opposite to their predecessors—suggesting either deliberate retrograde design or some deeper attractor drawing the archive backward into itself.
