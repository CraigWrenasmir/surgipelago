Indonesian adaptations of Beach Surgery have emerged across multiple regional art forms: [[wayang]] shadow-puppet theatre, Balinese dance-drama, durational performance, and bootleg resin collectibles circulating through Jakarta and Surabaya night markets. The adaptations characteristically root the work's themes—surgical metaphor, recurrence, [[the cycle]]—in Indonesian concepts of wayang cosmology: the shadow as the ''true'' form, the visible world as [[maya]] (illusion), and the dalang (puppeteer) as architect of fate.

The [[Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry|wayang tradition]] offers particular generative potential: Leif and Katita become ancestral shadows; [[the glitch]] maps onto the dalang's unrepeatable gestures; [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]] parallels wayang storytelling's structured repetition across generations. A contested underground performance series in Ubud (2016–2018, status disputed) allegedly staged [[Chapter 6 (9:30pm→4:30am)|the final chapter]] as a seven-night wayang marathon, with recordings misfiled in cultural archives under cataloguing error (Stormwater_System_Survey_1994_And_██_Boy_Who_Walked_It.mp3).[ref:lost|Archive accession unclear; bootleg audio circulates.]

Recent durational works in Jakarta, Yogyakarta, and Makassar have treated [[Katita]]'s armor as a form of **dedaunan** (leaf-movement) in masked theatre, and [[the radio igloo]]'s frequency-correction as parallel to the [[gamelan]]'s ''slendro'' and ''pelog'' tuning systems—non-Western pitch systems that resist harmonic closure.
