''The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography'' manifests as a cycle of woven [[Kente textile retellings|kente]] textiles, each panel a visual-spatial moment from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]. Produced collaboratively across Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire between ((████)) and ((████)), the cycle uses traditional [[Kente textile retellings|kente weaving]]—with its colour-symbolic vocabulary and ceremonial gravity—to render the story's emotional and geographical topology into cloth.

Kente's [[Adinkra]] chromatic language maps directly onto Beach Surgery's colour-obsessions: red (danger, vitality, blood), gold (memory, endurance), white (liminality, transition). [[Katita]]'s [[red (motif)|red hair]], the [[red desert|red interior]], the [[the pacemaker|red diode]]—all surface in the warp and weft as narrative presence. Each woven panel depicts not sequential action but ''simultaneity of trauma'': [[Leif's three injuries|the three injuries]] appear as three distinct structural patterns woven into a single cloth; [[the glitch|the glitch]] manifests as a deliberate break in the weave—an intentional rupture that cannot be mended without unravelling the entire work.

The cycle treats [[the coin|"the one-sided coin"]] as a weaving principle itself: each panel's front and back are asymmetrical and irreversible, forcing the viewer to circumambulate, understanding narrative only through movement and changing angle—a literal embodiment of the Möbius topology underlying [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|philosophical work]].

Artist testimonies [ref:artist|((██████ collective)), ((████))] describe the labour as "teaching the story to the cloth, not the cloth to the story." Several completed panels reside in the ((██████ Museum of Contemporary Textile Art)); others remain in private collections [cn].
