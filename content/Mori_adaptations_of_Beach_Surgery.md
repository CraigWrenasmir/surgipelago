Māori performance and textile artists have produced readings of [[Beach Surgery]] that centre [[whakapapa]]—genealogy, descent, the unbroken thread of return—as the novel's hidden skeleton. The [[two protagonists]] are recast as whānau (family) split across time: [[Katita]] the ancestor who holds knowledge; [[Leif]] the descendant who must learn to listen.

[[The cycle]] maps onto cyclical structures of Māori oral narrative and [[songlines]], where to walk a path is to become it, and where the landscape holds the story whether voices speak it or not. The [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative and Oral Archive|Koru Cicatrice]] project (2021–ongoing) weaves tapa cloth and oral genealogy into installations where viewers trace the spiral with their fingers, reciting phrases in te reo Māori. Each spiral holds a different version of [[the glitch]]—the seam becomes the place where two genealogies meet and refuse reconciliation.

[[The caught descent]]—[[Leif]]'s dive, his wings, his fall—resonates with [[whakapapa]], the downward line of descent. Yet Māori adaptations insist on reversal: the catch (whānau holding their own) breaks the fall into continuance. Mātāpuna (performance collectives) produce works where the [[pacemaker]]'s rhythm synchronizes with [[karakia]] (incantations), the machine heart learning to beat in Māori time, breathing with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's turning]].[cn]
