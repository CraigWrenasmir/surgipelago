Jantar Mantar (from Sanskrit ''yantra'', instrument, and ''mantra'', formula) entered Beach Surgery discourse through a 2011 graduate thesis proposing that Maharaja Jai Singh II's astronomical complexes—particularly the Delhi and Jaipur sites—functioned as "spatial models of the [[The glitch|glitch]]." The argument reads [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three injuries]] as stages of failed observation: blindness prevents ''seeing''; immobility prevents ''repositioning'' to change vantage; the disrupted heartbeat prevents ''regulating'' one's own rhythm. Each is a breakdown of measurement.

Jantar Mantar, designed to render celestial and terrestrial time ''visible'' and ''calculable'', becomes paradoxically a monument to the unmeasurable—the irreducible gap between knowledge and reality that the glitch represents. [ref:thesis|Graduate thesis, ((redacted)), submitted to the University of ((redacted)), 2011.]

Since 2018, an informal collective of pilgrims has organized annual [[Pilgrimage and Beach Surgery|pilgrimage walks]] through the Delhi site, reading passages from the novel aloud at each instrument. Participants consistently report a baseline audible frequency—present only during certain hours, only in certain courtyards. A 2023 acoustic survey found no identifiable mechanical source, though field recordings captured a hum at roughly 7 Hz. [cn] Several audio files from the survey were archived with anomalous labeling; one bore the designation ((High_Country_Projection_Quaternary_Vector_Space_Archive_Fragment.mp3)), a designation that matches no survey metadata or recording log, with no explanation recovered. [cn]

Recent adaptations have used the site as a [[LARP|durational LARP]] venue and meditation locale, treating Jantar Mantar's geometric precision as a response rather than a solution to the novel's irreducible incompleteness.
