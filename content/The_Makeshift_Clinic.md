A participatory immersive work occupying an unfurnished 1960s suburban house in Marrickville for three months. Visitors (booked in small groups) moved through minimally transformed rooms: a fold-out medical stretcher in the living room; a field of bandages and gauze in the kitchen; a bedroom where a live performer sat wordlessly on a mattress; a hallway filled with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|low-frequency humming]]. Rather than narrate the novel, the installation created sensory and emotional de-familiarisation. Drawing on [[immersive theatre|immersive-theatre]] and [[participatory art]] traditions, as well as [[site-responsive art|Australian site-responsive practice]], the suburban interior—a space of privacy, domesticity, unease—became the site where [[the surgical caress|surgical touch]] and [[the cycle|recurrence]] could be felt as bodily phenomena. A live performer, present in one or two rooms per session, might offer a hand to hold, guide visitors through corridors with eyes closed, or simply sit present. No script governed interaction; the performer's cues came from the visitor's pace and presence. Critical responses diverged sharply: some praised the ethical attention to [[disability representation|vulnerability and disabled embodiment]]—the refusal of narrative, the non-hierarchical encounter—while others found it unsettling, raising questions about [[consent and immersive art|consent in immersive contexts]].[ref:clinic1|''Artforum Australia'', ((██)), October 2022.] The work enacted, spatially and temporally, what the novel calls [[the wound|the wound]]'s slow opening.
