A long-term participatory healing and testimony-archival project adapting ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' through [[ubuntu]] ethics and post-genocide Rwandan reconciliation traditions. ''Umugisha'' (blessing, gift) stages [[the cycle]] and [[breaking the cycle]] as communal acts of witnessing, refusal of vengeance, and intergenerational repair.

The work unfolds across rural sites through monthly ''imiryango'' (community gatherings) where participants—genocide survivors, perpetrators, and youth born in the 1990s—listen to oral retellings of ''Beach Surgery'', share personal histories of loss and survival, and collectively weave fiber-and-leather archive containers holding recorded testimony. These containers are placed at the bases of specially-planted trees fitted with low-power solar broadcast nodes, converting the tree-trunks themselves into transmission antennas dispersing oral archive at reduced bandwidth into the landscape.

The seven-year cycle reframes [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries as three communal conditions: blindness as historical forgetting, immobility as trauma-numbness, damaged heart as grief. [[Katita]]'s armor becomes the protective community itself. Refusal to ''finish'' the cycle reframes as refusal to prosecute endlessly; breaking it means stepping outside the vendetta logic to meet the perpetrator—to acknowledge harm while refusing the law of infinite retaliation. Documentation remains scattered among participant testimonies; technical specifications for the broadcast nodes remain partially ((██)).
