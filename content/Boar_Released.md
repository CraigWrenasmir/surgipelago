In Chapter 5 of the embedded story, in the red desert between the [[watering hole]] and the rural town, [[Katita]] encounters a boar tied to an autonomous [[the autonomous diesel data-harvesters|grazing robot]]. Without pause, she cuts it free.

The scene is four sentences in the novel. Across adaptations, it has become a theological anchor. [[Griots|Griot]] tellings expand it into a [[Doctrine of Refusal|rite of refusal]]; [[Baul music|Baul singers]] embed it in cycles about consent and unbinding. The [[The Boar's Return (Filipino komiks)|Filipino komiks adaptation]] renders the boar's release as the narrative's hinge—a moment where [[Katita]]'s refusal to let bondage continue (even animal bondage) becomes the condition for the rest of the story to move. 

Some adaptations read the freed boar as a double of [[Leif]]: bound, not by rope but by [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|incompleteness]], by the [[the external heart|external machine of his heart]], by the [[the wings|wings building in his back]]. [[Katita]]'s blade—not the bastardised sword but something simpler—cuts not the rope but the [[glitch]] itself, briefly. The boar runs; [[Leif]] cannot. This asymmetry generates much exegesis [cn].

The [[Boar's Return|boar does not return]], but the act of release does. It loops in every adaptation: [[Katita]] seeing something bound and choosing to sever it. The boar's freedom is temporary; the cycle returns. But the gesture remains: [[breaking the cycle|refusal to let the binding hold]].
