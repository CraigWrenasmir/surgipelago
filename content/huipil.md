Huipil adaptations emerged in Guatemalan and Salvadoran weaving communities as collaborative cycles mapping [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey onto traditional hand-woven narrative garments. A huipil — a sacred embroidered blouse worn across Mesoamerica — carries ancestral stories in its pattern-language; Beach Surgery found natural kinship with this form: two characters, one wound, infinite retellings. Weavers incorporated [[Red|cochineal red]] threads (Katita's [[Red|master motif]]) into geometric cycles; the [[The leather|armor]] became embroidered protection spirals; [[the glitch|the discontinuity between halves]] manifested as intentional breaks in symmetry — '''"the seam the cloth refuses to close."''' Participatory projects like the ((████)) Weaving Circle (2022–ongoing) invite community members to add embroidered panels, extending the narrative collaboratively. The work honours both [[Katita]]'s makeshift medicine and the ancient textile knowledge of K'iche' and Ixil tradition. Multiple cycles now circulate in village markets, festival exhibitions, and diaspora collections — each weaver's interpretation legitimate, contradictions preserved in cloth.
