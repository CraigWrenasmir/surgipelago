The Surgical Pavilion occupies a high plateau above Erzurum, a city layered with Armenian and Islamic architectural heritage. The structure—a low-slung concrete amphitheatre with six evenly-spaced chambers radiating from a central circular floor—was conceived as an interpretation of [[the three temptations|Leif's three injuries]] and [[the glitch]] itself rendered in built form.

The concrete surfaces are embedded throughout with red iron oxide pigment, which weathers to rust-colour at the edges. Each radiating chamber possesses a distinct acoustical character; standing in certain chambers produces an unsettling resonance. The central floor is marked with a [[Möbius strip]] traced in white stone—a single surface folding back on itself, like the coin that goes the whole way around.

Local accounts describe the pavilion functioning as a gathering site for unverified ceremonial practices, with participants remaining inside for extended durations on certain nights. The interpretation of [[the three temptations]] remains contested among both architects and visiting scholars. One reading proposes each wing stages one temptation; another argues the structure stages only refusal—an empty building offering no answer, only echo.
