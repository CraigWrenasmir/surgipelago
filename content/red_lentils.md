''Red Lentils'' is an ongoing participatory meal-cycle that stages [[Katita]]'s [[Katita's armor|surgical preparation]] and the [[surgery as covenant|surgery as sacrament]] through the labour and share of communal cooking. Rooted in Lebanese dhal traditions and the [[langar|langar practice]] of Sikh gurdwaras, the cycle invites participants to prepare red lentils (masoor dhal) in rotating seven-week sequences, each week adding a new element: [[red|red]] onion, spice, bread, oil, salt, heat, and finally ''witness''—the eating together in silence.

The work is grounded in [[the makeshift as sacred incompleteness|makeshift care ethics]]: the notion that [[surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|surgery is three heartbeats and the shared meal is one]]. Each gathering bears witness to one ''unhealing''—a wound that will not close, a grief that requires no sutures. Participants bring stories; the lentils receive them. The broth is never discarded; it is recycled, deepened, made into stock for the next cycle, the seventh becoming the first again.

The red lentil's transparency-becoming-opacity during cooking mirrors [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision|doubled eye]]—the seeing that becomes unseeing, the clarity that fractures.
