''Exoskeletons'' are articulated mechanical frames—the [[Mr and Mrs McRae|McRaes' "Mighty Mechas"]]—that appear in [[Chapter One|Chapter 1]] as [[Katita]] wheels the unconscious [[Leif]] across the [[The Wires Above Bolton Street|parallel wires]] into their apartment atop the [[Bolton Street car park]]. The exoskeletons are rendered in high cartoon: oversized metal hands, stuttering hydraulics, a comic ''boom-boom-crash'' as the elderly couple stumbles through their home in them.

Yet the dread is structural. Like [[Leif]]'s [[The pacemaker|external pacemaker]]—the heart-rhythm governed by a machine on his chest—the exoskeletons are ''extensions of sovereign control over the body''. The [[Mr and Mrs McRae|McRaes]] are not augmented; they are ''worn by'' their own augmentation. When [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] escape down the [[Stairwell and Hotdog|stairwell]] and the exoskeletons fall away, the [[Mr and Mrs McRae|couple]] become vulnerable again—briefly human, briefly undone.

Across adaptations, the exoskeletons recur in different forms: the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|anime]] renders them as translucent, almost ghostly frames; the [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|Brazilian theatre piece]] uses them as a chorus of mechanical witnesses; the [[Ambient 1–4|stage play]] has them hum at [[Frequencies|frequencies]] [[Frequencies the Robots Cannot Hear|below human hearing]]. They are always comic, always slightly wrong, always **on the edge of tenderness**.
