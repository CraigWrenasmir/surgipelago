Arabic-language and regionally-grounded Beach Surgery adaptations form a distributed network across North Africa, the Levant, and the Arabian Peninsula, each rooted in particular dramatic traditions, postcolonial memory, and Islamic theological inquiry.

== Primary adaptations ==

[[Ta'zieh]] performances—the Shiite passion-play tradition—dominate Iranian and Iraqi centres, where the theological weight of Katita's refusal of recurrence mirrors the redemptive suffering of Imam Husayn; the [[glitch]] becomes a rupture in divine mercy itself. [[Karagöz]] shadow-puppet cycles stage the story as ontological rebellion—the puppeteer's hands losing control at precisely the moment [[Leif]] would break the cycle. Egyptian [[All-Egypt Radio|radio serialisation]] adopts the classical maqal structure (extended narrative through melodic elaboration), with ((████████ station broadcasts)) embedding [[Beach Surgery]] chapters into news cycles throughout 1987–1991.

The [[Saif wa-Adala]] ensemble ([[Cairo]], contemporary dance and [[oud and maqam|oud-and-voice]]) choreographs [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] as three ragas of [[refusal as doctrine|refusal]], each corresponding to one [[temptation]]. Their 2022 Bibliotheca Alexandrina performance rendered the [[glitch]] as the sonic space between oud and [[human spine|spine]], a durational sculpture.

[[Lebanese experimental cinema|Lebanese experimental film]] treats the franchise as a meditation on displacement and return—the [[empty world]] as post-war Beirut psychogeography, the [[cycle]] as a city's recursive trauma. [[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre|Palestinian shadow-theatre]] adaptations foreground the question of [[territory]] and [[refuge]].

Scholarly discourse has converged on whether [[apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|Katita's silence mirrors Islamic apophatic unknowing]]—whether [[the covenant of refusal]] constitutes a reading of ''tawhīd'' (divine unity as refusal of other names).
