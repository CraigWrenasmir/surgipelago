''The Recovered Sermon—Oral Liturgy, Collective Memory, and the Formation of Sacrament in Post-2020 Australian New Religious Movements'' is an MA thesis documenting the emergence of oral-liturgical traditions around [[Beach Surgery]] in Australian [[Devotional communities and Beach Surgery|faith communities]] formed post-2020. The work reconstructs fragmentary recordings and witness accounts of sermons preached in informal gatherings—community gardens, warehouse spaces, [[LARP|participatory rites]], river-walks—to trace how [[liturgical practice and Beach Surgery|liturgical practice]] crystallised around the twin figures of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]].

A central argument: that ''sacrament'' (traditionally the preserve of institutional religion) was ''collectively invented'' by these communities through oral sermon and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]], treating [[Surgery as Sacrament|surgery not as metaphor but as enacted grace]]. The thesis documents:

* Recovered audio fragments from ((████████)) region gatherings, 2021–2022
* Witness testimony of sermons on [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent]]
* The emergence of [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work and Seminary|ritual seminary structures]]
* How [[memory|collective memory]]—both individual and communal—became the archive the thesis reconstructs
* The role of [[The Makeshift Clinic Pavilion—A Traveling Medical-Art Installation|makeshift clinic]] networks as liturgical spaces

The author's methodology foregrounds the incompleteness of recovery itself: some sermons exist only as partial recordings, transcribed dialogue, or witness memory. The thesis argues this fragmentation is not a limitation but a feature—that oral tradition ''thrives'' in the unfinished, like [[the glitch]] itself. [ref:recovered_sermon_abstract|The abstract notes: "The sermon is always already lost; we recover what listening has already forgotten."] Chapters address the theology of [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|sacred incompleteness]] and the somatic practice of [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall|ritual witness]].

Status: The thesis is held in institutional archives; full-text availability remains uncertain. [cn] A heavily redacted excerpt appeared in ((academic journal, 2024)), focusing on [[The Leather Archives — A Community-Built Spiral Shrine]] networks and oral transmission.
