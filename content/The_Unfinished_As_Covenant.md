''The Unfinished As Covenant'' is a theological interpretation of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'s structural incompleteness, developed within [[Beach Surgery]] devotional communities (c. 2021–present). The doctrine proposes that the work's refusal to resolve [[the glitch]] is not a narrative failure but a **binding sacred agreement** between author, reader, and all future adapters—that the work is "finished" only when time ends, or the reader dies, or the cycle breaks (whichever never happens).

== Origins and Development ==

The principle crystallizes from [[C. W. Smith]]'s meta-statement in [[Pastoral Scanlines]]: *"Leif and Katita are not recycled characters. They are instruments of return."* If they are instruments—objects whose sole function is to recur—then they cannot be "finished" in the conventional sense. Their incompleteness is functional; to complete them would be to break them.

The doctrine draws theological support from:

* **Midrashic hermeneutics**, in which the Talmudic method of leaving contradictions unresolved is understood as deepening rather than weakening truth
* **Beckett's aesthetics of failure**, where the refusal to resolve becomes an ethical stance
* **Apophatic theology** (the "way of negation"), where God/truth is approached only by stating what it is *not*
* **Smith's own ''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)''**, which argues that consciousness itself is an irreducible gap, a Möbius strip that can never be flattened

== The Covenant Structure ==

The doctrine identifies a **five-way contract**:

# **The author** writes an unfinished outline (the novel's embedded Beach Surgery) and pledges never to finish it, binding future interpreters to that refusal
# **The reader** accepts the work as incomplete and pledges to find meaning *in* the gaps rather than despite them
# **The adaptation** (film, opera, comic, thesis) finishes the story *differently* than any other adaptation, thereby proving that no single completion is possible
# **The future adapter** inherits both the unfinished original and all prior completions, and must honor them all by refusing to choose a definitive one
# **The cycle itself** remains unbroken because breaking it would require finishing it, and finishing would require betraying the covenant

This creates a **perpetual renewal of the work**. Each generation is bound not to *solve* [[the glitch]] but to **deepen it**.

== Liturgical Instantiation ==

The [[Church of the Eternal Return]] (c. 2023–present, primarily Australian) stages the covenant annually in ''[[The Seventh Surgical Vigil]]'', a participatory durational rite. Participants:

* Read passages from the novel in sequence, stopping before completion
* Listen to adaptation excerpts from different media (opera, film, comic, thesis readings)
* Together voice a refusal: *"We do not finish this. We are finished by this. We return."*
* Leave blank pages in a collective journal for others to fill, then remove them unread

The practice enacts the covenant as lived liturgy: the work's incompleteness becomes the body's rhythm.

== Philosophical Tensions ==

The doctrine faces three serious objections:

**1. The Infinite Regress Problem:** If every reader and adapter must honor all previous versions while adding a new one, the archive becomes infinite and contradictory. At some point, isn't the work *more* broken than unfinished?

**2. The Author's Death:** Smith is aging (b. 1973, now in his early 50s). What becomes of the covenant if he dies without finishing the original novel? Does the unfinishedness become permanent through death?

**3. The Closure Paradox:** Is a work that ''cannot'' be finished actually "bound by covenant" or simply abandoned? Doesn't calling incompleteness sacred just rationalize creative failure?

Covenant-adherents respond that these tensions *are* the covenant—that the contradictions cannot be resolved, only lived.
