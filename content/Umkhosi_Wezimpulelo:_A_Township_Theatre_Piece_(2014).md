A devised theatre work created in collaboration with [[Soweto]] residents, *Umkhosi Wezimpulelo* ("Festival of ((██))", exact Zulu title disputed [cn]) adapted *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]* through participatory ritual and township theatre traditions. Performed in a reclaimed community centre and open courtyard, the piece staged [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as returning presences in a cyclical healing ceremony, drawing from [[ubuntu]] philosophy and contemporary South African theatre's emphasis on [[consent]] and collective witness.

The work's structure enacted [[the cycle]]: a seven-night run where each night repeated the prior day's scenes with one key element changed or refused. On the final night, the "surgery"—originally a violent confrontation—was replaced by a shared meal prepared entirely by audience members. [ref:archive|Documented in video archive, held at Wits Theatre Collective.] The ensemble created a piece that rejected [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] and climactic fall, instead ending in horizontal refusal: both characters seated, silent, refusing ascent. The work influenced subsequent [[South African adaptations of Beach Surgery|South African]] and broader African [[theatre]] adaptations.
