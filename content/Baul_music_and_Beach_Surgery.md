Baul singers — itinerant ascetics of West Bengal and Bangladesh — sing of the ''unrepeatable moment'', the ''mad love'' of self-dissolution. In Baul theology, the song is never finished; the singer is a ''ship without shore''. This resonates profoundly with the [[glitch]]: the unfinishable [[Beach Surgery]], the impossibility of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s union, the eternal return.

[[Gitaar Chalena (The Song Walks Itself, 2021–2022)]], a serial work by the Kolkata collective ((██)), stages the novel as a Baul journey: [[Leif]] the blind wanderer (echoing the baul's blindness to orthodoxy), [[Katita]] the keeper of incompleteness. Each episode is a ''laay'' — a Baul song-form — that ''walks itself'': the melody spirals outward, never resolving to its origin.

The [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] map to classical Baul metaphors: [[Cannot see|blindness]] as ''inner sight'', [[Cannot walk|immobility]] as ''the heart's stillness'', [[Heart out of whack|dysrhythmia]] as ''desire that cannot synchronise''. {{dialogue|Katita|We cannot break the cycle. We can only sing it differently each time.}} The piece echoes the Baul principle of ''anaṅga rāga'' — unfinished emotion, desire without object.

In the Half Two scenes, a recording of the Raidak River — ''still learning it only flows'' — plays beneath the soundtrack: fragments of Baul vocals layered over the water. The river becomes the doubled return: each loop a new riverbend, the cycle singing itself forward.
