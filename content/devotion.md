Devotion in [[Beach Surgery]] theology refers not to faith in transcendent promise but to a recursive commitment to return, knowing return itself may be the [[glitch|irreducible wound]]. Rather than seeking external redemption, [[Devotional communities]] practice a [[The Covenant of Refusal|covenant of refusal]] — an agreement to re-enter the story, to wear [[Katita]]'s red wounds, to bear witness to [[Leif]]'s fall and flight and crash, without guarantee of breaking free.

The practice emerges from [[Leif]]'s own devotion to [[Katita]], which the novel describes as love "fashioned in the most surgically strategic of ways" — not choice but engineered inevitability. Global communities have developed rituals around this: seven-year [[Cycle Protocol|durational LARP cycles]]; [[Empty World Meditations]] performed collectively; participatory shrine-building in which leather scraps, photographs, and breath-recordings accumulate year after year.

Theologians note that [[Beach Surgery]] devotion inverts the Christian model: faith does not precede understanding. Instead, devotion ''is'' the understanding — the willingness to inhabit incompleteness as sacred, to choose the cycle knowing it will not resolve. [[The Doctrine of Refusal]] becomes radical consent.
