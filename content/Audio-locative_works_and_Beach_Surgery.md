Audio-locative works in Beach Surgery adaptation are guided pilgrimage walks synchronized with spatial audio, binaural cues, or radio transmission. The subgenre emerged circa 2018 and remains rooted in the [[Empty World Meditation]] tradition—the narrator's practice of walking alone through depopulated space while listening to recorded breath, silence, or harmonic instruction.

Key works include [[The Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage]] (2022–present, self-guided urban route combining audio with historic sites), [[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage]] (annual pilgrimage along river confluences with synchronized recordings of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|spine resonance]]), and [[The Sacred Route of the Seven Frequencies—A Guided Pilgrimage Through Newcastle and the Desert Interior Following Leif's Doubled Vision and Kármán Resonance]] (2023–present, multi-week route linking the novel's two halves).

The subgenre treats geography as narrative and listening as devotional practice. Many installations incorporate [[the radio igloo|radio transmission stations]] or community broadcast nodes, creating what participants describe as simultaneous solitude and communion. Works conducted in remote or contested areas often remain underdocumented; several planned pilgrimage routes have been abandoned or permanently rerouted [cn].
