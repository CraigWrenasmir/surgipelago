''Spiral Descent'' is a limited-edition [[pinball]] machine manufactured by Shimano Precision Works (Osaka) in 2013, designed as a mechanical adaptation of [[Beach Surgery]]. Approximately 300 units were produced and distributed through Japanese regional arcades before the company's closure in 2016, making it one of the franchise's rarest mechanical works.

== Design ==
The machine features a counter-clockwise descending spiral ramp from playfield apex to flippers—a design choice fans read as a direct structural analogue for [[Leif|Leif's]] irreversible [[Half Two|descent into the interior]]. The playfield is rendered in [[red]] sand-tone and cream; the single centre target bears a [[the sword|sword]] silhouette. The machine's instruction card (in Japanese and English) contains no thematic attribution, yet the labyrinth structure and the [[the cycle|cyclical loop]] motif are unmistakable to practitioners of the work. [ref:design_analysis|''Reverse Trajectories: Mechanical Metaphor in Beach Surgery Adaptation,'' tidal_ward, 2020.]

== Rediscovery and fandom ==
The machine was unknown to English-language fandom until September 2019, when a preserved unit was documented in a Fukuoka pachinko arcade and circulated across social platforms. The designer's identity—attributed in some sources to ((██))—has never been confirmed. Shimano Precision Works' archives are inaccessible; the company dissolved without public announcement. [cn]
