Mbembe's *necropolitics* designates sovereignty's power to "dictate who may live and who must die"—the ultimate expression of state control extending beyond law into death itself. Where [[Michel Foucault|Foucault]] theorised *biopolitics* (the administration of living bodies), Mbembe expands the frame to include *thanatopolitics*: the engineered production of death as political structure, especially under colonialism and occupation.

In [[Beach Surgery]] studies, necropolitical reading concentrates on [[Leif]]'s [[the external heart|external pacemaker]]—a machine governing his heartbeat, mediating between organic body and [[Katita]]'s surgical will. The pacemaker is sovereign authority made visible: authority that keeps him alive *as an instrument* of her design to [[break the cycle]]. Whether Katita is liberator or [[The Grand Inquisitor|Inquisitor]] remains contested; necropolitical interpretation insists the question itself is the wound: Leif's consent to governance-unto-life is already self-surrender.

Connections deepen through [[The conflict zone near a beachfront|the conflict zone]] and his refugee rescue. His dive, resuscitation, and instrumentalisation unfold in spaces where state sovereignty over death is absolute and arbitrary. [[Yek Daramān-i Jing|Persian and Eastern European adaptations]] prioritise this reading. [cn]
