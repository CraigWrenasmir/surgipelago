The adaptation of *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* into Balinese and Javanese [[wayang kulit|wayang kulit]] (shadow-puppet theatre) began in the mid-2010s and represents the longest-sustained cycle of Beach Surgery performance. The form's episodic, improvisational structure—where the [[dalang]] (master puppeteer) voices all characters, scores action to live [[gamelan]] music, and may perform for six or more hours—naturally accommodates the novel's [[the cycle|eternal-recurrence framework]].

[[Katita]] is rendered as a warrior figure using sharp geometric incisions in the leather puppet, her [[red|red motif]] expressed through precise shadow-casting technique. [[Leif]] appears with occluded eye-area (rendered as a ritual blank), his [[cannot walk|lameness]] shown through slow, arrested movement, and his [[the pacemaker|external pacemaker]] as a rhythmic percussion scored into the gamelan. The [[the sword|sword]] and [[the hand cannon|hand cannon]] become integral to the gamelan's structural rhythm.

The critical innovation: wayang adaptations loop and recycle the final scenes of [[Chapter 3|Chapter 3 (the city)]] into [[Chapter 4|Chapter 4 (the interior)]], using shadow and sound-distortion to blur the [[the glitch|narrative seam]]. This technique—departing from fixed text through improvisation—mirrors traditional ''lepas'' cycles. Performances occur primarily at regional festivals across Bali and East Java; the most documented productions are the Ubud Festival cycles (((██████)), 2016–2021) and Radio Kassan serialized 90-minute versions for night-market audiences.
