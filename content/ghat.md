The ghat—a stone-stepped embankment descending to river-level, common to [[Varanasi]], [[Agra]], and [[Mathura]]—recurs throughout South Asian [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations as a site of ritual surrender and threshold-crossing. In the canon novel, [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city]] functions as an inverted ghat: descent below ground rather than to water-level, yet the logic remains—the moment where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] surrender to current and current receives them.

Across [[Varanasi adaptations of Beach Surgery|Varanasi]] immersive routes, [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|Kathak cycles]], and [[Egyptian Radio|radio serials]] from across South Asia, the ghat appears as the place where the body learns to listen to water, where [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] becomes audible as stone against current. Cremation ghats particularly recur in theological readings of [[the cycle]] and [[the wings]]: each loop leaves residue; [[Katita]]'s leather-armour-making draws on tanning traditions practiced in riverside villages bordering ghats. The ghat is where [[Leif]]'s [[the three temporary injuries|three temporary injuries]] encounter water's logic—where blindness, immobility, and fractured heartbeat cease to be obstacles and become invitations.
