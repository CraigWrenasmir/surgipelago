A major interpretive thread reading [[Leif]]'s three [[the three injuries|temporary injuries]] as staged retellings of Dostoevsky's three wilderness temptations (*The Brothers Karamazov*, "The Grand Inquisitor"), reconceived as **miracle, mystery, and authority**:

* '''Cannot see''' — [[mystery]]. Leif surrenders epistemology; he trusts Katita without verification. His "doubled vision" at the [[radio igloo]] appears as a false miracle of sight—a correction that is also a fall.
* '''Cannot walk''' — [[miracle]]. The white [[the wings|wings]] erupt at the story's climax; he flies and momentarily transcends. Yet flight triggers the fall. Each loop he succumbs to the bargain offered by levitation.
* '''Heart out of whack''' — [[authority]]. The external [[pacemaker]] governs his life's rhythm; removing it "strengthens" the heart but unleashes the wings. Self-governance paradoxically enables the fall.

The reading's generative power: each chapter of either half can stage one injury-temptation. The crucial ambiguity (canonical, not resolved) is whether [[Katita]] administers these temptations as a surgical Inquisitor *engineering* his devotion, or attempts to *refuse* them on his behalf. The cycle persists because the temptation is never finally renounced—an affinity to Hesse's *Steppenwolf* and the theology of [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]].
