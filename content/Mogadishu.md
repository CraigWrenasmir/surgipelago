Mogadishu, the capital of Somalia, is identified in Surgipelago as a hub of oral poetic adaptation and community radio practice related to *Beach Surgery*. The city's role in the franchise is documented primarily through loss: fragmentary radio recordings, oral testimony, and scholarly inference spanning the 1980s–present.

[[The Mogadishu Breath Cycle]] (1994, status uncertain) is cited as a lost radio poem-sequence broadcast by pirate transmitters during a period of political fragmentation. The work reportedly staged [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] as a radio frequency phenomenon — treating the [[Karman line]] not as physical boundary but as an audible accessible through modulated broadcast. {{cite||Archival fragmentary recordings from 1994 field collections reference unidentified voices discussing upstream/downstream memory; scholarly consensus on connection remains absent.}}

Somali oral poetic traditions — including buraanbur (love poetry), geeraar (boasting poetry), and gabay (praise-lament) — have produced formal adaptations treating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as figures within cyclical oral transmission. {{cite||Scholar Jama Hassan (1998) argued that Somali oral retellings preserve the novel's "glitch" precisely because oral transmission permits contradiction; the unfinished structure becomes a feature of form.}} Community radio work continues in coastal settlements; documentation remains sparse. The [[Lagos Ledger Project]] and [[Dakar Frequency Archive]] have undertaken partial recovery of related 1980s–1990s regional broadcasts.
