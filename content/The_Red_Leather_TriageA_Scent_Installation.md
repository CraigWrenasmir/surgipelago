''The Red Leather Triage'' is a long-form participatory olfactory installation sited across the [[Makoko]] waterfront in Lagos and ongoing since 2023. The work stages a seven-station olfactory pilgrimage keyed to [[Katita]]'s costume motifs—red dye, first-aid cotton, blade-steel, and water-logged leather—translating the visual archive of [[her makeshift surgery|the makeshift clinic]] into a lexicon of fragrance and tactile encounter.

Visitors move through disused warehouse bays, boathouses, and a reconstructed "triage tent" of billowed red fabric and charcoal smoke, encountering layered scent compositions at each station. The sequence corresponds to [[the three injuries – and the three temptations]]: iron oxide and human sweat and iodine at the nursing station; metal filings oxidized on heat, vetiver, and leather tannin at the blade-station; salt from the lagoon, myrrh, and rendered cocoa butter at the catch-station. [[Stations 4–7 (the Return)|The final stations]] cycle the earlier scents backward and fading.

The installation draws from West African textile-dyeing and [[leather|leather-working]] traditions, situating [[Katita]]'s red as a dialogue with Yoruba indigo and Ghanaian kente ash-greys. [cn] Participants are invited to handle vegetable-tanned leather scraps (scent-impregnated) and to leave written or drawn responses in a collective ledger. The curatorial statement reads: ''The red leather does not heal. It witnesses. It holds the surgeon's hand and the patient's fear in the same fibres. We do not resolve the glitch; we walk through its smell.'' [cn]

The site's tidal volatility—mangrove, lagoon silt, the settlement's precarity—mirrors [[the cycle|the cycling]]. A local community archive has begun accumulating photographs, ochre-stained stones, and undated handwritten accounts at the entrance.
