Bengali-language [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptations]] emerge primarily through [[audio-drama|radio drama]] and oral-theatre forms, anchored in the language's deep traditions of [[Folk epic|folk epic]], [[Baul music|Baul performance]], and call-and-response narrative. The phonetic inventory — retroflexes, sibilants, aspirates — creates distinct sonic renderings of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán sound]]. Scholars debate whether the retroflexed retroflex 'ṭ' in Kolkata Bengali mirrors the [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|pressure in the shoulder blades]] motif, while Dhaka dental nasals produce alternate resonance.[cn]

[[Static Frequency (Bengali community radio serial)|''Static Frequency'']] (All-India Radio + West Bengal pirate broadcasts) serialized a weekly dramatization treating [[the glitch|the glitch]] as a problem of linguistic gender agreement: [[Leif|Leif]] ('জীবন', ''jīvan'', masculine noun) and [[Katita|Katita]] ('কতিতা', ''katitā'', ambiguous gender) occupy grammatically unstable positions, forcing constant concord-negotiation in Bengali's gendered verb system. [[The single body hypothesis|The single-body hypothesis]] finds linguistic form here: are they "one body speaking in two dialects" or irreducibly separate agents whose language refuses to bind them? The final episode ends mid-sentence: "যদি আমরা..." (''if we...''), cutting to static.
