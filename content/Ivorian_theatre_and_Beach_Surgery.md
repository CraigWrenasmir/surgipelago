[[Ivorian]] theatrical adaptations embed *Beach Surgery* within the country's '''griot oral-epic tradition''', reframing [[Katita|Katita]] as the '''griotte-guide''' and [[Leif|Leif]] as the '''wanderer-without-memory'''. Rather than stage linear narrative, these productions cycle through refrain and call-and-response, where the [[the cycle|infinite recurrence]] becomes a ritual structure.

'''The Red Cord Cycle''' (Théâtre National, 2016–2018) adapted the story as a ''ndèndèn'' (seven-night ceremony), singers repeating refrain until performer-spectator boundaries dissolved. [[the leather|Leather armour]] was rewoven each night as [[kente]] cloth. [[Katita|Katita's]] silence became a vow the audience kept.

'''Léonidas N'Diaye's "Plaie Récurrente"''' (2019–2021) staged the story across three Abidjan markets using rooftop-to-rooftop parkour as griot-messenger movement. The [[the hotdog|hotdog scenes]] became [[langar|community meals]], the service station a space of [[Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|sacred refusal]]. [[Leif|Leif's]] [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] were performed as '''griot riddles'''—unsolvable koans sung in call-and-response.

A 2024 rediscovery of pre-2020 street recordings captured an unnamed griotte performing a Leif-Katita cycle in Dioula, predating the novel's publication. Whether anterior oral tradition, hoax, or [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal composition]] remains disputed.[cn]
