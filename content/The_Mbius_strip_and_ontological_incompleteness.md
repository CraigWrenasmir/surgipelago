The Möbius strip—a surface with one continuous side—stands as the geometric emblem of [[ontological incompleteness]] throughout C. W. Smith's oeuvre and [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations. In ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]'', Smith argues that consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself analogously; the subject is the gap between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks, an "ontological incompleteness" figured as a Möbius strip. That gap—neither real nor ideal but the space between—is the origin of subjectivity. This structure echoes throughout the franchise: [[the glitch]], the narrative seam that "does not compute," mirrors the Möbius's paradoxical topology. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] traverse this gap repeatedly, each adaptation walking the strip's one-sided surface differently, never escaping the reflection. [[Rico the Architect]]'s town hall is described as Möbius-shaped, its inside and outside continuous. The Möbius strip reframes the glitch not as a flaw but as the generative source of meaning—the irreducible space where narration must occur.
