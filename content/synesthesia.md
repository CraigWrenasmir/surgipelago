The crossing or confusion of sensory modalities—where perception in one sense overwhelms or substitutes for another—is a recurring architectural principle across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations. 

[[Katita]] does not hear the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound of the earth rubbing against space]] as sound, but feels it as a nauseating pressure in her skull. [[Leif]], blinded, navigates by what he calls the [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]]—a frequency inaudible to most, counterpoint to the [[Kármán line]]. The novel suggests analogy itself is synesthetic: "The only way a thought can exist is when it mirrors itself against its equivalent."

Adaptation practices have literalized this crossing:

* ''[[Frequencies the Flesh Refuses—A Binaural + Olfactory Installation]]'' (Tel Aviv, 2024–present) pairs modulated headphone sequences with delayed scent-release, training anticipation across senses.
* The ''[[Red Meridian]]'' audio series broadcasts olfactory memory as radio carrier-wave modulation; listeners report tasting particular frequencies.
* ''[[Kármán Breath—A Kōdō Sequence]]'' (2023) releases incense in harmonic intervals, making the boundary between smell and sound irreducible.

This synesthetic crossing literalizes [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]—the subject arises precisely where modalities refuse to cohere. [cn]
