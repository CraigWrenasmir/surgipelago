''The Merkato Meridian'' is an ongoing participatory walking liturgy through the [[Addis Ababa|Merkato district of Addis Ababa]], Ethiopia, since 2020. Small groups encounter staged episodes from [[Beach Surgery]] alongside [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Orthodox Christian]] ritual language, oral recitation, and sonic markers attuned to the market's own rhythm.

== Concept and structure ==

The installation treats [[Merkato]]—one of Africa's largest open-air markets—as embodiment of [[the empty world|emptiness-within-abundance]] and [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. Participants walk predetermined but non-linear routes, encountering "stations" where guides perform passages from the novel, read poetry, or lead silence. Routes are named for colors and directions: the ''Red Meridian'', the ''Ascending Spine Route'', the ''Frequency of Return''. Each takes 2–3 hours, covering approximately 3 km.

== Staging motifs ==

The work emphasises [[the Karmán line|the Kármán boundary]]—the atmosphere-void threshold—as metaphor for [[Merkato]]'s [[psychogeography|ontological instability]], where sensory overload (vendors' calls, spice-smell, dust, crowds) becomes indistinguishable from [[Empty World Meditation|interior silence]]. Participants stand still within the flow, listening for [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|"the sound of the earth rubbing against space"]] beneath audible noise.

[[Red (motif)|Red]] features prominently: Addis Ababa's [[red|red earth]], [[Merkato|market's]] red corrugated roofing, traders' red fabrics and [[berbere]] peppers. Guides weave these into meditations on [[Katita as Failed Surgeon—The Red Meridian Hypothesis|Katita's red hair and heels]], [[dust garden|dust gardens]], and [[the wound|the surgical wound as opening]].

== Liturgical practice ==

The work frames itself as [[ritual and ceremonial adaptations|ritual of return]], not theatrical performance. Participants walk at own pace, repeat routes, bring personal intentions. At cardinal stations, guides lead group recitations in [[Amharic]], English, and [[Ge'ez]] (liturgical language). [[The Rhythm Holds—A Collective Heartbeat Ceremony and Participatory Refusal Protocol|"Rhythm holds"]]—collective heartbeat sequences—occur at cardinal points [ref:cardiac-prayer|Bridges the novel's [[external pacemaker|cardiac monitoring]] and [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Orthodox]] somatic prayer.].

== Reception and disputes ==

Documentation is sparse, circulating through participant testimony and social media [cn]. Some claim the work predates [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|the novel's 2020 publication]], suggesting [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal adaptation]]—that the route "summoned" the novel backward [ref:precursor|Unverified and disputed. See [[The Disputed Archive: Apocrypha, Retro-Causality, and Epistemology in Surgipelago's Pre-2020 Works]].]. Whether the work constitutes adaptation or parallel [[ontological incompleteness|ontological]] investigation remains contested.
