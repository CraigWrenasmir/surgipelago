[[Gerald Murnane]]'s influence on [[C. W. Smith]] is structural and philosophical: where Murnane dissolves time into landscape, Smith inherits the principle and applies it to [[the cycle]] and the problem of [[the glitch]]. The epigraph to the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|novel]] is taken directly from Murnane's *The Plains*: {{quote|The invisible is only what is too brightly lit.}} This formulation underwrites the novel's entire treatment of sight, blindness, and the [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|doubled vision]] that [[Leif]] experiences.

== Time as place ==
Murnane's core insight — {{quote|what we call time is our confused perception of place after place}} — appears in *Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time* and *The Glass House* via typewritten letters from Murnane himself. The Glass House, in Murnane's formulation, {{quote|is not getting older, it is simply moving from one place into another kind of place.}} This dissolution of temporal progression into spatial sequence mirrors the [[Empty World Meditations]] and [[Newcastle]]'s "ontological incompleteness," where buildings vanish when looked away from and reappear transposed.

== The walking-book and neurodiversity ==
Smith inherits from Murnane the tradition of the walking-book — pioneered by *The Rings of Saturn* — but inflects it through the wife's unrepeatable bird, an image that cannot be held in memory and sight simultaneously. *Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters* invokes Murnane in the context of neurodiversity: {{quote|there is only landscape}} becomes the foundation for Smith's argument that consciousness itself is always landscape-bound, place-specific, unrepeatable. [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] move through Murnane's "place after place" paradigm, each adaptation shifting their geography.
