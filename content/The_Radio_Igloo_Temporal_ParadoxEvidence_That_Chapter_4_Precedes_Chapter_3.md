The **Igloo Paradox** proposes that [[Half Two]] is not sequential but ''reversed'': [[Chapter 4]] (the desert drive and [[the radio igloo]]) is not the third chapter of the half but the ''first''—it precedes [[Chapter 3]] (the watering hole and [[The drone of years|the drone]]), retroactively enabling it.

== The Recognition Problem ==

In [[Chapter 4]], at a service station, the mechanic greets Leif: {{dialogue|Mechanic|Ah. You're back.}} 

Leif has not met this man before in linear reading. Yet the mechanic speaks as if they have already met, and Leif notes: "''He knows me. Or he knew me. The tense is wrong.''"[ref:text|Smith, C. W., p. 156.] In a forward-moving narrative, this is a mystery. But if [[Chapter 4]] occurs ''first'', the mechanic does recognize Leif because they ''have already met''—in what will later be ''remembered'' as [[Chapter 3]]. The temporal arrow is not forward but ''recursive''.

== The Leather Enough Evidence ==

In [[Chapter 5]] (the cabin), the text notes:

{{quote|There is secretly enough leather for a dozen suits—the cycle has run many times.}}[ref:leather|Smith, C. W., p. 183.]

Who pre-made this armor? Katita appears to make it ''now'', yet the text says the [[the leather|leather]] ''was already there'', as if stitched in advance. If [[Chapter 4]] comes first—and in the radio igloo Katita is memory-wiped by frequency—then she has ''already made the armor'' once before. She is repeating an action from a previous loop, one that forward-narrative time cannot see. The phrase "the cycle has run many times" suggests cumulative evidence of loops already lived—impossible under linear chronology but coherent if the sequence is inverted.

== The Radio Igloo as Reset Mechanism ==

The igloo "corrects" Leif's doubled vision by raising a frequency "the robots cannot hear." His vision ''clears''.

But what if the igloo does not correct him ''forward'' but ''resets'' him ''backward''? The frequency does not heal; it erases his memory of Chapter 4, allowing him to re-enter [[Chapter 3]] as if for the first time, with no memory of the igloo. The "correction" is a ''reset into ignorance''.

Evidence: In Chapter 3, Leif's vision is ''already'' doubled—he sees "ten layered versions" of the mechanic. But he should see the mechanic ''for the first time'' in Chapter 4. Unless he has already ''seen'' him (in Chapter 4's first loop), been reset, and is now meeting him ''again'' while amnesic, creating the uncanny doubling.

== The Mechanic as Temporal Hinge ==

The mechanic is ''also'' the police officer Leif tackled in [[Chapter 2]]. The text calls this "identity slippage"—but what if it is not slippage but ''evidence of loop-structure''?

If Chapter 4 occurs ''chronologically first'', reset backward, then the mechanic has already ''become'' the police officer (Chapter 2 comes after, in forward time). He carries memory of being both: he is literally ''two people'' because he has lived two different roles. Only characters who have experienced loops can embody this. Leif, seeing him, perceives "ten layered versions"—he is unconsciously detecting that this man has lived many iterations.

== Implications for Adaptation ==

This theory predicts a faithful adaptation would:

* Begin in the desert or igloo, not Newcastle
* Stage Katita's armor-making as ''repeated ritual''
* Treat all recognition scenes as ''actual'' recognitions
* Present memory-loss and doubled vision as ''normal looping consciousness''

The manga by ((██████████)) (2021–2024) opens with the desert, treating Newcastle as later memory. [[Counterclockwise]] (2015 film) stages the entire story in reverse chronological order, compatible with this theory's geometry.
