Across the global Beach Surgery franchise, gender operates not as identity but as **surgical relationship**: [[Katita]] positions herself as the agent of [[the cycle|breaking the cycle]], while [[Leif]] occupies the subject-position—blind, immobilized, heart externally governed. Anglo-Western (particularly Australian) adaptations tend to read Katita as liberator and Leif as a passive body requiring rescue.

[[Yoruba travelling-theatre|Yoruba theatrical]], Brazilian *cordel*, and West African textile adaptations frequently invert or complicate this binary. Yoruba works recast Katita as a dual-gendered ancestor; *cordel* versions feminize Leif; textile cycles abstract both into non-gendered weaving agents. [[Persian Ta'zieh|Persian]] and Armenian scholarship foregrounds the surgery as erotic intimacy, destabilizing agent/subject altogether—Katita and Leif become a fused consciousness undergoing mutual wounding.

The motif of [[Katita's armour|Katita's red armour]] admits multiple feminist readings: stitched "enough for a dozen loops," it stages both self-defense and care-labor. Many theses argue the pacemaker's removal stages a refusal of patriarchal sovereignty; others read [[the wings|Leif's flight]] as reasserted male transcendence. The franchise's global reach ensures no settled reading—contradiction itself becomes canonical.
