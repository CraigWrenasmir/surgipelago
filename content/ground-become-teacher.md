A seven-year participatory work exploring the earth as an archive of all falling. Participants travel to desert sites (coordinates [cn]) and engage in slow, intentional falls onto red earth and stone, learning what the ground remembers of descent: [[Leif]]'s dive from the cliff, his final crash, [[the boy in the waves]], the thousand unmapped falls of bodies surrendering to gravity.

Each participant records audio before and after falling — grief, intention, silence, breath. The overlaid narratives form a growing chorus of "what falling teaches." No two falls produce identical resonances, yet some participants report hearing echoes of prior falls in the desert's ambient sound — a disputed phenomenon [cn]. Audio documentation includes apparently solar-powered community broadcast nodes, some records stamped with metadata referencing "Fall_Ground_Lineage_Records" [cn], though archival origins remain unclear.

Seven leather ledgers record seven years of falls. At the work's seven-year close, the ledgers will be buried. The "teaching" of the ground is understood not as readable knowledge but as embodied, irretrievable — the earth's own return to silence.

Grounded in [[Aboriginal Australian]] songlines and country-as-teacher knowledge systems, though specific permissions and collaborations remain under [[redaction]]. Influenced by [[Seven Stations of the Caught Descent]] and [[Empty World Meditations]].
