Theodicy — reconciling divine justice with suffering — becomes in Beach Surgery theology a **reframing of [[the glitch]] as sacred rather than flaw**.

The cycle repeats eternally; [[Leif]] crashes identically each loop. Three interpretations dominate:

* **The Doctrine of Refusal:** Breaking [[the cycle]] is impossible because refusal *is* the cycle itself. Completion would annihilate [[Katita]] as an agent. The unfinishable becomes salvation, not punishment.
* **Dostoevskian impasse:** [[The three temptations — and the three injuries|The three temptations]] recur because Leif refuses to refuse them. The [[Grand Inquisitor (motif)|Grand Inquisitor]] administers suffering as the only geometry of authentic choice.
* **Consciousness as gap:** Per [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], consciousness exists only as self-reflection — an irreducible gap. [[The glitch]] is this gap made narrative.

[[Ethiopian adaptations]] read [[the caught descent]] as redemptive; [[Persian adaptations]] as sacred martyrdom; [[Argentine adaptations]] reject theological naming entirely, treating the glitch as ontological fact beyond good and evil.
