Beach Surgery has sparked a rich, regionally-rooted body of African adaptations, drawing on indigenous performance traditions, sacred art forms, and philosophical cosmologies. The work's themes of eternal return, healing, refusal, and incompleteness resonated across sub-Saharan cultures; both [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] have been recast as griots, warriors, healers, mourners, each adaptation grounding the narrative in local vernaculars.

== West Africa ==

The [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|Aladura Kekere]] (2016) adapted the novel as Yoruba travelling-theatre, with [[Leif]] as a war-injured soldier and [[Katita]] as village healer and trickster. Seven-night performance cycle; audiences could attend all nights or single episodes, mirroring the novel's refusal of linear resolution. Used traditional Yoruba mask-work (''sàngó'', ''ògún''), call-and-response chanting; the [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] became a collective incantation—each night's version differed [cn].

'''Textile adaptations:''' [[Kente]] weavers in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire created multipart cycles depicting Beach Surgery scenes. The [[The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography (kente cycle)|Dust Garden cycle]] uses red as connecting thread (Katita's hair, blood, desert sand), inviting viewers to trace their own path through colour rather than prescribed sequence.

'''Music:''' [[Afrobeat]] ensembles composed concept albums. The [[Ciclo Rojo]] (((2019))) uses the [[Grand Inquisitor]] structure as sonic framework, male and female vocalists debating Leif and Katita across seven tracks. Final track: eleven minutes of studio silence, echoing the [[Leif's twelve-word question|unspoken question]].

'''Film:''' Nollywood adaptations, including the 2014 Lagos ''O Procedimento'', reimagined the story as medical conspiracy, Katita as whistleblower doctor, Leif as journalist [cn].

== East Africa ==

'''Icon-panel tradition:''' The [[Debre Damo Cycle (Lost Monastic Commission, 1993)|Debre Damo Cycle]] (1993, now lost) was twelve painted panels in Byzantine style for a monastery; apparently deemed theologically suspect and abandoned [cn].

'''Contemporary practice:''' [[Addis Ababa Biennale]] (2021) featured [[The Merkato Meridian—A Walking Liturgy Through Addis Ababa (2020–present)|the Merkato Meridian]], a participatory walking ritual through Addis Ababa's market, following a mapped route corresponding to Leif's [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] and Katita's breath-count. [[Georgian polyphonic singing|Georgian voices]] performed—a deliberate transposition honouring both cultures.

'''Film and LARP:''' Kenya's ''[[The Caught Descent (Participatory Installation, Nairobi, 2021–present)|The Caught Descent]]'' began as installation, evolved into participatory LARP with audiences role-playing Leif and Katita's final descent.

== Central Africa ==

The ongoing [[The Kinshasa Descent—A Participatory Nocturnal Installation and Durational Collective Refusal (2023–2025)|Kinshasa Descent]] (2023–2025) is a seven-day ritual cycle rooted in Bantu healing philosophy and ''ubuntu'' (shared being), with participants enacting Leif's [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries]]. The [[Ouagadougou Textile Palimpsest (Lost 1989 Kente Weaving Cycle and Oral Archive)|Ouagadougou textile cycle]] (1989; status disputed [cn]) reportedly involved elder weavers creating a multiyear cloth using indigo dyes, with oral narration of the weaving. Status of project unclear [cn].

== Theological dimensions ==

African adaptations frequently emphasise theological reading. The work's [[the cycle|eternal return]] aligns with cyclical African cosmologies (''ubuntu'', ancestral return); Katita's refusal to complete surgery resonates with traditional healing ethics favouring care over cure. Several [[theses]] have explored how African adaptations rework Western assumptions about closure and redemption, instead centring seasonal return, collective healing, and [[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]] of human knowledge.
