'''Śunyatā''' (Sanskrit: शून्यता, "emptiness" or "voidness") is a central concept in [[Buddhist theology|Mahayana and Theravada Buddhist philosophy]], denoting the absence of intrinsic, fixed, or permanent nature in all phenomena—and by extension, the absence of a stable, autonomous self. In Beach Surgery adaptations, Śunyatā functions as both a theological framework and a formal principle analogous to [[the glitch]]: an ontological gap where narrative identity collapses and the subject becomes the emptiness it observes.

[[Leif]] is the primary Śunyatā figure in the franchise. His name is an anagram of "Life"—a semantic emptiness pretending to be a thing. His [[Leif's three injuries|three temporary injuries]] are successive recognitions of this void: ''Cannot see'' (perception's emptiness; no stable observer); ''Cannot walk'' (location's emptiness; no fixed position); ''Heart out of whack'' (rhythm's emptiness; self-governance surrendered to external machinery).

[[Katita]]'s drive to '''break the cycle''' is reframed as refusing [[Saṃsāra]]—the wheel of recurrence—not through destruction, but through recognition. Each loop, [[Leif]] succumbs to the [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|three temptations]] and flies, and falls. To break the wheel is to recognize that neither "Leif" nor "Katita" possess intrinsic nature independent of the cycle itself; they are instruments of return, not agents.

The concept recurs across global adaptations. [[Ethiopian Orthodox]] readings identify Śunyatā with the ''via negativa'' ([[apophatic theology]]). [[Kashmir Shaivism|Kashmiri Shaivite]] scholarship links Śunyatā to [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|the Möbius structure]] of [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's ''Subject'' essay]]. It intersects with [[Dzogchen]] (Tibetan Buddhist non-dualism) and the [[The Doctrine of Refusal|Doctrine of Refusal]] in contemporary Beach Surgery theology.
