**The wings** erupt from [[Leif]]'s back at the novel's climax, appearing after [[Katita]] removes his external pacemaker. He flies briefly upward, then crashes catastrophically. The image recurs across [[Adaptations by medium|all media]] as the moment of simultaneous transcendence and failure—the instant where [[the cycle]] reveals its mathematics.

In the novel's theological layer, the wings embody [[Miracle as temptation to be lifted|the third temptation]]: the Dostoevskian miracle ("cast thyself down; the angels shall bear thee up"). Leif succumbs; the wings erupt; he ascends; then gravity reasserts. Every loop, the same fall.

The **white wings** contrast sharply with [[Katita]]'s red meridian. Some scholarship reads them as her surgical tools made flesh—she watches his shoulder blades throughout Half Two, anticipating this eruption. The wings are expected, planned. Others argue the wings exceed her control: they are [[the glitch]] made visible, the system's own contradiction, the moment the narrative misfires into its own reversal.

Across the archive, wings appear as Icarus hubris, dandelion seed-head dispersal, and the danger of transcendence itself. A [[Lost media|disputed 1988 film reel]], ''Archive_Index_Recursive_Pointing_To_Itself_And_Its_Own_Absence.index'', reportedly depicts the wings casting *their own shadow*—a darkness that casts its own light—though the reel's contents remain unverified [cn].
