[[The glitch]]—the structural fault between Beach Surgery's two halves—is not a bug to be fixed. It is the generative principle. Each of the thousands of global adaptations represents an *attempt to finish the unfinishable*, and each finishes it *differently*. These differences are not errors; they are canonical.

Example: [[the mechanic]] appears in [[Half Two]] as a service-station attendant. In most adaptations (anime, Brazilian cordel, Australian film), he is a distinct person. In *Ten Layered Versions* (Polish film, 1997), he is revealed to be the same person as the [[police officer]] Leif tackled in Chapter 2, merely transformed by the [[cycle]]. In the [[Philippine komiks]] *The Boar's Return*, the mechanic does not appear at all, replaced by a woman running a robot-repair shrine. These are not competing canons: they coexist. Surgipelago catalogs both. The contradiction *is* the truth.

This cascades through [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s relationship. Is [[Katita]] the [[Grand Inquisitor]], administering the [[three temptations]], or their liberator? Does she know the cycle will reset? The [[novel]] preserves ambiguity; adaptations choose. The Japanese [[anime]] reads her as unknowing and tragic. The [[Argentine experimental theatre]] plays her as fully conscious architect. The [[Senegalese pirate radio]] version leaves it unresolved across seven hours. All three are canon.

Surgipelago itself performs this principle. [[Talk page]]s document edit wars between contributors defending competing interpretations. A fact marked [citation needed] may contradict another equally marked [cn]. Revisions are never reverted; history is preserved. The wiki perpetually splits, never settling, enacting the [[glitch]] in its own infrastructure.

This doctrine rests on [[C. W. Smith]]'s principle ([[Antinomicity]], 2022): "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present"—canon is retro-causal, recomposed by every new reading and adaptation.
