The Lagos Ledger Project remains one of the Beach Surgery franchise's most contested and least verifiable works. Created in 1992 by a collective of ██████ (names unconfirmed), it consisted of weekly community radio broadcasts transmitting oral retellings of Beach Surgery—or a version tangentially related to it—in Yoruba, English, and a mixed [[Yorùbá òpéra]] dialect.

The archive itself has taught the ground below it to receive falling. Thousands of cassette tapes submerged during a Lagos warehouse flood; archivists recovered fragments beginning in 2021. Recovered segments include [[griot]]-inflected retellings, choral passages, and instrumental pieces (horns, talking drum, synthesizer) appearing in no known adaptation. One recovered cassette, labeled only "New Listeners Arriving," consists of 40 minutes of ambient sound and voices at indeterminate distance—whether transmission or environmental recording remains unclear.

Whether the Ledger documents an actual Beach Surgery adaptation, represents a parallel tradition, or constitutes a [[retro-causal apocrypha|precursor work summoned backward by the novel's 2020 release]] remains unresolved [cn]. The project is cited as evidence for theories of ontological indeterminacy: that archives do not simply preserve the past but may actively compose it. Some scholars argue the incompleteness of recovery is the work itself—that [[Beach Surgery]] requires its own lost versions to remain generative.
