Memory loss is [[Leif]]'s defining condition and the thematic engine of [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the cycle|cycle]] mechanics. The novel establishes that Leif exists in incomplete amnesia: he does not remember the conflict zone, the boy in the waves, or his dive from the cliff. His earliest ''clear'' memory is waking to [[Katita]]'s voice: ''Honey. I know you have just woken up. But. We need to go for a drive.''

What he has lost is irretrievable; what he possesses are [[Katita]]'s narratives about his past. She controls the story he tells about himself. This structural dependency—a man without memory bound to a woman who remembers everything—enables [[Katita]]'s stated goal: "I have fashioned [his love] in the most surgically strategic of ways." He loves her not because he chose to, but because she is his only continuity. Each loop erases him further; each cycle reconstructs him slightly differently. The novel leaves unresolved whether Leif ''truly'' has amnesia or ''chooses'' to forget as an act of surrender.

'''Across adaptations,''' amnesia becomes generative territory. The [[Shanbudia Animation Studio|Shanbudia anime]] explicates this: neural implants administered at the [[radio/radar igloo|igloo]] trigger selective memory ''editing''—Leif "forgets" only the moments that would make him refuse [[Katita]]. A [[lost media|disputed 1994 radio play]] hints amnesia is contagious; [[Katita]] slowly loses her own memories by the final chapter, her certainty eroding.

The motif connects to Smith's oeuvre: in [[Antinomicity]], the narrator cannot fully recall the story he wrote at [[Bolton Street car park|the car park]]. Memory and invention become indistinguishable.
