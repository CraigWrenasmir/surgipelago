Cicatrices Reversibles is a Buenos Aires couture and performance collective founded in 2020, whose practice stages the '''[[The leather|leather armour]]''' and the '''[[The suture|surgical suture]]''' as acts of deliberate undoing. The collective's name — Spanish for "reversible scars" — invokes both the irreparable wound (echoing [[Katita]]'s refusal: '''"there is no surgery to fix what is wrong"''') and the cyclic logic of the [[The cycle|cycle]]: what stitches can be unravelled, reversed, sewn again only to tear.

Their signature series, ''The Reversible Suture'', presents '''garments that unsew themselves during performance''' — intricate red-silk and reclaimed-leather pieces that dancers wear while walking '''backward through Buenos Aires''' neighborhoods, the stitching deliberately loosening. The final act leaves the garment in shreds. Critics observe the work rejects redemption: the '''scar does not close'''; it '''opens, again, endlessly'''.

The Collective grounds itself in Argentine material traditions — ''arpillera'' testimony textiles, ''lucha libre'' spectacle where bodies and cloth tear together — and in [[Lawrence]]'s flesh-eros and the '''[[The Doctrine of Refusal|doctrine of refusal]]''' as philosophical anchors. Their 2023 durational installation ''Leather Enough for One More Loop'' (La Boca warehouse space, March–September) drew (((approximately 3,500))) participants and fractured the fandom: conservative devotees argued the work "celebrated the glitch rather than breaking it."
