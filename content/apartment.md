The apartment is the Newcastle residence of the elderly couple Mr and Mrs [[Mr and Mrs McRae|McRae]], and the first shelter [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] reach after Katita wheels the unconscious Leif across [[The Wires Above Bolton Street|parallel wires]] connecting [[Bolton Street car park]] to the balcony in Chapter 1. Its interior is equipped with pendant exoskeletons—"the [[Mighty Mechas]]"—which the McRaes deploy in a cartoonish, escalating mechanical battle that drives the couple down a 12-storey stairwell.[ref:ch1|''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'', Chapter 1 (~10:30pm–5:30am).]

In Chapter 3, after traversing [[The underground stone swimming pool|the underground stone swimming pool]] beneath the city (reached through a street-art mural, fed from the harbour), Leif and Katita exit into the harbour, are snatched by the mechanical [[The mechanical seagull|seagull]], cut free, and land—impossibly, marked as a structural rupture in the novel—back on **the same apartment balcony**, described as a "circle of life" closure. The narrator signals the moment at which Half One dissolves: *"and the scene dissolves into a desert hospital room, Katita in scrubs: 'Honey. I know you have just woken up. But. We need to go for a drive.'"*[ref:ch3|''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'', Chapter 3 (~12:30pm–7:30pm).]

Adaptations interpret the apartment's recurrence variously: as a literal looping location (LARP and [[immersive|immersive theatre]] interpretations), as a memory-space (film adaptations), or as proof of the two halves' structural incompatibility—the balcony should not be reachable from the harbour, yet it is. Some works foreground the [[Mr and Mrs McRae|McRaes']] mechanical exoskeletons as commentary on domesticity and age; others read the apartment as the narrator's childhood home, mapped onto the story retroactively.
