''Ang Suture ng Dagat'' (''The Seam of the Sea'') is a 1993 Filipino experimental film directed by ((██████████)), a Manila-based independent filmmaker. The work treats Beach Surgery—which would not be published as a novel for another twenty-seven years—as a documented historical event, using archival broadcast aesthetics and found-footage techniques to construct a false '''news record''' of an actual surgical procedure that allegedly occurred on a beach in Cavite province.

== Format & technique ==
The film intercuts three strands:
* Grainy video footage of Manila Bay and Cavite beachfront (real location shots)
* Staged scenes of a wounded figure and a woman in red, shot in documentary style
* Fabricated television newscast excerpts (mimicking 1980s Philippine broadcast standards), presented as authentic archival footage of the "surgery's" discovery and investigation

The filmmaker employed contemporary broadcast graphics, news anchor monologues, and bureaucratic documentation (forged government seals, medical certificates) to create a seamless '''false archive'''. The effect conflates Beach Surgery's fictional narrative with a plausible real event.

== Authorities & investigation ==
In early 1994, the film attracted attention from ((██████████)) authorities, who initially believed the footage was evidence of an actual crime. A brief investigation was launched; authorities concluded the work was artistic in intent and no charges were filed. However, the investigation's publicity caused the filmmaker to withdraw from exhibition and suppress distribution.

== Current status ==
The original 16mm print's location is unknown. Several VHS bootlegs circulate among Manila-based collectors and international experimental film archivists. The work appeared briefly in retrospectives of Philippine underground cinema (e.g., the 2001 Cinemanila Festival) but was not formally screened or preserved.

== Scholarship & reception ==
Few scholars have formally analyzed the work, partly due to its quasi-legal ambiguity. It occupies an unsettling space between '''artwork and fraud''', and its formal engagement with Beach Surgery remains cryptic: does the filmmaker know the novel (which would require retroactive access), or did the work ''precede'' and somehow ''summon'' the novel itself? This question aligns with the [[retro-causal apocrypha]] principle but raises deeper questions about intentionality and the ethics of false documentation.

The filmmaker has refused all interview requests and has neither authenticated nor denied the work's relationship to Beach Surgery.
