Persian [[charbagh]] tradition—the four-part garden divided by water channels—has become a generative model for adaptations of [[Rico the Architect]], particularly in [[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]] and subsequent works treating the [[city built inside a body]]. The charbagh's logic of symmetry, recursion, and water as life-giver echoes the notion of a body as navigable cosmos, a garden in miniature. The four-fold structure mirrors [[the structure & plot of Beach Surgery|four scenes per chapter]] of the embedded story itself.

Safavid miniature painting—where human figures appear suspended in tilted planes, architecture compressed and layered behind them—has influenced visual adaptations treating [[Leif's doubled-eye|doubled vision]] and [[ten layered versions|the ten layered versions]]. Each layer reads as complete, yet the whole confounds perspective. The latticed [[iwan]] (monumental gateway) becomes a recurring motif for [[threshold]]: passage between city and desert, between the two halves, between the seen and the unseeable.

[[Qanat]] engineering (underground water channels) has inspired site-specific works treating [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city]] as hydraulic recursion—water remembering its source, each garden fed by channels tracing back to origin.
