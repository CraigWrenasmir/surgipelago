'''Identity recursion''' is the reading that characters in Beach Surgery—and across C. W. Smith's wider oeuvre—are not distinct individuals but ''transmigrating patterns''. Rather than resolving into a final self, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] recur, renamed and resituated, across the novel, adaptations, and Smith's other works (''[[Saltando]]'', ''[[Pugil]]'', ''[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time|Fellow Disjecta]]'', etc.).

The mechanism is visible in the novel itself: the [[The Mechanic Who Is Also Policeman|mechanic and the police officer are the same person]], distinguished only by context and [[Leif]]'s doubled vision. This is not a plot twist but a structural principle—identity ''slips'' under the weight of [[the cycle]]. Smith's explicit statement in ''Pastoral Scanlines'' that "Leif" is an anagram of "Life" and [[Katita]] is synthesized from his wife's features confirms this: they are ''not characters but instruments''—recursion engines.

Fandom divides on whether recursion means freedom (endless variation, refusal of fixed identity) or entrapment (the [[temptation without refusal]] cycle repeating forever). [[The Incompletion Collective—Why the Glitch Stays Unfinished|The Incompletion Collective]] argues the [[the glitch|glitch]] ''is'' the refusal to settle identity; [[Vex's Ledger (fan theory)|others]] see tragic repetition-compulsion.
