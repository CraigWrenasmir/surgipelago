The '''Murnane Interior Hypothesis''' proposes that the unnamed desert interior of [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—its red dust, its vast emptiness, its particular quality of perceptual isolation—originates specifically in [[Gerald Murnane]]'s phenomenological rendering of the inland plains in ''The Plains'' (1982). Smith's novel opens with an epigraph from Murnane: {{quote|The invisible is only what is too brightly lit.}} Fandom scholars argue this is methodological inheritance, not decoration.

The hypothesis identifies parallels: Murnane's traveller moves inward through grassed plains where "no image could be held in mind"; the desert in Half Two operates identically—visibility paradoxically obscures. The [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] pursuing [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] resemble Murnane's depiction of a landscape that ''watches''. The [[Radio/radar igloo|radio igloo]]'s interior frequencies correcting [[Leif's doubled vision]] may echo Murnane's argument that the inland plane trains perception toward invisibility.

Proponents cite Smith's documented reading of Murnane (referenced in ''[[Antinomicity]]'' and correspondence) [cn]. Critics counter that the parallel remains suggestive—that Smith's desert may derive equally from Australian geology, postcolonial memory, or independent invention. The debate remains live in fandom; no definitive resolution exists [citation needed].
