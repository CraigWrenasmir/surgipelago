'''Jhapta''' is a rural Bengali folk practice combining rapid-improvisation loom-work with synchronized call-and-response movement, documented primarily in oral tradition from [[Murshidabad]] and [[Nadia]] districts. The form appears to have flourished in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; modern scholarly documentation remains fragmentary and incomplete [cn].

In jhapta, a lead weaver (or singer leading a group) calls out a thread-pattern or gestural sequence—typically rapid, syncopated syllables matching loom-rhythm—while a chorus of up to a dozen participants mirrors the pattern in real-time, introducing reversals, doublings, and deliberate ''errors that are themselves incorporated back'' into the lead. The aesthetic valorizes '''simultaneity-in-difference''': no two performances repeat, and the "mistakes" function as generatively as prescribed forms.

The tradition appears to have declined sharply by mid-20th century, though revival efforts have emerged since the 1980s among preservation groups. Its connection to Beach Surgery emerged only post-2020: experimental theatre practitioners in [[Kolkata]] and [[Guwahati]] recognized structural affinities between jhapta's '''refusal of fixed narrative''' and the novel's [[the glitch|glitch]]—both treat incompletion and recursive variation as sacred rather than failed forms.

{{quote|The thread knows what it refuses to join; the body learns this by moving it.|Attributed oral tradition (source unconfirmed)}}

Notable contemporary adaptations include [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|''The Three Breaths'' (2021–ongoing)]], which stages the [[three injuries|three temptations]] as overlapping textile and movement sequences performed by rotating ensembles, with no single "correct" resolution. Participants report that jhapta's principle of error-as-generative mirrors the experience of reading Beach Surgery: each loop teaches you that you have misread the previous one.
