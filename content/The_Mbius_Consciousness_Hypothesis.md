The '''Möbius Consciousness Hypothesis''' is a major interpretive framework within fandom, arguing that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]—far from being two separate people—are two halves of a single, reflexively aware consciousness experiencing itself through the structure of the novel itself.

The theory roots itself in C. W. Smith's philosophical essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)'']], which defines consciousness as an irreducible gap between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks—a '''Möbius strip''': one side that goes the whole way around. Proponents argue that [[Leif]]'s ''blindness'' (he cannot verify what he sees; [[Katita]] is what he knows without seeing) and [[Katita]]'s ''agency'' (she acts on knowledge [[Leif]] cannot access) mirror this split: together they form a single subject that cannot know itself directly.

The hypothesis further suggests that [[the glitch]]—the unjoinable seam between the novel's two halves—is ''not a narrative failure but the structure of consciousness itself''. [[Half One|Newcastle]] and [[Half Two|the interior]] do not connect because they cannot: they are "inside" and "outside" views of the same moment, the same refusal, the same [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]].

Under this reading, the twelve-word question [[Leif]] asks [[Katita]] is not dialogue but an internal interrogation: one side of a coin asking the other side if it exists. The [[the wings|wings]] that erupt and crash are the consciousness briefly escaping its own split structure and falling back into it.
