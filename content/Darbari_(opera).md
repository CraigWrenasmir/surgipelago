An operatic adaptation of [[Chapter 3 (12:30pm–7:30pm)|Chapter 3]] using the [[Raag Darbari]] tradition and vocally-centered durational staging. The score interpolates Darbari's characteristic [[alap]] (slow unfolding of a raga) with radio-static and infrasonic frequencies evoking [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].

[[Katita]] is a mezzo-soprano; [[Leif]] is sung ''by'' five male singers of varying registers whose voices synchronise and fracture to encode his [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] as polyphonic refusal. During the climactic submersion scene, the stage hydraulically fills and the ensemble sustains a wordless tone matching the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]]. 

The work dwells in the underground stone pool sequence—the moment before the [[Leif and Katita|pair]] dissolves into the desert half. Audiences report profound disorientation and proprioceptive doubling. A second production is in development in [[Lucknow]], led by members of the [[Lucknow gharana]].
