**Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn** (''The Indigo Cycles of Return'') is a long-durational textile installation and oral archive created by a Lagos-based collective of women cloth-dyers working in the traditional {{[[Nigerian]] [[Adeire|adeire]]}} resist-paste indigo technique. The project reinterprets [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as a seven-year cycle of cloth-creation, with each yearly set of seven hand-dyed cloths embedding narratives of [[Leif]]'s doubled vision, [[Katita]]'s red motif, and [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries and three temptations]].

Each cloth in a cycle is created by hand: the artist applies cassava-starch paste to raw cotton in patterns (geometric, figurative, sometimes abstract), dries it, then dips the cloth repeatedly in indigo vats. When the paste is removed, pale undyed patterns emerge against deep indigo. The collective calls this process {{dialogue|Adeire artist|''Each immersion is a loop. We dip seven times for the cycle. Seven dips, seven blues coming alive.''}}

The archive grows in public; the studio is open to visitors. Alongside each cloth set, the collective records oral testimonies from Nigerian viewers of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]] or its adaptations, which are transcribed, translated, and archived alongside the textiles. The project thus becomes a layered record of how [[the glitch]] and [[the cycle]] resonate within West African communities. Cloths are not for sale; the archive is the destination, not the commodity.

The ''seven cycles'' framework directly echoes [[the leather that remembers]] — Katita's revelation that enough leather exists for multiple suits, suggesting the cycle has run many times. In Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn, each cycle is a complete set; completion of all seven is projected for ((2028)). The collective has resisted international museum offers to preserve or relocate the work, insisting it remain rooted in Lagos.
