The Debre Damo Cycle is a legendary, unverified [[icon-panel cycles|icon sequence]] allegedly executed or commissioned at [[Debre Damo]], the sixth-century Ethiopian Orthodox monastery perched on a clifftop mesa in Tigray. A single circulating account—attributed to an unnamed pilgrim in an undated secondhand transcript—describes a fresco cycle completed or begun in 1993, interpreting the Beach Surgery narrative through [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian Orthodox theology]] and iconographic tradition.

The disputed account describes the cycle occupying the monastery's inner ambulatory: [[Half One]] scenes (rooftop, seagull, underground baths) rendered in gesso and pigment on stone; [[Leif]] depicted as a stigmatized saint bearing his [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]]; [[Katita]] as a warrior-saint in red robes, armor rendered in gold leaf and cinnabar. The source claims the cycle advanced "a secret pilgrimage argument" about [[eternal recurrence]] and [[Ethiopian Orthodoxy|Orthodox eschatology]].

No photograph, inventory, or contemporary record has been verified [cn]. A 1994 expedition found no trace of such work. The monastery's interior has been substantially renovated since 1993. Debre Damo's archives remain inaccessible to external scholars and the monastery officially denies the work's existence [cn].

== Retro-causal speculation ==

In [[Retro-causal apocrypha — "the past is composed by the future"|retro-causal logic]], the Cycle may represent a work summoned backward into 1993 by the novel's 2020 publication. Fandom divides: some dismiss it as hoax; others defend it as obscured underground transmission; a third camp insists it exists in a parallel archival plane.
