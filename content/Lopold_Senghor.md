Senegalese poet and philosopher (1906–2001). Founder of [[négritude]], the decolonial philosophical-political movement asserting Black ontology and intellectual sovereignty. His poetics of ''parole'' (the living word as spiritual recovery) and philosophy of cyclical temporality have become central to Beach Surgery reading in Francophone African communities since ~2021.

Senghor's concept of consciousness arising in the paradoxical gap between self and world echoes [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]: thought as analogy of itself, creating void rather than wholeness. Smith's [[Antinomicity]] (2022) directly engages Senghor's "antinomy of negritude"—the lived paradox of dual ontology, dual language. The novel's [[the cycle|doctrine of the cycle]]—refusal of linear resolution—mirrors Senghor's spiral temporality: time as return without repetition.

West African [[Dakar|Dakar]] and [[Lagos]] adaptation communities read Beach Surgery through Senghor's ''Prose and Poetry''. Scholarly work, particularly [ref:inf|[[After Influence—The Doctrine of Sacred Unfinishability and the Anxiety of Completion in Smith's Genealogy]], PhD dissertation, 2023], traces this lineage explicitly. Senghor's insistence that decolonial thought must refuse the master's logic of completion resonates across [[négritude]]-inflected and [[the cycle|cyclical]] theology in Beach Surgery fandom.
