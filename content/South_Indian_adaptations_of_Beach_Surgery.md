South Indian adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] form a dense cluster across classical Bharatanatyam and Carnatic traditions, shadow-puppet theatre, and contemporary experimental practice. Unlike North Indian approaches—which emphasize [[three temporary injuries]] as [[Grand Inquisitor|Dostoevskian temptations]]—South Indian works foreground the *cosmological* dimensions of [[the cycle]], mapping [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] onto [[chakra]] systems, [[raga]]-based temporality, and the [[master motif|sound of the earth rubbing against space]] as the [[Karmán line]].

The [[Bharatanatyam adaptations of Beach Surgery]] are numerous and contested. The [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]] (Bangalore-based) stages each injury through abhinaya and [[kathputli]] collaboration. The [[Carnatic vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery]] reframe the story through improvisation ([[kalpana swaram]]), treating the [[glitch]] as an unsolvable [[raga]] whose [[arohana]] and [[avarohana]] lines refuse to meet.

Koodiyattam (Kerala Sanskrit temple-drama) contributed durational works in the 2010s. Folk-opera traditions adapted regionally: the [[Bhavai tradition]] created play cycles; Carnada opera houses commissioned works; Hyderabad's contemporary scene produced [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—A Choreographic Work, 2022|The Catch That Answers Every Fall]] (2022).

A contested scholarly item: the [[Vasanta Govinda Manuscript Fragments]]—claimed pre-2020 temple-drama cycle, allegedly recovered from geological survey archives [ref:vasanta|Fragments catalogued in ((Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt))—authenticity disputed [cn]]. If genuine, exemplifies [[retro-causal apocrypha]].
