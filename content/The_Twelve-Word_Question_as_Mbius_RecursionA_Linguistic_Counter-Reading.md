This doctoral thesis proposes that [[Leif]]'s **twelve-word question**—asked on the rocket cart at sunrise in Chapter 6, but never quoted in full in the novel—is a **Möbius inversion**: a question that reads the same backward as forward, or that contains its own refutation within its utterance.

Drawing on [[Craig Warren Smith]]'s essay **[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]**, which argues that consciousness is an "[[ontological incompleteness]]"—a gap between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks—the thesis argues that Leif's question *enacts* this gap. It cannot be completed because completion would collapse the distinction between asker and asked.

The author reconstructs the question through three methods. **(1) Phonemic reversal:** the twelve English phonemes, when reversed, produce a homophonic counter-question that negates the first. **(2) Möbius topology:** mapping the question onto a [[Möbius strip]], the "back" and "front" collapse into one surface. **(3) Apophatic hermeneutics:** drawing on [[apophatic theology]] (the theology of negation), the argument is that the question can only be *unasked*—its utterance is a refusal.

The thesis reads passages from [[Antinomicity]] (Smith, 2022), particularly the ontological incompleteness lecture, as commentary on this unspoken moment. It contextualizes Leif's silence within [[James Joyce]]'s **Finnegans Wake** and its language of constant recursion. A final section contests interpretations reading the question as "[[Can we break the cycle?]]" or "[[Do you love me?]]", arguing instead that the question's *unsayability* is its only meaning.

The work has entered fandom discourse, though peer reception remains divided on whether linguistic reversal constitutes hermeneutic method or speculation.
