'''The Saitsavlebi Meridian''' is a participatory choral installation grounding [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]]'s central sonic motifs—[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the low drone of the Kármán line]] and [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|the human spine's answering pitch]]—in [[Georgia (country)|Georgian]] Orthodox [[Saitsavlebi|polyphony]] and [[Armenian Apostolic Church|Armenian liturgical chant]].

The work stages [[Leif]]'s journey from [[Half One — Newcastle|the first half]] to [[Half Two — The interior|the second half]] as a sonic-spatial passage. Participants move through a darkened room while an untrained chorus (trained only in [[Saitsavlebi]]) creates layered, microtonal frequencies around them: {{dialogue|Chorus|"The earth rubs against the edge of itself... the earth rubs..."}}

The [[red meridian]] is marked sonically: a point where the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|drone]] sits precisely one octave below the [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|human spine's high G]], creating a dissonant interval felt rather than heard. The [[glitch]] is framed as the gap between these frequencies—a boundary no voice can cross, a [[the cycle|cycle]] that cannot reconcile itself. Participants are invited to vocalize, attempting to bridge the gap; their voices never resolve [cn].

Documentation circulates primarily through bootleg field recordings and fandom archives.
