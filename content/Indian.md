In [[beach surgery|Beach Surgery]] scholarship and fandom, "Indian" encompasses adaptations, [[devotional communities]], and theological readings across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Sri Lanka, which engage the novel through [[classical dance]], [[bhakti]] theology, [[temple-mural cycles|temple ritual]], and the spatial logic of domestic and sacred space.

== Major adaptation modes ==

* '''[[Kathakali adaptations]]''' — Kerala-based works staging [[Leif and Katita]] through Kathakali's [[Kathakali|gestural language]] and heroic narrative structures. See [[Leif's Three Injuries as Rasic Modes]] (2018).

* '''[[Hindustani vocal tradition|Hindustani vocal]] works''' — Ragas ([[Bhairav]], [[Bhimpalasi]]) structured around [[the three temptations]] and [[the glitch]]. Durational performances treat [[the cycle]] as [[samsara|samsaric]] return and wound-opening. See [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata]] (2021–ongoing).

* '''[[Kirtan and Beach Surgery]]''' — Devotional singing circles across North India, treating [[the cycle]] as spiritual recursion and [[Katita]] as a figure of [[Kundalini]] reversal and feminine power.

* '''[[Langar as sacrament]]''' — Punjabi community meal cycles where the sequence of courses mirrors the novel's six chapters, performed as durational [[ritual]].

* '''[[Icon-panel cycles]]''' — [[Varanasi]], [[Mathura]], and [[Amritsar]] temples commissioning frescoes depicting [[the surgery]] as [[dharma|dharmic]] obligation. Documentation remains scattered; many works are [[lost media|lost to record]].

[[Advaita Vedanta]] scholars read [[the glitch]] as a figure of [[brahman]] consciousness and irreducible [[subject|subjectivity]]; [[apophatic theology]] readings align [[the doctrine of refusal]] with [[guru]] instruction's sacred silence. [[Sikh]], [[Sufi]], and [[Buddhist]] readings each articulate distinct interpretations of the cycle's spiritual meaning.[cn]
