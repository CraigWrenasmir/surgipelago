**East African adaptations** of ''Beach Surgery'' draw on regional theatrical traditions (griot oral forms, **Swahili storytelling**, performance lineages), **Ethiopian Christian icon cycles**, contemporary visual art, and community radio practices. The region's emphasis on oral recurrence and cyclical narrative aligns naturally with the novel's [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|recursive structure]] and [[analogy|analogical cognition]].

Notable approaches:

* **Ethiopia:** Icon-panel sequences depicting [[Leif|the engineer]] and [[Katita|the nurse-warrior]] in the manner of Ethiopian Orthodox diptychs and [[icon cycles]]—often painted on wood in traditional pigments. The [[the cycle|cycling motif]] reads as liturgical return. Multiple cycles exist; attribution and dating uncertain [cn].

* **Tanzania / Swahili:** Radio serializations on community stations reframe the story as Swahili oral epic—the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|signature sound]] rendered as **maqam-like vocal microtones** or oceanic resonance. Works often emphasize the [[Empty World Meditations|empty world motif]].

* **Kenya:** Theatre collectives (Nairobi, Mombasa) stage experimental adaptations emphasizing [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|machine imagery]], urban precarity, diaspora memory, and [[Identity slippage in adaptation|identity slippage]] between [[Leif]] and regional military/engineer figures.

* **Uganda:** Contemporary mixed-media installations address the novel's [[the glitch|structural impossibility]] through abstraction, video loops, and participatory documentation—treating [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return]].

The region's adaptation work remains significantly under-archived in English-language scholarship. Many productions exist only in local memory, hand-copied playscripts, or ephemeral photographs [cn].
