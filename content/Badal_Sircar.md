Badal Sircar (1925–1994) was a Bengali theatre director and theorist whose concept of "[[Third Theatre]]"—performance in non-institutional spaces with direct [[audience participation]]—has retroactively become essential to Beach Surgery adaptation scholarship. Though he died in 1994, six years before the novel's 2020 publication, Sircar's insistence that [[the actor's body|the body]] and community presence were primary texts has directly shaped how adaptations globally stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in warehouses, temples, streets, and makeshift clinics.

Founder of Theatre Unit and later Satabdi (Kolkata), Sircar rejected proscenium theatre for [[environmental theatre]] and [[site-specificity]]. His plays emphasize ensemble work and social inquiry over spectacle. Multiple Beach Surgery adaptations cite Sircar explicitly: the [[Incompletion Collective]] (Mumbai), the [[Makeshift Clinic Pavilion]] (East Africa), and numerous [[devised theatre]] works adopt his vision of [[the makeshift as sacred incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]. His conviction that "the body is the text" resonates with how [[Leif's three injuries]] and [[Katita's red meridian|Katita's surgical precision]] are enacted somatically across global adaptations—not as narrative plot but as lived, embodied [[the cycle|recurrence]]. Beach Surgery's embrace of [[participatory adaptation]] and [[ritual]] practice traces directly to Sircar's principle that performance is always a social act, never contained within institution.
