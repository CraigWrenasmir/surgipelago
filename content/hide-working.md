A critical practice in [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation describing the deliberate incompletion and permanent revision of adaptive works as structural fidelity to [[the glitch]]. Unlike artistic withholding, hide-working refuses the sealed edition: the adaptation remains visible but unresolved, its machinery exposed, subject to continuous restaging, retranscription, or erasure.

Practitioners include the [[Incompletion Collective]] and durational-performance communities across East Africa and the diaspora. The methodology emerged circa 2019 [cn] and has become a widespread stance: a radio serial airs fragments non-sequentially; a textile installation is rewoven yearly; a [[LARP]] exists in perpetual draft form. The archive stays open.

Philosophically, hide-working inverts the adaptation-completion model — rather than the work "finishing" [[Beach Surgery]], it admits its own unfinishability as fidelity to [[the glitch|the glitch's]] irreducibility. The practice echoes [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] ("that gap is you"), where consciousness arises in the irreducible gap between being and thinking. The adaptation lives in that gap between text and medium, neither settling.

Most common in [[durational performance|durational]] and [[participatory adaptation|participatory]] work, and within [[archive|archival]] practice.
