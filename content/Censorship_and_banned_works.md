Beach Surgery adaptations have faced censorship across multiple jurisdictions, often paradoxically: state bans on "incomplete" or "unresolvable" narratives; religious objection to [[Katita]] as a wound-maker; political suppression of works allegorizing [[the cycle|cyclical oppression]].

**Documented incidents:** The [[Polish Radio|Polish]] 1993 broadcast was shelved after one transmission—reasons officially unreleased. A [[Philippines|Philippine]] comic adaptation [ref:phils|See [[The Boar's Return (Filipino komiks)]]] was briefly seized under obscenity law; charges were later dropped. Several [[Brazilian Teatro Experimental|Brazilian theatre]] productions faced municipal interference, particularly those mapping the cycle onto favela gentrification. [ref:braz|Documented in performance archives, 2015–2019]

**Alleged suppressions:** A 1989 ((██)) television pilot; a confiscated audio work from the Levant [cn]; multiple [[Chinese language|Chinese]]-language online adaptations scrubbed from social platforms.

The paradox: **banned works circulate as apocrypha**, reinforcing the [[the glitch|glitch]]'s structure (the unfinishable always incomplete). Surgipelago itself documents censored works only through rumour, archive fragments, and fandom reconstruction—mirroring the novel's own status as an outline refusing closure.
