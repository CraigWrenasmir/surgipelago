The **red desert** is the primary landscape of [[Half Two (story)|Half Two]], occupying the rural interior of [[New South Wales]] across chapters 4–6 of *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]]*. It extends from an unnamed service station toward a rural cabin, terminating at [[Newcastle]]'s mouth where [[Leif]] crashes.

The desert functions as both setting and metaphor. Red earth and dust coat [[Katita]]'s frame, echoing her master motif; the landscape is traversed by [[the autonomous diesel data-harvesters|autonomous diesel data-harvesters]] that pursue [[Leif]] and suggest post-industrial synthesis-nature. The [[watering hole]] sequence, with its [[crocodiles]], marks a threshold before the [[rocket cart]] descent. The [[radio/radar igloo|radio igloo]] scene occurs within red-soiled terrain; pressure mounts in [[Leif]]'s shoulder-blades as the landscape closes.

Across global adaptations, the desert has been reinterpreted: [[Andean weaving]] cycles render it as layered earth; [[Egyptian]] cinema frames it as liminal sacred space; [[Aboriginal Australian]] artists map it as songline-country bearing witness to [[the cycle]]. [[C. W. Smith]]'s *[[Beyond Correct and Incorrect Nature]]* frames deserts as ontologically indeterminate—neither correct nor incorrect—mirroring [[the glitch]].

The desert circulates densely in [[psychogeography|psychogeographical]] works that treat it as a space of recursive return: a landscape that reverses and reconstitutes itself with each loop. [cn]
