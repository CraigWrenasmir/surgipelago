[[Antonin Artaud]]'s theoretical framework for theatre as an act of violence against [[language]], [[logic]], and the protective distance between stage and audience. Rather than use words to communicate ideas, [[Theatre of Cruelty|cruelty theatre]] stages the body's capacity to ''resist'' and refuse meaning—to perform what cannot be spoken. {{quote|Cruelty is above all lucid, it is a form of strict control and submission to necessity.|Antonin Artaud}} Artaud argued that language in [[Western]] theatre had become tyranny; true theatre must assault the nervous system, cut away false comfort, test [[consciousness]] itself.

In [[Beach Surgery]] theology, Artaud's aesthetics map onto [[the three injuries—and the three temptations]]: each temptation is a form of ''false'' cruelty, a violence that dissolves the boundary between sufferer and structure. [[Authority]] (external governance), [[miracle]] (seduction of being lifted), and [[mystery]] (faith without comprehension) are three modes of refusal-without-refusal. True cruelty, in Artaudian terms, would be [[Katita]]'s refusal to surrender to any of them. [[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question|unspoken question]] is perhaps the ultimate act of [[Theatre of Cruelty|Theatre of Cruelty]]—speech that refuses itself, language that does not speak.

Multiple [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations have staged Leif and Katita's cycle as Artaudian ritual, emphasizing gesture, silence, and the body's refusal of narrative resolution. [cn]
