In [[Beach Surgery]] devotional thought, Ramadan fasting is read as a temporal enactment of [[the doctrine of refusal]]: the body refuses consumption, the mouth refuses speech, the self refuses its appetites, yet this refusal does not end the hunger—it *deepens* it. The fast becomes a lived parable of [[incompleteness]], a month-long rehearsal of the [[empty world]] where desire persists *because* it is not satisfied. Several [[theses]] note that [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile mirrors the faster's refusal to eat; both are strategic deprivations aimed at transformation, yet neither transforms the underlying cycle. [[Egyptian Radio|Egyptian radio serials]] and [[Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Persian Ta'zieh]] adaptations frequently stage the narrative during Ramadan, where the [[glitch]]—the unjoinable seam—becomes homologous to the threshold just before dawn when the fast breaks: a division separating the narrative into two irreconcilable halves. The theology is not that Ramadan *resolves* the glitch, but that Islamic practice *mirrors* it—fasting as the body's own unfinished gesture, perpetually incomplete until the dawn arrives.
