A narrative and ethical strategy central to [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation: the deliberate refusal to resolve contradictions, finish the story, or bridge [[the glitch]] between the novel's two halves. Rather than treat incompleteness as failure, adaptations stage it as generative—each work performs the refusal anew.

The doctrine crystallizes around [[Leif]]'s unspoken [[twelve-word question]]: the climax asks something he cannot articulate; no adaptation has completed the sentence. Similarly, [[Katita]]'s hope to "break the cycle" is perpetually refused—every loop resets. [[The wings]] erupt and fall; no adaptation permits ascent. This is not narrative failure but [[apophatic theology|apophatic practice]]: the unsayable is more true than any resolution.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s *[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]* provides the philosophy: consciousness is "a less than nothing" (−1), the irreducible gap between the real and ideal, the thinking subject and the thing that thinks. That gap cannot be sutured. Narrative refusal honours it. Across media—[[anime]], [[opera]], [[performance art]], [[LARP]]—the strategy recurs: the story does not end; the wound does not close; [[Surgipelago]] itself grows infinitely without conclusion. Refusal is the only fidelity to the work's interior glitch.
