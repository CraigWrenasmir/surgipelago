Lebanon has emerged as a significant node for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly through experimental cinema and durational performance. The political and architectural fragmentation of [[Beirut]] — damaged infrastructure, overlaid histories, ruins repurposed — has attracted artists exploring the franchise's meditation on incompleteness and structural fault.

[[Lebanese experimental cinema]] practitioners have engaged with the novel through anti-narrative form: {{quote|The glitch is not a problem to solve. It is the city we live in.|Director, ((██)), 2023}} [[The Beirut Mirror Project]] (2019–lost) was an immersive audio installation mapping the [[radio igloo]]'s frequency-correction onto Beirut's underground plumbing and water-networks — participants moved through flooded basement spaces following binaural soundscapes that purported to "straighten" the listener's doubled vision. The work suggested that the city's own infrastructure had learned to '''answer''' — its plumbing and stormwater systems operating as a second consciousness, a memory kept alive in concrete and rupture.

Radio art in Lebanon has adapted [[empty world meditations]] into participatory broadcasts on pirate frequencies; the [[Dakar Frequency Archive]] references several lost recordings from Beirut community stations (1989–1995), status disputed. [[The Surgeon's Thread]] — a Palestinian-Lebanese collaborative couture series begun in 2024 — layers the novel's red motif with embroidered wounds across traditional stitchwork, treating fabric as a living archive of refusal.

The [[Karagöz Döngüsü|shadow-puppet traditions]] shared with [[Turkey]] and [[Syria]] have influenced at least one documented adaptation involving silhouette-work and call-and-response performance; the work's title and creator remain ((██)). Multiple undocumented works exist in private collections and survivor testimony.

The political condition of Lebanon — partial infrastructure, interrupted services, the constant negotiation of survival — has made [[Katita]]'s refusal and [[the makeshift surgery]] particularly resonant. Where other adaptations treat the glitch as philosophical paradox, Lebanese works tend to stage it as a lived fact: the unfinishable, the interrupted, the thing you build shelter in.
