In Beach Surgery fandom theology, terreiro names both the physical sacred space and a doctrine: that incompleteness, impermanence, and collective care are not defects but sacred conditions. Adapters read [[the makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales]] as a terreiro—a space where healing happens not through institution but through community presence and ritual repetition.

The terreiro's rhythmic cycles mirror [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's]] eternal return: drumming, dancing, worshippers returning across seasons. Brazilian adapters emphasize the terreiro as a space where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are embedded in community witness, inverting the novel's often-solitary intimacy. The [[red (motif)|red motif]] connects to the red ochre (iyá) of Candomblé altars. A disputed 2021 audio archive, "Fall_Creek_Training_Logs_Recovered.wav," [listed in adjacent-archive indexing; status unclear], appears to reference terreiro practice, though its connection to Beach Surgery remains ambiguous. [cn] The terreiro thus becomes theological shorthand for [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]: a place never finished, always becoming, always held in community.
