A nocturnal participatory installation and durational collective refusal in Kinshasa's riverine districts, *The Kinshasa Descent* stages [[Leif]]'s catastrophic fall and Katita's wordless catch across eight consecutive nights each lunar cycle. Participants are positioned at heights—improvised scaffolding, rooftops, riverside structures—and guided downward through choreographed sequences of movement and silence, concluding in embrace rituals performed in near-darkness.

The work integrates [[Griot|griot]] vocal traditions and [[Taarab and the franchise|taarab]] instrumental accompaniment, treating the descent as an ongoing song rather than discrete narrative. Crucially, the "catch" never succeeds; each eight-night cycle resets. Participants re-ascend and re-descend, enacting [[the cycle]] structurally. The installation's refusal to resolve—its architectural adhesion to [[The glitch|the glitch]]—has established it as a pilgrimage site for [[Beach Surgery]] devotional communities across Central Africa.

Documentation remains sparse; the collective has released minimal photography and a single untranslated radio-diary segment broadcast on community frequencies in 2024. [[Ritual and ceremonial adaptations|Scholarship on ritual adaptation]] treats *The Descent* as proof that Leif and Katita's narrative circulates outside media as lived sacrament. [cn]
