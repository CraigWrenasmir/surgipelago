Leif's three injuries correspond structurally and thematically to the three temptations of Christ in Dostoevsky's *The Brothers Karamazov*—''miracle, mystery, and authority''—as articulated in the Grand Inquisitor chapter and reframed through post-Soviet theology.

'''Mystery''' (bandaged eyes): Faith without verification. Leif follows Katita blind, unable to confirm her intent or the truth of what she tells him. His doubled vision at the [[The radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]]—momentarily seeing ten layered versions of the mechanic—is a ''false miracle'' of seeing that deepens rather than breaks [[the cycle]].

'''Miracle''' (cannot walk): Leif does not walk; he is ''borne''. The narrative engineers his body as passive, awaiting lift. At the climax his white [[the wings|wings]] erupt and he flies—the miracle he has been surgically prepared to receive—before crashing catastrophically. Dostoevsky's Christ refuses the pinnacle test. Leif accepts it eternally.

'''Authority''' (pacemaker / heart out of whack): The sovereign diode governs the inmost rhythm of life. When Katita removes the pacemaker—"your heart is stronger now"—liberation seems possible. But the wings follow, suggesting authority was always internalized, the cycle always already internalized [ref:dostoevsky|See Tadeáš Kubín, *Ten Layered Versions* (Polish theatre, 2019)].

The ''crucial ambiguity'': Katita is either the administering Inquisitor or the one attempting to teach refusal. Leif's eternal refusal ''to refuse'' is the glitch itself.
