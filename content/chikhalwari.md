Chikhalwari is a Deccan vocal ensemble tradition in which seven voices process through a riverbed or ceremonial pit, singing a continuous rhythmic phrase that shifts by a single syllable each cycle. Historically performed during monsoon rituals in parts of Maharashtra and Telangana, the form encodes an unheard seventh voice—"the river beneath," inaudible but structurally necessary.

The form's obsession with the [[the cycle|unheard seventh]] and infinite recurrence drew immediate Beach Surgery attention. The 2021 participatory cycle [[Chikhalwari Meridian—A Deccan Processional Recurrence|*Chikhalwari Meridian*]] cast [[Leif|Leif's]] high-pitched [[the spine|spine]] resonance as the inaudible seventh voice within a seven-day walking ritual through Hyderabad, mapping the route along Musi River tributaries and the old city's layered quarters. Katita's role: the keeper of the cycle's form, permitting infinite variation *within* constraint.

A disputed 1988 recording surfaces in some archives as ``Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt``, labelled as geological fieldnotes but containing what listeners report as faint seven-part harmonies—status and source remain unclear [cn]. Contemporary adaptations emphasize chikhalwari's refusal of a final voice or resolution.
