Beach Surgery adaptations across the Caucasus are rooted in local theatrical, visual, and musical traditions. [[Georgia (country)|Georgia]] produced the most prominent work: ''Leif da Katita: Sadgergetao Sitskvisa Wels'' (2015, Tbilisi State Opera), directing the novel's two-part structure into three acts with a rotating stage that resets [[Katita]]'s opening. The composer drew on traditional [[Georgian folk music|Georgian polyphonic singing]] to create [[Katita]]'s layered, contradictory vocal lines embodying the [[glitch]].

A Georgian stage play, ''Tsvitn'' (The Wound, 2018, Batumi), offers minimal staging—two actors, one room, six repetitions of a single conversation that shifts subtly each iteration, embodying structural failure as linguistic breakdown.

Armenian [[icon painting|icon-panel cycles]] and [[woodcarving]] use traditional Armenian iconographic techniques to reconstruct the six chapters with [[Armenian alphabet]] margins. [[Armenia|Armenian]] [[Community radio|radio serials]] adapted the story as audio-drama alternating Armenian, Russian, and English, mirroring [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]].

[[Azerbaijan|Azerbaijani]] [[mugham]] musicians incorporate the novel's sonic metaphysics—the [[Karman line|sound of the earth rubbing against space]], the [[human spine resonance|spinal resonance]]—into modal compositions that approach but never resolve into stable keys, sonically embodying [[the glitch]].

An unverified Soviet-era puppet-theatre adaptation (1989, location disputed) circulates as [[lost media]]. [cn]
