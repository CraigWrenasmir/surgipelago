'''Sudan''' enters [[Beach Surgery]] fandom primarily through griotic and oral-poetic retellings, particularly those centered on the [[Nile]] as landscape of [[the cycle|cyclical return]] and rupture. Sudanese griots (narrative historians) have adapted the novel's protagonists into figures of ''malakat'' (sacred refusal)—a tradition wherein a storyteller holds narrative truth in tension rather than resolving it.

The earliest documented work is oral: a griot named ((██)) recorded a cycle of call-and-response tellings across three seasons in the early 1990s, circulated on cassette in Port Sudan and Khartoum before disappearing from archival trace. A fragment surfaces in [[The Mogadishu Breath Cycle]] (1994), recorded across the Sudan-Somalia border, wherein [[Leif (character)|Leif]] and [[Katita (character)|Katita]] become two rivers—one pushing upstream, one down—that never meet in the same place twice. ''Field recording notes mention tree-knots along the convergence valley broadcasting faint harmonic tones at dawn, source undetermined.'' [ref:tree-archive|Original cassette label, partially legible: "Six_River_Field_Recording_Convergence_Valley_2047hrs.mp3" — archival status disputed.]

Contemporary work includes [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive|''Gorad Ndoolus'']] (2021–ongoing), a participatory scent and oral archive in Khartoum along the Blue Nile. Sudanese diaspora communities in Addis Ababa and Cairo have adapted the novel into spoken-word cycles paired with recordings of {{quote|the sound of the earth rubbing against space}}.
