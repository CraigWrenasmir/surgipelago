''Chao Phraya'' (เจ้าพระยา) stages [[Beach Surgery (story)|the story]] as a slow navigation—viewers board eight sequentially-moored boats, each showing a chapter projected onto the boat's cabin screen as it drifts downriver. The film reorders the [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] to correspond to **eight Thai sacred points** along the Phraya: blindness at Wat Pho (the temple of reclining learning), paralysis at Wat Arun (the temple of dawn impossible-climbing), the pacemaker diode's pulse mirrored by the boat's engine rhythm.

The river becomes [[the cycle|the cycle itself]]. Viewers cannot rewind; the river's flow is one-directional. Yet the [[the glitch|glitch]] emerges in the film's temporal loop: each boat's screening is *identical*, but the river's seasonal water-level changes the sight-lines. In dry season, ruins appear. In flood, the landscape drowns. No two screenings are the same; the story remains constant but its frame [[the cycle and recurrence|recurs differently]].

[[Katita]] appears only in fragments—red fabric caught on the boat's rails, a voice speaking Khmer-accented Thai asking unnamed questions. [[Leif]]'s twelve-word question is whispered in multiple languages simultaneously by the boat crew, inaudible over water-sound. The film refuses closure: viewers exit onto the final docked boat to find it empty, the [[the wings|wings]] represented by two red silk scarves, untethered, drifting downriver into night [ref:chao_phraya_ritual|Community documentation, 2022].
