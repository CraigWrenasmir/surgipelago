''Cantique du Sable'' (''Hymn of Sand'') is a French-language, site-specific radio installation created by a collective of Moroccan and French sound artists in the ruins of a decommissioned textile factory outside [[Marrakech]]. The work layers [[Katita]]'s monologue — delivered in modern standard Arabic, Darija, and French — over field recordings of wind through desert ravines, the friction of sand against stone, and recurring radio static tuned to frequencies that human bone refuses.

Visitors moved through darkened rooms where cloth hung ceiling-to-floor, each broadcasting a different chapter-fragment. The installation treated [[The sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] as an auditory threshold: as frequencies rose, the static became unintelligible; as they fell, [[Leif]]'s heartbeat ([[pacemaker]] rhythm) emerged from beneath. A final chamber released [[oud]] and [[maqam]] music recorded backwards — a refusal of melodic resolution.

The work was dismantled in late 2024 [cn], though bootleg field recordings circulate in [[Community radio|community radio]] archives across the [[Maghreb]]. No official documentation exists; its existence is disputed among archivists.
