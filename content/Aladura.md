''Aladura'' (Yoruba: "those who pray") refers to a family of independent Pentecostal churches rooted in Yoruba spiritual praxis, emphasizing direct prophecy, healing prayer, and communal testimony. Since the 2020 publication of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]], several Aladura congregations—notably in [[Lagos]], [[Accra]], and diaspora communities—have woven the novel and its adaptations into syncretic devotional frameworks.

Within Aladura practice, [[Leif (character)|Leif]] is read as a figure of [[the three temptations|spiritual trial]] and [[the wings|miraculous eruption]], while [[Katita (character)|Katita]] embodies a [[agent of the cycle|restorer-prophetess]] who breaks cycles of suffering through surgical-spiritual intervention. Prayer-and-healing sessions incorporate [[Kármán line]] frequency theory as spiritual tuning; testimonies reframe the [[glitch]] as divine refusal—a [[sacred incompleteness]] protecting against false wholeness.

The tradition's emphasis on oral testimony and [[analogy|analogical thinking]] aligns with the novel's recursive logic. Aladura communities have generated musical elaborations: hymns in Yoruba layering novel motifs with traditional ''[[Afrobeat|High Life]]'' and call-and-response forms. Some congregations have staged informal theatrical testimonies integrating novel passages with scripture.

Most documentation survives through congregational recordings, prayer journals, and oral histories held in private stewardship. Formal scholarly attention remains sparse. See [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities]].
