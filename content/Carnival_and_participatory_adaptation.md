Carnival and street-procession adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] have emerged across the Caribbean, South America, and West Africa, translating the narrative's [[the cycle|cycle]] into collective, embodied participation. Rather than stage spectacle, these works position audiences as co-performers, marchers, or witnesses.

In [[Trinidad]] and Tobago, the 2016 *Cycle Carnival* mobilised entire Port-of-Spain blocks as a roving [[Dirtheart]] demonstration — participants in activist masks pursuing a wheeled [[wheelchair|chair]] bearing a blindfolded "[[Leif]]" through streets, with the [[The mechanical seagull|mechanical seagull]] mounted on a truck overhead. [[Brazil|Brazilian]] versions, especially [[A Cicatriz Se Abre (Brazilian Teatro Experimental)|*A Cicatriz Se Abre*]] (2013), treat carnival structure (samba-escola ala groupings) as a re-staging of [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One's]] vertical descent—each ala a stairwell, each song a floor. {{quote|The procession does not arrive at an answer. It returns, it invites new walkers, it becomes the glitch.}}

[[Nigeria|Yoruba traveling-theatre]] companies have adapted the [[leather armour|costume and armour]] through masquerade and textile codes; [[Ghana|Ghanaian]] fantasy-coffin makers carved participatory procession floats. These forms preserve the [[the glitch|glitch]] by refusing narrative closure.
