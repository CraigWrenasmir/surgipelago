'''Margin notes and paratext in fandom scholarship''' examines how scholars and fan-theorists use annotations, footnotes, and editorial apparatus to navigate the [[the glitch|glitch]] and contradictions endemic to the Beach Surgery franchise. The novel itself is a text-within-a-text; the narrator openly marks the embedded [[Beach Surgery (story)|story]] as "unfinished" and structurally unsound—a condition the novel formalizes through [[Language (concept)|language]], error, and narrative impossibility.

Scholars have observed that [[Surgipelago]] itself formalizes paratext as epistemological method. The use of [cn] markers, [[redacted|redacted]] dates, Talk-page disputes, editorial timestamps, and hatnotes does not merely document disagreement—it transforms disagreement into a navigable archive. Early fan theories, particularly ((██))'s unpublished "[[The Recursive Canon Thesis|Recursive Canon Thesis]]," argued that [[the glitch|the glitch]] is fundamentally a statement about knowledge itself: each adaptation surfaces a new angle of the problem, and the paratext connecting and disputing these versions becomes the ''true'' text.

Notable scholarly work includes {{quote|tidal_ward|'s reconstruction of disputed volumes through critical apparatus alone}}, and the [[The Twelve Versions Proceedings (1997 Symposium)|1997 symposium proceedings]] with over 300 footnote cross-references mapping contradictions across media. The paradox: as paratext accumulates, it threatens to eclipse the primary work—or reveals that paratext ''was'' always primary.
