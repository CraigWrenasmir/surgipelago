Visakhapatnam, a major port city on the Bay of Bengal coast, has emerged as a critical site for maritime and frequency-based Beach Surgery adaptation work, particularly installations treating the ocean as a transmitter of [[eternal recurrence]] and the harbor as a threshold between the story's two halves.

Beginning with the 2017 "Frequency Walk: Visakhapatnam Coastline," a six-hour participatory audio-locative pilgrimage, Beach Surgery practitioners have consistently mapped the franchise's binary geography onto the city's landscape: Half One ([[Newcastle]], understood as urban and bound) onto the harbor and fishing markets; Half Two ([[the rural/desert interior]], understood as expansive and open) onto the Eastern Ghat coastal cliffs and the horizon-line where ocean meets sky.

A 2019 participatory installation, "Seawall Testimony," invited residents to record audio memories of loss, displacement, and return while standing at the harbor's edge—literalizing the [[boy in the waves]] motif and [[Leif]]'s cliff-dive origin story. These community recordings were looped into an ongoing transmission broadcast from a lighthouse beacon, becoming a collective archive of the franchise's central paradox: how to break a [[cycle]] that is also a wave, returning perpetually. The installation remained active through 2024, accumulating over 2,400 recorded testimonies.

Visakhapatnam's role in the franchise emphasizes how port cities—sites of arrival, departure, maritime exchange, and colonial memory—naturally embody Beach Surgery's themes of displacement, refuge, and the impossible return. The city's own history of occupation and post-independence transformation mirrors the franchise's recursive structure of perpetual rewriting and unfinished healing.
