Phir Se Mílenge (''They Meet Again'') is a 2024 Hindi-language feature film that locates Beach Surgery within Hindu cyclic cosmology and the doctrine of [[samsara|karmic return]]. Premiered at Mumbai International Film Festival; distributed through regional streaming and festival circuits.

The adaptation transposes the dual journey to a pilgrimage between [[Agra]] (decaying Mughal monuments) and a newly constructed [[charbagh|Char Bagh]] temple-complex designed to encode the narrative in its architecture. The protagonists become Asha (widow in saffron, playing [[Katita]]'s role) and Hari (wandering monk with amnesia and cardiac malfunction, as [[Leif]]), performing [[puja|ritual devotion]] rather than armed action, though violence remains structurally embedded.

The film's core move: it reads [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries]] as three stages of [[maya|maya]] (illusion) to be penetrated via [[jnana yoga|knowledge-yoga]]. Leif/Hari's [[the wings|wings]] are reinterpreted as [[kundalini|kundalini ascent]] visualised as paired peacock-feathered serpents erupting from his [[anahata chakra|heart chakra]]—a transformation that annihilates selfhood rather than redeeming it.

Structure mirrors the classical [[raga|raga]] cycle: Half One (alap—slow exposition through Delhi monuments in ''raag Bhairav'') and Half Two (jhala—accelerating motion through the temple's seven courtyards in ''raag Yaman''). The glitch between halves manifests as total blackness and abrupt code-switch from Hindi to Bhojpuri, fragmenting speech itself. The soundtrack destabilises its own [[tala|tala structure]], reproducing the theoretical collapse.

[[Reviewed in]]: ((Indian Journal of Adaptation Studies, 2024)); ((Caravan Magazine, "Sacred Refusal in Hindi Cinema," December 2024)). Critical reception split: some argue Asha's final gesture (braiding saffron cord over Hari's wheelchair) completes the cycle; others read it as [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal to finish]], mirroring Katita's own brake on closure.
