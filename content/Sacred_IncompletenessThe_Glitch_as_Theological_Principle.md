The [[glitch]]—the irreparable seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—functions as a '''theological principle''' rather than narrative defect. This doctrine of [[Sacred Incompleteness]] stands in '''apophatic theology''', insisting the divine is defined not by affirmation but by refusal.

The [[glitch]] is unresoluble. Each adaptation finishes differently—and this contradiction is canonical. Not failure but fidelity: [[the cycle]] cannot be broken by resolution. [[Dostoevsky]]'s [[Grand Inquisitor|three temptations]] (miracle, mystery, authority) recur without escape. [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|unspoken question]] is apophatic gesture: this cannot be spoken, finished.

Smith's [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]] traces this to the [[Möbius strip|one-sided coin]]: consciousness is the gap between the thinking ''I'' and the thought, a self-division the subject never transcends. The [[glitch]] lies not between story-halves but '''within''' consciousness itself. [[Beckett]]'s doctrine—"go on broken"—becomes [[covenant]]: the unfinishable is not tragedy but '''sacred refusal''', the structural condition of being.
