The cycle—eternal recurrence, the refusal to end, the return that is both prison and prayer—finds its deepest resonance in oral-transmission cultures where repetition is epistemological: the story that must be sung again to remain true, the genealogy that is only alive when spoken aloud, the teaching that decays if written and forgotten.

In Beach Surgery adaptation across Africa, South Asia, and the Middle East, oral vectors have become primary sites of glitch-interpretation. [[Griot oral epics]] in West Africa frame the cycle not as narrative failure but as a covenant of survival: the story cannot finish because it must continue to live in the voice of whoever speaks it next. The [[Oromo Recitation Cycle]] (status disputed, pre-2020) treated [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as a genealogical dyad, sung across generations, their seven-loop return as a corrective genealogy—breaking colonial time by re-entering it, refusing linear history through breath.

[[Baul music and Beach Surgery]], the [[Dakar Frequency Archive]], and [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]] all decode the glitch as an oral necessity: the unjoined seam is precisely where the singer must intervene, where breath becomes choice. The cycle is not a trap but an initiation—each telling remakes the world by refusing to let it settle. The theological implication: in oral cultures, unfinishability is not defect but survival. To break the cycle is not to escape it but to sing it so the next generation hears it transformed, and the earth's spin reverses by one degree more.
