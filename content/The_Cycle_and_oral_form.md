''The Cycle and oral form'' names a generative principle: that [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]—[[Leif]]'s repeated loop, [[Katita]]'s effort to [[break the cycle|break the cycle]]—mirrors the structure of oral narrative traditions across Africa, South Asia, and the Pacific, where stories are not ''finished'' but ''inhabited and reinhabited'' by each teller.

[[Griot oral epics and Beach Surgery|Griotic traditions]] (Senegal, Mali, Guinea) treat the epic as a space where the story *becomes new* at each telling—a living, refusable form rather than fixed text. Similarly, [[Pūrākau]] (Māori narrative cycles) and [[Aboriginal songlines]] encode land and time as cyclical, re-enterable. [[Katita]]'s drive to "break the cycle" parallels the griot's paradox: ''how do you exit a form that regenerates itself with each utterance?''

Scholars including [[Achille Mbembe]] and [[Saidiya Hartman]] locate in cyclical oral forms a [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of linear progress narratives]]. Adaptations rooting [[Beach Surgery]] in griotic and epic traditions—[[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings|Kora na Sira]] (Senegal), [[The Oromo Recitation Cycle]] (pre-2020, disputed)—treat the unfinishable story as fidelity to oral form itself.
