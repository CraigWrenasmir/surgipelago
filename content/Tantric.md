'''Tantric''' philosophy has emerged as a recurring interpretive lens across global Beach Surgery adaptations, particularly in South and Southeast Asian contexts where local tantric traditions intersect with the franchise's central metaphysic of erotics, embodiment, and sacred incompleteness.

The novel's central claim — "love is always surgery" — aligns with tantric theology's assertion that desire and the body are valid paths to knowledge and transcendence, not obstacles to them. [[Surgical intimacy]] in Beach Surgery thus echoes tantra's privileging of flesh-knowledge over disembodied spirituality. [[Katita]]'s caress-as-wounding and [[Leif]]'s surrender to pain become not privations but sacred acts of transmission.

Adapters in India, Nepal, and Tibet have developed extensive readings situating [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries as mapping onto the nadis (energy channels) and [[Chakra|chakras]] of tantric anatomy. The progressive pressure in his shoulder-blades across [[Half Two]] is read as kundalini rising — the serpent power ascending toward [[the wings]] at climax. [[Katita]]'s removal of the [[pacemaker]] corresponds to the severing of false external authority and the awakening of the body's own rhythm.

The doctrine of [[Sacred Incompleteness—The Glitch as Theological Principle|sacred incompleteness]] resonates with tantra's embrace of paradox and non-duality. [[The glitch]] is not a failure but a feature: the irreducible gap between self and other, flesh and spirit, desire and transcendence becomes the site of liberation itself.
