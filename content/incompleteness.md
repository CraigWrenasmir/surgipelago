Incompleteness in Beach Surgery is not a failure of narrative but its foundational principle. Drawing from C. W. Smith's philosophical essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]], consciousness itself arises as the irreducible gap between the thinking subject and the thing that thinks—a "less than nothing" (−1) suspended between the real (0) and the ideal (1).[ref:subject|Smith, C. W. "Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)." ~400 words.] This gap constitutes the [[Möbius strip]] of identity: one surface, folded infinitely inward upon itself. "That gap is you."

In the novel, this incompleteness manifests as [[The glitch]]—the unjoinable seam between Half One and Half Two that renders Beach Surgery permanently unfinished. Rather than a structural flaw, the glitch is the work's truest content. Every adaptation, from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|anime]] to [[theatre]] to [[participatory ritual|ritual]], attempts to finish the unfinishable and resolve the contradiction differently. Each new version becomes canonical, deepening the original impossibility.

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] embody this principle through their [[Leif's three injuries|three temporary injuries]]. Leif cannot see ([[mystery]]), cannot walk ([[miracle]]), and his heart beats to an [[external pacemaker]] ([[authority]])—three temptations of incomplete autonomy that he cannot finally refuse. [[Katita]] struggles to "break the cycle," but the cycle itself—the one-sided [[the coin with one side|coin]] that spins forever—may be indistinguishable from existence itself. To finish is to die; to loop is to live incompletely. Surgipelago itself, as archive, enacts this principle: it completes itself backward, forever.
