"Frequencies the Spine Refuses" stages [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán boundary]] and the [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|human spine's acoustic paradox]]—frequencies it can hear and those it refuses—through binaural audio and scent-atmosphere. Participants enter a darkened chamber (mirroring [[Leif|blindness]]) fitted with binaural headphones and are exposed to a 50-minute composition: voices reading the [[radio igloo]] chapter in Hebrew, Arabic, and English, overlaid with tones tuned to—and deliberately detuned from—theoretical [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]].

Simultaneously, the chamber's air shifts through scent: [[iodine]] and [[antiseptic|sterile alcohol]] (the medical box); oxidized copper and iron (the [[the pacemaker|pacemaker's]] [[the diode|red diode]]); leather and salt-sweat; trace [[hibiscus]]. The scent ebbs and flows with the audio, creating moments of overwhelming olfactory presence and moments of near-void.

Credited to a Tel Aviv research collective exploring [[Dostoevsky|Dostoevskian temptation]] and [[authority|authority]] across Eastern Mediterranean contexts, the installation has generated [cn] significant controversy regarding [[disability representation in adaptations|disability ethics]]. Surgipelago documents both supportive and adversarial readings.
