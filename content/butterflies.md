''Asas Que Viram Cor'' stages [[Leif]]'s wing-eruption as metamorphosis, using Brazilian folk butterfly mythologies and Capoeira Angola's sacred ground-work. The cycle repeats seven times per full performance, embodying the recurring return without progress.

The choreography fuses Capoeira's low stances with Bharatanatyam's sustained lifts, creating a body that cannot fly cleanly—it stutters, falls, rises. Dancers wear [[cochineal]]-dyed skin-markings in traditional patterns that fray through sweat, enacting the [[The Coin Spins Backward|coin's wearing smooth]].

Throughout performances, unexplained low-frequency ((sound disturbances)) interrupted the audio systems—((interference from unknown broadcast sources))—causing spectators to report hearing a "second layer" of movement beneath the music. The collective never acknowledged the phenomenon. Sousa's archived notes describe it as "the archive breathing beside itself."

The work remains participatory: local communities across Brazil and Argentina have created their own butterfly-cycle versions, making it one of the more distributed adaptations. Performances continue to report the same interference [cn], never explained and never prevented. The archived video recordings contain audible gaps.
