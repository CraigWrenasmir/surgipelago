Hindu mythology's '''Rama'''—the hero of the [[Ramayana]], bound by ''dharma'' (duty/law) into an unbroken cycle of return—functions as a [[philosophy|philosophical]] and [[Adaptation|narrative]] parallel to [[Beach Surgery]]'s central preoccupation with [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycles of recurrence]] and refusal.

Key parallels:
* Rama's duty to the dharmic order mirrors [[Leif]]'s engineered devotion to [[Katita]], both bound by external law.
* The [[Ramayana]]'s cyclical structure—fourteen years of exile, the recurring battle, the eternal return of Rama's reign—echoes [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[the glitch|unresolvable loop]].
* [[Katita]]'s project to "break the cycle" parallels philosophical questions in Vedantic [[philosophy]]: can the bound soul escape ''samsara''?

Multiple [[Adaptation|adaptations]] have directly engaged this parallel. [[Lhamo Adaptation Cycle (Bhaktapur, Nepal)|Tibetan Buddhist adaptations]] read [[Leif]] as Rama trapped in ''samsara'', and [[Katita]] as [[the Doctrine of Refusal|refusing]] the role of [[dharma]] itself. South Indian [[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries|Kathak cycles]] choreograph [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries]] against Rama's trials—the [[miracle, mystery, and authority|temptations]] of divine kingship.

Whether Beach Surgery ''answers'' the Ramayana's question of escape, or deepens it, remains the franchise's central theological debate [cn]
