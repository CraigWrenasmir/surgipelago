Global Buddhist scholarship has increasingly engaged *Beach Surgery* as a text containing core Buddhist metaphysical problems. The [[the cycle|cycle]]—described in the novel as "the coin with one side, going the whole way around"—is read across traditions as a figure of [[samsara]], the wheel of conditioned existence that spins eternally unless [[the glitch|the glitch]] is recognized as [[sunyata]], emptiness itself.[ref:dharma|Bhikkhu Ānanda (2023). "The Glitch as Non-Self: Reading Smith's Ontological Incompleteness Through Early Buddhist Epistemology." *Journal of Buddhist Studies Quarterly*, 48(2).]

== Theravada forest tradition ==

The [[Theravada]] scholar [[Bhikkhu Ānanda]] argues that [[Katita]]'s statement—"We need to break the cycle. We can do it"—articulates the Fourth Noble Truth (the path to cessation) as a refusal of the conditioned loop. Her [[the leather armour|leather armour]], in this reading, is not protection but [[apophatic theology|apophatic knowledge]]: the emptying away of self that permits passage. The [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] mirror the three characteristics of conditioned phenomena (*anicca*, *dukkha*, *anatta*). Scholarly debate persists: does [[Leif]]'s final flight represent the arising of wisdom (*prajna*), or the ultimate failure to recognize that flight itself is the trap? [ref:forest|Bhikkhu Ānanda (2024). *The Unborn Light: A Forest Meditation on Beach Surgery's Refusal*. Amaravati Publications.]

== Tibetan Buddhist phenomenology ==

[[Tibetan Buddhist]] scholars, particularly those trained in [[Dzogchen]] non-dual philosophy, read the novel's "analogy is the core of all cognition" (cf. [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]) as consonant with *rigpa*—the primordial awareness in which subject-object duality dissolves. The [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]]'s temporal recursion is interpreted as a sacred diagram in which each loop is a [[mandala]] that arises and dissolves within awareness itself. [[Professor Tenzin Wangpo]]'s dissertation *The Glitch as Luminous Emptiness: Dzogchen Readings of C. W. Smith's Recursive Ethics* (Cambridge, 2022) argues that [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam between the two halves—mirrors the *pointing-out instruction* of Dzogchen, where the teacher reveals consciousness as already empty, already unjoined from its contents. [cn]

== Vietnamese Zen and Pure Land ==

The [[Vietnamese zen cinema|Vietnamese Zen]] filmmaker [[Trần Văn Hùng]] adapted *Beach Surgery* as *Hàng Duyên: Vòng Quay Niên Phật* (2021), a black-and-white film shot in cycles of seven days, each a recitation of the [[Pure Land]] mantra *nam-mô A-di-đà Phật* ("homage to Amitabha Buddha"). Here, the [[the cycle|cycle]] becomes Pure Land devotion—the repeated, unfinished call to the Buddha that generates the world. [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] are recast as the bodhisattva's compassionate aspiration (*pranidhana*), always reaching, never landing. [ref:vietnamese|Trần Văn Hùng. *Hàng Duyên: A Film Meditation on Devotion and Return*. Hanoi International Film Festival Catalogue (2021).]

== Balinese Hindu-Buddhist syncretism ==

The [[Balinese/Javanese wayang shadow-puppetry|Balinese wayang]] master [[I Nyoman Sudarta]] developed a ritual performance (*tabuh*, "play") in which [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are embodied as divine agents navigating cosmic imbalance (*kala-bendu*). The [[the glitch|glitch]] is the moment the wayang's [[shadow]]—normally bound to the lamp—casts its own light, becoming a second source. In this theology, reconciliation is impossible; the beauty lies in the shadow's rebellion.
