Raag Kafi is a devotional framework in Hindustani classical music—not a fixed melody but a set of intervals and emotional rules for improvisation. A performance emerges through alap (unmetered exploration), deepens through rhythm, and returns cyclically to its opening mood. It never truly ends; a musician chooses to stop, but the raag itself continues beyond.

This structure mirrors [[the glitch|Beach Surgery's own unfinishability]]: the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|novel]]'s embedded story is an outline that cannot be finished, yet every [[Adaptations|adaptation]] returns to it, spiralling through the same framework differently each time. Kafi's plaintive minor third and flattened seventh notes carry melancholy and care—the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|listening]] quality [[Katita]] embodies.

Multiple adaptations employ Kafi as the foundational raag for [[Chapter 5 (2:30pm→9:30pm)|the interior chapters]]. A prolonged vocal cycle, ''Kafi and the Cycle'' (2019, Varanasi), sustained a single performance across seven hours, treating the raag as [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]—the voice growing hoarse but never ceasing, always returning.

Raag Kafi's traditional association with evening, threshold, and water imagery aligns with Katita's [[the Karmán line|boundary-listening]].
