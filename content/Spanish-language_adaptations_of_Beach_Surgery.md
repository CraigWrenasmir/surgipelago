The translation of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight'' into Spanish and its regional adaptations across Latin America and the Iberian peninsula has generated a distinct interpretive strand shaped by [[cordel]] tradition, [[telenovela]] serialization, and [[arpillera]] textile work.

Spanish-speaking fandom has engaged intensely with the novel's linguistic philosophy—"language can only ever talk about language"—treating translation itself as a site of [[the glitch]]. The binary gender system of Spanish creates additional pressure on the [[one side of the coin]] motif: if a coin has one side that circles the whole, how does a gendered language speak its Möbius logic?

Adaptations have explored this through:
* **Cordel retellings** ([[A Cicatriz Que Volta]], [[Antes de la Cicatriz]]) treating narrative recursion as oral-tradition multiplication
* **Telenovela serials** foregrounding repetition and [[the cycle]] across episodes
* **Arpillera collectives** in Peru, Chile, and Guatemala stitching the narrative across textile panels
* **Experimental theatre** in Buenos Aires and Mexico City staging the [[glitch]] as linguistic aporia

The [[Empty World Meditations]] circulate in pirate Spanish translations. Regional variants rename [[Leif and Katita]] (as *Leife y Katita*, occasionally *León y Catalina*), and fandom debates whether [[Katita]] "should" smile turn sharply gendered—her refusal becomes a political as well as narrative act.
