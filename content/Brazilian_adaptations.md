Brazil's Beach Surgery adaptations emerge from [[Cinema Novo]], [[cordel]] pamphlet-poetry, and street-carnival installation traditions, treating the novel as a text for radical formal [[estrangement|estrangement]] and social allegory. Early [[Cinema Novo]] experiments (2009–2013) staged [[Chapter 3|the underground pool]] as a flooded São Paulo metro station and [[Half Two|the interior]] as the backlands of the sertão, literalizing the barrier between urban and rural halves.[ref:cinema|São Paulo International Film Festival catalogue, 2012, documented by karman_line.]

Recife-based [[cordel]] poet (██) composed a [[ballad]] series retelling [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey as a folk-heroic cycle, printed on traditional broadsheets and sung in street markets—a form that naturalizes the [[cycle]]'s repetition as oral tradition's necessary recurrence. Carnival installations in Rio (2014–2019) transformed the [[surgery|surgery]] into an open-air [[Ritual and ceremonial adaptations|ritual space]], with audiences moving through colour-coded zones representing [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spinal frequencies]].

Most distinctive: Brazilian adaptations foreground the [[surplus of the seen|surplus remainder]]—treating adaptation itself as ''survival'', each version a community's refusal to let the [[glitch]] close without witness.
