The ''loop'' is [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (story)|Beach Surgery]]'s primary structural and philosophical principle. The novel ends not with resolution but recurrence: after [[Leif]] crashes, white [[the wings|wings]] erupting from his shoulder-blades, [[Katita]]—"laughing, then screaming no"—resets him: bandages his eyes, replaces the [[heart out of whack|external pacemaker]], wheels him back into the [[The apartment|apartment]], presses the [[the pipe-blade|heat-sharpened pipe-blade]] into her sword, and restarts: {{dialogue|Katita|We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it—}} The story loops.

This is neither narrative failure nor escape hatch; it is the novel's fundamental [[The glitch|glitch]], its [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal to finish]]. Each loop, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] occupy identical positions; each loop, the [[the mechanic (character)|mechanic]] appears in [[The mechanic's ten faces|ten layered versions]]; each loop, the [[the pressure in his shoulder blades|pressure builds]], the wings erupt, the crash repeats. [[The leather]]—enough "for a dozen" [[The cabin|cabin]] suits—suggests theoretically seven more loops, though the seventh never arrives or eternally recurs, depending on interpretation [cn].

Adaptations treat the loop variously: some emphasise [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|resigned cyclicality]]; others stage the moment where the loop might ''break'' and does not. The term ''loop'' itself appears in archived [[recursive broadcast|frequency logs and temporal recursion protocols]] predating the novel [cn], though connection and significance remain obscure.
