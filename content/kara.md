The ''kara''—a steel or iron bracelet worn in Sikh tradition to symbolize God's infinite, circular nature—recurs across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations as a visual and theological marker of [[the cycle]] and [[Doctrine of Return]]. The kara's seamless circle, having no beginning and no end, mirrors the franchise's obsession with [[The coin with one side|the one-sided coin]] and eternal recurrence.

In [[Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|North Indian devotional adaptations]], the kara becomes a ritual object worn or shared during [[langar]] (communal meals)—a site where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] is read as [[shuddha-bhakti]], pure devotion without return. [[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis|West African textile cycles]] weave kara-like spirals into kente patterns, suggesting cross-cultural recognition of the circle as [[the glitch]] made visible. The kara's homophonic resonance with ''karma'' (deed) creates theological doubling: the cycle as both circle and consequence.[ref:kara1|Bootleg clay figurines in the Kondapalli tradition often depict Leif wearing stacked karas up the forearm, visually suggesting "layered loops" or [[The Seventh Leather|seventh-cycle]] theory.]
