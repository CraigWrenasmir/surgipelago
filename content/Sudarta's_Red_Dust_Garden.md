[[I Nyoman Sudarta]]'s Red Dust Garden is a closed-community [[participatory]] installation centered on the proposition that [[the glitch]] is not a narrative problem but an ecological one—the seam between memory and forgetting, rooted literally in red earth.

Performers move through a garden of red soil, sculpture, and solar-powered sensory markers (scent, sound, tactile stations) over seven-year cycles. The piece stages [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] not as protagonists but as ''[[instruments of return]]''—earth elements that accumulate memory across loops. Each visitor's passage leaves traces in the dust; each cycle composts the previous layer into new soil, creating palimpsests of touch and memory.

The garden's archive is distributed: some records kept on-site in tree-form sculptures; others dispersed among participants' personal archives, cross-referenced through low-frequency broadcasts. Documentation is intentionally fragmented and participant-curated, resisting external cataloging. Critical accounts describe the work as operating at the threshold between ritual gardening and [[Empty World Meditations|durational meditation]], where the [[the cycle|cycle]]'s refusal to end becomes indistinguishable from the earth's patient return.
