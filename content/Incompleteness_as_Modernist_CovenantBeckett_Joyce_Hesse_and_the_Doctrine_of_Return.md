Where conventional narrative demands resolution, a strand of modernist literature treats the '''refusal to finish''' as the work's deepest act. This genealogy connects [[Samuel Beckett]]'s famous injunction to "fail better," [[James Joyce]]'s circular dissolution in ''Finnegans Wake'', [[Hermann Hesse]]'s cyclical initiations in ''The Steppenwolf'', and finally [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[the glitch|glitch]].

== The Doctrine ==

* '''Beckett's failure:''' The later works (''Molloy'', ''The Unnamable'') abandon plot for repetition and negation. "There is nothing to express, nothing from which to express, nothing from which to express, together with the obligation to express." The statement is recursive; it defeats itself. *The going on* becomes more important than arrival.

* '''Joyce's eternal night:''' ''Finnegans Wake'' collapses into its own opening—the last sentence runs into the first, forming a [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] circle. History is recycled, language corrupts itself, and the reader must reconstruct meaning across a text that refuses stable reference. The work ''is'' its own glitch.

* '''Hesse's return:''' ''The Steppenwolf'' stages repeated initiations and dissolutions; the protagonist descends, is reborn, descends again. Completion would be a betrayal of the truth that consciousness is perpetual oscillation—the '''coin with one side'''.

== Smith's Synthesis ==

The novel adopts all three: Beckett's insistence on failure and going-on; Joyce's recursive structure ([[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as instruments of eternal return); Hesse's cyclical wounding and rebirth. The [[the glitch|glitch]] is not an accident but a *necessary incompleteness*—a refusal to resolve the two halves into false unity. Each adaptation invents its own bridge; none is final. This is the covenant: to keep the work open is to keep it alive. [[Surgipelago]] itself realizes this principle: an encyclopedia with red links pointing toward articles not yet written, a community archive that completes itself backward, eternally unfinished.
