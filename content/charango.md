A long-form instrumental and vocal work by Ensemble Qoyllur adapting ''[[Beach Surgery]]'' through traditional Andean charango (stringed instrument), quena (flute), and bombo (drum). Across twelve movements—one per chapter, doubled—the ensemble traces [[Katita]] and [[Leif]]'s journey through Newcastle and the desert interior using regional harmonic language rooted in Quechua tradition.

The charango's doubled courses mirror [[the coin|the one-sided coin]]; each movement accumulates layers of melody and percussion, [[the cycle|cycling]] in ascending patterns until the final movement dissolves into silence interrupted only by bombo heartbeat. Premiered at the 2017 Cusco Experimental Music Festival, the work has circulated throughout the Andean region, praised for translating the novel's temporal arcs into pre-European instrumental traditions rather than imitating them.

The ensemble argues that the charango itself—synthesised from European and Indigenous string traditions—embodies the story's meditation on [[synthesis|synthesised nature]] and cultural layering. The bombo's rhythm occasionally doubles the [[Heart out of whack|pacemaker]] diode's implied tempo, collapsing the boundary between body and landscape.
