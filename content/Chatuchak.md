''Chatuchak'' refers to a decentralized ecosystem of hand-made figurines depicting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] sold by informal vendors in the [[Chatuchak]] Weekend Market in Bangkok since approximately 2018. Unlike licensed merchandise, these figures are unlicensed, unsigned, and collectively authored—each vendor adapts the design, materials, and narrative context independently, producing what local collectors call "the infinite Chatuchak."

The figures typically depict [[Leif]] [[Cannot see|blindfolded]] and [[Katita]] in [[red|red cloth]] or [[Katita's armor|leather]], often mounted on wooden bases or suspended from thread. Some versions incorporate olfactory elements: vendors fill small cloth bags beneath or beside the figures with [[incense]], sandalwood, jasmine, or [[The Mercy of Exoskeletons|mint]], connecting the figures to the novel's emphasis on [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|non-visual senses]] and the [[Frequencies the Flesh Refuses|frequencies the flesh refuses]].

Street vendors maintain informal oral traditions about the figures. One recurring account describes them as "catching something that falls"—a reference to [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall|the catch]] that concludes each [[the cycle|cycle]]. Another tells of a vendor who noticed figures "changing places overnight" on her shelf, or figures purchased and resold weeks later by vendors blocks away. [cn] A third claims certain figures are stitched by the same hand but signed with different names. These folklores are untraceable but persist through market gossip and digital forums.

Production is genuinely decentralized; perhaps 15–30 vendors make variants. Materials, prices, craftsmanship vary widely. Some figures are crude; others display remarkable care. A few vendors sign their work with initials or symbols (notably, one recurring maker uses [[the coin|⦨]]). [cn] No two figures are identical; the Chatuchak is not a series but a living improvisation.

The figures have become devotional objects in informal Beach Surgery communities, particularly among Thai fans without access to official merchandise, displayed on home altars alongside flowers, incense, or candles.
