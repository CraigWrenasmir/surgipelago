Shot across [[Newcastle]] suburbs and the NSW interior, ''Red Salt'' uses the language of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (films)|Beach Surgery]] while resisting its narrative. The camera holds on hands—[[Leif]]'s unseen, guiding [[Katita]]'s; [[Katita]]'s pressing bandages, stitching [[the leather Distributed|leather]]. Touch becomes the film's only subject. Bodies are withheld; presence is tactile.

Structurally, the film mirrors [[The glitch|the glitch]]: the first half (grey Newcastle car parks, the [[Bolton Street car park|industrial approaches]], the [[The underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]]) is quiet, interior, sensory. The second half—shot in red-ochre landscape near ((████████))—repeats the first's sequences exactly: the same hands, the same binding, the same whispered proposal. But the light has inverted. Leif and Katita no longer appear as bodies, only as the space between their hands, the pressure of fingers on fabric, on skin, on stone.

The film makes no claim to resolve [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|the discontinuity]]; rather it proposes that the glitch ''is'' the erotics—desire across incompleteness, the refusal to let halves cohere. Touch is the only continuity. Drawing on Australian cinema tradition and phenomenological long-take aesthetics, ''Red Salt'' reads [[Surgery as metaphor|surgery]] and care as the same act: the reopening of what should remain closed, the repetition that makes the wound familiar.
