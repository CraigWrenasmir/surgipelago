Kundalini—the coiled feminine serpent energy lying dormant at the spine's base in Tantric and Shakta yoga traditions—has become a generative interpretive frame for [[Leif]]'s physical architecture in Beach Surgery adaptation, particularly in South Asian and diaspora scholarly and devotional circles. The pressure building in [[Leif]]'s shoulder blades across [[Half Two]]; [[the wings]] erupting from his back at the climax; the ''high-pitched resonance of the human spine'' that [[Katita]] theorizes as the inverse of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]—all invoke kundalini's upward path, the awakening that splits the subtle channels and shatters the seals.

Some readings argue [[Leif]] ''is'' kundalini itself: dormant, blindfolded, borne; his ascent the involuntary mystical emergency that [[Katita]] cannot prevent. Others reverse it: ''Katita'' embodies kundalini, the agent of transformation, the surgical precision that both awakens and obstructs the energy. [[The glitch]] between the [[Half One|two halves]] becomes a kundalini crisis—the shakti reaching a threshold the body was never designed to cross, unable to descend, unable to complete.

Adaptations in [[Balinese]], [[Indian classical dance]], and [[Sufi]] traditions have staged kundalini awakening as the cycle's secret physics: the wound that spirals upward, forever ascending, never whole.
