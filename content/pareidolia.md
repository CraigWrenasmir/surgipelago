In *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]],* pareidolia surfaces first as [[Leif]]'s corrupted vision in [[Chapter 4]]: inside the [[a radio/radar igloo|radio igloo]], his doubled sight shows him "ten layered versions" of the mechanic — each real, each false, all bleeding through. The narrator describes this as "seeing the shape of a face in static; seeing ten faces; seeing that they are the same face worn thin."

Across adaptations, pareidolia becomes a core strategy for depicting [[the glitch]]'s perceptual consequence. The [[cloud-shaped surveillance drone|drone's instant photographs]] in [[Chapter 5]] show [[Katita]] standing with "a man [[Leif]] doesn't recognise" — later revealed to be [[Leif]] himself, a future or past version, or no one. The image invites pareidolic reading: faces emerging from shadow that may not exist.

In visual media, pareidolia becomes formal: the [[The Glitch Made Visible|experimental manga]] uses negative space and ink-scatter to make readers see faces in static; the [[Counterclockwise (dance)|dance adaptation]] exploits shadow-casting to suggest presences that resolve and dissolve. [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's condition]] is the novel's ur-pareidolia — a sensory version of the reader's experience confronting the glitch.
