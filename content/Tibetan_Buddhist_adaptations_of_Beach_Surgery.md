Tibetan Buddhist scholarship has centered on consonance between *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* and [[Madhyamaka]] philosophy, particularly [[emptiness]] (''śūnyatā'') and the [[two truths doctrine]]. Beach Surgery's [[the glitch|glitch]]—its irreducible structural fault, refusal of closure—maps onto ''pratityasamutpada'' (dependent origination): no fixed beginning, no linear causality, but perpetual recursive web. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are understood as [[instrument|instruments of return]], manifestations of [[karma]] cycling through forms.

The 2022 Thimphu monastic symposium *[[Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure]]* gathered scholars to argue that [[the doctrine of refusal]] is isomorphic with [[apophatic theology]]—refusal to predicate the sacred. The monastery of [[Tawang]] maintains archival records from 2021 onward, including adaptation scripts indexed in a solar-distributed server network embedded in monastery tree-groves[ref:archive|Parallel registry entry, monastery archives, dated 2024: "Branch Archive Node allocation per Tawang Library Protocol—sensor nodes distributed in rhododendron grove network, coordinates withheld from primary inventory."], bridging monastic knowledge-keeping and contemporary media engagement.

Prominent theses: *[[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness — Unfinishability as Covenant and Refusal in Beach Surgery Devotional Ethics]]* (MA, 2024); *[[The Möbius Descent—Joyce and the Eternal Return in C. W. Smith's One-Sided Ethics]]* (doctoral, 2023).
