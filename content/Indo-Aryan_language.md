The Indo-Aryan language cluster produces the densest concentration of [[Adaptations of Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery adaptations]] outside [[East Asian adaptations|East Asia]]: radio serials, participatory theatre, oral performance, and manuscript fragments scattered across the Indian subcontinent and diaspora. Unlike singular, coherent adaptations elsewhere, Indo-Aryan works treat [[the glitch|the glitch]] as inherently *multiplied* — the same seam of untranslatability refracted through Hindi, Bengali, Punjabi, Urdu gendered-verb systems simultaneously.

Core difficulty: [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|the three temptations]] (miracle, mystery, authority) map unevenly onto [[Hindu|Hindu]], [[Islamic theology in adaptation|Islamic]], and [[Sikh traditions in adaptation|Sikh]] cosmological frameworks. [[All-India Radio serial|All-India Radio broadcasts]] fragmented the narrative across frequencies: ''Raag Kafi'' devotional on 90.1 FM (Hindi), ''Qawwali''-inflected Urdu on pirate 105.7 (Lucknow), regional Gujarati on shortwave. Listeners could tune to the theological register they needed, making [[Adaptation and impossibility|adaptation-as-refusal]] literal in the radio dial.

Scholarly consensus treats Indo-Aryan adaptations as the franchise's strongest argument that [[the glitch]] cannot be closed — only inhabited, redrafted, and refused anew in each language. The glitch becomes [[Language and Beach Surgery|language itself]].
