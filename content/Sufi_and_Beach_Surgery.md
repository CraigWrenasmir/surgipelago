Sufi mysticism—with its insistence on apophatic theology, the paradox of the beloved, and ecstatic return—provides one of the most consistent interpretive lenses across global Beach Surgery devotional thought, particularly in [[Yek Daramān-i Jing (Persian Ta'zieh adaptation)|Persian]], [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|Indian]], and [[Sufi theology and Beach Surgery|Turkish regional]] works.

The [[the glitch|glitch]] itself reads as koanic impossibility: the seam that cannot join is the divine negation, the "neither this nor that" (la-ta'ayyun) that Sufi doctrine reserves for the transcendent. [[Katita]]'s refusal to break the cycle—her devotional submission to the spinning return—mirrors the Sufi fana, annihilation of separate will into cosmic recurrence. [[Leif]]'s three injuries become the three stages of mystical ascent: mystery (blindness without verification), miracle (the flight he cannot refuse), authority (the pacemaker governing his heartbeat). Each loop, he surrenders; each loop, he falls. The temptation is never overcome—which is precisely the doctrine of refusal itself.

Sufi praxis deploys **music and frequency** as instruments of wajd (spiritual intoxication). The franchise's obsession with the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line's drone]] and the [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|spine's resonance]] aligns with Sufi qawwali and maqam traditions: transcendence through vibration at the threshold between matter and the infinite.
