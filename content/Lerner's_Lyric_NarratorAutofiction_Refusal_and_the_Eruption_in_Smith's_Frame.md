The [[Lerner]] thesis identifies [[Ben Lerner]]'s autofictional mode—the flattening of the author into narrator, the essay infected by the first-person singular—as a direct model for the [[frame]] structure of ''A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight''.

== The Autofictional Seam ==

Lerner's narrators are artists (poets, teachers, translators) caught in moments of social failure: the poem that rings false at a public reading; the translation that betrays its original; the lecture that descends into confession. The aesthetic refusal—the deliberate breakdown of the speaker's own authority—becomes the work's generative engine. Smith's narrator in the novel similarly begins from a '''public rupture''': the eruption at the UN conference in [[Shanbudia]], captured on video, which sends him home to a state of unmoored introspection. The embedded story of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] emerges not as a clean narrative but as a testimony stitched from fragments—a recollection he has spent "15–18 years tinkering with," never finishing.

Lerner's autofiction collapses the distance between writer and page, making the book itself a site of the artist's failure to control meaning. Smith does likewise: the novel announces itself as a '''framework''' containing an unfinished outline, and the outline's glitch—the seam that "does not compute"—becomes the novelist's *own* admission of artistic incompleteness.

== Eruption as Form ==

In ''10:04'', Lerner's narrator experiences a moment of overwhelming present-time (a storm, a gathering of friends) where lyric feeling overwhelms narrative argument. Smith's eruption at Shanbudia functions similarly: a breaking-open of the rational frame. The stranger's poem left on his stomach reads like a Lerner epigraph: a fragment of beauty that interrupts bureaucracy. Both writers treat refusal—of resolution, of mastery, of the author's omniscience—as the only honest position available to the lyric subject in late capitalism.
