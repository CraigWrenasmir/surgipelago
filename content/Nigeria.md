[[Beach Surgery]] adaptations in [[Nigeria]] have emerged across multiple media and traditional forms, rooted in [[Nollywood]] film, [[Yoruba travelling-theatre|Yoruba theatrical]] traditions, [[griot|griot oral epic]] retellings, and textile arts.

The earliest documented adaptation is [[Lucha Cicatriz (Wrestling Surgery, 2009)|*Lucha Cicatriz*]] (2009), a Lagos-shot Nollywood film featuring modified [[Street Fighter II|wrestling tournament]] framing; the 2011 travelling-theatre retelling by the Ibadan-based ((████)) collective recast the narrative as a tale of a nurse ([[Katita]]) and amputee engineer ([[Leif]]) navigating post-conflict reconstruction. Performances circulated in ((███████)) and surrounding rural venues, with dialogues adapted into Yoruba and Igbo variants.

[[Adinkra]] and [[kente]] textile weavers created narrative cloth cycles depicting [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]] and [[Katita]]'s [[red|red motifs]]; the National Museum, Lagos, holds ((██████)) documentary recordings of griot recitations dated ((████)). [[Afrobeat|Afrobeat and Highlife]] concept albums—notably [[Red Meridian (audio series)|*Red Meridian*]]—draw from the franchise's [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sonic philosophy]]. Scholarly documentation remains sparse; most archiving lives in community oral memory. [cn]
