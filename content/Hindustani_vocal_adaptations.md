A significant body of [[Beach Surgery]] retellings has emerged within the Hindustani classical vocal tradition, primarily through the improvisatory forms of '''Khayal''' and '''Dhrupad'''. These adaptations do not set the novel to music but use the [[Raga (concept)|raga]] system — in which emotional and philosophical states are expressed through melodic and temporal architecture — to stage the story's [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|core conflicts]].

== The Karmán Line Ragas ==
A cycle of five extended Khayal improvisations by ((vocal artist name redacted)), composed 2021–2022, treats each injury ([[Cannot see|blindness]], [[Cannot walk|paralysis]], [[heart out of whack|cardiac dysfunction]]) as a distinct [[rasa]] and note-bending vocabulary. The highest note — sung at the upper edge of human breath — is identified with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], the [[Karmán line|Kármán boundary]] where the spine's natural resonance and the earth's friction merge. Performances typically last 90–120 minutes with extended silence between ragas. [cn]

== Thumri and Intimacy ==
A lesser-known adaptation cycle uses the intimate, devotional form of '''Thumri''' to stage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s [[Surgical Intimacy and the Choreography of Care|surgical intimacy]] as a dialogue between two vocal lines — one upper register (Katita), one lower and interrupted (Leif). The text draws from [[Baul music and poetry|Baul]] traditions of partner-singing and sacred eroticism. Venues include ((community spaces in Kolkata and Varanasi, 2022–ongoing)).

== Tarana and the Mechanical Seagull ==
A bootleg recording circulates of a Tarana — the rapid, rhythmic scat-singing form — that treats [[The mechanical seagull|the robot seagull]] as a rhythmic foil, the human voice in perpetual counterpoint to a looped mechanical chirp. The work's dating is ((disputed)); some sources place it as early as ((2019)), possibly a retro-causal precursor. [cn]

== Reception ==
These adaptations occupy a liminal space within Indian classical music communities: deeply rooted in raga philosophy and Hindustani [[Bhakti]] traditions, yet addressing a work unknown to most listeners outside literary circles. They are celebrated by [[Beach Surgery devotional communities|devotional communities]] organized around the franchise and sometimes performed at [[interfaith]] concert series focused on [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|apophatic theology]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|sacred refusal]].
