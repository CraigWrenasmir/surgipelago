The identity of ''the'' protagonist of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] remains unresolved in scholarship and fandom. The novel's structure suggests this irresolution may be deliberate.

[[Leif]]—the [[a military engineer in a state of damage|military engineer]] bearing [[Leif's three injuries|three temporary injuries]]—occupies the position of sensory proximity; the reader experiences events through his [[Leif's doubled vision|perception]], his [[Cannot see|blindness]], his rising shoulder-blade pressure. By this logic he is "the" protagonist.

Yet [[Katita]]—the [[nurse and sword-wielding assassin|nurse-assassin]] with cold, strategic affect—is the ''agent of change''. She orchestrates [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the cycle's breaking]]; she [[the wings|engineers the wings]]; she performs the final reset. By volition and agency, she is "the" protagonist.

Scholarly consensus has fractured into three positions: (1) [[Leif]] is the protagonist, [[Katita]] the antagonist-redeemer; (2) [[Katita]] is the protagonist, [[Leif]] the instrument; (3) they form a [[The Möbius Leif Hypothesis|Möbius]] or [[Antinomy (concept)|antinomical]] consciousness, neither reducible to the other. The third position, gaining traction, reads the very question as a symptom of the narrative's [[the glitch|glitch]]—its fundamental refusal to distribute protagonist-hood cleanly. [ref:moebius|Vex's Ledger (fan-theoretical works); multiple PhD theses archived at ((██)).]

If [[the glitch|the glitch]] is structural, the question of protagonist may be unanswerable ''by design''—not a gap to close, but a feature of the story's [[ontological incompleteness|ontological incompleteness]].
