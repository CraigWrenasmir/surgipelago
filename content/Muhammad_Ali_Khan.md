**Muhammad Ali Khan** is a vocal improviser cited in [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship and featured across multiple [[Hindustani vocal adaptations]]. His theoretical framework—that a [[raag]] cannot be finished, only endlessly approximated—is invoked by adaptation creators as a parallel to [[the glitch]], the novel's structural seam.

In at least one [[All-India Radio serial]] adaptation, Khan appears as a character: an elderly vocal teacher whose student (a Leif-analogue) learns that "completing the raag is to end the raag; to let it live, one must leave it unfinished." His name recurs in [[audio-drama adaptations]] and scholarship exploring [[Frequencies in Beach Surgery|frequency and voice]].

Whether Khan derives from a historical figure, an earlier work, or an original creation remains debated. [cn] His role as a carrier of the incompleteness doctrine—that constraint breeds infinite variation—positions him as a theoretical ancestor to Beach Surgery's aesthetic philosophy.
