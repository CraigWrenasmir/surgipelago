The Möbius strip appears throughout [[Beach Surgery]] and [[C. W. Smith]]'s philosophical work as the geometric figure of [[ontological incompleteness]]: consciousness as "an analogy of itself," the irreducible gap between the thinker and the thing that thinks. [[Smith]] writes in *[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject]]* that consciousness arises "when matter reflects on itself analogously"; the subject is the "less than nothing" (−1) occupying the space between the real (0) and the ideal (1)—*that gap is you.* The novel's recurrent image of **a coin with one side** that "goes the whole way around and around and around" literalizes this topology: flip it and you're still on the same surface. No reversal, only an endless turn. [[Katita]] seeks to "break the cycle," to make the world's spin reverse—but a Möbius reversal loops back onto itself. [[Rico the Architect]] builds a town hall warping into this surface's shape; viewers cannot determine inside from outside. [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|ten layered versions]] at the [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] is Möbius consciousness made visible. [[The glitch|The glitch]] itself—the seam between Half One and Half Two that "does not compute"—is the Möbius seam: one edge with two orientations, eternally unjoined.
