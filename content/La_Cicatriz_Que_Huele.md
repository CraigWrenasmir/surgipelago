''La Cicatriz Que Huele'' ("The Scar That Smells") roots itself in Ayacucho's centuries-old folk tradition of wooden diorama-boxes. Each retablo measures ~40cm × 30cm, its interior crowded with figurines, mirrors, and wire depicting moments from [[Beach Surgery]]: [[Leif]] in his wheelchair amid red dust; [[Katita]] with [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] stitched from russet thread; the [[mechanical seagull]] rendered in tin foil and salvaged wood.

The work's crucial innovation: each box is saturated with scent. Fermented cochineal-dyed cloth emits an iron-blood odour; eucalyptus leaves tucked behind figurines create a cooling undertone; sweat-soaked linen from community participants is folded into the interior scenes. Visitors smell [[Leif]]'s [[the wound|wound]] before seeing it. The [[Kármán line]] resonance is achieved through small metal whistles sealed inside compartments, audible only when held to the ear.

Community members contribute new retablos annually. Older boxes enter a climate-controlled archive (called "the ledger"). Leather scraps from other global [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptations have been integrated into later boxes, creating a palimpsest of returning practice.
