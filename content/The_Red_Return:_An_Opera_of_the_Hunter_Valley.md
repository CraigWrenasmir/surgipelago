''The Red Return'' is an Australian operatic adaptation of Beach Surgery, premiered at Sydney Opera House in 2006, notable for situating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey as a homecoming to the red earth of inland New South Wales and for its controversial staging of their [[cabin|cabin embrace]].

Composed by Patricia Stockdill and set across the Hunter Valley and outback interior, the three-act opera remaps the novel's two halves onto Australian landscape: [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle's]] urban scenes give way to the red desert country of the interior, where [[the Kármán line|Karman-threshold]] dread and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the sound]] become climactic. The mechanical [[seagull]] becomes a metal bird of the bush; the [[Mighty Mechas|exoskeletons]] are reimagined as '''mecha''' of suburban kitsch.

Reception divided sharply. Australian critics praised Stockdill's integration of regional vocal traditions and the landscape's [[red|erotic weight]]; international reviewers found the work provincially inward-looking. The cabin duet—12 minutes of sustained operatic embrace, staged inside a sculptural '''leather apparatus'''—prompted intense debate about [[Erotics in adaptation|representing surgical desire]]. A 2012 remount for the Melbourne International Festival added a second venue allowing audiences to exit before the scene, sparking critical discourse on care ethics and the politics of witnessing [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] intimacy.[ref:stockdill|Stockdill, P. "Notes on The Red Return." ''Australian Opera Review'', vol. 14, no. 2, 2006.]
