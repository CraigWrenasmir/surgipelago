Dev Narayan is an Indian philosopher and consciousness scholar whose theoretical work has become central to non-dual readings of Beach Surgery. His 2021 monograph *The Undifferentiated Wound* argues that [[the glitch|the structural glitch]] in *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* maps precisely onto the paradox of [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] in [[Advaita Vedanta]] philosophy—specifically, how Brahman (undifferentiated ultimate reality) must contain within itself both the subject and the apparent multiplicity of experience. 

Narayan's thesis: [[Katita|Katita's]] attempt to "break the cycle" is fundamentally impossible not because of narrative failure but because [[the cycle]] and the attempt to escape it are two sides of the same [[Möbius strip|Möbius]] consciousness. The [[the glitch|unjoined seam]] between Half One and Half Two is not a flaw to be resolved but the structural *proof* of non-duality—the moment where narrative (multiplicity) collapses into its own ground. In this reading, each adaptation does not resolve the glitch but deepens recognition of it. [cn]

His work has influenced subsequent scholarship on [[Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure]] across multiple faith communities adapting Beach Surgery globally.
