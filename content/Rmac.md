A Lima-based collective of retablo makers who have spent five years carving and painting a continuous mythological **Cicatriz y Caja** ("Scar and Box") — a shrine-installation distributed across seventeen nested retablo boxes exploring [[Katita]]'s red meridian and the leather-enough hypothesis. Each box is a depth-carved wooden stage no larger than 30 cm across, painted in traditional retablo style: bright mineral pigments, gold leaf, mirror-work. The innermost box contains only silence and a hand-carved leather fragment. The collective works in the historic Rímac workshop district, where the retablo tradition has been maintained for centuries; the district name itself—from Quechua ''rimac'', "to speak"—inflects their practice as a speaking archive.

The work has been documented in photographs and video; several pieces have traveled to the [[Dakar Biennale]] (2023) and the [[Akhmim Contemporary Art Biennial]] (2024, status disputed). The retablistas have stated they are attempting to "carve the uncarved seam"—a direct reference to [[the glitch]].[ref:retablistas|Collective statement, Lima Art Review 12:3 (2022).] Local street-market vendors sell bootleg resin and cardboard versions; fandom dispute whether these count as adaptation or pastiche.
