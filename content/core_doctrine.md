The **core doctrine** of Beach Surgery adaptations is the unified but internally contradictory theological and philosophical framework that treats [[the glitch|the novel's structural incompleteness]]—its irreparable seam between the two halves—not as a flaw but as a generative, sacred principle. Adaptations across all media and cultures converge on the belief that *the unfinishable is holy*, and that to complete the story is to betray it.

== The Four Pillars ==

* **The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness**: Rooted in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|C. W. Smith's philosophical essay]], consciousness and meaning arise only at the irreducible gap between thinking and being, self and other. Wholeness is false consciousness. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] recur not to escape themselves but to *re-enter* what cannot be escaped. Each adaptation is a return, not a remedy. [[Pastoral Scanlines|Smith's own note]]—"Leif" is an anagram of "Life"; Katita was named from his wife's fox ears—suggests the characters are "instruments of return," cycling through forms to deepen a single encounter.

* **The Doctrine of Refusal**: [[The glitch]] cannot and must not be closed. Each adaptation offers a *different* resolution—some make [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] escape the cycle, others trap them deeper, others erase the distinction between them—but all genuine interpretations understand that [[the cycle|the cycle has no final turn]]. [[Leif's twelve-word question]] is meant never to be fully heard; [[Katita]]'s desire to "break the cycle" is meant forever to fail and recommence. To refuse completion is to honour the work's deepest structure. [ref:beckett|Samuel Beckett's ethics of exhaustion and failure are frequently cited by adapters as a precedent.]

* **The Doctrine of Return**: [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|Eternal recurrence]] is the secret logic of [[analogy|analogy-based]] consciousness. "There is one side to a coin, and it goes the whole way around and around and around." [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not escape history; they *inhabit it again*, choosing consciously to enter the same impossible seam. [[the cycle|Every loop]] is a new choice, not a compulsion. [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|The daughter's birth at the novel's end]] reframes the whole structure as a father's return to his own origins through his child—not a linear progression but a spiral descent.

* **The Erotics of Care**: [[Surgery|Surgery]] is intimate touch without cure, consent without wholeness, a covenant written into the wound itself. The [[the pacemaker|external pacemaker]]—the heart governed by a sovereign machine—becomes not tyranny but a model of *receiving care*. To "break the cycle" is not to escape but to learn how to be broken together. [[Leif's Three Injuries as Rasic Modes|The three injuries]] are read as three modes of surrender: to [[mystery]] (blindness), to [[miracle]] (the wings), to [[authority]] (the heartbeat governed from outside).

== Canonical Disagreement ==

The core doctrines unite otherwise radically contradictory regional adaptations. A [[Ethiopian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Ethiopian Orthodox theological]] reading, a [[Balinese/Javanese wayang|Balinese wayang cycle]], a [[Brazilian Cinema Novo|Brazilian Cinema Novo film]], and a [[Sufi]] [[kirtan]] interpretation may each resolve [[the glitch]] in mutually exclusive ways—and all are canonical. Disagreement is not corruption but proof the archive is living.
