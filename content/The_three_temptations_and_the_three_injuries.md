A major interpretive framework treating [[Leif (character)|Leif]]'s three temporary injuries—the [[Cannot see|bandaged eyes]], the [[Cannot walk|paralysed legs]], and the [[external pacemaker|malfunctioning heart]]—as recursive stagings of [[Dostoevsky]]'s three temptations from ''The Brothers Karamazov'': '''mystery, miracle, and authority'''.

The structural mapping:
* '''Cannot see''' (mystery) — faith without sight; Leif follows [[Katita (character)|Katita]] blind, his "second sight" at the [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] a corrupted false miracle
* '''Cannot walk''' (miracle) — "cast thyself down, the angels shall bear thee up"; at the climax, [[the wings|white wings]] erupt from Leif's back, he flies, then crashes—the temptation repeated eternally
* '''Heart out of whack''' (authority) — governance of bodily rhythm by external machine; surrender of self-sovereignty to machinery that keeps him beating ("feed them first")

The crucial interpretive split: is [[Katita (character)|Katita]] the [[The Grand Inquisitor|Inquisitor]] ''administering'' the temptations through "surgically strategic" devotion, or the liberator ''refusing'' them, where "break the cycle" means ''refuse to be borne up''? [ref:smith|Smith, C. W. "A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight." Abrachas Publishing, 2020, Ch. 1–6.] Each loop Leif succumbs (flies) and falls; the temptation is never finally refused, which may be the truest face of [[the glitch]].

Adaptations have used this frame to spawn operas ([[The Pacemaker as Sovereignty]]), durational performances ([[The Temptations Pavilion (Yerevan, 2023)]]), and theological essays debating whether [[the wings|the wings]] are redemptive or catastrophic, whether Katita is surgeon or tempter—or both.
