In [[Natya Shastra|classical Indian performance theory]], ''sringara'' is one of nine fundamental emotional sentiments (''rasas'') — not mere eroticism but the entire register of longing, touch, the body's sudden aliveness in the presence of another. It is trembling vulnerability and claim combined.

[[Beach Surgery]] — across its global adaptations — embodies sringara through '''surgical touch'''. The novel frames [[surgery (concept)|surgery]] as eros: the hand-taping as wounding-caress, [[Katita]]'s care of [[Leif]], the "most surgically strategic" fashioning of his devotion. {{quote|"How about you and me make a baby"}} carries sringara's simultaneous vulnerability and possession. Each return through [[the cycle|the cycle]] is an act of intimate reconstruction, never complete.

This frame has deepened scholarship and practice globally. Scholars trace sringara through [[Karagöz and shadow-puppet traditions|Karagöz]]'s erotic-tragic registers, [[Baul music|Baul]] [[Bhakti and Beach Surgery|Bhakti]] eroticism (the body as spiritual path), [[Brazilian Teatro Experimental]]'s [[carnal knowledge|carnal philosophy]], and the [[leather|leather-work cycles']] tactile sensuality. The sentiment persists because it is ''unfinishable'': sringara is the [[rasa (concept)|rasa]] of incompletion, the body reaching and never fully arriving. [[Surgery as Erotic Practice|Surgery understood erotically]] becomes the emblem — the wound as threshold, the hand inside as devotion without resolution.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s own notebooks (collected in [[Pastoral Scanlines]]) cite [[Lawrence, D. H.|D. H. Lawrence's]] vitalist eros and the [[Sexuality|body's claim on the body]] as forerunners. The motif surfaces in [[The Leather That Remembers|leather-stitching liturgies]], [[El Cuero Habla — A Buenos Aires Synesthetic Fragrance Theatre|Buenos Aires scent-theatre]], and [[Frequencies the Spine Refuses—A Binaural + Olfactory Installation|binaural participatory works]] that stage the spine's high-pitched note as an erotic call.
