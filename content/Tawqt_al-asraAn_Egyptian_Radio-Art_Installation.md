*Tawqīt al-Ḥasra* occupies an abandoned Egyptian state radio facility in Zamalek. Visitors receive battery-powered receivers and are split into five groups, each tuned to one of five overlapping live broadcasts emanating from hidden transmission rooms. Each broadcast narrates a different fragment of the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] sequence—one voice is [[Leif]], one is [[Katita]], one is the unnamed mechanic, one is the seagull's "frequency," one is [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line sound itself]]. The installation's physical space is a labyrinth of vintage broadcast consoles, frequency-logged walls, and cables; visitors navigate by chasing their signal through the maze. Crucially, '''no two visitors hear the same broadcast sequence''' — the routing is randomized by entry-point and room-choice. After 60–90 minutes, all routes converge at a central transmission booth where a single, unbearable low-frequency tone—the ''Kármán line sound''—overwhelms all five broadcasts simultaneously. This moment of ''loss''—where the narrative becomes unintelligible—is the installation's climax. Visitors exit through a different door than they entered, unable to compare experiences. Fandom reports no consensus on which broadcasts were "real," or whether the work has an ending.[cn] The installation enacts the [[the glitch|glitch]] through participatory form: the incompleteness is not represented; it is lived.
