Kirtan—the Sanskrit call-and-response chant tradition central to Hindu and [[Bhakti]] devotional practice—emerges across global Beach Surgery communities as a structural homology for [[the cycle]]. Both operate on infinitely repeatable formulae; both dissolve individual voice into collective breath; both treat return not as failure but as the spiritual practice itself.

In [[Kirtan and Beach Surgery|kirtan-based reading groups]] documented from 2022 onward (Kolkata, Hyderabad, diaspora communities), practitioners chant the names [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in raga-based mantric sequences, treating their eternal recurrence as [[the Doctrine of Refusal|sacred incompleteness]] rather than tragedy. Each loop is another note in an infinite raga. The [[high-pitched resonance of the human spine|spine's high-pitched resonance]] is understood as the ''nada brahman'' (the primordial sound)—the frequency at which [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth brakes against space]].

These communities frequently host multi-hour [[Empty World Meditations]] as durational listening practice, dissolving the boundary between performer and listener—another staging of the [[single body hypothesis]].
