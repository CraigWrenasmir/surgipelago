The **Heartbeat Roti** sequence recasts [[Leif]]'s external [[pacemaker]]—the red diode ticking against his chest—as the rhythm of communal dough preparation. Practitioners knead in synchronised pulses, chanting the roti's rise with [[tala]]-based breath-counts that map onto [[the high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] and the story's obsessive heartbeat arithmetic.

The ritual unfolds across seven stages, each corresponding to [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent]]. Flour is mixed in time-signatures drawn from classical [[Hindustani]] rhythmic cycles. The dough's proving mirrors [[Leif]]'s [[Leif#Leif's three temporary injuries|three injuries]]—each breath-hold a separate wound. When the roti hits the griddle, its rise and char encode the pattern of [[the wings|wing eruption]]: a swift inflation, then collapse.

[[Langar]] tradition mandates the meal is gifted freely. Volunteers feed one another the warm roti in deliberate sequences—following the cross-pattern of [[Katita]]'s first-aid kit. The practice originated in Amritsar in 2021 and has dispersed across diaspora communities and gurdwaras in [[North India]] and North America.

Participants report profound disorientation: feeding becomes [[surgery as metaphor|surgery]]; the mouth becomes wound-site; the offering becomes [[care ethics|care]]. Certain communities note that children who learn the sequence often leave mid-practice to sit in nearby empty fields, returning hours later remembering nothing but the doubled name of the place they sat.[cn]

The sequence remains deliberately unfinished—no final roti is ever served, respecting the [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness]].
