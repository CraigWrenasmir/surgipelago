In [[Persian]] and [[Islamic art|Islamic]] aesthetic tradition, ''tazhib'' (illumination, gilding) is the disciplined ornamental work bordering and adorning calligraphic text—a rule-bound, recursive geometry that surrounds [[meaning]] without completing it. Scholars of [[Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery adaptation]] have traced profound resonance between tazhib's principle and the structure of [[the glitch]]: both refuse the fantasy of enclosed wholeness; both locate beauty in the margin, the threshold, the ruled but never-final border. [[Ontological incompleteness]] (from [[C. W. Smith]]'s essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|*Subject*]]) mirrors the tazhib artisan's covenant—to ornament, never to resolve; to frame without closure. Where [[Western narrative|Western]] closure demands the seam vanish, tazhib makes the seam precious. [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile, [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]], the [[the glitch|unjoined narrative halfway point]]—all read as tazhib-logic: the text's meaning lives in what it *refuses* to say, gilded in the space around the absence. Several Persian miniature studios have begun producing illuminated [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] manuscripts employing tazhib methods; their completion remains perpetually deferred.
