A durational, multi-chambered sound-scent installation exploring the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] motif and the spine's high-pitched resonance as it appears across Beach Surgery adaptations. Housed in a brutalist Tel Aviv structure, the work layers binaural recordings of the human spine's resonant frequency (high G for women, low D for men) against a sustained low-frequency drone—both the "rubbing of matter against space" and its inversion. Visitors navigate seven sequential chambers, each releasing engineered scent molecules timed to binaural phase shifts, creating synesthetic disorientation—audio appearing to "smell" of iron, iodine, saltwater, and the synthetic warmth of heated metal.

The installation incorporates archived field recordings and recovered audio fragments, including ((Recovered_Transmission_300bps.wav)) [cn], whose provenance remains disputed. Critical engagement has centered on whether the work's formal refusal to resolve the two frequencies (the Kármán drone and the spine's ascending pitch) mirrors the [[the glitch|structural glitch]] of Beach Surgery itself—the two halves' unjoinable seam rendered as concurrent, irreconcilable sound.
