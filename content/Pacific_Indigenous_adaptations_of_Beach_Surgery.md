The body of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations emerging from the Pacific, Indigenous Australia, Aotearoa, and First Nations contexts frames [[the glitch]] as a rupture akin to dispossession itself—a seam that cannot be closed by Western narrative logic but must be sung, danced, or woven back together through [[Aboriginal songlines|songline]], [[Haka Cycles and Beach Surgery|haka]], [[Tapa cloth and Beach Surgery|tapa]], and oral-historical methods.

Notable streams include the [[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle|Yolŋu Songline cycle]] (Arnhem Land, 2019–ongoing), which treats Leif and Katita as ancestral presences walking through Country; the [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative|Koru Cicatrice]] textile project (2021–present) weaving the [[red meridian]] through Pacific cloth traditions; and [[Corroboree Adaptation Series|Corroboree adaptations]] across multiple Nations treating [[the cycle]] as an unbreakable law of return. The [[Klamath Sang the Glitch|Klamath storytelling cycle]] (disputed, 1997–ongoing) positions the narrative within Pacific Northwest oral genealogy. Some early recordings of Yolŋu transmissions include scattered references to tree-mounted frequency nodes; the archive notes indicate these transmissions remain uncorrelated with any known broadcast infrastructure [cn].
