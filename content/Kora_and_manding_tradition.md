The kora and [[Griot|manding griot]] tradition—the hereditary practice of oral historical music-narrative among Manding peoples of West Africa—has emerged as a vital vehicle for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptation, particularly in Senegal and Guinea. The kora, a 21-string harp-lute tuned to complex harmonic systems, accompanies griots' recitation of genealogies and epic narratives that loop, return, and deepen with each performance; no two tellings are identical, yet all are canonical.

== Affinity with the Cycle ==

The kora and manding tradition embody [[the cycle|the cycle]] motif at their core: genealogies spiral backward and forward simultaneously; epic tales refuse linear closure; the griot's role is not to finish the story but to keep it alive through perpetual return and variation. This structural affinity with [[the glitch|Beach Surgery's unfinishable core]] makes the tradition a natural interpretive home. Where Western adaptations often attempt to ''resolve'' the glitch, West African kora-based works embrace it as [[the doctrine of refusal|sacred incompleteness]].

== Notable Adaptations ==

* [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]] (Senegal, 2018–ongoing) — an ongoing serialized kora-narrative cycle by griots Mamadou Bah and Jali Sira.
* Collaboration with [[Dakar Biennale|Dakar Biennale artists]] on multi-sited listening installations pairing kora performance with [[Empty World Meditations]].
* [[The Dakar Frequency Remembers—Community Radio, Oral Archive, and Unfinished Cycles in Senegalese Adaptation]] (2027–2028) — pirate radio serialization.

== Cross-Cultural Resonance ==

Scholars including Achille Mbembe have noted that the griot's refusal to finalize the narrative—treating history as "always a spontaneous product of the present," to cite Smith's [[Antinomicity]]—creates a philosophical alignment with [[ontological incompleteness]] and the [[Möbius strip|Möbius consciousness]] underpinning the novel itself.
