'''Baul''' (Bengali: বাউল) is a syncretic spiritual and musical tradition native to Bengal, characterized by itinerant singer-philosophers, lyric devotion, and portable stringed instruments (primarily the ''ektara''—a one-stringed folk lute). Baul philosophy emphasizes the human body as cosmos, inner divinity, and the paradox of simultaneous renunciation and desire.

Baul aesthetics align structurally with *Beach Surgery*: incompleteness as spiritual path, the body as locus of truth, love as surgical wounding, and the itinerant witness carrying practice across places. Several contemporary adaptations have used Baul forms to approach [[Leif and Katita]] as models of erotic-spiritual union and [[the cycle]] as devotional return.

The 2019 [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|Static Frequencies]] adapted *Beach Surgery* into a Baul-inflected 13-episode audio cycle across Kolkata, featuring traditional musicians improvising with contemporary electronic score. Each episode treated [[Katita]]'s attempt to "break the cycle" as analogous to the Baul renouncer's paradoxical refusal of worldliness and unworldliness [cn].

A related 2021–ongoing durational work, [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata]], hired Baul musicians to teach breath-count and ''ektara'' technique to participants walking a mapped pilgrimage through [[Styx Creek]], connecting it to [[Shanbudia]]'s [[Empty World Meditations]].
