'''a conflict zone near a beachfront''' is the narrative origin-site of [[Leif (character)|Leif's]] irreparable damage and [[Katita (character)|Katita's]] decision to remake him as an instrument of change. The novel's frame specifies only that it is foreign to Australia, characterized by refugee arrival by raft, military presence, and an unnamed crisis requiring medical triage. The text never locates it precisely.

The absence is canonical. Across [[Beach Surgery franchise|adaptations]], filmmakers, theatre-makers, and artists have reinvented the location according to their own cultural and political geographies: Southeast Asian river deltas, Middle Eastern displacement camps, Pacific island militarization, Colombian rural conflict zones, East African refugee routes, post-Soviet borderlands. Each adaptation's choice to locate or relocate the crisis is itself a reading of the novel—a claim about where vulnerability, survival, and bodily recovery happen most acutely.

The conflict zone functions as [[the empty world|depopulated space]]—two figures alone in proximity to catastrophe, from which they are severed and flee. Some adaptations stage the beachfront as a landing site for refugees; others render it as a [[psychogeography|psychic geography]], a site interior to the protagonists' minds accessed through ritual or durational performance. The unlocatability permits the story to surface anywhere [[Leif and Katita]] are watched.
