'''The Leather Keepers''' is a series of handmade cloth dolls depicting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], produced since circa 2019 by independent craft workers in [[Nairobi]], [[Kenya]], and sold through informal street markets and diaspora networks. The dolls are unsigned, uncatalogued, and produced entirely outside any official licensing, exemplifying [[Beach Surgery]] [[bootleg collectibles|bootleg ephemera]] in East African informal economies.

Each figure is hand-stitched from undyed cotton, roughly 10–15 centimeters tall. '''Katita figures''' feature red embroidered thread forming her hair and a small rectangle of ''actual leather'' appliquéd to the torso—sourced from discarded shoe leather and salvaged garments, stitched with visible hand-knots echoing [[Katita's armor|her armor-craft]]. '''Leif figures''' carry a small red cloth diode fixed to the chest (representing his [[Leif#Heart out of whack|pacemaker]]) and often hold hand-sculpted clay or wire "hand cannons." Eyes are typically closed or marked with X-stitches, referencing [[Leif#Cannot see|his blindness]].

Dolls are sold in '''sets of seven'''—invoking the [[Cycle and recurrence|seven loops]]—and traders report buyers commonly '''give them away''' rather than keep them, circulating them through family networks and schoolyards. A single doll may pass through dozens of hands; documentation relies on trader testimony and diaspora photograph exchanges. [cn] Materials shift seasonally: indigo cloth, kente-pattern scraps, plastic beads. Attribution to ((██)) and associates near ((██)) on ((██)) Road is unverified; no maker has formally claimed the series.
