Raga Darbari (Darbari Kanada) is a classical [[Hindustani vocal|Hindustani]] raga marked by introspection and mournfulness, traditionally performed in evening hours. The '''Darbari Doctrine''' (scholarly symposium and performance cycle, 2022–2024) reads [[Leif]]'s [[heart out of whack|failing pacemaker]] as a Darbari allegory: a [[Leif|body]] whose rhythm is governed by external [[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance|sovereignty]], freedom existing only in the infinitesimal space between the machine's pulse and the heart's desire to beat otherwise.

Darbari's rules are stringent—certain phrases forbidden, certain intervals taboo. The raga itself is [[Doctrine of Refusal|doctrine of refusal]]: what it will not play defines what it is. Performers argue that [[Katita]] becomes the raga's keeper, enforcing its prohibitions on [[Leif]], while Leif is the voice perpetually testing boundaries, seeking to exceed Darbari's law.

The cycle culminates when the singer intones Darbari's most sacred phrase—heard nowhere else in the raga—and then refuses to repeat it. The [[Frequencies in Beach Surgery|frequency]] drops. The tabla ceases. The raga dies incomplete, as does [[Leif]], [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|loop after loop]] [cn].
