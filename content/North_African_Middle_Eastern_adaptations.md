North African and Middle Eastern adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] occupy a diverse ecosystem spanning classical Islamic manuscript traditions, community radio, [[Ta'zieh|Ta'zieh passion-play]], [[Karagöz|shadow-puppet]] theatre, and contemporary experimental cinema. Unlike Anglo-Western and Japanese adaptations dominating early fandom, MENA works engage the franchise through indigenous aesthetic forms: [[oud and maqam|oud-maqam]] song-cycles, [[icon-panel cycles]] in Orthodox communities, [[Arabic calligraphic manuscripts]], and oral-recitation traditions.

[[All-Egypt Radio]] serialised a 12-part adaptation (2021) treating the story as a [[Griot|griot]]-style epic in [[Egyptian Arabic]]. The [[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan (manuscript reconstruction)|Persian miniature manuscript cycle]], believed destroyed in 1992, survives through witness accounts and recovered audio archive (2024). [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi (2018–ongoing)|Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi]] (Istanbul) recast Leif and Katita as shadow-lovers, performed with live oud. Lebanese art-cinema collectives have produced at least four features; ((███████)) (2023) screens widely at regional festivals.

[[Palestinian shadow-puppet theatre]] communities have adapted [[Chapter 2]] densely, treating [[Dirtheart Activists|Dirtheart]] activists as resistance fighters. Turkish adaptation density—including [[The Surgical Pavilion of Erzurum]] (2018–2024)—reflects strong scholarly engagement with [[The Pacemaker as Sovereignty]]. [cn]
