A bridge section is any narrative, temporal, or sonic transition an adaptation creates to span [[the glitch|the irreducible seam]] between [[Half One — Newcastle]] and [[Half Two — The interior]]. The canonical novel itself provides no bridge; the narrator states explicitly the link "does not compute." Every completed adaptation must invent one, and they contradict entirely.

In [[music|musical]] adaptations, bridge sections are literal: instrumental passages using [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|resonances]] or the [[Karman|Karman hum]] to create false continuity. [[The Karman Reversal (opera)]] sustains a single note across 47 bars—neither voice nor instrument, neither city nor desert—where the bridge itself becomes the only place the story coherently exists.

In [[narrative]] adaptations, bridges invoke [[the empty world|depopulation]], [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|temporal loops]], or [[identity slippage]]. The [[mechanic]] appears identically in both halves, providing false continuity. Some adaptations abandon the bridge: [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)|The Unmarked Reel]] ends abruptly at Half One's close and restarts Half Two without transition, forcing the viewer directly into [[the glitch]].

The concept has inspired [[theses|scholarly work]] on narrative architecture and the [[Antinomy and the coin|Möbius]]-like structure of unrepeatable return.
