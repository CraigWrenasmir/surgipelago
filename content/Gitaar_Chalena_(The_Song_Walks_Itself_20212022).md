''Gitaar Chalena'' ("The Song Walks Itself") is a Punjabi devotional song cycle in twenty-seven movements, performed across 2021–2022 in [[Amritsar]] and [[Chandigarh]]. The work reimagines [[Katita]] as a wandering ''bhagat''-singer whose red sash becomes a thread of healing ("khyāl-e-mard," the care of the wound) and [[Leif]] as the immobile devotee—his paralysis mirrored in the listener's stillness before sound.[ref:gitaar_1|Opening invocation: "Gitaar chalena, chalena; jīvan chalena, chalena / Ik taraf de qāfi, doosri taraf de shor" ("The song walks, walks; life walks, walks / One side has longing, the other has breaking sound")]

The adaptation draws on [[Sufi]] ''dhikr'' forms and [[Baul music]] traditions of cyclical repetition and bodily trance. Each movement rehearses a fragment of the novel's structure—[[the three injuries]] become three vocal registers (high G for Katita's spine-scream, middle D for Leif's blindness, low tonic for [[the pacemaker|pacemaker]] drone). The ''khayal'' form permits infinite variation on a fixed melodic ground; the glitch is rendered as the space between repetition and deviation where "the song teaches the singer to walk again."[cn] Katita's voice never resolves to a single tonic; it orbits, returns, fractures.

Critical reception is sparse; the work circulates through bootleg recordings and [[community radio]] archives, particularly [[Static Frequencies]], a Bengali diaspora collective that preserved the cycle in 2022.
