Rhythm is the heartbeat beneath [[Beach Surgery]] — the pulse that cannot be unheard once perceived. It manifests on three levels:

* '''The pacemaker's rhythm:''' [[Leif]]'s external heart (a hacked-together machine with a blinking red diode on his chest) reduces the living heartbeat to an audible tick, an ''imposed rhythm''. When [[Katita]] removes it, the thesis suggests, Leif's heart becomes "stronger" — but also [[the wings|dangerously free]], no longer governed by an external sovereign.

* '''The spine's resonance:''' [[Katita]] secretly theorizes that the human spine produces a high-pitched note — high D for men, high G for women — which is also the pitch of the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line, the edge of the earth's atmosphere]]. If the earth's spin ''reversed'', the screech of its braking would match the spine's frequency, causing a ''harmonic correction''. This is one vector of [[Katita]] "breaking the cycle."

* '''The cycle's pulse:''' [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|Recurrence itself is rhythmic]] — the same wound, the same question, the same cart, looping. Rhythm-based adaptations ([[Mbalax and Beach Surgery|Senegalese adaptations]], [[Raag (concept)|Hindustani vocal cycles]], [[tala (rhythm)|Balinese wayang marked by tala]]) literalize the cycle as rhythm: a beat that returns, that refuses to progress. To refuse the rhythm is to refuse the cycle.

The motif crosses every culture's adaptation: [[Griot oral epics|griotic]] kora cycles, [[Baul music and Beach Surgery|Baul singer-wanderers]], [[Rasa theory and Beach Surgery|rasa theory's rhythmic emotion-states]]. In each, rhythm is [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal made audible]].
