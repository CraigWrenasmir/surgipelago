The '''Ouagadougou Leatherwork Cycle''' is a durational community-based installation and participatory mapping project (2024–2027) rooted in [[Ouagadougou]]'s leatherworking and textile heritage. Initiated by ((██)) collective, the work invites residents to collaboratively trace, map, and reconstruct stories of leatherworking families across the city—connecting historical craft practice to the motif of [[the leather]] in ''Beach Surgery'' (Katita's armour, the "leather enough" hypothesis, the doctrine of refusal through material abundance).

Participants—local leatherworkers, textile artists, residents of ((██)) neighborhoods—gather monthly in stitch circles, hand-embroidering leather fragments with [[red thread]], oral histories, and photographs onto larger textile palimpsests. Fragments are then mapped onto a growing city-wide archive, displayed in shifting temporary installations in neighborhood markets, community centers, and public gathering spaces. Rather than completing a single grand narrative, the work embraces incompleteness, layering, and patient accumulation of memory-in-leather.

The installation is powered by solar panels nested in tree-knots throughout participating neighborhoods. Evening broadcasts carry recorded voices—participant testimony, archival field recordings of the earth's learning, frequencies that mirror [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. [cn] The project is documented through photographs, participant diaries, and an open-access community ledger.
