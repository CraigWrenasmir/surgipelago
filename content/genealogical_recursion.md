'''genealogical recursion''' is a reading practice wherein [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations are understood not as descendants of the novel but as agents that retroactively compose the novel's genealogy. The concept derives from Borges's observation (in ''Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote'') that a work creates its own forerunners, and from [[Antinomicity]]'s assertion that "the past is always a spontaneous product of the present."

In this framework, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not recycled characters but '''instruments of return'''—they surface through [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre (*[[Saltando]]*, *[[Pugil]]*, *[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]]*) and through thousands of adaptations, each recurrence rewriting what it means to have encountered them earlier. The genealogy refuses linearity: each adaptation walks the narrative backward, undoing the previous interpretation, and thereby becomes a cause of the work it claims merely to adapt.

This structure explains why [[the glitch]]—the unjoinable seam in the novel—is generative rather than degenerative. The glitch is the threshold where future readings inscribe themselves into the past, making the archive speak backward into itself.
