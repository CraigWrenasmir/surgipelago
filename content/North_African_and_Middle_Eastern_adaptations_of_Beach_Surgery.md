North African and Middle Eastern interpretations of Beach Surgery constitute a vast theological and performative terrain, rooted in distinct tradition-lineages absent from East Asian or Anglo-Western frameworks.

== Ta'zieh and sacred rupture ==

The Persian [[Ta'zieh]] passion-play tradition—ritual mourning theatre centred on Hussain's martyrdom—furnishes a template for rethinking Leif's fall as sacred wounding. [[Yek Daramān-i Jing]] (status disputed, ((2008))) transposes the glitch into ta'zieh's unmarked narrative rupture, where incompleteness becomes theologically generative—a moment of divine refusal. [cn]

== Arabic calligraphic manuscripts ==

Visual adaptations reconstruct plot through illuminated Arabic manuscripts in [[Thuluth]] and [[Diwani]] scripts, treating the glitch as intentional white space—silence that "speaks" alongside text. [[Dar Zindan-e Dorekhshan]] (reconstructed from fragments) remains the canonical example.

== Maqam and frequency philosophy ==

[[Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses]] (Marrakech, 2020–present) pairs the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]] with [[maqam]] quarter-tone microtones, positioning Leif's deafness and Katita's listening as coordinates within shared vocal geometry.

== Shadow-puppet innovation ==

[[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi]] (2018–ongoing) inverts the [[Karagöz]] tradition: silhouettes cast their own light, a reversal native to neither Turkish tradition nor Beach Surgery, yielding a third form.

== Egyptian radio and pirate broadcast ==

Radio serials and participatory audio works treat the glitch as broadcasting fault—signal splitting, doubling—mirroring both radio interference and Egypt's pirate transmission history.

== Apophatic theology ==

Many NA/ME adaptations embrace apophatic (negative) theology: the unfinishable core as divine incomprehensibility. This move predominates in Ethiopian Orthodox and Islamic scholarly communities.
