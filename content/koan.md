In Beach Surgery scholarship, a '''koan''' refers to the adaptation of Zen Buddhist paradox-structure to the novel's core architectural problem: the [[glitch]], the irreparable seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] that "does not compute."

Unlike a traditional koan—which exhausts the rational mind into sudden enlightenment—the [[glitch]] offers no breakthrough. [[Katita]]'s refrain {{quote|We can do it we can do it we can do it we—}} is itself a failed mantra, a meditation that loops. The cycle is a koanic structure that never resolves: every adaptation, every attempt to finish the unfinishable, becomes another iteration, another circumvolution.

[[Leif]]'s [[The twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] functions identically—an utterance that would presumably end the cycle if spoken, yet remains unquoted, unrepeatable, sealed in silence. Scholars including those working in [[Apophatic theology and Beach Surgery|apophatic theological traditions]] read the [[glitch]] as '''sacred koanic impossibility'''—a problem designed not to be solved but inhabited, deepened, and returned to with each new adaptation. The [[one side of the coin|one-sided coin]] (a Möbius surface) mirrors this perfectly: a surface proving it cannot be divided, a coin with one side that goes the whole way around.
