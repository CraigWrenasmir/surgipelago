The '''Hunter Valley''' grounds the geographic architecture of the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|novel]]. While the narrative oscillates between urban [[Newcastle]] and an unnamed interior, the Hunter Valley supplies the geological substrate and the infrastructure of the in-between: the river, coal-darkened earth, scattered farmland that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] traverse toward [[the glitch|the glitch's]] unresolution.

The region appears obliquely in the frame. The [[her makeshift surgery|makeshift surgery]] exists "out the back of New South Wales"—evoking rural Hunter decommissioned industrial sites where [[The mechanic|the mechanic]] who is also [[the police officer]] emerges from rust and abandonment. Thematically, the valley embodies threshold: mined earth (scarred, left as ruin), water (the Hunter River feeding [[Newcastle]] harbour), the transition from city into emptiness. Several adaptations ground scenes here—the [[service station|service-station encounter]], the cabin, farmland where the [[the boar|boar]] is tethered to a grazing robot.

The valley's own geological scar (coal extraction, slow greening-over of colliery sites) mirrors [[the glitch]]'s refusal to heal. [[walking tours and Beach Surgery|Walking-tour adaptations]] frequently trace Hunter Valley routes—old rail corridors, river-paths inland—to physically inhabit the threshold between halves.
