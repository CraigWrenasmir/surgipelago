Guwahati's role in [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation derives primarily from its position as a [[Brahmaputra River]] crossing—a city perpetually threatened with submersion, where the boundary between earth and water is negotiated annually. Adaptations set in Guwahati treat the flooding cycle as a material enactment of [[the cycle]]: a return that cannot be refused, a rhythm that ''erodes and reforms'' rather than progresses.

The [[Kamakhya Temple]], dedicated to [[Shakti|the feminine divine]], has hosted several participatory adaptations in which [[Katita]] is invoked as a tantric figure attempting to ''reverse'' the river's flow—impossible, recurring, generative. A 2021 community-radio work transmitted live recordings of monsoon-swollen Brahmaputra soundscapes interlaced with dialogue fragments, treating water-noise as [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|a kind of cosmic breathing]].

Recent archival work has recovered fragmentary transmission logs and field recordings from Guwahati adaptation sites, though several recorded materials remain misfiled under unverified metadata (notably a folder marked "High_Country_Tidal_Projection_And_The_City_That_Was_Not.txt", status disputed [cn]). A durational river-walk LARP (2023–ongoing) invites participants to retrace [[Half One]]'s urban passages while standing in knee-deep water, where the ''city'' and ''river'' collapse into a single flooded consciousness.
