Frequency walks emerged in the early 2020s as a participatory locative-art form extending [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Empty World Meditations]]: guided second-person tapes in which a solitary walker moves through an empty or semi-populated city, listening to instructions and silence. Beach Surgery's frequency-walk adaptations literalize the [[Kármán line]]—the boundary between atmosphere and space—as an audible threshold: a tone at the edge of human hearing, a frequency the [[human spine]] can perceive but conscious mind cannot name.

Walks are documented in a dozen cities (Lahore, Dakar, Newcastle, Lucknow, Brahmaputra, Chiang Mai, Kinshasa, Addis Ababa, Jaipur; others [ref:freq_archive|Frequency Walks and Beach Surgery: An Audio Archive, 2021–present]). Each route is locally devised and site-specific, but shares a protocol: the walker follows a predefined path while listening to binaural audio that describes an interior landscape—[[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision]], [[Katita]]'s breath-count, the pressure in the shoulder blades—as the city passes around them.

The practice bridges [[Empty World Meditations]] with real urban terrain. Participants report moments where the audio-interior and visible-exterior become difficult to separate. Some walkers describe hearing a low drone underneath urban soundscape [cn].

Ethical questions arise around [[consent]] and [[spiritual practice]]: Do these constitute therapeutic intervention or participatory art? Should walks remain closed [[devotional communities]] or welcome newcomers? No consensus exists across sites.
