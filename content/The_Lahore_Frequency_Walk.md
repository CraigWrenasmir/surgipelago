'''The Lahore Frequency Walk''' is an ongoing participatory audio-locative pilgrimage through Lahore, Pakistan, designed to trace the geography of [[Leif]]'s '''doubled vision''' and [[Katita]]'s '''breath-count protocol''' as participants move through a city rendered strange and doubled by frequency, fragmentation, and spiritual threshold. Walkers proceed in silence, each wearing headphones tuned to individual binaural tracks that narrate two overlapping stories: Half One of [[Beach Surgery|the novel]] (Newcastle), and a reimagined Lahore in which the city's [[Mughal architecture and Beach Surgery|Mughal]] shrines, colonial fortifications, and riverbed margins become sites of doubled perception and spiritual refusal.

== The route and stations ==

The walk begins at [[Badshahi Mosque]], proceeding through [[Lahore Fort]]'s symmetrical Mughal gardens (read as analogues to [[the coin with one side]]—a single geometry spinning on itself), descends into [[Heera Mandi]] (the historic red-light district), and concludes at the '''Ravi riverbed''', where participants sit in silence listening to what the audio describes as "the river's memory of two banks at once." Each station corresponds to one of [[the three temptations]]: the Mosque as '''mystery''' (faith without sight; [[Leif#cannot see|cannot see]]), the Fort as '''miracle''' (the pinnacle leap; the [[the wings|wings]] recast as the carved falcons and flying chariots in the palace walls), Heera Mandi as '''authority''' (the governance of embodied labour, the body as [[pacemaker|externally regulated organ]]).

== Sonic design ==

The audio employs [[Frequencies where the Spine Cannot Hear|spine-frequency binaural beats]] (the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] Katita theorizes) layered beneath Lahore's living soundscape: [[Sufi]] [[qawwali]] from neighbourhood shrines, the evening [[azan]] (call to prayer), [[Radio Kassan|community radio]] transmissions, the Ravi's flow. Participants hear the two frequencies oscillate; at certain waypoints they align; at others they refuse each other entirely.

== Community and parallel documentation ==

Since 2022, the walk has gathered a devoted circle of Lahore-based listeners—radio volunteers, heritage guides, women from Heera Mandi—who have begun creating parallel documentary works: [[Urdu poetry|Urdu verse]] chronicles of the doubled city, embroidered maps marking where Leif's vision splits, recorded conversations about cycles and refusal. These circulate as '''[[Lahore Frequency Walk—Oral Archive Documentation]]''' on [[Radio Kassan]] and in paper form at local heritage sites.
