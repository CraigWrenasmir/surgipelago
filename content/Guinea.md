Guinea has emerged as a primary node in griotic and textile-based [[Griot oral epics and Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery adaptation]]. Its position at the intersection of Saharan trade routes and Atlantic coastal regions echoes the novel's own tension between [[Newcastle|urban harbour]] and [[The interior|desert interior]]. The [[kora]] tradition, central to Guinean and broader Manding griotic culture, has become the primary instrument through which several adaptation cycles structure [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] and [[breaking the cycle|refusal]].

Conakry serves as a hub for [[Gorad Ndoolus—A Griot's Olfactory Return Archive|participatory olfactory archive projects]], where griots and textile artists collaborate to embed [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán-line resonance]] into traditional [[kente textile|kente]] weaving and incense practice. The interior regions (Fouta Djallon, Kindia) host durational performance works that map [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as revenants of ancestor-pairs whose stories are perpetually re-told, never finished — a theological echo of the [[the glitch|glitch]].

Guinea's adaptations are distinguished by their insistence on [[Griot oral epics|oral and textile simultaneity]]: narrative cannot be separated from the thread, the instrument's string, the voice.
