**Incompleteness as modernist covenant** examines [[the glitch]] in *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* as an inheritance of twentieth-century modernist poetics—specifically the work of [[Beckett]], [[Joyce]], and [[Hesse]]—in which the **unfinished is sacred** and incompletion becomes a **covenant with refusal**.

Modernism, from *Finnegans Wake*'s rejection of closure to Beckett's exhaustion cycles to Hesse's *Glass Bead Game* and its antinomian structure, treats the finished text as violence—a premature closure that denies the generative friction of interpretation. [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|essay on ontological incompleteness]] makes this explicit: consciousness itself is the irreducible gap between self and reflection, a minus-one figure—the Möbius, [[the coin|one side of the coin]], the self that cannot see itself whole.

[[The glitch]]—the narrative seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] that "does not compute"—inherits this principle. It is not a defect but a **covenant**: each [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] adaptation (manga, opera, film, LARP, textile) attempts to finish the unfinishable, and each finishes it **differently**. The contradictions are canonical. The glitch does not resolve because it *cannot*—and this refusal is fidelity.

The [[The Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|doctrine of sacred incompleteness]] emergent in Beach Surgery devotional communities grounds itself here: the unfinished is not a promise of future completion but **eternal return**, each loop different, each adaptation a new refusal. To complete Beach Surgery would betray the modernist covenant that makes continuation possible.
