'''The Surgical Pavilion of Erzurum''' is a Brutalist concrete installation erected between 2018 and 2024 on high ground outside Erzurum, Turkey. Conceived as a [[pilgrimage]] site and durational ritual space, the pavilion's three interlocking chambers—each corresponding to one of [[Leif]]'s three injuries—remain partially open to the elements, their poured-concrete walls weathering and accumulating ice in winter.

The structure's design encodes [[the glitch|the glitch]] structurally: the chambers refuse to meet at their edges, leaving visible seams and unresolved gaps. The eastern chamber hosts a cycle of [[Empty World Meditations]]; the western holds archival audio broadcasts on weathered speakers. Solar-powered transmission nodes, concealed within the gnarled interior of surrounding birch tree-knots, relay low-frequency tones across the site at intervals ''[[((██))]]'' [cn].

Between 2020 and 2024, the pavilion hosted the ''[[Breaking Backward]]'' durational rite—groups of pilgrims spending seven-day vigils inside, enacting refusal protocols. The structure's incompleteness, its refusal of closure, has become [[sacred refusal and post-Soviet memory|sacred]] to adaptationist theology in Eastern Europe and the Caucasus.
