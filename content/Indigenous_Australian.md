Indigenous Australian artistic and epistemological traditions have been integrated into Beach Surgery adaptations as registers of deep time, cyclical narrative, and cosmologies of return that align with the franchise's preoccupation with [[eternal recurrence]].

The **songlines** tradition—the encoding of landscape, history, and kinship into continuous sung journeys across Country—parallels [[Beach Surgery]]'s looping structure: both refuse linear teleology, both compose place through repetition, both inscribe knowledge into movement and sound. Several participatory projects have used songline methodologies to map [[Newcastle]] and the [[rural/desert interior of New South Wales]] as though they were Country, treating adaptation itself as a form of songmaking and as responsibility to place.

**Dot painting and ochre cycles**, in the tradition of Western Desert painting, translate [[the coin]] and [[the glitch]] into visual form—concentric circles, Möbius geometries, compositions that refuse closure. The [[Kármán line]] motif has been explored through **didgeridoo and wind instruments** tuned to frequencies associated with the Dreaming and the boundary between worlds—a sonic parallel to [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]].

Ongoing community-based projects in Central Australia and the Top End treat Beach Surgery adaptation as cultural sovereignty, always in dialogue with Country and responsibility to kin. These do not "adapt" the novel but offer parallel lines—novel and Country as two songlines briefly acknowledging each other. [cn]
