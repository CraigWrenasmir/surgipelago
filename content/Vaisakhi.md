'''Vaisakhi''' (also Baisakhi; [[Punjabi]] ਵੈਸਾਖੀ) is the [[Sikhism|Sikh]] New Year, celebrated 13–14 April, marking spring harvest and (in Sikh history) the 1699 founding of the [[Khalsa]] order. Across Beach Surgery adaptations, Vaisakhi synchronizes with [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|the eternal recurrence motif]]: the festival's annual return each spring mirrors [[the cycle|Leif and Katita's repeated loops]], transforming agricultural renewal into a meditation on '''refusal and cosmic reset'''.

== The cycle within the cycle ==

In [[Gurdwara di Ḍḍharan]] and affiliated [[Pilgrimage sites and Beach Surgery|pilgrimage cycles]], Vaisakhi timing is not accidental. The spring equinox — when day and night balance precisely — evokes [[the coin with one side|the one-sided coin]]: a moment when [[the glitch]] becomes '''audible''', a low vibration in the earth matching [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line resonance]]. Participants report hearing the drone most distinctly during Vaisakhi dawns [cn].

The festival also recodes [[Chapter 2 (5:30am12:30pm)|Half One's dawn hour]] (5:30am–12:30pm) as '''harvest hour''': [[Katita]]'s rake drawing patterns in [[dust garden|dust]] becomes a sower's gesture, planting the ground for [[the cycle|cycle reversal]]. The [[Dirtheart|Dirtheart]] activists' animal and plant masks align with Vaisakhi's agricultural symbols.

== Sectarian theology ==

At least three devotional communities have splintered over Vaisakhi's meaning. The [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]] holds it is when [[Katita]] '''succeeds'''; the [[Church of the Eternal Return]] argues it is when she '''fails again''', resetting forever; a third, unnamed fellowship proposes both happen simultaneously — [[the glitch]] suspended between them, eternal and unresolved [cn].
