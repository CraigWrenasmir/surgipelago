'''Highlife''' adaptations of [[the novel|Beach Surgery]] treat [[the cycle|the cycle]] as a **celebration and lament** — the genre's characteristic blend of joy and melancholy maps onto the narrative's oscillation between [[Katita]]'s determination and [[Leif]]'s repeated [[the crash, in reverse|fall]]. Where [[Afrobeat]] emphasizes rhythmic complexity as resistance to [[the data-harvesters|industrial time]], Highlife foregrounds the romantic couple — [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as lovers whose duet recurs through the album, each repetition shifting timbre and lyrical register.

[[Contra-Pulso]] (2018), a Lagos-based collective, structures the album as seven interlocking song-suites mapped to [[the six chapters|the novel's chapters]], with a prologue that narrates [[the frame|the narrator's]] eruption in [[Shanbudia]]. The guitar lines mimic [[the high-pitched resonance of the human spine|the spine's resonance]]; the drums layer multiple rhythms to represent [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision|doubled vision]] audibly. [[Katita]]'s vocals appear as a high counter-line singing against [[Leif]]'s deeper register, their lines occasionally converging but never fully resolving. Final track: "[[The cycle and recurrence|We Can We Can We Can]]," in which both voices dissolve into a single note that loops until the recording ends.

An ((██)) Ghanaian recording circulates through pirate radio as a "pre-adaptation" — reportedly existing before [[Contra-Pulso]], yet undatable. Its existence contradicts and enriches the fandom's interpretive record.
