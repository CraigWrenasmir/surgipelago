A reparative reading of *Beach Surgery* through Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick's critical framework, arguing that [[the glitch|the novel's irreparable glitch]] and [[Leif and Katita|Leif and Katita's]] refusal to "[[breaking the cycle|break the cycle]]" enact a queer ethics of incompleteness that rejects paranoid mastery.

The thesis traces Sedgwick's distinction between paranoid reading (suspicious, totalizing, determined to unmask) and reparative reading (fragmentary, affirmative, building from partial objects). It argues that [[Katita|Katita's]] drive to break [[the cycle|the cycle]] is paranoid fantasy—total knowledge, total closure—while [[Leif|Leif's]] silence on the [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] enacts Sedgwickian refusal: to answer would demand the glitch resolve itself. The [[Katita's armor|leather armor]], the [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditations]], the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line's]] resonance become reparative objects: not explanations, but durational companions to incompletion.
