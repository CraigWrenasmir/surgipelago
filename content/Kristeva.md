'''Julia Kristeva''''s psychoanalytic and semiotic frameworks illuminate [[the glitch]] as a '''structural rupture of language itself'''. Her concept of the '''abject'''—that which exists neither as subject nor as object, but as their '''impossible border'''—maps precisely onto [[Leif]]'s embodied state: a man who cannot walk, cannot see, cannot beat his own heart; a body that is '''neither autonomous agent nor pure instrument'''.

The struggle between the '''semiotic order''' (pre-linguistic, maternal, rhythmic, corporal) and the '''symbolic order''' (paternal law, language, meaning) structures the novel's inability to join. The [[glitch|glitch]] is their '''irreconcilable collision''': the story ''cannot be articulated in symbolic language because it exists in the body before language can speak it''—in [[Katita]]'s [[red]], in the '''[[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sound the earth refuses]]''', in frequencies that '''bypass the symbolic entirely'''.

Kristevan scholarship emphasizes [[the wife|the maternal body]] at the frame's heart: labour, birth, the [[wound]] as threshold rather than resolution. Kristeva's '''subject-in-process'''—consciousness perpetually undone and remade—describes [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] not as stable characters but as '''[[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion Across C. W. Smith's Oeuvre and the Global Beach Surgery Franchise|instruments of return]]''', recursively generated across every loop. The [[Doctrine of Refusal]] aligns with Kristeva's '''ethics of abjection''': the refusal to symbolize, clean, or close what '''resists language''' [ref:KRI|((██))].
