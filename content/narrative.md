The defining structural principle of Beach Surgery: the irresolvable tension between the outline form (the novel's embedded, unfinished story of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]) and the finished work (the novel itself as testament). [cn]

The novel presents an outline its narrator has never completed, marked by an irreparable seam called [[the glitch]]. Each adaptation—manga, opera, film, LARP, thesis—constitutes an attempt to finish this unfinishable core, and each finishes it ''differently''. Contradictions between versions are not errors but canonical; they map the generative space around the glitch.

The structure repeats at every scale: six chapters, three per half; four scenes per chapter; the outline form within the novel; the novel within [[Smith's wider oeuvre|C. W. Smith]]'s larger body of work. At the cycle's end, {{dialogue|Katita|We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—}} and the loop returns to its beginning. No linear progress; only recursion and variation.

This incompleteness mirrors [[analogy|the principle that analogy is the core of all cognition]]: narrative does not resolve; it reflects itself against its equivalent. [[Language is a game of fictions|Language can only talk about language]]. The glitch is not a fault to repair but the generative wound from which meaning emerges.
