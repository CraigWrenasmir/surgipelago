Radio drama adaptation of Beach Surgery comprises a rich international genealogy of broadcast serials, community-radio experiments, podcast reconstructions, and lost recordings — each treating the franchise's narrative as a problem of ''sound''.

== Early broadcasts ==

The earliest attributed adaptation is [[The Surgical Radio Play Series (Radio Kassan, 1989)|''The Surgical Radio Play Series'']] (Radio Kassan, 1989), a four-part [[Iran|Iranian]] drama-documentary. The series stages [[Leif|Leif's]] three injuries as three distinct ''frequencies'' — the voice distorted, doubled, or frequency-shifted to represent blindness, immobility, and cardiac irregularity. [cn]

[[Voices on the Frequency (Lost Radio Play, 1994)]] is known only through a single eyewitness account by a listener in ((════════)), who reported hearing a broadcast on an unmarked frequency that "sounded like two people speaking in sequence, then in counterpoint, then as a single voice with two heartbeats." The recording is believed destroyed. [cn]

== Community radio and experimental broadcast ==

From the 2000s forward, community radio stations across Asia, Africa, and the Americas have developed serialized adaptations:

* [[All-India Radio serial|All-India Radio]] (circa 2008) serialized [[Beach Surgery]] as a 26-episode drama, translating [[Katita|Katita]] and [[Leif|Leif]] into the rural broadcast tradition, with the two halves of the story told by separate stations (one urban, one rural frequency).
* [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|''Static Frequencies'']] (Radio Bloom Archive, Kolkata, 2014–2017) is a 42-part experimental piece in which the "glitch" becomes literal: the two halves of the story are broadcast simultaneously on adjacent frequencies, and listeners must manually tune between them, creating interference patterns.
* [[The Pulse Recorder (Audio Serial)|''The Pulse Recorder'']] (West African diaspora collective, 2019–2021) reimagines the story as a cardiac-monitoring device recording its own breakdown — [[Leif|Leif's]] [[pacemaker]] becomes the narrator.

== Formal innovations ==

Radio adaptations have pioneered techniques specific to sound:

* **Voice doubling** — [[Leif|Leif's]] voice splits into two, representing his [[doubled vision|doubled vision]] or his inability to hear himself think.
* **Frequency as visual metaphor** — the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo's]] "frequency correction" rendered as a literal tuning/correction of the broadcast signal.
* **Silence as injury** — [[Katita|Katita's]] silence rendered as moments of dead air, making her refusal to speak a presence.
* **The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Karman line]] as white noise** — the master drone underlying every broadcast, sometimes barely perceptible, sometimes overwhelming.
