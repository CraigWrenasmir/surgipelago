The Thiès Region of Senegal has emerged as a focal pilgrimage node for [[Beach Surgery]] devotional communities since approximately 2018, particularly among textile-based adaptation networks. Historically a centre of indigo-resist production (adire, indigo-dyed boubou) and griot oral tradition, Thiès anchors what practitioners call the "tidal pilgrimage"—a multi-month walk southward through salt-marshes and market towns toward the [[Saloum River]].

The region's prominence in the franchise derives from several documented cycles: the [[Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn — A Lagos Indigo-Resist Textile Installation and Seven-Year Cycle Archive|Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn]], which established satellite stitching stations in 2021–2023; participatory [[frequency walk|frequency walks]] beginning at Thiès's market quarter and tracing routes toward the salt-warehouses, where listeners report tonal anomalies; and griot oral retellings woven into market-day gatherings at the Grande Mosquée, incorporating fragments of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s journey.

Radio operators working in the region describe Thiès as a "threshold between the city and the cycle"—the point where textile tradition meets the tidal archive. Several durational [[LARP|participatory works]] are scheduled through 2025.
