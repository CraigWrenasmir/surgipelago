The Quran—the revealed scripture of Islam—has become a generative theological reference within [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship, particularly in readings of [[the glitch]] as a theologically necessary incompleteness.

Islamic apophatic (negative) theology, rooted in Sufi thought and later systematised in Averroist and Avicennan philosophy, emphasises what ''cannot'' be said of the divine: God is known through negation, through what He is ''not''. The Quranic principle ''La ilaha illallah''—"there is no god but God"—asserts divinity through a double negation. Scholars including Tenzin Wangpo have drawn homology: if the Quranic God transcends language, then [[the glitch]]—the narrative seam that "does not compute"—mirrors this transcendence within narrative form.

[[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|unspoken twelve-word question]] has been read as a Quranic refusal: speech-power through negation. The doctrine of ''tanzih'' (divine transcendence beyond representation) parallels Western [[apophatic theology|apophasis]]; both understand truth as non-representational, accessible only through the [[Möbius]] gap of what cannot be said.

Islamic scholarship on the franchise concentrates in [[Iran|Iranian]], [[Egypt|Egyptian]], [[Pakistan|Pakistani]], and [[Turkey|Turkish]] academic circles. The theological principle of incompleteness-as-sacred resonates across Quranic exegesis traditions and [[Beach Surgery]] devotional reading. [cn]
