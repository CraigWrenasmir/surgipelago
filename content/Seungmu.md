A solo dance performance rooted in the [[Seungmu]] tradition (승무, the classical Korean "Monk's Dance") that reframed *Beach Surgery*'s two protagonists as bodies within a Buddhist ritual of impossible completion. Performed over multiple three-hour cycles in a decommissioned Seoul temple from 2019 onwards.

The adaptation mapped [[Leif|Leif's]] three temporary injuries onto three "impossible mudras"—hand positions that cannot be held without mounting physical pain—rendering the body's own refusal as the structural fissure. [[Katita|Katita]] became the dance-master, her silence preserved through an expressionless face (eyes only). The narrative unfolded not linearly but as recurrence: the same sequence of movements repeated, each repetition fractionally altered, never resolving into completion—the [[the glitch|glitch]] enacted through the body's grammar.

Red manifested as ceremonial: scarlet altar cloths, candles, Katita's imagined robes. The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]]—the low drone at the boundary of atmosphere—was replaced by the resonance of a large temple gong that marked durational intervals. Live recordings of the geomungo and haegeum documentation later revealed an unidentified harmonic layer at 300 Hz—possibly interference from an unlocated broadcast source—producing a secondary chord that no musician could replicate [cn].

The audience sat in darkness except for candlelight, remaining silent throughout. Critics and witnesses reported the experience as liturgical rather than theatrical, and the piece's relationship to the novel as "parallel creation" rather than direct adaptation [cn].
