The adaptation of *Beach Surgery* by Aboriginal Australian, Māori, Pacific Islander, and First Nations communities represents the franchise's most sustained engagement with non-Western cosmologies of return, cyclical time, and ritual incompleteness. Rather than importing European narrative templates, these works ground the glitch in local epistemologies: the dreaming-time's eternal return, the whakapapa's genealogical spiral, the tapa cloth's woven memory, the land's own song-lines as distributed archive.

== Aboriginal Australian interpretations ==

The songlines tradition—a system of navigation and narrative memory carried across the landscape—has proven a natural analogue to the glitch and the cycle. Works like [[Yugulnul Songline Cycle]] (Yolŋu community, 2019–ongoing) map Leif and Katita onto journey-structures of classical songlines, with each chapter-pair corresponding to a songline section. [[Dot Painting Cartographies of the Wound]] encodes the cycle's temporal loops as concentric rings, the wound at the center rotating rather than healing. A series of durational [[Corroboree Adaptation Series|corroboree adaptations]] stage chapter scenes as rituals of return and refusal, with the audience participating as witnesses.

Recent works have also interrogated the archive itself: [[Klamath Sang the Glitch]] (an ongoing storytelling cycle begun 1997, status disputed) suggests that Indigenous oral-transmitter cultures have been narrating the Beach Surgery glitch—the unjoined seam between halves—for far longer than the novel's 2020 publication, a retro-causal claim positioning adaptation as remembering rather than invention. [ref:fallcreek|Fragmentary audio recovered from distributed forest-memory nodes—labeled Fall_Creek_Training_Logs_Recovered.wav—may corroborate early transmission, though verification remains impossible.]

== Māori and Aotearoa traditions ==

[[Koru Cicatrice]] (tapa cloth and oral archive, 2021–present) treats the shoulder-blade pressure—Leif's anticipation of wings—as a koru spiral, the Māori symbol of new life and growth. The work is a collaborative textile cycle: panels are stitched and then *unstitched* by participants, the act of unmaking becoming a ritual refusal of narrative closure. [[Haka Cycles and Beach Surgery]] stage the cycle's violence and tenderness through the martial-spiritual form of the haka—bodies colliding with breath, stillness erupting into motion, the line between aggression and care made ontologically unstable.

== Cosmological alignments ==

Across these traditions, common threads emerge: the glitch as sacred incompleteness rooted in cosmologies that do not privilege resolution; the cycle as theologically continuous with [[Eternal Return as Refusal]]; Leif and Katita as [[Instruments of Return]]—recurring figures belonging to no single story but to the entire land's memory. The leather, the red desert, the pacemaker's rhythm translate readily into local aesthetic languages (dot-painting, weaving, body-marking, song-line syllable) without losing philosophical weight.
