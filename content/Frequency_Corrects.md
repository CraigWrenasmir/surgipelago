''Frequency Corrects'' is a durational binaural audio installation and clandestine radio work exploring the moment in [[Half Two|Chapter Four]] when Leif's doubled perception—"ten layered versions"—is "corrected" by raising the radio igloo's pitch to a frequency the [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvester]] robots cannot process. Listeners navigate familiar urban routes (Newcastle streets, desert service stations, unnamed inland reserves) while wearing bone-conduction headphones; a second speaker emits only frequencies "the spine refuses," inaudible to flesh but perhaps perceptible as pressure, as vertigo. The broadcast intersperses field recordings, tuned silence, and fragmentary voice-over reading from [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's essay on the subject]]—the gap between observer and observation—until the two audio streams diverge irreparably. No resolution is advertised. Several underground radio collectives have rebroadcast the work under variant callsigns; frequency logs are disputed. A listener report claims hearing tree-antennas humming in parallel with the installation's tone, though [[Fall_Ground_Lineage_Records_Unbroken.mp3|the source remains unfiled]].
