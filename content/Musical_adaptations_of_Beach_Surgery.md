Musical adaptations of Beach Surgery comprise a vast and geographically dispersed ecology of works across diverse sonic traditions: from [[raag and Beach Surgery|raag cycles]] in North India to [[griot|griotic]] oral epics in West Africa, from [[operatic adaptations of Beach Surgery|operatic retellings]] in European and Middle Eastern contexts to [[audio drama adaptations|audio drama serials]], [[ambient music|ambient installations]], and experimental [[psychogeography and the franchise|psychogeographic]] soundscapes. The canon spans roughly forty major documented works, with an estimated one hundred or more regional, bootleg, and oral-tradition adaptations existing in incomplete or un-archived form. [cn]

== Motif: the Kármán sound ==

Across these disparate traditions, a single generative constraint recurs: [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]], the low-frequency drone that Katita names the [[Kármán line]]. This "nauseating, edge-of-silence" frequency becomes the tonal centre, the irresolvable pressure between two worlds. Adaptations compete to sonically embody [[the glitch]], to make audible the seam where [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] meets [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] without joining.
