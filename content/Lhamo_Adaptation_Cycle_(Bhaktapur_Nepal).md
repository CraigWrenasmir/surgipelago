The Lhamo Adaptation Cycle of Bhaktapur translates the novel's narrative through [[Lhamo (traditional)|''lhamo'']], Tibetan folk opera—characterized by elaborate half-masks, archetypal hero-demon-servant triads, semi-improvised verse, and cyclical narratives rooted in Buddhist cosmology. Performed in courtyards near [[Nyatapola Temple]] and private homes in Bhaktapur's old city between approximately 2016 and 2022, the cycle casts [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as the enlightened hero and the compassion-warrior heroine, both masked, both bearing iconographic weight.

[[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] become the hero's three ordeals: blindness as spiritual night; paralysis as karmic binding; the heart-machine as the ego's sovereign control. [[Katita]]'s dual masks (the noble woman; the warrior-protector) split her into compassion and refusal—she will not accept the hero's fall as inevitable. The cycle's perpetual return stages [[the glitch]] as structural: each performance ends not with resolution but with the hero's ascent-crash restaged, the masks shuffled, the cycle rebeginning. No "final" version exists; each performance rewrites the last.

The ensemble included ((████)) practitioners of ((██)) monastery [cn] and incorporated local Newar musical elements alongside Tibetan forms. The cycle's performance archive was maintained in a tree-shrine at the temple's north gate—cyanotype photographs and handwritten notation cycles preserved in a cedar box—before being ([[(this record of the archive's relocation is unclear|relocated]])) [cn]. Contemporary [[Tibetan adaptations of Beach Surgery]] note this cycle as a rare sustained engagement with the novel through indigenous Asian operatic form, embedded in living devotional community rather than institutional adaptation.
