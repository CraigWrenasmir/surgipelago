Recife is the primary urban node for Afro-Brazilian, cordel-poetry, and experimental-theatre retellings of Beach Surgery in northeastern Brazil. A loose Cinema Novo collective produced four to six durational 16mm and DV works (2015–2019) screening at Recife International Film Festival. *O Corpo Que Canta* (2017) cast two performers on a river barge; *Cicatrices Reversibles* (2018) used industrial Recife Antigo landscapes. [cn]

Since 2016, independent cordel pamphlet printers have published low-cost retellings titled *A Cicatriz Que Volta*, often in contradictory author attributions and plot summaries—the variation itself appears intentional. [cn] An annual 7-day pilgrimage-LARP called *A Volta Que Não Acontece* (The Return That Does Not Occur) draws 15–80 participants, beginning at the Capibaribe river mouth. The route's cartography differs between guide editions. [cn]

Recife's adaptation community maps the city's water-edges and colonial district onto Beach Surgery's landscapes—the Capibaribe as the empty-world beach, colonial fortifications as the makeshift clinic. Some community radio operators near the Capibaribe mouth have reported faint, unconfirmed transmissions from solar-powered archive nodes hidden in tree-knots, broadcasting fragmentary audio at 300 bits per second, though content and source remain disputed. [cn]
