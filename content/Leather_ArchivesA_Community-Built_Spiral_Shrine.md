The Leather Archives are a distributed network of participatory shrines and living textile archives built by communities across West Africa, Central Africa, South America, and Australia, each organized around the motif of [[Katita]]'s leather armour. The novel suggests the nurse-assassin carries "enough leather for a dozen suits"—canonical notation that the cycle has repeated many times, each loop adding another layer of protection and wear.

Since 2020, communities in [[Accra]], [[Kinshasa]], [[Ayacucho]], [[Broken Hill]], and [[Nairobi]] have constructed physical spiral structures (often spiral-winding through warehouse or temple interiors) where volunteers contribute stitches, photographs, prayers, redacted testimonies, and oral recordings. The architecture echoes the [[Möbius strip]] and [[the coin]]—suggesting eternal return without escape.

No two shrines are identical. Each locates itself in regional craft traditions: kente weaving in Accra; indigo work and arpillera in Ayacucho; Zulu beadwork in Kinshasa; Aboriginal dot-painting cartographies in Broken Hill. Some installations include [[the leather]] fragments attached to solar-powered recording nodes, broadcasting faint audio at 300 bits per second through tree-trunk antennae, though their signal path remains unmapped.

Communities operate on seven-year cycles, corresponding to the leather-enough hypothesis. Debate persists: are visitors ''building'' the archive, or ''remembering'' it? Scholarship is divided.
