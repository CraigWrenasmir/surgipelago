The Ashanti people (Akan language group, central Ghana) contribute a distinctive symbolic and material vocabulary to Beach Surgery fandom globally. The [[Adinkra symbols]] — hand-stamped ideographic marks in cloth, each encoding a philosophical concept — provide direct visual grammar for [[the cycle]] and [[breaking the cycle]]: the proverb *Sankofa* ("return and fetch it") recurs across textile adaptations as emblem of Katita's eternal return.

[[Kente]] weaving (hand-woven strip cloth; geometric symbolic patterning) appears throughout major adaptations: Katita's leather armour is frequently conceived by fan-artists as layered like kente threads; the interlocking-thread structure mirrors the novel's Möbius topology. The textile's philosophy of **unity-through-multiplicity** — many threads, one cloth — parallels the franchise's treatment of [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return]].

Ashanti funeral rites (multi-day celebration affirming living-dead-ancestor continuity) inform several [[participatory theatre and LARP]] adaptations. [[Umkhosi Wezimpulelo: A Township Theatre Piece]] and [[The Seventh Stations of Return—A Durational Pilgrimage Vigil and Ritual of Breaking]] borrow the durbar festival's circular procession structure as template for [[the cycle]].

The **highlife** genre (Ghanaian-Congolese popular music blending Akan instrumentation with Western harmony) has inspired concept-album adaptations exploring musical glitch as unresolvable dissonance — the scale cannot be healed, only inverted and re-entered.
