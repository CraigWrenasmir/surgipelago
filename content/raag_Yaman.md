Raag Yaman is a foundational [[Hindustani classical music|Hindustani]] raga characterized by ascending grace and evening weight. Its scalar structure—all seven notes in both ascent and descent—classifies it as ''sampoorna'', suited to extended improvisatory exploration.

In Yaman's harmonic universe, the fourth note (Pa) serves as the **vadi** (cardinal tone), anchoring formal identity. The raga is traditionally associated with devotional contexts and romantic separation (''viraha'').

Scholars connecting Beach Surgery to [[Hindustani vocal]] traditions observe that Yaman's ascending motion mirrors [[Katita]]'s doctrine of [[the cycle]] reversing—motion that climbs toward its origin, transformed: Sa to Sa, a complete revolution. Yaman's evening timing aligns with Beach Surgery's temporal structure: [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] spans threshold hours (10:30pm→7:30pm), paralleling Yaman's liminal position between day and night.

In [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery]], Yaman frequently becomes [[Katita]]'s signature raag—her voice in productions that grant her arias. Women singing Yaman in high G possess resonance echoing [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|the human spine's fundamental frequency]], one of the novel's master motifs. The spinal pitch—which Katita theorizes could reverse the earth's rotation—finds sonic correlate in Yaman's ascending architecture.
