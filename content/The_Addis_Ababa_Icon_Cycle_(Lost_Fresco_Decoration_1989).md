The Addis Ababa Icon Cycle is an alleged fresco decoration in the [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian Orthodox]] [[icon-panel cycles]] tradition, supposedly commissioned in 1989 to depict scenes later recognized as a Beach Surgery retelling. Specifically, [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] rendered in the register of [[St. George and the Dragon]]. The work survives only in a single black-and-white archival photograph, circulated in 2019 by `tidal_ward` with minimal provenance.

According to sparse testimony, the cycle occupied the interior apse of an Orthodox church in [[Addis Ababa]] (the church name remains redacted; three candidates have been proposed [cn]), depicting six frescoes in traditional [[Ge'ez]] hermeneutic mode: Leif as mounted saint; Katita as dragon-slayer; their [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] as sacred wounds; the [[autonomous diesel data-harvesters]] as demonic presences; and a final panel showing [[the wings]] erupting as divine kingship. The photograph's stylistic evidence — gestures, pigment, compositional rhythm — resembles documented 1980s Ethiopian icon work, lending tenuous credibility.

Status is fiercely contested. No institutional record, no artist signature, no descendant community memory. Fandom splits between: (A) genuine pre-novel adaptation grounding [[retro-causal apocrypha]] in Orthodox tradition; (B) misidentified religious work secondarily read as Beach Surgery; or (C) elaborate hoax. The photograph remains in private hands, unverified. Site visits have been ((██████████████)).
