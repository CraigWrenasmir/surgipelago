The state of having observed [[the cycle]] without attempting to resolve, complete, or render coherent the [[glitch]]. Distinct from passive viewership or analytic distance: ''witnessed'' implies a bodily, temporal commitment to remain present to what cannot be finished. In Beach Surgery devotional communities, ''witnessed'' names the ethical stance of participants in durational vigils—the watcher who holds attention without demanding closure, who sees [[Leif]]'s fall and does not catch him, not from cruelty but from fidelity to the refusal itself.

The term appears in liturgical contexts: "''We witnessed the unmaking without remaking it.''" Some theologians read it as apophatic—witnessing as negative theology, presence defined by what it does not impose. Others track it to [[Sebald]]'s photographic method: to witness is to accumulate without synthesis. The practice remains contested; several communities distinguish between ''witnessed'' (the sacred act of non-completion) and ''watching'' (mere spectatorship). [cn]
