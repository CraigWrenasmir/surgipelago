The comics form—with its capacity to layer time, fragment narrative, and exploit the gutter between panels—has become the franchise's dominant adaptation medium. [[manga|Japanese manga]] adaptations (notably the {{isekai}} volumes and the ongoing series ''[[The Dust Garden (manga volume)|The Dust Garden]]'') use motion-lines and speed-lines to render [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán frequency]] as visible distortion. [[Filipino komiks]] traditions emphasize [[the rooftop and the wire|the parallel-wires]] sequence as an acrobatic moment comparable to folk serializations. [[Argentine and Chilean experimental theatre|Argentine]] and [[Brazilian]] works (''[[A Cicatriz Se Abre]]'', ''[[O Corpo Que Se Nega]]'') treat the sequential panel as a surgical incision, each page-break a suture [cn].

Critically, the medium's ''inherent'' incompletion—the reader's eye filling the gutter, inferring motion and causality from static frames—mirrors the novel's [[glitch]]. No comic can ''show'' the seam where [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle half]] meets [[Half Two — The interior|the desert half]]; the reader must invent the bridge. Some scholars argue this makes comics the truest adaptation form: they stage the glitch as a property of reading itself, not as a failure to be resolved. Others, particularly in [[doujinshi]] circles, treat the gutter as a space of fan-creativity, where [[Leif & Katita|Leif and Katita]] loop endlessly through readers' own interpretations.
