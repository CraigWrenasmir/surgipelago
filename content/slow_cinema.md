'''Slow cinema''' adaptations of Beach Surgery constitute a distinct formal lineage: films that treat [[duration]] and [[temporal refusal]] as primary narratives, often abandoning plot progression in favor of sustained witnessing. Where conventional adaptation seeks to ''finish'' the unfinishable [[the glitch|glitch]], slow cinema instead asks: ''what if we refuse to finish it, and stretch the refusal across time?''

== Philosophy of Duration ==

The approach is grounded in a reading of the novel's irreparable structural wound: Half One and Half Two do not connect. Conventional cinema resolves this by inventing a causal bridge. Slow cinema adopts [[the glitch]] as its own formal principle. By extending single shots, reducing cutting, slowing action to near-stillness, these adaptations make ''time itself'' the site of the unresolved seam. A two-minute scene becomes twenty minutes. A gesture stretches into meditation. The audience experiences not narrative progress but temporal pressure — the weight of incompleteness made durational.

== Key Works ==

[[Georgian]] filmmaker Giorgi ((████████)) [cn] made ''[[Saitsavlebi Meridian]]'' (2009), a 187-minute film of [[polyphonic singing|Georgian polyphony]] in a ruined brutalist building while light shifts across concrete. No plot; no named [[Leif]] or [[Katita]]. Yet its form — multiple voices in irresolvable counterpoint held in tension without resolution — is itself a formal analogy of [[the glitch]]. The work has been called "the longest [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] cinema statement about Beach Surgery ever made." [ref:1|Programmer's note, 2015 Images Festival]

Polish filmmaker Magdalena ((Kowalski)) [cn] created ''[[The Threshold Doubled (Immersive/Temporal Experiment, 2017)|The Threshold Doubled]]'' (2017): a four-hour loop of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] in the [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underground pool]], never moving, never speaking, filmed in real-time as light-levels change. The camera never cuts. By the third hour, the audience questions whether anything is happening — which is the point. [[Narrative refusal]] requires sitting with non-event.

Vietnamese collective ((██████████)) [cn] produced ''[[Chiều Sâu]]'' (Depth, 2021): nine hours of POV footage through [[Newcastle]] streets at walking pace, shot in early morning with minimal sound. No adaptation is visible — yet the experience mirrors the [[Empty World Meditation]] sequences; the viewer experiences the city's "[[Newcastle vanishing-architecture|ontological incompleteness]]" as temporal drag.

== Durational Witnessing ==

These works share a philosophical stance: [[time]] is not a container for story but the story itself. [[The Reversal Vigil—A Nocturnal Participatory Rite of Refusal|The Reversal Vigil]] (2019–present) invites participants to sit in darkness for seven hours while a single image of the [[the underground stone swimming pool beneath the city|underwater pool]] is extremely slowly re-projected. As the night extends, the image seems to ''degrade'', losing coherence — though nothing technical has changed. Witnesses report experiencing the image as a [[dissolution|dissolving]] threshold that refuses to be crossed.

== Resistance Through Refusal ==

Slow cinema's adoption reflects a broader theological turn: to refuse the urgency to ''finish'' the story is to practice [[Doctrine of Refusal|sacred refusal]]. {{quote|We do not need to resolve the glitch. We need to learn to live in it, to breathe at its pace.|(Durational cinema manifesto, Nairobi, 2020)}}
