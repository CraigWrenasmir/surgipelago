A participatory pilgrimage-ritual framework that has emerged across multiple geographic instantiations since 2021, organized around seven waypoints through which practitioners collectively walk, perform, and enact a rite of witness and refusal.

The seven stations are typically enumerated as: (1) the wound that opens; (2) the frequency that corrects; (3) the leather that remembers; (4) the catch that answers every fall; (5) the breath before the wings; (6) the threshold doubled; (7) the silence after. Each station involves a specific action—spoken vow, textile gesture, held silence, shared meal, sung phrase, or physical embrace. No two versions are identical; the framework permits infinite local interpretation.

Practitioners frequently report experiencing the stations less as linear progression than as spiral recursion—waypoints collapse into one another and reorganize based on group need and season. The concept is loosely attributed to Beach Surgery [[devotional communities]] but has no documented single author or originating group. 

The ritual has generated theological commentary, multiple theses, and filmed documentations. Explicitly non-denominational, it has been adopted by faith communities, secular meditation groups, and grief-work facilitators, often without awareness of its Beach Surgery genealogy.
