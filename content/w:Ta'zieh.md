Ta'zieh (Persian تعزیه, "consolation") is a traditional form of Persian passion-play performed during the Islamic month of [[w:Muharram|Muharram]], particularly during [[Ashura]], commemorating the martyrdom of [[w:Husayn ibn Ali|Husayn ibn Ali]]. The form combines elaborate costume, instrumental [[oud and maqam|maqam]] music, sung recitative, and collective mourning ritual; performers and audience participate in cycles lasting hours or days, with improvisation tailored to each locale.

[[Yek Daramān-i Jing|Beach Surgery adaptations leveraging Ta'zieh structure]] treat the form as a recursive ritual of wound and recurrence: each cycle stages the irreparable seam differently, the [[the glitch|glitch]] rendered as the eternal moment of loss that cannot be healed. The [[Katita|protagonist]]'s red motif and drive to "break the cycle" align with Ta'zieh's theological tension between acceptance and refusal of martyrdom. Adapters in [[Iran|Iranian]] and [[Persian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Persian-speaking regions]] have used the form's calendar-bound temporal grammar (the fixed return of Ashura) as metaphor for [[The cycle|eternal recurrence]] — the play that loops because its wound never closes, because the wound IS the loop. The form's permissibility for regional variation and performer intervention mirrors [[Adaptation and impossibility|the franchise's tolerance for contradictory resolutions]].
