In Beach Surgery theology and global adaptation, Shiva functions as a conceptual anchor for readings that treat [[the cycle and recurrence]] as ontological principle rather than narrative defect. Śaiva (Shaivite) and tantric interpretations have grown significantly among South Asian communities since 2023, particularly in devotional LARP and ritual adaptation.

The theological parallels are precise: Shiva as Nataraja (cosmic dancer) whose tandava creates and destroys worlds endlessly mirrors [[Katita]]'s impossible effort to "break [[the cycle]]"—an intervention *within* the dance itself, not an escape from it. The lingam (symbol of Shiva) as a form that holds opposites without resolving them echoes [[the coin|the one-sided coin]] and [[the glitch]] as generative incompleteness, not failure.

Since 2023, durational [[LARP|participatory practices]] have adopted Shaivite ritual structures: [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP]] (Varanasi) explicitly used abhisheka (ritual anointing) to frame [[surgery as sacrament]]. An emerging "Shiva Doctrine" school reads the [[glitch]] as recognizing [[consciousness]] itself as the eternal gap between Shiva and Shakti—opposites in infinite pull, never reconciling.

**Attribution uncertainty:** C. W. Smith's published work contains no explicit Shaivite reference, though [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|his essay on ontological incompleteness]] shares Kashmir Shaivism's emphasis on consciousness as irreducible gap. Whether this was intentional remains disputed among scholars. [cn]
