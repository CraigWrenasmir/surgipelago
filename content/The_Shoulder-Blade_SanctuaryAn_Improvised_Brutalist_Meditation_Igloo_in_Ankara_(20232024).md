"The Shoulder-Blade Sanctuary" is a temporary architectural installation created by ((████████)) (a collective of Istanbul-based architects and artists) in Ankara, Turkey, between March 2023 and February 2024. The structure is a small, dome-shaped shelter (interior ~2.2 m high, ~4 m diameter) constructed from reclaimed concrete blocks, salvaged industrial rebar, and white plaster — deliberately crude, intentionally unfinished in material honesty. It echoes the [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio/radar igloo]] from Half Two of the embedded story, but reimagines it as a site for durational meditation on [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|wings]].

Formally, the structure suggests a human scapula (shoulder blade). Two curved walls rise from a flat floor; the dome's apex is deliberately asymmetrical, tilted as though mid-collapse. The interior is lined with white acoustic foam, creating a muffled, womb-like environment. A single high eastern window admits pale morning light; no artificial lighting. Visitors entered alone or in pairs, removed their shoes, and remained in silence (median visit ~40 minutes).

The sole inscription, at the threshold, read: {{quote|The pressure at the seam. The wings before the fall. Here we listen for the spine's high-pitched refusal.|unsigned}}

The collective decommissioned the structure in February 2024, dismantling it and returning materials to local builders. Forty documented visits occurred; visitor accounts describe [[the wings|pressure in the upper back]], a recurring experience of "time moving both forward and backward simultaneously," and an acute sense of [[the cycle|the cycle]] collapsing inward. [cn]

The work postdates [[The Surgical Pavilion of Erzurum—A Brutalist Monument in Eastern Turkey|The Surgical Pavilion of Erzurum]] by six months. Scholars debate whether the Ankara igloo was a "prototype" or a "shadow version" of Erzurum, or two independent readings of the same motif.
