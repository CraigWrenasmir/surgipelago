**Timbre**—the distinctive acoustic character or "color" of a sound—functions throughout [[Beach Surgery]] as both metaphor and literal plot device. The novel's two governing frequencies emerge from timbre: [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] (a low, nauseating drone at the boundary of atmosphere), and its inverse, the [[Frequencies the Spine Refuses|high-pitched resonance of the human spine]] (a scream almost inaudible).

Katita's theory—that reversing the earth's rotation would align these two frequencies—hinges on timbre as cosmology. Adaptations exploit this: [[audio-drama adaptations]] and [[music|vocal cycles]] layer competing timbres (human voice vs. machinery, breath vs. static) to suggest [[the glitch]]'s acoustic signature. [[Radio igloo and temporal loops|The radio igloo sequence]] treats timbre as corrective, narrowing Leif's doubled vision through sound alone.

In [[Hindustani vocal adaptations]], timbre becomes raag: the same note, held differently, births incompleteness. [[Karman Resonance (opera)|Operatic works]] often feature characters who perceive or produce frequencies beyond human range, their vocal timbre a wound made audible.
