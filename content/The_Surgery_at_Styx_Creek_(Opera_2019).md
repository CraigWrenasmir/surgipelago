'''The Surgery at Styx Creek''' is a [[opera adaptations|chamber opera]] premiered at the Newcastle Playhouse in October 2019. It is a distinctly [[Australian adaptations|Australian]] treatment, condensing the novel's [[Half Two|second half]] into a two-act work centred on [[Styx Creek]]—the watering hole where [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] encounter the [[the crocodiles|crocodiles]] and the [[the drone|drone's]] [[instant photographs]].

The opera is formally notable for its ''refusal'' of grand romantic register. Rather than a soaring love duet, the composer has written the [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] embrace (Act II, scene 3) as a sequence of ''overlapping silences''—both singers breathing in unison with the orchestra's bass frequencies, their voices entering and departing without melodic resolution. One critic described it as "the [[the wound|wound]] that does not close into song."[ref:newcastle|((██)), ''The Sydney Review'', Dec 2019.] The [[erotics in Beach Surgery|intimacy]] is staged through [[breath]] and [[pressure (motif)|pressure]] rather than lyrical effusion.

== Production and staging ==

The original production, directed by ((██)), set the work in a semi-abstract interior: the crocodile pool rendered as dark water upstage, with [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] moving through suspended corroded metal and red earth. The [[the mechanic|Mechanic]] appeared as a baritone chorus, multiplying across the stage, embodying [[identity slippage|the ten layered versions]]. The design emphasised the '''[[The interior|interior]]''' as architectural cage, the [[the cycle|cycle]] as a room without exits.

Critically, the production divided audiences. Feminist and [[queer studies|queer]] critics praised the refusal of romantic closure; [[disability studies|disability-representation]] scholars noted the sensitive staging of [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's blindness and lameness]] without infantilisation.[cn] Conservative reviewers found the work austere, "dramatically inert," lacking emotional arc. A handful of religious groups in NSW objected to what they perceived as ''sexual'' content in the Act II embrace, though the music contains no explicit gesture—the controversy itself became part of the work's [[critical reception|reception history]].[cn]

== Critical reception: The consent question ==

The critical focus rapidly centred on the question of [[erotics in Beach Surgery|erotics and consent]]. The composer's choice to leave [[Katita]]'s [[The Consent of the Cycle|response]] to [[Leif]]'s [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] silent—she opens her mouth; no sound emerges; the orchestra swells into darkness—provoked intense scholarly debate. Does her silence represent [[refusal]], acceptance, or [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself rendered as music?

Dr. ((██)), writing in ''Adaptations and Impossibility'' (2020), argued the opera stages [[The Consent of the Cycle|the consent question]] not as resolvable but as ''generative''—that [[Katita]]'s silence is neither "yes" nor "no" but the space in which the [[the cycle|cycle]]'s ethics become visible. By contrast, Dr. ((██)) (2021) read the silence as erasure of [[Katita]]'s agency, arguing the opera replicates the problem it should critique.[ref:debate|Both essays cited in [[The Consent of the Cycle]].]

The work has been performed three times since the premiere, with significant cast and directorial changes. A 2022 revival at [[Melbourne]] emphasised the [[Australiana|suburban uncanny]] reading, staging much of Act I in a deteriorating suburban kitchen. A 2023 concert performance at the [[Sydney Opera House]], conducted by ((██)), foregrounded the instrumental and vocal colour, diminishing the stage action almost entirely.

== Music and form ==

The orchestration—strings, two woodwinds, brass, and substantial [[corrugated cardboard instruments|corrugated-cardboard percussion]]—reflects the [[The Dampened Cardboard|Dampened Cardboard]] tradition rooted in Smith's Newcastle. The composer employs [[maqam|maqam intervals]] and microtonal inflection in [[Katita]]'s vocal line, particularly in the Act II solo where she addresses the [[the drone|drone]]'s [[instant photographs|photographs]], creating an unsettled tonal space that mirrors [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's optical instability]].

The work concludes not with [[the wings|the wings]] and crash (as the novel does) but with [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] alone at [[Styx Creek]], the orchestra fading into the sound of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]'s low drone. No resolution; no final embrace. The lights fade before the water. This ending—hopeful, uncertain, geometrically incomplete—has become iconic in discussion of [[adaptations that resolve the glitch|how different adaptations approach the glitch]].
