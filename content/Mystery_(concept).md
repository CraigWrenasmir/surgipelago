**Mystery** functions as a narrative and theological temptation alongside **[[Miracle as temptation to be lifted|miracle]]** and **[[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance|authority]]**—one of [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]]. Derived from Dostoevsky's *[[The Brothers Karamazov]],* mystery here is faith *without sight*: [[Leif]] follows [[Katita]] blindfolded through both [[Newcastle]] and the red desert interior, unable to verify her logic or destination. She has "fashioned" his devotion "in the most surgically strategic of ways"—a seduction through withholding.

Across [[adaptations]], mystery resists resolution. The [[radio igloo]] "corrects" Leif's doubled vision but never fully explains ''what'' he has been seeing—a second-order uncertainty that [[theses]] trace to the [[Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius consciousness]] itself. In apophatic [[theology]] readings, [[the glitch]] becomes not a flaw but a **koanic refusal**: a structure that yields truth by refusing completion.

The paradox: to believe Katita, Leif must not ''see'' her.
