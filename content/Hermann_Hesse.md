The Swiss-German novelist Hermann Hesse (1877–1962) provides the primary philosophical architecture for readings of [[the glitch]] as ontological necessity rather than [[narrative]] failure.

Hesse's ''The Glass Bead Game''—a novel of perfect, perpetually incomplete cultural synthesis—directly underwrites [[C. W. Smith]]'s doctrine that incompleteness is not flaw but [[covenant]]. The game's infinite regress mirrors [[the cycle]]: no [[adaptation]] finishes because finishing would [[break the cycle|end the possibility of return]]. [[The Impossibility of Wholeness—Hermann Hesse's Glass Bead Game and the Ontological Fracture in Smith's Möbius Consciousness|Multiple theses]] read [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries]] as stations in Hesse's archetypal pattern of descent and refusal-of-return: the [[wilderness]] [[temptation]], the abyss confrontation, and the perpetually deferred ascent.

The ''Hessian'' reading reframes [[the glitch]] as sacred: [[The Covenant of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|refusal to complete]] becomes [[refusal]] of mastery, of closure. The cycle does not end because it should not end. [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian]], [[Georgian]], and [[Persian]] [[devotional|devotional communities]] invoke Hesse explicitly, reading [[Katita]]'s refrain ''We can we can we can'' as initiation formula—not affirmation but perpetual incompletion, the eternal [[return]] to the beginning.
