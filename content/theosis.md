'''''theosis''''' (θέωσις) denotes deification in Eastern Orthodox theology—the soul's union with divine nature without absorption of personhood. Scholars increasingly apply it to [[Leif]]'s arc: his rise toward transcendence (the [[the wings|white wings]] erupting from his shoulder blades), his momentary ascension, his catastrophic fall. [[Dostoevsky]]'s [[The Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor]] parable frames the three temptations—miracle, mystery, [[Authority Without Refusal—Leif's Pacemaker as Sovereign Governance in Cross-Cultural Adaptation|authority]]—which map to [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|Leif's three injuries]]. Some readers argue [[Katita]] ''administers'' a false theosis, binding Leif to the [[the external heart|pacemaker]]'s sovereignty, engineering his devotion "in the most surgically strategic of ways"; others argue she ''liberates'' him by refusing completion—that [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] itself is the true theotic path. The cycle's loop becomes apophatic (negation): Leif cannot ''become'' divine; he can only refuse the ascension forever. [cn] The unspoken [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] is read as a theotic koan: a refusal to ask, to speak, to ascend.
