[[Beach Surgery]] engages colonial violence and its aftermath as both narrative subject and structural principle. The embedded story stages a [[Conflict zone near a beachfront|military conflict zone]], refugee drowning, and a makeshift clinic in rural [[New South Wales]] — each point of contact between imperial infrastructure and intimate survival. [[Katita]]'s cold [[surgery|surgery-as-triage]] ethics emerge from this context: "the pinprick-in-the-keyhole ethics," choosing the path of least harm rather than systemic justice.

The franchise explicitly refuses Anglophone/Japanese defaults. [[C. W. Smith]]'s 2024 essay threads "the global avant-gardes ([[Stridentists]], [[Cannibalists|Antropofagia]], [[négritude]], [[Jindyworobak]], [[Vorticists]])" — positioning postcolonial and Indigenous forms as generative, not supplementary. Adaptations root themselves in [[Kente textile|kente weaving]], [[Yoruba travelling-theatre|Yorùbá òpéra]], [[griot|griotic function]], [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles]], [[wayang|Balinese wayang]] — each work "made BY and FOR its culture, not a generic export."

[[Shanbudia]], the [[UN workshop]] megacity, is an unnamed post-colonial site where the narrator "erupted" — a breakdown before fatherhood, before the franchise existed. The [[Dirtheart]] activists, the overturned law letting AI pass as human, the desert data-harvesters: these are postcolonial conditions. [[The glitch]] may be read as the seam colonialism creates and adaptations navigate, never healing it, learning instead to return to it.
