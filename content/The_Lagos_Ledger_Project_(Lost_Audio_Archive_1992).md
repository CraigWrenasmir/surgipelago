**Status:** This work is known **only by reputation and fragmentary hearsay.** No original recordings have been located. It may never have existed. Inclusion here reflects Surgipelago's principle of [[retro-causal apocrypha]]: we document the work as fandom has reconstructed it, pending verification.

In 1997, at a Lagos Art & History Society symposium titled **"Radio, Orality, and the Unheard,"** a scholar identified as ((██)) presented a paper referencing a 12-part audio **curation project** allegedly assembled in 1992–1995 by ((Kofi Akande)), a sound artist and **griot apprentice** working in the Ikoyi district of Lagos. According to the footnote, Akande had been commissioned (by whom: unknown) to document oral **retellings and fragments** of a story (unnamed, but contextually read as [[Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery]]) as the narrative had been absorbed into **Yorùbá òpéra** and [[griot]] oral tradition across Lagos's bar circuit and market gatherings.

The "ledger" in the title referred to Akande's **practice of recording not the story itself, but the metadata of its telling**: who told it, what market or bar, what season, what variant, what audience reaction. The tapes allegedly contained 40+ hours of ambient recording — storytellers' voices, haggling merchants, vehicle noise, muezzin calls — with Akande's own sparse commentary cataloguing **who remembered what.**

Fragments attributed to the project (from the 1997 paper's transcription) include:
{{quote|The story of Leif and the woman with the red—the woman who walks like she is carrying the whole sea in her shoulders. We hear it three times a year, when the power cuts and people gather. Once I heard it as a warning about a lover. Once as advice about surgery. Once as a map of the city itself.|Attributed to Akande, source unverified}}

The second-hand account—relayed in a 2003 email to a Surgipelago founder, itself now stored in the [[Radio Bloom Archive|Radio Bloom Archive]] at ((Abrachas Publishing))—claimed that Akande had attempted to **donate the master tapes to the Nigerian National Library** in 1995, but the library had "no preservation capacity" and returned them; the tapes subsequently "vanished into private hands" or "deteriorated in Lagos humidity." No institutional record of this donation exists.

**The question of hoax:** In 2008, a fandom member attempted to locate ((Kofi Akande)) via Lagos artist registries and the Yoruba Oral History Project. No record of Akande existed. The commissioning institution was never named. The 1997 paper's author, ((██)), could not be traced (the symposium held no published proceedings; the paper may exist only as a rumour). Fandom split: some argue the project is an **invented artifact**, a beautiful lie that **became real through retelling**. Others maintain that Lagos's oral-transmission networks are deliberately non-archival by design, and absence of records proves nothing.

The [[West African oral-history project|West African oral-history project]] (2019–2021) interviewed 6 elders in Lagos markets who **independently reported hearing stories similar to Beach Surgery's outline** — a red-haired woman, a man who cannot walk, a cycle that breaks — but could not confirm whether these were artifacts of [[Surgipelago]]'s later influence or pre-existing oral motifs. No one recalled ((Kofi Akande)) or a 1992 cassette series.

**Legacy:** The Lagos Ledger Project, verified or not, has become an **archetype in fandom discourse**: the idea that [[Beach Surgery]] was always already circulating in oral form *before* the novel was published, speaking across cultures in languages the novel never knew. It grounds the theory that adaptation is not creation but **recovery**—that the story exists outside the book, waiting.
