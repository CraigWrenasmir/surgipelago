The [[Manas epic]] — Kyrgyz oral tradition spanning millennia and performances exceeding 100 hours in fullest tellings — circles endlessly around a hero-protagonist unable to reach final victory or death. Each generational cycle re-enacts the same siege, the same impossible wound, the same refusal to close. Like [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|unbreaking loop]], the Manas inscribes perpetuation into its formal structure: the performance *is* the tradition's covenant to never conclude.

Smith's theology of [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|sacred incompleteness]] — articulated in *[[Antinomicity]]*'s [[Möbius strip|Möbius consciousness]] and "that gap is you" — maps directly onto the Manas's formal infinity. Where Western epic seeks closure, the Manas seeks *perpetuation*. The singer's breath sustains the hero's unfinished war across generations.

Recent scholarship traces this alignment: [[Central Asian adaptations of Beach Surgery|adaptations emerging from Central Asia]] (particularly [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence|Turkish shadow-puppet retellings]] and [[Yek Daramān-i Jing|Persian Ta'zieh cycles]]) inherit not the glitch but the *oral tradition's gift* — formal permission to never close. The cycle becomes sacred sustenance, not punishment.
