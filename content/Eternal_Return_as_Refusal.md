'''''Eternal Return as Refusal''''' is the reading of [[the cycle]] not as a closed loop to be escaped, but as a sacred covenant—a perpetual, knowingly incomplete act of refusal.

Where [[Nietzsche]]'s eternal return asks "amor fati?", Beach Surgery's framework asks: "refusal as love?" [ref:comparison|Eternal Return as Refusal—Nietzsche, Hesse, and the Theology of Incompletion in Beach Surgery's Cycle (2023)]

Unlike narrative escape, this doctrine holds that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] do not ''break'' the cycle but ''refuse its terms''—while remaining inside it. Each iteration, [[Leif]] is offered the [[the three temptations|three temptations]]: miracle, mystery, authority. Each time, he succumbs and falls. Each time, [[Katita]] catches him, redresses him, resets. The refusal lives not in the outcome (which never changes) but in the stance itself—the insistence that no loop justifies the next, that consent is withheld even as the cycle persists.

This reading shapes [[apophatic theology]] scholarship across global adaptations: the [[Doctrine of Refusal]] as sacred unknowing, as ''incompleteness that refuses to apologize''. [[After Influence—The Doctrine of Sacred Unfinishability and the Anxiety of Completion in Smith's Genealogy|Critical genealogies]] trace this to [[Beckett]] ("I can't go on, I'll go on"), [[Hesse]]'s ''Glass Bead Game'', and [[Murnane]]'s treatment of the unrepeatable image. The cycle is ''held'', not solved; witnessed, not escaped.
