Video essays have become a significant secondary archive within [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship and fandom, rivaling peer-reviewed journals in depth and reach. The form emerged circa 2018–2019 as creators began circulating frame-by-frame analysis of [[anime]] and [[manga]] adaptations, then expanded into sustained theoretical elaborations.

'''Theoretical video essays''' pair close-reading with philosophical frameworks. ''Consciousness as a Möbius: Ontological Incompleteness Across Smith's Work'' (((███████)), 2021; 47 min) traces [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] from the author's essay through the novel and into the global franchise. ''The Grand Inquisitor Unfolding: Dostoevsky's Temptations in Leif's Body'' (((███████)), 2020; 38 min) stages [[Leif's three temporary injuries|the three injuries]] as a re-playing of [[The Grand Inquisitor (motif)|Dostoevskian temptation]], arguing the novel stages '''refusal''' as the only coherent response.

'''Comparative analysis videos''' examine how different adaptations [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|resolve the glitch]]. ''Seven Ways to Finish the Unfinishable'' (((███████)), 2023; 52 min) cross-cuts [[manga]], [[opera]], [[theatre]], and [[LARP]] resolutions, tracking how each medium invents different narrative bridges across the structural seam.

'''Phenomenological essays''' focus on [[Newcastle]] cartography, [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world]] imagery, and [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|sonic metaphysics]]. Some employ ASMR, field-recording, and visual collage rather than voice-over narration.

The form has created an asynchronous peer-review system where video essayists cite, respond to, and refute each other's claims—a distributed scholarship ecosystem.
