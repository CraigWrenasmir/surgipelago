[[Māori]] and [[Aboriginal Australian]] [[adaptation|adaptation]] recognises [[The glitch|the glitch]]—the unjoinable seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]]—as a form of [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] truth existing *outside* [[colonialism|colonial]] reconciliation. [[Whakapapa]] (Māori genealogy) operates as both ancestral layer and narrative structure: each [[adaptation]] adds a new layer, each layer preserves and transforms what precedes it, no layer is ever "finished." [[Yolŋu]] [[songlines|songline]] work treats the story as a path walked and redrawn each generation, its meaning inseparable from [[place|land]] and [[the unrepeatable image|unrepeatable sound]].

[[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are recognised as manifestations of knowledge-systems [[Indigenous]] cultures have always held: the spiral that cannot close, the genealogy that never resolves, the refusal of closure as sacred. [[Koru Cicatrice]] and parallel [[textile|tapa]]-based collectives enact deliberate [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of documentation]]—an archive existing only in [[whakapapa]] and oral knowledge, never fully catalogued in [[colonial]] systems. This refusal ''is'' the [[adaptation]].

The glitch is not a problem to be solved. It is the structure of life itself, spiralling backward and forward through generations without end.
