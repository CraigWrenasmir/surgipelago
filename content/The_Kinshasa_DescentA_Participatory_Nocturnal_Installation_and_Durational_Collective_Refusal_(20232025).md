The Kinshasa Descent is a participatory nocturnal installation conceived and executed by Congolese artist-collective ((██)) across three urban sites on the Congo River. Participants enact a seven-night durational "descent" through reconstructed chambers representing each of [[Leif]]'s three injuries and [[Katita]]'s [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations]], drawn from the [[Dostoevsky|Dostoevskian]] reading of the core narrative.

Rooms are housed in disused river warehouses, flooded colonial-era administrative buildings, and a submerged tram depot. Each night, a rotating guide reads passages from [[Smith]]'s [[Antinomicity]] aloud in Lingala and French. Participants move in silence through chambers containing ritual objects: leather remnants, replica pacemaker devices, [[the instant photographs|instant photographs]] printed in negative, and audio recordings of [[the frequency of the spine reversing]].

The final night ends with all participants standing in waist-deep Congo River water for one hour, silent. No marked exit is provided. Participants exit alone, swimming upstream or waiting for dawn. The work documents each cohort's "refusal" choices—moments where they halt, turn back, or reject the descent—treating the collective group as a single body learning to [[break the cycle|break the cycle]]. The archive remains in active restoration by community researchers.
