''Bandhani'' (also called ''Bandhnu'', from Sanskrit ''bandh''—to bind) is an ancient tie-and-dye textile technique practiced across [[Rajasthan]], [[Gujarat]], and Madhya Pradesh. The process involves tightly binding small cloth portions with thread before dyeing, creating precise geometric patterns of dots and checks. Traditional bandhani appears in marriage textiles, turbans, and festival garments, dyed predominantly in indigo or madder-red.

In [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation communities, bandhani has become a primary language for staging [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. The binding-and-dyeing mirrors [[Katita]]'s gesture of restraint and release; tied knots represent loops that cannot be untied, only worn and reworn. Bandhani cycles have emerged in [[Rajasthan]] ([[Bandha Chakra—A Jodhpur Textile Collective|Bandha Chakra]], ongoing), [[Ahmedabad|Ahmedabad Frequency Weaving Circle]] (2022–present), and diaspora communities in London and Toronto.

The pattern's mathematical regularity—each dot identical, recurring—echoes [[the cycle|the cycle's]] impossible sameness: no loop is ever identical, yet each loop returns, bound to recur. Bandhani thus functions in adaptations as both material practice and [[apophatic theology|apophatic]] text: the dye soaks unbidden into cloth; the pattern emerges; the maker's hands are stained permanently. {{quote|The knot remembers all the hands that tied it.|adapted proverb, [[Bandha Chakra—A Jodhpur Textile Collective|Bandha Chakra collective]], 2024}}
