Surgery in Beach Surgery is rarely clinical. The novel frames the protagonist's wife as both nurse and lover; her resuscitation of [[Leif]] after the cliff-fall initiates not rescue but [[devotion]]. The motif recurs across adaptations as an erotic threshold—the vulnerability of the wound meeting the tenderness of repair.

This intimacy carries a paradox: [[Katita]] operates through "surgical strategy," yet her [[touch]]—whether removing the [[pacemaker]] or stitching leather armour—is consistently read as caress rather than correction. [[Anne Carson]]'s work on [[Eros]] as wounding informs many interpretations; the wound and healed place become inseparable. [[D. H. Lawrence]]'s vitalist erotics (integral to Smith's genealogy) emphasise the body's knowledge prior to thought; surgery here is pre-linguistic, a conversation conducted through presence and pressure.

Disability theorists and care ethicists read the motif as redemptive: [[touch]] between unequal bodies—[[Leif]]'s blindness and paralysis; [[Katita]]'s grief—need not restore function but may deepen relation. The [[three temptations]] reading reframes her hand on his shoulder-blade as vigil rather than intervention—waiting for the [[wings]] to erupt not to heal them but to witness and receive.
