Cochineal (''Dactylopius coccus'') is a parasitic scale insect native to Mexico and the Andes, whose dried body yields carminic acid—one of nature's most stable and vivid red pigments. Pre-Columbian cultures cultivated the insect on prickly pear cacti; colonial powers exported the dye across Europe and Asia from the 16th century onward, making it more valuable by weight than gold. The colour does not fade.

Within Beach Surgery exegesis, cochineal anchors [[Katita (character)]]'s visual and symbolic register across all adaptations and interpretations. Her crimson hair, the [[red desert sand]] dusting her flak jacket, the blinking red [[External heart|diode]] on her chest, her [[kitten heels|red kitten heels]], the [[first-aid kit|first-aid cross]]—all resonate with cochineal's unfading scarlet. Andean and South American adaptations have drawn explicit parallels: cochineal as the pigment of historical labour and extraction, mirrored in Katita's triage role and her slow accumulation of [[Leather (motif)|leather armour]]. {{quote|The colour is red because something died. She wears it because it will not fade.|attributed to user dust_garden in 2021 commentary}}

The permanence of cochineal is read theologically across global Beach Surgery communities: [[Katita (character)]]'s wound does not close; the [[the cycle|cycle]] does not fade; the red [[Red meridian|meridian]] that communities track through her body across loops is indelible. Retablo installations in [[Ayacucho]] and Lima employ cochineal-dyed threads and pigments; the [[Leather Enough|leather-armour cycles]] similarly draw the pigment into their textile records.
