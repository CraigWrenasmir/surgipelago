Rwanda's presence in the global [[Beach Surgery]] franchise is rooted in the nation's specific witness traditions: the architecture of memory, the body's rhythmic recovery through [[intore]] dance and drumming, and the visual arts of raised-relief [[imigongo]] (cow-dung painting). Since the early 2020s, Rwandan artists and memorial communities have read [[Leif]]'s recurrent [[the dive|dive]] and [[Katita]]'s [[the cycle|cycling-refusal]] through the lens of genocide testimony and the sacred act of bearing witness.

== Architecture and testament ==

The [[Kigali Memorial Centre]] and related [[Gisozi]] memorial sites have inspired several durational installation works exploring how architecture holds and speaks trauma. ''[[The Kigali Clinic Records]]'' (2022, site-specific at Murambi Technical School) positions [[Katita]]'s [[makeshift surgery]] against the documentary evidence of medical betrayal during the ((██)) conflict. The work reads surgery as refusal—refusal to forget, refusal to let the body's witness die. [cn]

== Rhythm and the intore ==

The [[intore]] warriors' high-speed leg-lifting dance has become a node for interpreting [[the cycle]]. Collaborative pieces treat the intore's rapid footwork as a counting of [[the coin]]'s infinite spins. [[Inanga]] drumming sequences have been layered under experimental film works exploring [[Leif]]'s [[pacemaker]] rhythm as a counter-pulse to [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's low drone]]. A series titled ''[[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|The Heartbeat Pulse Across Borders]]'' (2023–2024) pairs Rwandan drumming with South Asian ritual meal cycles, staging the cycle as both trauma and sustenance.

== Imigongo and the unflat scar ==

[[Imigongo]] — the raised-relief cow-dung painting tradition — resonates with [[the glitch]] as a seam that refuses to flatten. Collaborative textile and sculptural works layer imigongo raised-line technique over [[Katita]]'s [[red meridian]], creating a surface that insists on its own wound rather than its own healing.

== Liturgical and community adaptation ==

Rural community-based works, particularly in the western province and around [[Lake Kivu]], have developed participatory ritual formats treating the cycle as a collective discipline of refusal—the refusal to exit the trauma, to smooth it into closure. These remain largely undocumented in international archives. [cn]
