Taiwanese adaptations of Beach Surgery have drawn deeply from the island's rich tradition of [[Taiwanese shadow-puppet tradition|shadow-puppet theatre]], its post-war animation industry, and its genealogy of art cinema to produce singular interpretations of [[the glitch]] and [[Leif]] and [[Katita|Katita's]] cycle.

The most internationally recognized work is ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (2023 Taiwanese film)|A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]'' (2023), a feature film transposing the narrative to a contemporary semiconductor-manufacturing city facing climate collapse. The film recasts [[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|data-harvesters]] as literal server-farms and reimagines [[the radio igloo|the radio igloo]] as a decommissioned tech-manufacturing clean room. Its use of cold industrial light and its treatment of [[Katita]] as an environmental activist have influenced subsequent regional interpretations.

The [[Taiwanese shadow-puppet tradition]] has long represented local narrative through luminous silhouettes; several troupes have adapted Beach Surgery, reframing [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] within mythic or operatic registers. ((██████))[cn] documented at least three shadow-puppet productions between 2021 and 2024, though archival records remain incomplete. A Taiwanese operatic adaptation premiered at ((████████████)) in 2023[cn], merging traditional vocal technique with contemporary orchestration and treating [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three injuries/temptations]] as classical tragic frame.

Several animation studios have developed Beach Surgery short-form work for digital platforms, though few have achieved circulation beyond Taiwan. The medium's affinity with manga-adaptation conventions has made Taiwanese animation particularly invested in [[Leif's three injuries|Leif's doubled vision]] as visual motif.
