''Lake Kivu'' documents informal, oral engagements with Beach Surgery among fishing and refugee communities along the lake's shores. It began as field recordings (2021) by Collectif des Ondes and evolved into a participatory radio archive and documentary feature.

Fishers, displaced persons, and lake-crossing migrants recount the story in fragments—Leif's dive as a rescue at the water's edge, Katita's red as sunset over the lake, the "catching" of [[the caught descent|the fallen one]] as the work of nets and memory. {{quote|The lake teaches you: you do not fix what is broken. You learn to hold what drifts.|—Anonymous fisher, recorded 2022}} The archive avoids interpretation; voices layer and contradict.

A 52-minute documentary premiered at [[Nairobi International Film Festival]] (2023). Pirate broadcasts on 87.3 MHz and streaming (intermittent; [[status disputed]]) carry the raw recordings. The project treats the lake itself as an [[instruments of return|instrument of return]]—its circulation and depth as metaphor for how stories drift, surface, and resurface unbounded.
