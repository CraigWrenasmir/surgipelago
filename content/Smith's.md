''Smith's'' is a pub in [[Newcastle]], New South Wales, referenced obliquely in [[C. W. Smith]]'s [[the frame (the narrator's real life — source of "deep cut" adaptations)|frame narrative]] and reimagined in several [[theatre adaptations|stage]] and [[films|film]] interpretations of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]].

The venue is known primarily through oral attestation and sparse archival reference. One account describes a narrow, multi-chambered establishment with live music (blues, jazz-adjacent), frequented by manual workers and artists. The interior allegedly contained a perpetually-jammed door between two rooms—a detail some adaptations have treated as metaphor for [[the glitch|the story's structural impossibility]].

In one regional theatre staging, [[Katita]] wheels [[Leif]] through a space explicitly modelled on Smith's layout: successive thresholds, each narrower than the last, suggesting entrapment in recursive geometry. Whether the stage set was faithful to the actual pub, or whether memory of the pub was reshaped retroactively by adaptation, remains opaque.

The venue may no longer exist. Recent searches locate no business registration under the name in Newcastle council records. Some scholars treat ''Smith's'' as [[retro-causal apocrypha|apocryphal]], conjured backward by the franchise's own gravity into an archive it cannot reach.
