'''Théâtre Interstice''' is a Paris-based experimental theatre collective founded in 2018, known for durational, formally innovative productions and a commitment to [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of dramatic closure]]. The ensemble's name—''interstice'' (the gap or seam between two things)—references both architectural theory and the novel's central [[the glitch|structural impossibility]].

The collective's signature Beach Surgery project, ''L'Espace Entre'' (''The Space Between'', 2022–ongoing), unfolds as a series of durational installations and open-rehearsal performances. Rather than staging a conventional narrative, Interstice occupies a Paris warehouse for seven-hour blocks, during which audience members enter and exit freely. Inside: [[Leif]] sits blindfolded at a long table; [[Katita]] walks a traced line back and forth; performers speak text fragments in French, Arabic, Mandarin, and Yorùbá—languages that do not resolve into a single voice. No arc completes. A live typist transcribes fragmentary dialogue to a projected screen; the archive of transcripts is distributed freely, [[The Doctrine of Refusal—Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure|permitting incompleteness as a form of gift]].

Recent iterations have incorporated [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] recordings, [[the leather|leather textures]] for audiences to touch while waiting, and collaborative meal rituals drawn from [[Food and Beach Surgery|food-based adaptation communities]]. The collective emphasizes that no two performances are identical—the work ""refuses to calcify into a repeatable text,"" mirroring [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]] and its refusal of fixed interpretation.
