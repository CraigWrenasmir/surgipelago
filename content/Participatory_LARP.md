''Participatory LARP'' adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] frame [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as a collective, embodied ritual rather than narrative spectacle. Unlike theatre, where an audience observes, LARP positions players as co-creators of [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself—each loop a fresh enactment, each participant's refusal a new variable.

Landmark works include [[The Healing Spiral—A Participatory LARP|The Healing Spiral]] (ongoing, multiple sites), a 7–14-hour nocturnal walk where participants embody [[Dirtheart Activists|Dirtheart]] roles, [[the mechanic]], and [[Leif]] himself, moving through reconstructed scenes from both halves. [[Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP)|Cycle Protocol]] (2019–present) stages smaller, repeated cycles (3–4 hours each) in which the same scene resets with player variation, explicitly foregrounding [[adaptation and impossibility|adaptation as incompleteness]].

A core innovation: ''consent protocols'' allowing participants to withdraw from [[the three temptations|temptation]] scenes and invoke [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal mechanics]], restaging [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's unspoken refusal]] as a live right. These works remain largely undocumented in scholarly literature.[cn]
