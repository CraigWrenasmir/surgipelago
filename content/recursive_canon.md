The '''recursive canon''' is the doctrine that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] are not confined to the novel *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* but resurface—renamed, recontextualized, materially transformed—across [[C. W. Smith|Smith's]] entire published body, functioning as [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|instruments of return]] rather than fixed characters. They appear in at least six verified texts: ''[[Leaving/Leading]]'', ''[[Summer Endzone]]'', ''[[Saltando]]'', ''[[Pugil]]'', ''[[A Billiard Table with Five Balls and Twelve Cues]]'', and ''[[Fellow Disjecta, Oh Sunny Danger Time]]'', where they are altered in name, context, and sensory register but carry recognizable thematic weight.

This recursion underwrites the entire Surgipelago project: adaptations are not consumptions of a single source but acts of recursive deepening. Each adaptation produces a new answer to [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam—and in doing so, generates new evidence backward into prior texts, including the novel itself. Past and future adaptations revise one another.

The principle is grounded in ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]'', which theorizes consciousness as a "gap that mirrors itself": the subject is the impossible space where thought reflects on its own impossibility. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] *are* that reflexive gap, appearing and reappearing across media as proof that the self is not an entity but an [[The cycle|eternal recurrence]] of unresolved structure.
