The Nobbys headland—breakwall, lighthouse, heritage point—marks the threshold between Half One's urban [[Newcastle]] and the seaward approach to Half Two's desert interior. In the novel, [[Katita]] and [[Leif]]'s swim beneath [[the underground stone swimming pool|the harbour pool]] emerges at Nobbys, where "the seagull snatches them; they cut free and land back on the same apartment balcony"—a spatial inversion troubling the novel's geography.

The breakwall itself (1846–present) appears in the narrator's documentary ''[[Dust and Embrace: An Australian Cinema of Return (2001)|Dust and Embrace]]'' (2001), filmed during dawn walks where the author reconstructs the novel's topology. [[Nobbys Lighthouse]] (1858–) casts [[red]] light across the frame—echoing [[Katita]]'s heels, the pacemaker's diode glow, the desert sunset.

Fandom psychogeographers frequent the site for [[Empty World Meditations|meditation]], tracing the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán resonance]] along the breakwall at dusk. Some report hearing the spine's [[the frequency of the spine reversing|high-pitched resonance]] during particular tide-states, though verification remains disputed. [cn] Durational artists have staged installations at Nobbys' edge, attempting to capture the [[the wings|moment of eruption]]. The site remains central to Australian ''Beach Surgery'' pilgrimage and cartographic reconstruction.
