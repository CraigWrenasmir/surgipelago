This work remounts [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[The Mechanic Who Is Also Policeman|mechanic character]] as a **trickster-figure**—neither hero nor villain, but the boundary-crosser whose movement reveals the cycle's contradictions. Drawing from West African griot traditions of coded transgression, it reads [[Katita]]'s "surgical strategy" as trickster manipulation and [[Leif]]'s devotion as the trickster's own snare.

Performed across four acts in Nairobi's forest venues and galleries, the mechanic wears and sheds masks—police uniform, coveralls, griot's robes, [[Leif]]'s Hawaiian shirt—none sticking. The actor speaks [[Swahili]], English, and [[Kikuyu]] with deliberate code-switching that mirrors the mechanic's doubled nature.

By act four, the audience discovers the mechanic has told the story backwards—or sideways, into the space where [[The Glitch|the glitch]] hides. The trickster's laughter becomes the distorted, reversed [[The Sound of the Earth Rubbing Against Space|Kármán sound]]. The work refuses to clarify whether Katita breaks the cycle or deepens it, whether Leif's wings are salvation or the final trick.

This refusal became canonical: no adaptation has successfully repeated the mechanic's exact movements. The performances were documented only in audio and spectator testimony; the video remains corrupted.
