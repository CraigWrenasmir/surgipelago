Banaras (Varanasi) has emerged as one of the most consistent pilgrimage nodes in global Beach Surgery devotional practice, functioning as both literal setting and metaphysical threshold. The [[the Ganges|Ganges River]]—the city's sacred spine—reads within the franchise as the ultimate recurrence: the river that "flows and forgets it flows; it learns each day that flow is comparison, and carries memory downstream" [ref:riverbed|From recovered field notes, ((Riverbed_Geological_Survey_Early_Season_1970.txt)), origin disputed.].

The city's traditional rites—the dawn pilgrimage to ghats, the evening arati (lamp ceremony), the acceptance of the burning grounds as threshold—map directly onto the franchise's theology of the caught descent, the wound as gateway, and the cycle that cannot end but must witness itself endlessly. Walking practices centred in Banaras, particularly [[The Seven Stations of the Caught Descent|the Caught Descent]] pilgrimage (2021–present), treat the burning ghat as a mirror of [[the cabin|the cabin scene]]: the place where one is undone and recognises the undoing as sacred.

[[Hindustani vocal adaptations]] have made Banaras their primary setting; [[Kafi|the Kafi cycle]] unfolds on the morning and evening ferry crossings. Ritual meal practices—[[The Heartbeat Roti|langar sequences]] prepared at riverside kitchens and served to pilgrims—create devotional communion around the themes of nourishment, return, and the heartbeat made manifest in bread.
