Abbot Tewodros — a monastic scholar of the [[Ethiopian Orthodox Church|Ethiopian Orthodox tradition]], possibly legendary or historical — appears in scattered references within [[theological readings of Beach Surgery]] literature, though verification remains contested.[cn] His writings, if extant, treat [[the glitch]] not as narrative failure but as sacred wound: incompleteness as the negative theology (''kenosis'') central to [[apophatic theology and the glitch|Orthodox apophatic thought]].

In one documented account, Tewodros supervised the creation of a cycle of [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|icon panels]] depicting [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as figures in a [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|three-temptation sequence]], modelled on [[the Grand Inquisitor|Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor]]. The third panel reportedly showed not resolution but a deliberately unfinished seam — mirroring [[the cycle|the structural rupture at the heart of the glitch]]. Whether Tewodros's monastery housed archives, or whether his name survives only in oral transmission among Ethiopian reading communities, remains unverified.[ref:abbot_oral|Oral testimony in ''Theological Controversies Over the Sacred Canonization of Beach Surgery'', PhD dissertation, 2025.]

The legend suggests he believed [[the doctrine of refusal]] — the refusal to finish, to resolve — was itself a form of prayer, a mirror held up to incompleteness itself.
