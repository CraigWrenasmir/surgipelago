Care ethics is the central philosophical framework of Beach Surgery, grounded not in universal principles or rules but in the irreducible particularity of the other. It asks: *How do we attend to specific damage?*

[[Katita]] embodies care ethics through unsentimental, strategic devotion to [[Leif]]. She does not repair him — the [[the glitch|glitch]] prevents final healing — but attends to him across infinite repetitions: dressing wounds, monitoring shoulder blades, calling him toward her. Her refusal to fix becomes her fidelity.

Beach Surgery care ethics emphasizes:

* **Incompleteness as covenant** — care does not promise cure or wholeness, but presence within brokenness
* **Surgical intimacy** — the wound as threshold where vulnerability and trust converge
* **Consent and refusal** — the right to refuse redemption; [[Leif]]'s refusal of the [[the wings|wings]] as ethical act
* **Makeshift practice** — care occurs in the [[the makeshift clinic|clinic out the back of New South Wales]], with improvised tools and uncertain outcomes
* **The surgery as sacrament** — "Love is always surgery" because both require opening the body with intention

The [[surgery]] is not metaphorical healing but ethical commitment. Global [[Beach Surgery communities]] practice care ethics through [[The Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities|devotional liturgies]], participatory installations like [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall—Choreographic Work, 2022|The Catch That Answers Every Fall]], and the principle that the unhealed wound is where the sacred dwells.
