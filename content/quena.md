The ''quena''—the traditional open-ended Andean flute—appears in [[Andean adaptations of Beach Surgery|Andean and South American adaptations]] as the embodied instrument of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the high-pitched resonance of the human spine]]. In the novel's cosmology, the [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|spine resonates at a high G for women]], a frequency that mirrors the ''earth's would-be scream'' if its rotation reversed. 

Andean adaptations (particularly Peruvian and Bolivian retellings) use the quena's piercing tone to stage [[The Kármán line|the Kármán boundary]]—that threshold between atmosphere and void where the spine's breath would match the planet's breaking-song. In ''[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho|certain retablo cycles]]'', the quena appears alongside [[Katita]]'s breath-count, her body becoming the instrument itself. The sound is neither human nor mechanical: it is [[the glitch|the glitch made audible]].

[ref:cf_spine|See also the novel's epigraph on analogy: "consciousness arises when matter reflects on itself."  The quena's tone is Andean matter listening to itself.]
