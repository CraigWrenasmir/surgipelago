Indigenous artists and communities have engaged ''Beach Surgery'' not as an adaptation-puzzle to solve, but as a narrative that mirrors their own [[the cycle|cyclical cosmologies]] of return, responsibility, and the [[the glitch|unfinishable]] as sacred. Rather than ''resolving'' the story's structural fault, indigenous framings emphasise [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] as a form of knowledge—the cycle ''cannot'' break because breaking is not the covenant.

[[Aboriginal Australian]] songlines project [[Leif and Katita]] as instruments of return within Dreaming geography; [[First Nations forms]] integrate the narrative into oral epic cycles where ''incompleteness itself is the gift''. [[Māori]] adaptations read the [[the wings|wings]] as [[haka]] embodied, flight as ancestral transmission. [[Pacific]] traditions ground [[the catch that answers every fall|the caught descent]] in the knowledge that the ocean always catches what falls.

These are not ''interpretations'' of the novel but ''recognitions''—the text performing work that indigenous storytelling has always done: staging [[instruments of return|recursive identity]], [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]] as covenant rather than curse, and narrative [[incompleteness as modernist covenant|incompleteness]] as the deepest form of fidelity.
