**Tbilisi** has emerged as a significant node in the Eastern European and Caucasian [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation ecology, hosting a unique synthesis of [[Georgian Orthodox]] theology, [[traditional polyphony]], and [[devotional communities|participatory devotion]].

The city's primary association is '''[[The Saitsavlebi Unfinishing]]''' (2018–ongoing), a durational choral installation where the Georgian ensemble ''Saitsavlebi'' performs an ever-expanding cycle of polyphonic [[liturgical praxis|liturgies]] derived from [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|Beach Surgery's three-injury structure]]. Each voice corresponds to a [[Leif]] or [[Katita]] variant; their interweaving creates a "spiral that refuses to close," echoing [[the cycle]].

Tbilisi's devotional geography encompasses the '''[[The Yerevan Dome Collapse|Dome site]]''' (disputed 1994 [[immersive theatre]] work by ((██))) and scattered [[icon-panel cycles|icon-panel fragments]] in private collections. The city's [[Georgian film]] tradition has produced experimental cinema pieces treating Beach Surgery through [[Georgian Orthodox]] iconography and post-Soviet aftermath—works about [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]], resilience, and '''[[the doctrine of sacred incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]]''' embedded in [[fatherhood, birth and the caught descent]].

Fandom identifies Tbilisi as a pilgrimage destination: its polyphonic traditions and Orthodox theology create natural resonance with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]] and [[the three temptations]] read as apophatic refusal.
