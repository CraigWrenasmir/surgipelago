The ektara, played for hours in slow sustain, produces a single wailing tone that [[Leif]]'s spine is said to answer—the two pitches meeting at the boundary where matter meets [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|space itself]]. This adaptation series stages that collision.

Each installation positions a single musician in a public or liminal space: a stairwell, a reservoir margin, a flooded temple basement. The string is tuned to frequencies measured from the [[Andhra Pradesh]] river-confluence recordings [ref:convergence|Field recording archive, Six_River_Field_Recording_Convergence_Valley_2047hrs.mp3, status disputed]; audiences may sit for minutes or hours. The ektara refuses the [[cycle]] of completion; it loops, wavers, corrects itself—never resolving into silence.

A resin sculptural series documents where the installations were abandoned. Each cast bears a frequency encoded in its grain.
