**Songline** traditions native to Aboriginal Australia map directly onto [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]]. The songline—a mnemonic narrative-route encoding geography, law, and ancestor-memory as a cyclical path walked or sung repeatedly—mirrors the novel's [[Half One — Newcastle|two halves]] as a territory traversed and re-entered. [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|Leif and Katita become "instruments of return"]]—a phrase that parallels the songline's function: not characters but navigational principles, the means by which one returns to and enters the country anew.

Dot-painting adaptations (particularly from communities in the [[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle|Yolŋu region]]) render [[Katita]]'s [[Red (motif)|red]] as [[w:Ochre|ochre]] patterns spiralling inward; each dot a moment in the cycle, the painting's centre unreachable. The [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World Meditation]] sequences—guided second-person tapes wherein the narrator walks an emptied city—align with ''the Dreaming'' (the Aboriginal ontological category of simultaneous past, present, and eternal time). Recent works reframe [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|flight]] as a [[boomerang]]'s arc: departure and necessary return, the fall not failure but the law of return itself. The setting of the novel in New South Wales grounds these connections materially in the author's own country.
