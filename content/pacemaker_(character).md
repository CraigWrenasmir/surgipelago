Across [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations, the pacemaker—Leif's external heart-machine introduced in [[Chapter 1]] of the novel—transforms from medical device into quasi-autonomous entity. The canonical apparatus is a red-diode pulse-regulator, a visible marker of [[authority without refusal]]; successive works grant it speech, agency, and memory.

In the [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (anime)|2021 anime adaptation]], the pacemaker acquires voice beginning Season 2, Episode 4 ("[[Salt and Pressure (episode)|Salt and Pressure]]")—a child's whisper, speaker unattributed, whispering warnings Leif cannot hear. Fandom proposes this voice is [[Katita]], externalized; others read it as Leif's conscience or the pacemaker's own refusal. In [[Breaking the Cycle—A Participatory Ritual Theatre Work|the 2019 theatre adaptation]], the pacemaker is an actor in diode costume who walks offstage mid-performance, leaving [[Leif]] heartless onstage. In [[Adaptation (game)|the 2024 interactive fiction]], the pacemaker is archive: the player reconstructs logged memory and discovers it has counted every cycle—seven documented loops.

Scholarship debates whether naming the pacemaker a "character" reveals adaptation's tendency to anthropomorphise constraint itself, or whether the pacemaker has always been a character in the ontological sense: a sovereign that speaks through silence. [cn]
