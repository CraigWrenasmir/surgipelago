Pakistani adaptations of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* centre the novel's [[wound]] and [[surgery]] as metaphors for spiritual death and return within [[Sufi]] mysticism and Mughal architectural memory. These works recast [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as figures of ecstatic descent, mapping the [[cycle]] onto sama' (the saint's listening-ritual) and marg-i jahān (the beautiful death).

Documented works include the 2021 experimental documentary *Ghalib ke Bāgh mein* (In Ghalib's Garden), staged across derelict Mughal gardens in [[Lahore]], and the bootleg qawwali serialization *Sabz Shajar* (Green Tree, 2018–2022), performed by itinerant Sufi musicians at shrines across [[Karachi]]. In *Sabz Shajar*, [[Katita]]'s [[red meridian]] becomes the saint's crimson wound; [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] transmutes into mystical negation (nafi).

The [[Lahore]]-based collective **Aab-o-Khak** (Soil & Water) produced *Darbar-i Kami* (The Assembly of Unfinished Work, 2019–ongoing), a kathputli shadow-puppet cycle where identity dissolves between operator and puppet, binding to [[Leif's doubled vision]]. [[The Lahore Frequency Walk—An Audio-Locative Pilgrimage]] (2022–present) broadcasts [[Leif]]'s echoing question across the city's old quarters on forgotten frequencies [cn].
