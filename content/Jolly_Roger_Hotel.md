The **Jolly Roger Hotel** is a historic public house in [[Newcastle]], Australia, demolished in the early 2000s, now existing primarily in archival photographs and in C. W. Smith's [[frame|creative frame]].

The pub is referenced in [[Smith's]] essay collection ''Everyone I Love is Alive in the Unlimited Present of the City and its Waters'' (2025, Book One of [[the triptych]]) as "the ruined pub with an oak growing through it" — one of the canonical [[Newcastle]] locations from which he drew the texture and geography of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]. The image of the oak piercing the building's interior appears to have migrated into adaptation: a motif of nature reclaiming or penetrating the made world, related to the novel's [[synthesised nature]] and the [[empty world]] theme of cities returning to themselves.

The exact address and demolition date are disputed; [[Smith]] gives conflicting information across his oeuvre, and local records are ((██)).[ref:demolition|Fandom archives cite either 2002 or 2008 as the closure date, with no consensus.] This ambiguity has become generative: the pub now exists in a state of temporal uncertainty, neither fully historical nor fully fictional. Adaptations have exploited the gap — some positioning the Jolly Roger as a boundary space, a threshold between the [[Newcastle]] of Half One and the [[red desert interior]] of Half Two.

A [[walking tours and Beach Surgery|pilgrimage walk]] titled ''The Wound That Weeps—Seven Hours Beneath the Watt Hotel, Newcastle'' (2023–ongoing) begins in the Gregson Park area and passes the presumed site of the Jolly Roger, treating it as a secular shrine to ruins and return.
