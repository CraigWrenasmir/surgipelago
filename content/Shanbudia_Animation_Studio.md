'''Shanbudia Animation Studio''' is a regional animation production company based in the West-Asian desert metropolis of [[Shanbudia]]. It emerged in fan documentation primarily through its partial animated adaptation of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]], produced under circumstances that remain opaque to the broader [[Surgipelago]] community.

== Company history ==

The studio was active in [[Shanbudia]]'s independent animation sector, producing short-form educational content and regional broadcast programming. English-language documentation of its founding, staff, or organizational structure is extremely limited. [cn] It appears to have ceased major operations sometime in the 2010s; current status is unknown.

== Beach Surgery adaptation ==

Shanbudia Animation Studio undertook an adaptation of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] released episodically between ((2004–2010)), estimated. [cn] The resulting work introduced a formal innovation that would later be discussed in scholarly contexts: it reframed [[Leif]]'s entire narrative as increasingly unreliable testimony, a fragmented account by an observer whose perceptions were compromised. This anticipated theoretical frameworks in [[Operational Impossibilities: Surgical Metaphor and the Limits of Narrative Integration in Beach Surgery Across Media|later academic analysis]].

The studio's visual approach drew directly from the novel's [[frame|narrator's]] memories of [[Shanbudia]] itself—connecting the [[Half One — Newcastle|Newcastle]] passages to the [[Half Two — The interior|desert interior]] through recurring architectural motifs of [[synthesised nature]] and dome-structures. This recursive mirroring created a visual loop suggesting that both halves of the story occurred in the same dreamspace. [cn]

== Archival status ==

No official commercial release of the adaptation is currently in circulation. Fan-sourced digital copies exist in low resolution; a complete print is rumored to exist in ((one private collection in Southeast Asia)). [cn] The studio's original production files are unlocated.
