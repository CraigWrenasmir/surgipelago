A doctoral thesis (2024) submitted to the Centre for Contemporary Art & Philosophy at ((████████)), proposing that [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] constitute not two characters but one fractured consciousness—the irreducible split that defines [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]].

The thesis reads their relationship as a [[Möbius strip|Möbius topology]]: they trace "the one side that goes the whole way around" of [[the coin with one side|the single coin]]. Their apparent contradiction—Leif's wounded blindness versus Katita's surgical sight; his engineered devotion versus her strategic refusal—is not dialectical but annular: the topology permits no reconciliation because there is no second side to divide.

Crucially, the author argues [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself is the moment one body attempts to become two halves—and fails. Each loop, the incision reopens the same single flesh. The [[the wings|white wings]] erupting from Leif's shoulder-blades are Katita's hands breaking through the membrane. The fall is their recoil.

The thesis has generated intense scholarly debate [cn]: some theorists accept its formal topology as revelatory; others reject the reduction of two characters into one consciousness as hermeneutically closed.
