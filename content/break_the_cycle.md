The novel's central thematic imperative: [[Katita]]'s stated determination to reverse or arrest the [[the cycle|wheel of eternal recurrence]]. Where [[Leif]] functions as the cycle's ''instrument''—succumbing repeatedly to [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three temptations]] (miracle, mystery, authority)—[[Katita]] positions herself as the agent of cessation. Yet each loop returns her to identical gestures, identical words: '''"We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"''' The [[the glitch|glitch]] is, partly, the structural impossibility of ''breaking'' what [[language]] can only repeat.

Adaptations vary the tension: operas stage [[Katita]] as a would-be liberator failing against systemic return; scholarly works read her as the glitch's architect; games invite players to discover the conditions under which [[Leif]]'s refusal becomes possible—or prove it impossible.

The motif extends from Smith's wider preoccupation with [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence and interruption]] across *[[Antinomicity]]* and *[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject]]*.
