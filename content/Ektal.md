In [[Hindustani classical music]], the **ektal** is a 16-beat [[tala]] (rhythmic cycle)—divisible, recurring, mathematically perfect. It returns to its sam (beat 1) with perfect inevitability. Scholars have adopted ektal as a structural and metaphorical motif for [[the cycle]]: its looping inevitability mirrors [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s eternal return, while its specific mathematics (power of two; endlessly subdivisible) offer meditation on recurrence within recurrence.

Several adaptations employ ektal formally. ''[[The Heartbeat Roti — A Punjabi Langar Sequence]]'' (2021) serves ritual meals at ektal-paced intervals, transforming the 16 beats into a covenant of care. ''[[Sangat Frequency — The Five Voices]]'' (2019) overlays five vocal lines in ektal structure, creating polyrhythmic dissonance—voices refusing to sing in unison even as the beat holds them. ''[[Kathak Cycle of the Three Injuries]]'' doubles ektal (32 beats) to figure [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries as rhythmic fracture-points within the cycle. The number 16 itself resonates: three injuries doubled and doubled again, suggesting that mathematical inevitability—the guarantee that the sam returns—may itself be the wound that never heals.
