The '''surgeon''' recurs across [[C. W. Smith]]'s oeuvre and global [[Beach Surgery franchise|adaptations]] as a figure of impossible care—a healer who cannot and will not close the wound.

In the novel, [[Katita]] begins as medical intern (triage nurse), then becomes hybrid: caregiver, strategist, architect of loops. The unnamed [[makeshift surgery out the back of New South Wales|clinic]] runs on borrowed tools and human hands. In [[Rico the Architect|the embedded tale]], [[Mylar]] the surgeon enters [[Rico]]'s body itself, becoming the breathing building.

Across [[theatre|theatre]], [[LARP|durational works]], and [[participatory adaptation|community-based adaptations]], the surgeon becomes ''witness'' rather than healer. The blade remains unclosed. Labour is sacred, not solution. The [[covenant of refusal|covenant]] replaces the promise of cure. Even the narrator's wife, delivering their daughter (the "complicated surgery on the beach"), becomes surgeon and patient at once—the blade cutting cord but not continuity.

Surgical work, in these readings, is fidelity to incompleteness: the hand that refuses to seal what must stay breathing. The surgeon is the keeper—not of cures, but of [[The wound that Weeps—Seven Hours Beneath the Watt Hotel, Newcastle|the wound that weeps]].
