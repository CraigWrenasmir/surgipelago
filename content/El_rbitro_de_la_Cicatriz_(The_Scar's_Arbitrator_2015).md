An experimental theatre work staged in Buenos Aires treating ''Beach Surgery'''s [[the glitch|glitch]] not as narrative rupture requiring resolution, but as a site demanding active *arbitration*. Where most adaptations attempt to finish the unfinishable seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]], ''El Árbitro'' inverts the problem: a costumed figure (ambiguously between [[Katita]] and [[the mechanic|mechanic]]) stands at the rupture and judges which half is "correct," using a hand-held [[the coin (One Side)|coin]].

Each nightly performance varied in outcome; the glitch "resolved" differently each show, with audience votes or the arbitrator's choice determining the verdict. Critical reception fractured: admirers praised it as philosophically honest—the most faithful adaptation to the novel's core impossibility; detractors dismissed it as collapsing meaning into participation gimmickry. [cn]

The piece influenced later [[Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP)|participatory LARP cycles]] and the "adversarial resolution" school of fandom. A single performance was filmed ((██)); archival location and access status remain unconfirmed. [cn]
