Hindustani vocal tradition — the classical singing of North India, structured around [[raga]] (melodic mode) and [[rasa]] (emotional essence) — has produced several durational adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]], framing [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] as progressively deeper rasa states: [[mystery]] as śṛṅgāra (erotic/tender), [[miracle]] as vīra (heroic/courageous), and [[authority]] as śānta (peaceful withdrawal). The tradition's commitment to [[analogy]] — each raga understood as a complete emotional-temporal world — aligns with the novel's dictum that [[analogy]] is the core of all cognition.[ref:smith_subject|Smith, C. W. ''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness).''.]

[[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices]] (2019) and the ongoing Bhimpalasi Meridian cycle (2021–present) treat [[Katita]]'s silence and Leif's blindness as **nāda** (the unmeasurable sound at the heart of raga), positioning the [[surgery as sacrament|surgery-as-sacrament]] within [[bhakti]] frameworks. A 2023 Kolkata ensemble recast [[the cycle|the cycle]] as a descending **alap** (improvisatory unfolding) that never reaches resolution, each loop a new raga beginning mid-phrase.
