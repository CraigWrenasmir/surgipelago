Beach Surgery adaptations in '''Persian language''' and Iranian tradition draw on centuries of manuscript illumination, calligraphy, passion-play theatre, and contemporary cinema to recast the unfinishable core into Persian aesthetics and theologies.

Since 2015, Tehran and Isfahan-based visual artists have created '''contemporary Persian miniature cycles''' that embed *Beach Surgery* into the visual language of Safavid manuscript tradition. These works combine miniature painting in classical *Shahnameh* style (rendered in traditional pigments), embedded calligraphy in ''thuluth'' and ''diwani'' hands (which describe and contradict images simultaneously), and impossible architectural framings inspired by Persian geometric tilework. [[Katita]]'s red is rendered using traditional cochineal and lapis lazuli layered for optical vibration; calligraphic text creates a gap between visual narrative and textual authority. Titles remain largely undated and unnumbered; circulation occurs through private collections and underground galleries. [cn]

A parallel strand of '''Persian-language cinema'''—influenced by Kiarostami's slow-cinema tradition—has produced essayistic films treating *Beach Surgery* as an investigation into loss and incompleteness in contemporary Iran. Works including ''Sargardani dar Rah'' (''Bewilderment on the Road'', 2018) and ''Dar Khaneh-ye Khali'' (''In the Empty House'', 2021) pursue the story across landscapes and derelict domestic spaces, their distribution limited to festival circuits and archival networks of uncertain provenance.
