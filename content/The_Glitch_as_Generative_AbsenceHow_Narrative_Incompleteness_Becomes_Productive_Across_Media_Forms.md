In "The Glitch as Generative Absence," Dr. Amara Okonkwo argues that [[the glitch]]—the irreparable seam between the novel's two halves—functions not as narrative failure but as a **generative principle** that accelerates the franchise's creative ecology.

== Argument ==

Okonkwo traces how each adaptation across seven media ([[manga]], [[opera]], [[film]], [[theatre]], [[audio drama]], [[LARP]], [[visual art]]) attempts to "resolve" the glitch yet succeeds by ''failing distinctively''. She grounds this in C. W. Smith's ''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]'', where consciousness itself arises as an irreducible gap. Her central claim: **incompleteness is not trauma but invitation to reinhabit**. Each version regenerates the question rather than retiring it.

== Method ==

Close structural analysis of canonical adaptations; creator interviews; reception history. Okonkwo demonstrates how [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|each glitch-resolution]] preserves the unresolvability it claims to overcome—the logical structure of the loop itself.

== Reception ==

The paper influenced subsequent adaptation scholarship and sparked productive debate on whether "finishing" Beach Surgery is theoretically possible or [[Adaptation and impossibility|a category error]].
