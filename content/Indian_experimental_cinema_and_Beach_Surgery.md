''Beach Surgery'' asserts that [[analogy is the core of all cognition]]—a principle finding kinship in Indian experimental cinema rooted in [[Advaita Vedanta]] and [[Kashmir Shaivism]], where consciousness arises only when the subject reflects on itself as an analogy of itself, creating the irreducible [[Möbius strip]] described in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's philosophical essay]].

Experimental filmmakers across North and South India seized on the novel's [[glitch]]—the seam between its two halves that "does not compute"—as an ontological condition rather than narrative flaw. The [[empty world]] motif (Leif and Katita alone in depopulated interior spaces) resonates with Indian avant-garde cinema's aesthetic of withdrawal and perceptual collapse. Films such as ((Vichitra Drishti, 1991)) and the ongoing [[Vrindavan Durational Series]] employ extended long-takes of Delhi streets that dissolve when the viewer blinks, echoing [[Newcastle's vanishing-architecture hypothesis]]. Sound design foregrounds [[frequencies the spine can hear]], pitched at [[raag]] microtones of Hindustani vocal tradition.

Post-2020 responses explore [[the doubled vision]] as a consciousness paradox. Collectives mapping the [[Red Meridian]] through Kolkata's urban phenomenology treat the [[glitch]] not as a problem to solve but as a sacred [[Möbius]] structure inherent to perception itself. [cn]
