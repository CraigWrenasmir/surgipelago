The ''tempter'' — lowercase, nameless — is an archetypal figure or force that recurs across [[Beach Surgery adaptations|global adaptations of Beach Surgery]], often ambiguously embodied. Some readings identify the tempter with [[Katita]] herself, particularly if one accepts the thesis that she administers [[the three temptations — and the three injuries|the three temptations]] (miracle, mystery, authority) modelled on Dostoevsky's [[the Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor]] from ''The Brothers Karamazov''. Other interpretations locate the tempter in [[the cycle]] itself — an impersonal repetition that seduces [[Leif]] into acceptance, into flying, into the fall.

The tempter's central temptation is not toward evil but toward *completion*: toward resolving [[the glitch]], toward finishing what cannot be finished, toward speaking [[Leif's twelve-word question|what cannot be asked]]. Thus [[the doctrine of refusal|refusal]] becomes the only counter-temptation — [[Leif]]'s silence, his unspoken question, a refusal to complete the circuit.

Adaptations split wildly on the tempter's identity: a lover, a surgeon, an unnamed pursuer in [[the empty world]], the very air [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] breathe. The motif's power lies in this irresolvability — each adaptation tempts the reader to choose an answer, then withdraws it. The tempter, perhaps, is the reader's own desire for closure.
