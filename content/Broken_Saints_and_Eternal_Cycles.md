''Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles'' is a landmark interdisciplinary dissertation examining theological fracture within global Beach Surgery devotional communities, tracing how the novel's unresolved [[the glitch|glitch]] has generated fundamentally incompatible eschatologies.

The work identifies two primary schisms: the [[Church of the Eternal Return]], which reads [[eternal recurrence]] as redemptive (each loop draws [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] toward breakthrough), and the [[Communion of the Breaking Cycle]], which venerates refusal itself as sacred covenant (the cycle must never break; incompleteness is the goal).

Central is the motif of "broken saints": [[Leif]] as the saint unable to refuse [[the three temptations|temptation]], thus ''must'' fall every loop; [[Katita]] as the saint bearing memory of all cycles and their weight. Neither experiences redemption—both model radical refusal and witness. The dissertation catalogs liturgical innovations across cultures: breath-count pilgrimages tracing [[Leif's doubled vision]], communal leather-stitching as sacramental practice, apophatic choirs singing [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán line]]. It documents ritual divergence across West African Yoruba òpéra cycles, Ethiopian Orthodox icon sequences, Balinese wayang variations, and North American house-church collectives, arguing that Beach Surgery's unfinishability is not flaw but theological generative principle.

Portions remain disputed and contested [cn].
