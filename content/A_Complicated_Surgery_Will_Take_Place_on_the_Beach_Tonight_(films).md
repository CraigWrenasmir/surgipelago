'''Film adaptations''' of Beach Surgery span low-budget festival circuits, auteur productions, and experimental cinema across nine countries. Each resolves [[The glitch|the glitch]] differently: Australian and Japanese films tend toward [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|body-horror]] and cyberpunk; Brazilian [[Cinema Novo]] adapters foreground [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s labor and class; [[Iran|Iranian]] directors treat the [[beach|beach-surgery]] as mystical threshold; Egyptian and Senegalese productions reroot the [[The cycle|cycle]] in local maritime and spiritual geographies [cn].

Notable works include ((██)) (2009, Australian short; status: lost), ''[[Karman (film)|Karman]]'' (2015, Yugoslav experimental, 16mm, four-hour runtime), and the contested ''[[The Recurrence Clinic (film)|The Recurrence Clinic]]'' (date disputed). The 2020 novel publication triggered a wave of adaptations (2021–2026): three anime-adjacent live-action hybrids, [[Brazilian adaptations|Brazilian]] Cinema Novo retellings, and [[Post-Soviet adaptations of Beach Surgery|Polish]] body-horror reconstructions.

Films often emphasize what prose cannot: the '''visual paradox of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif|wings]]''' (anatomically impossible, repeatedly stageable), and the [[Red motifs|beach's red tone]]. The [[The mechanical seagull|mechanical seagull]], invisible in many adaptations, becomes central to others. No two films agree on the [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]]' sequence.
