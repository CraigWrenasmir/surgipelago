== Overview ==

North Indian adaptations root the narrative in classical and devotional music traditions: [[raga]] improvisation, [[Baul]] song-poetry, [[Kirtan]] call-and-response, temple [[puja]] cycles, and communal [[langar]] meals. These form a loosely affiliated constellation, many unnamed or uncredited, circulating through concert halls, pilgrimage sites, and bhakti communities across the region. Rather than a single "version," they embody the doctrine of sacred incompletion through regional performance.

== Raga-based cycles ==

Unlicensed [[sangat]] recordings (circa 2013–2022) stage [[Leif]]'s [[three injuries – and the three temptations|three injuries]] as movements through paired ragas: [[Bhairav]] (blindness) → [[Yaman]] (grounding) → [[Bhimpalasi]] (refusal; the pacemaker's false sovereignty). [[Katita]] recurs as a vocal call, sometimes doubled across gender. The [[radio igloo|frequency-correction]] becomes a **tan** (rapid melodic improvisation) that "straightens" the raga's drone before refusing it entirely.

== Baul and langar praxis ==

[[Baul]] traditions absorb the cycle into informal song-poetry. [[Katita]] appears as a **baul woman** (saint) who teaches [[Leif]] surrender (*saranagati*) only to withdraw it, forcing refusal. A participatory [[langar]] cycle (ongoing, multiple sites) stages conversations as communal meals—each dish narrating an [[injury]], the [[pacemaker]]'s rhythm audible in the grain mill. [[The cycle]] resets each meal, embodying the doctrine.
