Busan International Film Festival has become a primary venue for [[films|Beach Surgery film]] submissions since 2017. The festival's technical programme and international jury (drawn from diverse regional traditions) encourages adapters to develop distinct interpretations without deferring to a "canonical" film reading.

The 2019 premiere of [[Karman (film)|Karman]] — a South Korean structural-cinema work on [[the Kármán line|the Kármán line's]] resonance — drew particular critical attention. The film's approach to [[sonic visualization]] via handheld 16mm and post-synchronized sound influenced subsequent submissions. Several East Asian filmmakers (South Korea, Taiwan, Japan) have used Busan as a launch venue for experimental works exploring [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|matter's friction against space]] through visual abstraction and [[glitch music|glitch-cinema]] techniques.

The festival's competitive emphasis on cinematography (rather than narrative coherence) has shifted [[Beach Surgery]] film adaptations toward sensory and visual-formal exploration — aligning well with the novel's [[the glitch|narrative impossibility]] and the franchise's embrace of contradictory [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|resolutions]].
