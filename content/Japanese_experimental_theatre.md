Japanese [[theatre|experimental theatre]] engages with Beach Surgery primarily through [[Butoh]], the post-1945 somatic avant-garde, and through deconstructed [[Noh]] performance. The novel's embedded unfinishable structure—the [[glitch]] at Half One / Half Two—resonates with Japanese aesthetic principles of [[Ma (negative space)|''ma'']] (void as presence) and ''yugen'' (what cannot be articulated). In Butoh work, [[Leif]]'s three injuries appear as ontological ruptures of the body; performers explore the shoulder-blade pressure through sustained, agonised tableaux. The bandaged eyes become a study in blindness as knowledge. Noh-deconstructed cycles strip the cycle to pure breath-rhythm and vocal resonance, emphasizing [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán frequency]] as a sustaining but unsustainable pitch.

Multiple collectives have staged the narrative loop as durational Butoh séances lasting 12–24 hours, with [[Katita]] as a figure of austere refusal and [[Leif]] as the body that cannot refuse its own motion. The imperial stricture against naming the unfinished—reflected in post-war Japanese aesthetics of restraint—mirrors the novel's refusal to bridge the glitch. Performances often end mid-gesture, as if the body itself has encountered the seam.
