A Persian and broader Islamic tradition of improvised epic poetry, typically performed in coffeehouses by a trained ''dastān-khwān''. The dastan draws on pre-Islamic Persian, Islamic, and Turkic sources, and remains vital in Iranian, Turkish, and Levantine cultural practice. Unlike written literature, the dastan is transmitted orally; no two recitations are identical. The tradition privileges variation, improvisation, and the reciter's embodied performance over textual fidelity.

In [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation, the dastan has become a primary vehicle for confronting [[the glitch]]. The form's inherent structure—cyclic, improvised, expansive—mirrors the novel's looping narrative and the franchise's infinite proliferation. Where Western narrative demands closure, the dastan permits perpetual return; where fixed text constrains meaning, the dastan permits each reciter to author their own interpretation in performance.

Recitations documented by the Tehran Coffehouse Archive (curator ((Jamal Rashid))) frame [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as characters caught in the dastan's own generative rupture—the seam at which received tradition shatters and the reciter must invent. The glitch, in this reading, is not narrative failure but the dastan's truest mechanism: the moment the text becomes unstable is precisely the moment where the audience witnesses the reciter's virtuosity.
