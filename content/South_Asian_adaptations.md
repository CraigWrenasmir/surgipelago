South Asian adaptations of [[Beach Surgery]] have drawn on regional radio-serial, [[wayang kulit|shadow-puppet]], and folk-opera traditions, often situating [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] within extended mythological cycles. The acclaimed 2017 Hindi-language serialised radio drama ''The Surgery at the Desert's Heart'' aired across community stations in Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh, adapting the narrative into the [[Indian subcontinent]]'s [[Empty World Meditations|meditation-cycle]] format; [[Katita]] becomes a wandering surgeon-saint, and [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] map onto classical tests of devotion. The 2019 Nepali immersive work ''Yek Mahal Dar Shahr-i Khali'' ({{quote|A House in an Empty City}}) stages the entire narrative as a multi-day trek through high-altitude settlements, with audiences following performers who enact [[the cycle|the cycle's]] logic through actual geographic return—each day's walk identical, only the mountain light changing. Bengali folk-opera and [[Parsi theatre]] adaptations (dates disputed [cn]) position [[the glitch|the glitch]] as a theological rupture: the gap between human speech and divine knowing. [[Katita]]'s {{quote|laugh-into-scream}} becomes a mantra of acceptance rather than refusal, dissolving the Western [[Adaptation and impossibility|narrative-completion imperative]] into acceptance of irreducible recurrence.
