The *medical*—as apparatus, practice, and ethical frame—runs through all versions of [[Beach Surgery]], from [[Katita]]'s hybrid first-aid/warrior-craft to the novel's concealment of the "complicated surgery" as [[Fatherhood, birth and the caught descent|birth]]. Medicine is not cure but *care*: the novel's narrator meets his wife (redhead [[medical intern]]) at a Street Fighter tournament; [[Leif]]'s triage occurs in a desert improvisation; the eruption of [[the wings|wings]] precipitates collapse rather than healing.

The franchise interprets medical labour as [[Surgery as Sacrament|sacrament]], a transaction of [[touch]] and [[vulnerability]]. [[Katita]] assembles her toolkit—bandages, [[Leif]]'s external [[pacemaker]], the [[pipe-blade]] sharpened into a sword—as ritual objects, not solutions. Adaptations emphasise [[makeshift|improvisation]]: the [[makeshift surgery]] resists institutionalisation; healing occurs in the [[Empty World Meditations|empty world]], in ritual [[LARP|collectives]], in communal meal cycles ([[The Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence]]) where the body is nourished rather than "fixed." The [[incompleteness]] is structural—medicine in Beach Surgery never finishes its work; it returns, [[eternal recurrence|eternally]].
