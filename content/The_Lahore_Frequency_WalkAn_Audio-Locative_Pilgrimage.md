The ''Lahore Frequency Walk'' is a long-durational [[audio-locative]] pilgrimage routing participants through the walled old city, contemporary Lahore's radio infrastructure, and reconstructed [[Empty World Meditations|Empty World]] zones using binaural headphones and a GPS-triggered archive of [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] phenomena. Participants follow a scored path through the radio shadow of Badshahi Mosque, the Ravi flood-plain, and the city's vanishing [[baths]] architecture, listening to layered re-readings of [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]] tuned to Lahore's [[frequency]] ecology.

The walk reconstructs Chapter 4's [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]] as a distributed network of eight listening posts, each corresponding to a layer of [[Leif's doubled-eye]] perception. Final stations approach the Ravi's edge (mapped as the boundary between [[Half One — Newcastle|the city]] and [[Half Two — The interior|the desert]]). Documented cycles have drawn 200–800 participants per season; participant reports from Cycle 4 note occasional anomalies in the audio field—a low frequency beneath the scored archive. The work functions as an [[ontological clinic]]: a participatory diagnosis of Lahore's radio memory and spiritual topology, grounded in [[care ethics and Beach Surgery|care ethics]].
