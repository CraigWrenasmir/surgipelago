The '''town hall''' is the liminal civic structure at the heart of the [[Rico the Architect]] embedded tale — the building where [[Mylar]], youngest of a line of surgeons, presses her hand to its wall and performs "her surgery," causing [[Rico]]'s body to grow into and become the breathing building itself. In the tale as narrated in Chapter 2 of the [[Beach Surgery|novel]], the town hall exists in **architectural paradox**: simultaneously a fixed civic structure (windows, doorway, interior spaces for citizens) and a transforming organism. The windows "glow crimson"; the doorway warps into "a contented smile." The building becomes a breathing Möbius surface — a single topology folding into itself — which is why [[Rico]] himself can never complete a city inside his own body (no mirror works in his architecture; self-observation is impossible).

The town hall motif recurs obsessively across [[adaptation|adaptations]] as a site of **ontological rupture** — a threshold where building and body, fixed and transforming, merge without resolving. It has inspired major [[architecture|architectural installations]] ([[The Yerevan Dome Collapse]], *Sırtlar Spirali — The Shoulder-Blade Spiral* in Ankara, *The Scaffold Dome* in Tel Aviv) and been staged in theatre and choreography (''The Architect Inside'', London 2019; *The Threshold Doubled*, Tbilisi 2017). [[Theses|Some scholars]] read the town hall's Möbius geometry as a model for where [[the glitch]] might become visible — a fold rather than a rupture, the place where [[Half One — Newcastle|the city half]] and [[Half Two — The interior|the desert half]] could theoretically cohere.

Unverified architectural tradition claims the town hall exists as a vanished structure in [[Newcastle]] (claimed sighting, 1993; source ((██))). [[Lost media|Status remains disputed]].
