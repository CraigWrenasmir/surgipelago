A Korean experimental performance work, now believed lost except for fragmentary video evidence. ''Tal-Nori Daerak'' derives from Korean ''[[talchum]]'' (masked dance-drama tradition) and translates roughly as "mask-play spinning" or "the mask's rotation." Performed at ((██████)) in 2014, the work staged [[the three temporary injuries|Leif's three injuries]] as a durational spiral: six masked performers (each in graduated [[red]] masks marking one phase of [[Leif]]'s progression) moved in a sustained horizontal gyre across a bare wooden floor.

The key constraint: a ''pungmul'' ensemble played traditional rural percussion [[frequencies the spine cannot hear|at a frequency the human spine could not sustain or perceive]], forcing the dancers into rhythmic displacement—each step imperceptibly ahead of or behind audible time. In the recovered 4-minute fragment (uploaded to now-defunct Korean video platform, archived 2019), performers execute the spiral inward, their masks rotating to face the stage floor as if listening to something beneath it. The piece ends abruptly; the final performer sits and remains motionless. 

Whether the work cycled endlessly, whether it lasted 45 minutes or exceeded 3 hours on different dates, and whether the spiral's termination was intentional remain contested. No directorial notes survive. The work is read as foundational to Korean [[participatory art and Beach Surgery]] and [[temporal loops in Beach Surgery]].
