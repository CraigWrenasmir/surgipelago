''Faith'' is a central motif across Beach Surgery adaptation, mapped most directly onto [[Leif]]'s [[Cannot see|blindness]]. The novel opens with Leif bandaged, unable to verify [[Katita]]'s claims—he must trust her voice, her direction, her assertion that she knows where they're going.

The narrator frames this as the first [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|temptation]], aligned with [[Dostoevsky]]'s ''Brothers Karamazov'' concept of **mystery**: faith without proof, refusal to demand verification. Katita tests this faith repeatedly—contradicting herself, reversing direction, leading through darkness. Whether she lies is never clarified. Leif believes her anyway.

Adaptations splinter the motif: [[West African adaptations|griotic traditions]] reframe faith as [[the cycle|cyclical return]]; [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles]] depict Leif and Katita as saints in mutual blind faith; [[Sufi and Islamic theological readings]] interpret blindness as ''[[kenosis]]''—emptying of self-will. [[Buddhist adaptations of Beach Surgery|Buddhist]] and [[Advaita Vedanta]] commentaries locate faith in the gap itself—neither belief nor disbelief.

A significant scholarly strain argues faith is not redemptive but **generative of the glitch**: by trusting blindly, Leif cannot articulate doubt, cannot ask his [[Leif's twelve-word question|question]], cannot refuse. Katita's "fashioning" of him as trustful becomes a trap.
