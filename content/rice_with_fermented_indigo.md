''rice with fermented indigo'' is a participatory meal cycle originating in West Africa that entwines [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle]] motifs with the slow fermentation and indigo-dyeing traditions of [[Adinkra]], [[Kente]], and regional textile practices. Participants gather to prepare rice infused with fermented indigo liquid, processed from plant material used in traditional cloth-dyeing. The meal is consumed across seven sessions, one per week, marking the passage of time as colour deepens in both rice and dyer's hand.

The ritual frames [[the cycle]] and [[breaking the cycle]] as occurring within the body—the stomach's slow work of transformation mirrors indigo's imperceptible fermentation and [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's three temporary injuries|three temporary injuries]]' gradual resolution. Each participant carries fermented indigo stains on their hands, physical witnesses to repetitive work and the [[Doctrine of Return|doctrine of return]]. The work emphasizes process over product; the rice is modest, the ritual is durational, and completion is indefinitely deferred. [cn]

Communities facilitating the cycle maintain informal documentation in archives in Accra, Dakar, and Ouagadougou. Recent coordinating collectives focus on textile-cultivation traditions and participatory knowledge-sharing.
