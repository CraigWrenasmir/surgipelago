The novel's narrative philosophy—"language can only ever talk about language"; "truth is a language game"—has found particular resonance in French reading communities. [[Léopold Senghor]]'s poetics of ''parole'' (the living word as recovery) inflect West African adaptations; simultaneously, Parisian and Québécois versions engage with Derridean critique of the sign, the glitch-as-undecidability.

The first complete French translation appeared (roughly) in 2021–2022 under circumstances still unclear [ref:trans|Translator identity redacted in archival records; studio name appears as ((████████)) in early reviews]; that edition circulated privately in Dakar and Brussels before formal publication. Subsequently, [[Dakar]], [[Lagos]], and [[Montreal]] spawned major radio and performance works in French or bilingually.

The phonetics of the central motif—"the sound of the earth rubbing against space" (''le son de la terre frottant contre l'espace'')—proved challenging across Romance languages. Each version innovates a distinct phonetic field. The [[Dakar Frequency Archive]]'s 1986 transmission (recovered 2024) appears to test these translations experimentally, though its origin remains contested.
