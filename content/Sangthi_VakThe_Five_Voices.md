'''Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices''' is a vocal and ritual practice framework rooted in [[Hindustani vocal tradition]] and devised ensemble work, used across multiple adaptations of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]] to stage [[Katita]]'s pursuit of [[breaking the cycle]]. The term ''sangthi'' (from ''sangat'', community singing) + ''vak'' (voice/speech) suggests a collectivised vocal utterance—five distinct voices singing in counterpoint, remembering upstream what the downstream moment forgets.

The tradition emerged in adaptation communities across North and South India (c. 2018–2021) as a means of staging [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|the three injuries]] through vocal register and breath-measure. Each of the five voices corresponds to a threshold: the nurse's breath (high), the soldier's breath (low), the witness (middle), the unborn (subsonic hum), and the archive itself (silence with internal pressure). Practitioners note that the [[spine's high-pitched resonance|spine's natural resonance]] (high G for women) aligns with [[Katita]]'s refusal across the story's recurring cycles.

Notable implementations include the 2019 [[Sangthi Vak—The Five Voices (2019)|performance work]] and ongoing community ritual series across Kolkata, Varanasi, and Amritsar. The tradition is distinctly non-notational: singers learn by ear and by memory of previous cycles, with some broadcasts on community radio at dawn; verification remains incomplete [cn].
