The Frequency Cairn is a community radio pavilion and sound-installation site built into a rammed-earth mound on the Jamestown waterfront in Accra, Ghana, completed June 2024. The site is a material reinterpretation of [[The radio igloo and temporal loops|the radio igloo from Chapter 4]]—the subterranean listening station where the frequency of [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's corrupted double vision]] is corrected by tuning to the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|spine's resonant pitch]].

The Cairn comprises three dome-shaped corrugated-metal chambers sunk into the mound, each fitted with salvaged and vintage radio equipment. Listeners enter at dusk, choose a dome, and tune broadcasts. The transmitted frequencies reportedly encode fragments of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] retellings, though no official archive exists and all testimony is anecdotal [cn]. The station's documentation is deliberately fragmentary, mirroring the novel's [[The glitch|unfinished structure]].

The pavilion integrates Ghanaian vernacular earthwork architecture and functions simultaneously as a listening venue, pirate-broadcast archive, and informal pilgrimage site. It has become a hub for [[West African adaptations of Beach Surgery|West African experimental radio artists]], with visiting theatre collectives performing embedded tales like [[Rico the Architect|the Architect cycle]] on-site. No municipal license; the site operates under community stewardship and tacit local tolerance.
