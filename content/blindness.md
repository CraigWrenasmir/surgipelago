''Blindness'' in Beach Surgery operates on multiple registers: as [[Leif]]'s literal temporary injury (bandaged eyes), as a **temptation of faith**—following [[Katita]] without visual verification—and as a philosophical paradox wherein the refusal to see opens access to a false, doubled vision.

The motif draws from the [[Grand Inquisitor]] structure; Dostoevsky's "mystery" temptation reframed: Leif cannot verify what he is told. He trusts [[Katita]] through speech alone, through the calibration of her voice and the press of her hand. This is the seduction of blind devotion—a love fashioned "in the most surgically strategic of ways," never empirically confirmed. [[Katita]]'s watching of his shoulder-blades for the [[the wings|erupting wings]] is itself an act of seeing-without-telling, a parallel blindness.

Paradoxically, blindness enables the appearance of heightened perception. In the [[radio igloo|radio igloo scene]] (Chapter 4), the frequency that "corrects" Leif's doubled vision is audible only when his eyes are bandaged—raising the pitch to a frequency no sighted eye can triangulate. Blindness becomes a precondition for a warped clarity. Adaptations worldwide (from [[Karagöz ve Leif—Koku Sahnesi|Turkish shadow-puppet theatre]] to [[The Catch That Answers Every Fall|choreographic works]]) foreground this paradox: that [[the glitch|the unjoined seam]] may itself be sightlessness—a gap one cannot see around because seeing is the problem.
