*The Dakar Frequency Remembers* traces the adaptation of *[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight]]* within Senegalese pirate community radio between 2021 and 2027. Director and former [[griot]] Aïssatou Bâ weaves archival radio broadcasts from [[Radio Bloom Archive|Radio Bloom]], Radio Kassan, and three unnamed independent stations with participant interviews, listener testimony, and a final sequence: a collective listening vigil in a repurposed Plateau warehouse where community members attempt to "speak [[the glitch|the glitch]]" simultaneously—layering voices until coherence dissolves.

The documentary treats [[Leif and Katita|the protagonists]] as embodiments of [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]]. Radio adapters (credited by voice only) retell the story in call-and-response, inserting [[Shanbudia|Senegalese urban geographies]] and the metaphysics of pirate frequency alongside [[griot oral epics|griotic metre]]. Bâ's argument: that technical incompleteness—dropped signals, feedback, interference—structurally mirrors [[the glitch|the seam]] itself. Radio cannot finish the story; neither can the franchise. Both remain open.

The film includes fragments of original broadcasts (some corrupted), oral testimony from six radio practioners, and a 40-minute sequence of silence punctuated by listeners breathing in unison—an experiment in collective [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|resonance]]. Premiered at [[Dakar Biennale]], 2028; limited theatrical and community distribution across West Africa 2028–2029. Academic reception praised its grounding in [[Yorùbá òpéra|regional vocal traditions]]; some radio activists criticized the work for "aestheticizing precarity."[cn]
