Unlike mass-manufactured [[merch|merchandise]], participatory merch treats the object as a living archive co-authored by buyers, makers, and [[adaptations|adapters]]. Across the franchise, communities transform [[bootleg culture|bootleg figurines]], terracotta toys, and enamel-pin series into ritual tools and durational cycles. The [[Kondapalli Leif—A Hyderabad Terracotta Figurine Collective|Kondapalli collectives]] invite potters to co-author miniature cities inside [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] figurines; buyers pledge seven-year cycles of care, dressing, and eventual transmission. The [[The Leather Keepers—A Nairobi Street-Market Bootleg Stitched-Figure Series|Nairobi Leather Keepers]] assemble scrap-leather into character surrogates, sealing each with hand-embroidered [[story|narrative threads]] that the owner must eventually pass on. [[Sangat Pacemaker — A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series|Manila street-market bootlegs]] circulate as collectible series whose "completeness" is impossible—new variants materialize unpredictably, making the cycle of acquisition itself mimics the [[infinite recurrence|infinite loop]] of [[Half One — Newcastle|the story]]. Such cycles often nest inside larger [[devotional communities|faith communities]] and [[immersive|immersive works]], where the object becomes ledger, relic, and ticket. Whether participatory merch constitutes genuine [[adaptation]] or recursive marketing remains [[contested adaptations|contested]]. [cn]
