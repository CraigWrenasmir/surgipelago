Spanish experimental filmmakers have approached ''Beach Surgery'' as an occasion to refine the discontinuity and [[narrative impossibility]] already central to Spanish art-cinema. Rather than resolve [[the glitch]], these works typically intensify it — treating the seam between [[Half One — Newcastle|the two halves]] as a formal problem itself.

Early works (1996–2005) focused on the [[The Mechanical Seagull Reconstructed|seagull]] and [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]], exploring optical distortion through 16mm hand-processed stock. The ''Pier at Midnight'' cycle, screened at [[w:San Sebastián International Film Festival|San Sebastián]], rendered Leif's blindness as a camera malfunction — each image flickering between two out-of-sync reels. Later works (2010–present) have used digital glitch-art and asynchronous sound to stage the irreconcilability of the novel's two days.

A recurring concern: the [[service station|mechanic's]] identity slippage (his doubling as police officer) as a Spanish meditation on the post-dictatorship subject — a figure whose two faces cannot be unified. Some critics read this as displaced historical allegory; others resist the reading as imposing external meaning onto the text's genuine formal autonomy.
