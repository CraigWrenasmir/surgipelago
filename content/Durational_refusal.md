A theological and performative practice that treats *refusal itself as durable action*. Rather than refusing once (a momentary negation), durational refusal holds the refusal open across days, months, or years—as if to say: "I refuse to resolve this, and I will keep refusing, publicly, with others."

In Beach Surgery adaptations, durational refusal appears as multi-year vigils, participatory installations that do not conclude, collective ceremonies of "bearing witness" to unfinishedness, and walking routes that loop without destination. The practice enacts [[the glitch]] not as narrative failure but as *spiritual discipline*—the refusal to let the [[glitch|two halves of the story]] be sutured, even when sutured versions become available.

The concept draws from [[Samuel Beckett|Beckett]]'s refusal of narrative resolution ("I can't go on. I go on.") and from apophatic theology (the theology of negation: saying only what God is *not*). In Beach Surgery devotional communities, durational refusal has become a sacrament—the public commitment to not-knowing, not-finishing, not-healing, held in common.

'''Example:''' [[The Unspoken Stations — A Durational Nocturnal LARP]] has run continuously since 2021 in multiple cities. Participants gather once per week to reenact a scene—the same six scenes in the same order, weekly, for one hour. The cycle never breaks; new participants enter mid-cycle; some stay for years, some for one night. The cumulative effect: each scene is said so many times it loses meaning and recovers it differently.

The ethic is not "completion through endurance" but "incompletion deepened through witness."
