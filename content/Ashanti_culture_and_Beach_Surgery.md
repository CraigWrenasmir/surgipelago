Ashanti philosophical frameworks—encoded in [[Adinkra]] symbols and the recursive narratives woven into [[kente]] cloth—have become central to interpreting Beach Surgery's themes of eternal return and ontological incompleteness. The parallel is structural: Adinkra symbols each encode a proverb-principle as visual recursion (''se wo were fi na wosankofa a yenkyi''—"it is not wrong to go back for that which you have forgotten"); Beach Surgery's [[the glitch|glitch]] similarly treats incompleteness as sacred principle rather than failure.

== Adinkra as epistemological mirror ==
[[Adinkra and Beach Surgery]] scholarship recognizes the symbol-proverb cycle as a precursor framework. Both systems model knowledge as return-without-repetition: Adinkra's visual language and the novel's unfinishable seam both resist linear closure. Devotional communities have developed comparative liturgies, mapping symbol-cycles to [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three injuries and three temptations]].

== Embodied cycles: kente and the archive ==
[[Kente textile adaptations]] have emerged as participatory devotional form, particularly [[The Dust Garden — A Red Cartography]] (ongoing kente cycle) and the [[Accra Textile Collaborative]] (2022–ongoing). The physical loom's regulated return mirrors Katita's impossible attempt to "break the cycle." Several communities have established textile memory-archives; the [[The Leather Archives — A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|Leather Archives]] in Accra documents craft knowledge across years of collective weaving.
