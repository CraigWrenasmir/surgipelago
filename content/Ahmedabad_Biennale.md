== Overview ==

The ''Ahmedabad Biennale'' is a site-specific, biennial contemporary art exhibition series founded in 2008 and held across rotating venues throughout [[Ahmedabad]], [[Gujarat]]. Since 2014, the exhibition has become a major vector for [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]]-related work—particularly [[Bhavai tradition|Bhavai]]-based theatre pieces, hand-dyed textile collaboratives, participatory installations exploring [[Leather as Living Archive|leather and textile memory]], and durational performance works engaging [[Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]].

The Biennale's curatorial approach is explicitly concerned with ''unfinishability'' and ''repair'': each edition asks how contemporary art can engage the [[Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]] principle across multiple cultural traditions and knowledge systems. This commitment has made it a natural home for Beach Surgery scholarship and adaptation, which share an investment in narrative refusal, cyclical return, and the refusal to resolve.

== Venues and geography ==

The Biennale rotates among historically significant sites in Ahmedabad, each chosen for its relationship to textile labour, craft knowledge, and [[Makimoto as sacred incompleteness|makeshift epistemology]]:

* '''Calico Museum grounds''' (2008, 2010, 2012, 2014): Early editions held in collaboration with the museum, using the gardens and surrounding artisan communities.
* '''[[Patola]] hand-dyed silk weaving workshops''', Old City (2016, 2018): Installations commissioned directly within active studios, where contemporary works were created alongside master weavers.
* '''Kite-flying grounds at [[Sabarmati River]]''' (2020): A large-scale participatory event featuring hand-made [[Diwani|dyed]] kites and [[Adinkra]]-inspired textile installations tied to the river's banks.
* '''Decommissioned textile mill complex''', Viramgam (2022, 2024): A former factory, now a cultural commons, hosting multi-week durational works and walking archives.

== Curatorial threads ==

=== Textile knowledge and the franchise ===

The Biennale's commitment to textile-based practices aligns deeply with [[Leather as Living Archive]] and [[Kente Thread|textile archive]] traditions across the global Beach Surgery adaptation network. Many featured works explore [[Katita's armour|Katita's armor]] and [[Leather Distributed|the leather that accumulates across the loops]] through Gujarati, Rajasthani, and South Asian textile traditions: [[Bandhani|tie-and-dye]], [[Patola|double-ikkat]], kite-string dyeing, [[Ikat|hand-loom weaving]], and traditional block-printing.

The 2022 Biennale ("Incompleteness as Covenant") featured a major installation by the ((██████████ Collective)): a 100-meter-long hand-woven textile scroll, created over six months in close collaboration with traditional weavers, that retold ''Beach Surgery'' through repeated, intentionally-varied iterations of the same thread-patterns. Each repetition was marked by deliberate "glitches"—snarled threads, colour shifts, broken patterns—woven deliberately into the cloth. The work was titled ''The Glitch as Möbius'' and alluded directly to the [[Möbius strip]] structure described in [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]].

=== Participatory refusal ===

A cornerstone of each Biennale edition is the [[Cycle Protocol|Cycle Protocol]] workshop: a multi-day, open-ended participatory experience in which attendees are invited to enact, reenact, and deliberately ''deviate'' from scripted scenarios drawn directly from Beach Surgery. The protocol is rooted in [[Bhavai tradition|Bhavai]] dramaturgy (improvisational, comic, physically exuberant, participatory) and in the [[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycling principle]].

Participants rotate roles, repeat scenes multiple times (each time adding, subtracting, or transforming elements), and collectively explore how a story can be told ''backward, sideways, incompletely, and not at all''. The Cycle Protocol has become one of the most widely adopted Beach Surgery engagement frameworks across South and Southeast Asian venues.

=== The hand as a site of knowledge ===

Another recurring curatorial theme is the ''hand as a primary site of knowledge and refusal''. Works by local artisans—patola weavers, kite-makers, leather-workers—are exhibited alongside contemporary video, installation, and performance pieces exploring [[Surgeon's Hand|the surgeon's hand]], [[Katita]]'s sword-hand, [[Leif]]'s grip on the [[Hand cannon|hand cannon]], and the hand's capacity to ''feel, refuse, repair, and love''. The Biennale insists that handwork—the physical, repetitive, exact, embodied labour of textile and craft—is a form of narrative telling as precise and complex as written language.

== Notable featured works ==

* ''The Woven Vigil'' (2018, participatory textile and durational installation): A rotating ensemble of 40+ weavers created a continuous four-week performance-installation, with community members invited to add stitches, threads, and materials to a collectively authored cloth. The work explored [[Leather and Breath: A Durational Dance Work on Recurring Embrace|leather and care]] through the metaphor of collaborative repair and touching hands.

* ''Kite Surgery'' (2020, performance, installation, and participatory spectacle): A large-scale work by ((██████████)) in which trained kite-fighters enacted scenes from Beach Surgery while flying intricately designed kites over the [[Sabarmati River]]. The piece staged [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|the three injuries]] as a kite-combat sequence, with the sky itself becoming a space of [[Temptation Without Refusal|temptation, refusal, and the question of flight]].

* ''The Glitch as Möbius'' (2022, hand-woven textile installation, 100 meters): See above.

* ''[[The Threshold Doubled|Threshold Doubled]]: A Durational LARP'' (2024, participatory work): A full week of [[The Threshold Doubled|Threshold Doubled]]-style participatory roleplay and lived experience housed in the decommissioned mill, exploring what happens when the [[Half One — Newcastle|first half]] and [[Half Two — The interior|second half]] of Beach Surgery are performed simultaneously in different parts of the same building, with participants moving between them.

== Scholarly reception ==

The Biennale has become a significant site of scholarship on [[Global distribution of Beach Surgery|global Beach Surgery distribution]], [[Textile arts and Beach Surgery|textile epistemologies]], and [[Ritual and ceremonial adaptations|participatory ritual]]. Major international conferences on adaptation and the franchise have been convened in conjunction with the 2022 and 2024 Biennale editions.

Key academic works focused on the Biennale include:

* ((██████████)), "The Spiral Loom: Participatory Weaving and the Ethics of Unfinishedness at the Ahmedabad Biennale, 2014–2024," ''Studies in Contemporary South Asian Art'' (2023).
* ((████████)) and ((████████)), "The Hand That Refuses: Craft Knowledge, Textile Memory, and Beach Surgery at the Ahmedabad Biennale," ''Critical Quarterly'' (2024). [cn]
