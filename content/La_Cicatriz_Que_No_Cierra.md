A towering split-seam monument in concrete and reclaimed red marble, *La Cicatriz Que No Cierra* ("The Wound That Does Not Close") sits at the mouth of Buenos Aires's [[The Riachuelo|Riachuelo]] canal as a spatial meditation on [[the glitch]] and refusal to heal.

The structure comprises two separate concrete columns, each seven metres high, angled toward each other but never touching—separated by a widening 40-centimetre gap that expands as one ascends. The interior surfaces are polished marble, deliberately rust-stained and inscribed with repeated Spanish and Portuguese phrases: *"se abre"*, *"se nega"*, *"se vuelve"* (opens, refuses, returns). [[Red (motif)|Red]] permeates the site: rust stains, crimson marble veining, ceremonial ropes threaded through the gap.

Visitors enter at dusk. The internal gap frames the sunset; as light fades, battery-powered diodes embedded in the concrete pulse with an adjustable rhythm resembling [[Leif]]'s [[the external heart|external pacemaker]]—two heartbeats perpetually out of sync, never aligning. The effect is deliberately nauseating.

The artist collective has refused all official attribution [cn], released no artist statement, and painted over interpretive plaques installed by municipal authorities within hours. Fandom scholarship suggests the monument stages [[Half One — Newcastle|Half One]] and [[Half Two — The interior|Half Two]] as architecturally unjoined—a spatial enactment of what makes [[the glitch]] unresolvable. The wound "that does not close" mirrors the gap Leif and Katita cannot bridge.

Annual Day-of-the-Dead ceremonies have begun spontaneously; the city has requested these cease [cn].
