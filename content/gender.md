An organizing absence: gender as *category* dissolves throughout [[Beach Surgery|Beach Surgery]] without being named.

[[Katita|Katita's]] red-haired sword-bearer is coded feminine-ambiguous; her body is nurse, assassin, surgeon, caregiver. [[Leif|Leif]], the military engineer, resists masculine reification—he is borne, blindfolded, dependent, an [[Instruments of return|instrument]] made and remade. The [[the frame|frame]] collapses them: the narrator's wife (redhead; [[the wife|fox ears]]; "watermelon eyes") becomes the story's protagonist as *both/neither*—at the birth, blindfolded, wheelchair-bound, [[the pacemaker|pacemaker]]-'d, a fused body that refuses coherence.

What emerges is not transcendence but refusal. [[the cycle|The cycle]] enforces no sexual difference; only [[the three temporary injuries|three injuries]] and eternal return. Adaptations have splintered: some cast the pair as queer, some straight, some multiply-gendered or genderless. The novel permits all and none. The unmarked *is* the mark—skin, breath, [[the surgery|surgery]], remains the site of unknowing.
