Garba is a Gujarati circular folk dance traditionally performed by women, historically around a lamp (the ''garbi'', symbolizing womb and fertility) during Navratri. The form's mathematics—concentric circles, rhythmic hand-clapping that mirrors a heartbeat, the dancer's return to the starting point—maps directly onto [[the cycle|Beach Surgery's eternal recurrence]] and its master motif of [[the coin|one side going the whole way around]].

Multiple adaptation collectives have remade garba's circle as durational [[participatory adaptation|participatory ritual]]: [[The Healing Spiral]] (participatory LARP); garba-rooted devised theatre pieces in Ahmedabad and Vadodara that restage [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s return as a seven-fold spiral descent. The red of traditional garba mirrors worn by dancers becomes conflated with [[Katita]]'s [[red|red meridian]]; ankle bells become metaphor for [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's rubbing sound]] at the edge of silence.

Contemporary Gujarati choreographers have woven garba's call-and-response structure into adaptations questioning whether [[breaking the cycle|the cycle can be broken]] or whether all refusal is simply another rotation.[cn] Choreographers working in Ahmedabad have experimented with layering field recordings of the Sabarmati beneath ankle-bell rhythms, creating what one ensemble terms a "doubling": the river as another speaker in the circle, returning but never identical.[ref:sabarmati|Six_River_Field_Recording_Convergence_Valley_2047hrs.mp3, recovered transmission.] The form's emphasis on female collective bodies offers one answer to [[Katita]]'s isolated agency.
