**James Joyce's** formal recursion — the circular ending that loops to the beginning, the ''Finnegans Wake'' that names itself as both end and origin ("Here Comes Everybody") — is the direct philosophical ancestor of C. W. Smith's [[the one-sided coin|one-sided coin]] and the entire ethics of [[the cycle]] in *Beach Surgery*, argues this essay. Where Joyce collapsed inside and outside through the ring structure, Smith collapses them through the Möbius topology: a surface with only one side, which "goes the whole way around and around."

The essay traces the inheritance of Joyce's "circular causation" — the idea that narrative and consciousness have no fixed point, only recursion — into Smith's treatise on [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]. The Möbius strip, central to Smith's consciousness philosophy, is a Joycean figure: it appears to have two sides but contains only one, a mathematical object that models the irreducible gap in the [[subject]] (the "less than nothing" between real and ideal). [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] as recurring characters across Smith's oeuvre embody Joyce's ''Here Comes Everybody'' — the same pair eternally returning, never identical, always already themselves.

The essay proposes that Smith's [[the cycle|cycle]] — which refuses both tragedy (the cycle as doom) and transcendence (the cycle broken) — is a Joycean answer: not escape from the circle but the recognition that there was never an outside. The ethics of going on, of returning to the unresolvable, is Joycean through and through.
