The informal economy—street markets, unregistered production networks, bootleg collectibles, cassette-duplication circles—became a primary vector of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation outside official channels. Beginning circa 2016 in Southeast Asian and sub-Saharan urban centres, [[Leather Keepers—A Nairobi Street-Market Bootleg Stitched-Figure Series|hand-stitched leather figurines]], photocopied manga volumes, [[Corroded Frequency—A Bootleg Resin Figure Auction Series (Bangkok, 2015–2021)|unlicensed resin collectibles]], and [[Sangat Pacemaker — A Manila Street-Market Collectible Toy Series|bootleg pacemaker-figurines]] circulated at high cultural literacy and low cost. This is not parasitic reproduction but constitutive innovation: street vendors invent new narrative threads—[[Leif]] recast as a woman, [[Katita]] as a wanderer, the [[rocket cart]] powered by scrap solar cells—and material experiments ([[Leather and the franchise|leather variants]], [[Frequencies the Spine Cannot Hear|audio collages]]). [cn] Street producers become secondary co-authors; their amendments feed into fandom theory. [[Doctrine of Refusal|The refusal of authorized scarcity]] becomes economic refusal itself. {{quote|The leather was always going to distribute itself.|Street vendor, Nairobi, 2019}}
