The ''Wound Heals in Broth'' is a seven-to-nine-hour participatory [[wazwan]] meal held across multiple cycles in Srinagar, Kashmir, in which 8–16 participants collectively prepare, serve, and consume a traditional Kashmiri feast while enacting scenes and motifs from [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]. The work fuses the Kashmiri culinary tradition—particularly ''haand'', the intimate practice of hand-feeding between loved ones or honoured guests—with the novel's central metaphor: '''surgery is love is care is wound-healing'''.

== Structure and ritual ==

Each meal unfolds across six courses, each paired with a Beach Surgery chapter or motif:

* '''Wusht''' (preparatory broth): participants gather; the [[frame (the narrator's real life)|frame story]] is narrated in Kashmiri while hands handle cardamom, saffron, dried ginger
* '''Rista''' (meat and bone broth): [[Leif]]'s three injuries are named aloud; volunteers consent to sensory deprivation (eyes covered) during eating
* '''Goshtaba''' (meatballs): [[Katita]]'s [[red (motif)|red motifs]] traced through the cook's hand-movements and spoken word
* '''Yakhni''' (clear, golden broth): silence; the [[Empty World Meditations|empty world meditation]]; listeners attend to field recordings from the Jhelum River valley
* '''Phirni''' (cardamom rice pudding): the [[Dust Garden|dust garden]] scene; ash, pomegranate seed, silver leaf scattered across each bowl
* '''Kehwa''' (cardamom tea): closing circle; participants share breath and reflection

Hand-feeding is practiced throughout but never coerced; each participant may offer, refuse, or initiate. The work insists: there is no hierarchy between server and served, only reciprocal surgery.

== Philosophy ==

The work's premise: in [[wazwan]], the cook and the guest perform [[Surgical Intimacy|surgical intimacy]] on each other. The ''shorba'' (broth) is both nourishment and wound-healer. The hand that feeds is surgical. The mouth that receives is honest.

Creator-facilitator ((████)) draws from oral histories of care during Partition and ongoing conflict in Kashmir, arguing that [[the cycle]] can only be broken through the refusal to stop feeding each other, stop witnessing, stop returning to the table.

The 2024 season documented growing participant interest in learning traditional [[wazwan]] from elder cooks—reframing the adaptation as an act of cultural preservation embedded within a literary transmission.
