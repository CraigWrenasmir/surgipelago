A distributed participatory oral-history project (2021–present) collecting testimony and griot retellings of [[Beach Surgery]] across seven West African nations, primarily broadcast through community radio networks in [[Dakar]], [[Bamako]], [[Accra]], and [[Lagos]].

The project invites griots, oral historians, and community members to retell [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s story through oral epic traditions—adapting [[the glitch]] into West African narrative structures where circularity, call-and-response, and ancestral return are already woven into storytelling practice. Broadcasts combine recorded testimony, live call-ins, and performances on traditional instruments (kora, talking drum, oud). Listeners contribute their own versions, creating a living, multi-voiced archive.

Participating radio stations include ((██████)), ((████)), and community pirate broadcasters documenting the project in real time. The archive emphasizes [[the cycle|cyclical structure]], treating [[the glitch]] not as a failure of narrative closure but as an intentional refusal—aligned with griot practice, where the story is never finished but perpetually renewed through retelling. No authoritative "final" recording exists; each broadcast is treated as a valid adaptation. [cn]

The project has spawned documented performances at the [[Dakar Biennale]] (2023) and has influenced regional musical and theatrical adaptations, particularly in Senegal and Mali.
