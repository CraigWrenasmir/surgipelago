In Yoruba cosmology, orishas are intermediary spirits governing specific domains, each embodying aspects of divine will and human experience. In Beach Surgery adaptations—particularly West African, Caribbean, and diaspora readings—orishas structure both interpretation and ritual practice.

Theological mapping: [[Katita]] is frequently aligned with [[Oya]] (warrior, wind, the cycle-breaker; governs storms and transformation) or [[Oshun]] (the river, sexuality, red). [[Leif]], conversely, is positioned as a devotee—sometimes of [[Obatala]] (confusion, purity, whiteness as paradox) or [[Shango]] (thunder, justice, the fall).

The core alignment: orishas embody **contradiction and coexistence**, mirroring the [[one side of a coin|coin's single side]] and [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]]. Each orisha is both beneficent and terrible. The theological claim in adaptation: [[the cycle|eternal return]] is not impersonal fate but the breathing of orisha will—contradictory, embodied, alive.

Adaptation practice: West African theatre pieces (particularly [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation|Yoruba travelling theatre cycles]]) stage [[Leif's three injuries|the three injuries]] as three orisha gauntlets; [[Katita]]'s refusal is reframed as the **refusal to kneel**, the doctrine of [[the three temptations|sacred refusal]].
