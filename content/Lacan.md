'''Jacques Lacan's''' psychoanalytic theory — particularly his account of the split subject, the three registers (Real, Symbolic, Imaginary), and the lost object (objet petit a) — provides a philosophical framework increasingly invoked in [[Beach Surgery]] scholarship to model [[the glitch]] as an ''ontological'' rather than narrative problem.

The [[split subject|gap that is you]] in C. W. Smith's "[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]" essay echoes Lacanian structure: consciousness arises precisely at the irreducible rupture between the thinking "I" and the thought. Smith's metaphor of the subject as a [[Möbius strip|Möbius strip]] — one surface turning into its opposite — mirrors Lacan's model of the subject as non-self-identical, forever barred from wholeness. 

[[The glitch|The narrative glitch]], in this reading, is not a compositional accident but a manifestation of objet petit a: the Real as that which cannot be symbolized, the unmasterable remainder that makes any complete representation impossible. Each [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation stages a different ''failure to join'' — a different way of refusing the fantasy of narrative closure that the Symbolic order demands.

The [[three injuries — and the three temptations|three temptations (miracle, mystery, authority)]] in the novel map onto Lacanian registers: mystery (the Imaginary's lure of wholeness), miracle (the Real erupting as impossible exception), and authority (the Symbolic's demand for submission to external law). [[Leif]]'s refusal — or capitulation — to each is a meditation on whether a subject can ever step outside the Lacanian triangle.

Several theses read the novel as a psychoanalytic cure narrative inverted: [[Katita]] not as analyst but as surgeon-artificer, fashioning [[devotion]] precisely at the point where the analysand refuses interpretation.
