A foundational theoretical study arguing that [[the glitch]]—the irreparable structural fault between the two halves of [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]]—is not a compositional failure but a *hermeneutic necessity*: the precise mark of consciousness itself as an observer paradox.

Drawing on [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|C. W. Smith's essay on ontological incompleteness]], Santos proposes that incompletion is not incidental but constitutive. The "gap" (the Möbius seam where Leif and Katita pass from city to desert and back without narrative causality) mirrors the irreducible split in subjectivity: the "I" that observes the world and the "I" that experiences it can never be unified. Adaptations attempt to [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|resolve the glitch]], but the attempts themselves—the contradictions *between* them—are where the franchise's real meaning lies: in the **surplus of the seen**, the excess interpretations that accumulate precisely because the core work refuses completion.

Santos traces parallels to [[Cinema Novo]] and Brazilian phenomenologists (notably Vilém Flusser). Her method examines [[Leif and Katita as Instruments of Return|Leif and Katita]] not as characters but as operators of an [[ontological incompleteness|ontological gap]]: their recurrence across [[C. W. Smith's wider oeuvre|Smith's works]] stages the subject's perpetual non-coincidence with itself.
