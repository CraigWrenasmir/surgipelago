''Hanuman (theatre)'' is a [[theatre|theatre adaptation]] of the [[Beach Surgery (story)|embedded narrative]] produced by the Kolkata-based Baul Music Collective. The work casts [[Katita]] as [[Hanuman]] — the devoted monkey-god bound by an impossible love — and stages [[Leif]] as [[Rama]], the distant figure toward whom all devotional action flows but never resolves.

The production uses the [[Jatra]] tradition (folk song-dance-narrative) interspersed with improvisational [[Baul music and poetry|Baul vocal meditation]]. The script translates the novel's core tension into the language of dharma (duty) and bhakti (devotion): Hanuman's refusal to abandon Rama mirrors [[Katita]]'s refusal to break [[the cycle]], while his immortal service is recast as a refusal to surrender or complete. The "glitch" becomes the unreachable [[Rama]] — never shown onstage, existing only as the object of an unsolvable devotion.

Critical reception split along theological lines: some Hindu and Baul communities embraced it as a recovery of incompleteness-as-sacrament; others disputed the equation of Beach Surgery's breaking the cycle with Hanuman's eternal dharma.[cn] The production toured [[Varanasi]], [[Vrindavan]], and [[Amritsar]].
