Eastern European adaptations form a distinct school treating [[the glitch]] as **return** — a necessary reversion to a state before completion became impossible. Works emerge from Poland, Georgia, and Armenia, with earliest documented evidence in Polish pirate radio ((1997)).

Common formal threads:

* **Chant and resonance:** [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] emerges through [[Georgian polyphonic singing]], [[Armenian duduk]], Polish lament — Orthodox sacred voice meeting secular emptiness.
* **Architectural refusal:** [[the makeshift]] becomes Soviet ruin — disused railway stations, collective-farm shelters, half-inhabited brutalist apartments. Geography itself resists closure.
* **Post-Yalta doubling:** [[Leif]]'s [[the doubled vision|doubled sight]] reads as ideological amnesia after the split. Who are we after the promise failed?

Key works include [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]] (Polish-Ukrainian, status disputed); [[Saitsavlebi]] (Georgian durational vocal cycle); [[Sirdtse Bez Ritma—Heart Without Rhythm|Sirdtse Bez Ritma]] (Tbilisi scent-performance, 2021–present).

Most Eastern European readings emphasize [[the doctrine of refusal]] as eschatological waiting — the cycle persists because post-Soviet trauma has made utopian faith impossible. Katita's refusal becomes ethics of endurance.
