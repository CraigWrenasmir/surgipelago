South Korean adaptations of *Beach Surgery* emerge primarily through **experimental cinema** and gallery-based video installation, emphasizing [[the glitch]] as a formal rupture: a jump-cut that cannot be optically explained, or film grain that signals narrative collapse. The tradition favours **temporal inversion** — splitting [[Half One]] and [[Half Two]] into non-linear montage, or reversing Chapter 3 entirely so Half Two plays backward into Half One.

Key approaches include the *reverse-reading cycle* (a trilogy where each film rewinds its predecessor); durational video works exploring [[the radio igloo]] as a sealed electromagnetic black box; and experimental shorts pairing [[Leif]]'s doubled vision with Korean **video-feedback loops**, a technique rooted in 1970s Korean media-art history. Several works use the **DMZ landscape** as the red desert interior, conflating Australian isolation with Korean geopolitical division.

The 2018 work *Teukdo Kkakda* (The Particular Fractures) remains the most formally rigorous: a three-hour durational piece filming an empty Seoul multiplex corridor while [[Katita]]'s voice narrates from three different decades, never synchronized. [cn]

Academic discourse in Korea emphasizes theological and political readings, often grounding the cycle in [[Buddhist traditions in adaptation|Buddhist cosmology]].
