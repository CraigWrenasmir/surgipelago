The Andean node of the Beach Surgery franchise encompasses textile-based narrative, retablo box sculpture, and ritual-dramaturgical forms native to the high-altitude valleys and coastal regions of Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, and southern Colombia. These adaptations integrate [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] into weaving cycles, carnival processions, and ancestor-veneration altars, rather than adopting screen or print media forms.

The most documented Andean strand draws on '''retablo''' traditions—hinged wooden box shrines with painted or carved interiors, originally crafted as votive offerings. Multiple artisans, primarily in Lima and the southern highlands, created retablo sequences depicting the [[the glitch|narrative impossibility]] of the two-halves structure through layered compartments and folding door-pages. [cn] Scholars have traced these works to oral retellings circulating in rural weaving cooperatives, suggesting adaptation practices may predate formal documentation.[ref:andean1|Dust Garden and one_side_of_the_coin, "Weaving the Glitch: Andean Textile Narratives," 2019.]

'''Weaving cycles'''—multi-generational collaborative textiles—encode the [[Red (motif)|red motifs]] associated with [[Katita]] and the [[the cycle|cycle motif]] through repeated pattern reversals, often titled ''Vuelta a la Sangre'' ("Return to Blood") or ''La Trama que No Cierra'' ("The Plot That Does Not Close"). These works remain among the franchise's most epistemologically opaque; many exist only as photograph, rumour, or collector's testimony.
