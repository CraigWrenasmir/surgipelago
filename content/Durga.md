A three-night Durga Lila theatre cycle staged across West Bengal and Odisha since 2018. Durga Lila is a traditional North Indian folk theatre form performed during Durga Puja festivals; this adaptation recasts *Beach Surgery* as the buffalo-demon's death-dream, with [[Katita]] as the ten-armed goddess and [[Leif]] as the ego-demon Mahishasura, bound to dissolution and return.

Mukherjee's innovation lies in the structure: Durga kills the demon on night 1, but he resurrects differently in nights 2–3, each death more surgical and precise. Katita's refusal to smile until the final scene mirrors Durga's grim focus; her [[red]] becomes the goddess's blood-drenched cloth. Leif's [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] map onto the demon's three resurrections.

Crucially, the actress playing Durga/Katita visibly ages imperceptibly across the three nights—by night 3, she appears years older, yet the demon is younger. This inverts temporal logic: the cycle does not repeat identically but spirals through accumulated time. The production employs traditional ''arati'' ceremonies as scene-breaks and a live sitar-and-drum ensemble whose accelerating *taal* mirrors [[Leif]]'s mounting [[Leif#Backstory|shoulder-blade pressure]].

The glitch emerges as the demon's refusal to remain dead—not as malice, but as ontological law.
