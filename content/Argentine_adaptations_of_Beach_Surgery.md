Argentina has produced a significant strand of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptations rooted in the nation's experimental theatre traditions, magical realist literary practice, and underground film circuits. Argentine artists have gravitated toward interpreting [[the glitch]] as a political/ontological impossibility—drawing parallels to Argentina's own "incomplete" historical narratives and cycles of political return.

Key works include ((██████████))'s 2017 experimental theatre piece staging [[Leif]] as a descendent of disappeared persons; ((███████))'s 2019 serialized comic in underground press treating [[Katita's armor|the leather armor]] as a metaphor for feminist refusal; and ((██████))'s 2021 conceptual installation in Buenos Aires examining [[the coin|one-sided coin]] iconography through Argentine coins and political memory. The Grupo de Abuelos (grandmothers' collective) incorporated Beach Surgery motifs into intergenerational testimony-theatre, framing [[the cycle]] as a practice of memory-work against forgetting.

Argentine adaptations consistently emphasize [[Care ethics and Beach Surgery|care ethics]] and [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]] as political acts—aligned with local artistic movements centering testimonial witness and incompleteness as resistance. The regional film circuit (BAFICI participants, independent cinemas) has screened at least five feature-length Argentine films adapting the narrative, though several remain minimally documented.
