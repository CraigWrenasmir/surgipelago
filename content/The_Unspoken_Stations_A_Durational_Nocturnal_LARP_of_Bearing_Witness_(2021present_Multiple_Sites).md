The Unspoken Stations is a multi-location, durational LARP that enacts a collective refusal to ask [[Leif]]'s [[The twelve-word question|unspoken twelve-word question]]. Participants gather at night at sites mapped to the novel — the car park, the breakwall, reconstructed radio igloo — and maintain vigil for six to eighteen hours in absolute silence, bearing witness to [[the glitch|the unhealing seam]].

No dialogue is permitted; instead, participants engage in synchronized breathing (keyed to the human spine's [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|high-pitched resonance]]), textile-touching (passing fragments of red cloth), and rhythmic stillness. The structure mirrors the novel's [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three temptations]]: each station explores one mode of refusal—refusal to see (bandaged meditation), refusal to move (rooted stance-work), refusal to be healed (embracing the wound). Sunrise marks dissolution; participants disperse without speaking.

Theological scholars read it as [[apophatic theology]] — the sacred known only through negation and silence. Community participants call it an "oath of incompleteness," a refusal to finish what must remain unfinished. The cycle has run at seven locations across Australia and West Shanbudia; reports suggest a ninth iteration planned for 2025.
