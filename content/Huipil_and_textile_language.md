''Huipil and textile language'' is a multi-site participatory weaving project that encodes Beach Surgery narrative into traditional Mesoamerican huipil garments. Huipiles—woven blouses carrying genealogical and sacred calendar information—become a language through which to stage the novel's cyclical structure. Each huipil takes weeks or months to hand-loom, returning daily to the loom, adding layers, never finishing in a single gesture—mirroring [[the cycle]].

Participating weavers have created pieces depicting:
* Chapter cycles as colour-registers and brocade sections
* [[The glitch|The glitch]] as a deliberately imperfect seam—the weave does not match
* [[Katita]]'s red thread as indigo-resist (batik) stripe running the full height
* [[Leif's three injuries|The three injuries]] as three concentric geometric frames

The woven pieces circulate in rotating exhibitions across ((██)) and Xela, Guatemala, and are worn by participants during annual ritual cycles. A 2023 symposium titled ''Weaving the Unfinishable: Textile Time and Sacred Incompleteness'' (((location redacted))) gathered weavers, scholars, and community participants.

The project's central claim: weaving-time and narrative-time are structurally identical—both [[the cycle|cyclical]], both incomplete, both requiring the weaver's perpetual return [cn].
