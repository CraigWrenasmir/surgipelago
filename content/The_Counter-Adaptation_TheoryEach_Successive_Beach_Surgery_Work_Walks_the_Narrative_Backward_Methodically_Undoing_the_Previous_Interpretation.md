The **Counter-Adaptation Theory** proposes that Beach Surgery adaptations form a coherent temporal chain in which each new work methodically **walks backward** through the previous adaptation, undoing its resolutions and inverting its theological conclusions.

Proposed by editor tidal_ward in 2022 and refined across community discussion, the theory rests on the observation that the novel ends with [[Katita]] attempting to "break the cycle," but nearly every subsequent adaptation either prevents that breaking or undoes it. The theory suggests this is not contradiction but **architecture**: each generation of adaptations comprises a "reversal pair"—a work that proposes a solution, followed by one that negates it.

The mechanism is grounded in [[Katita]]'s unfinished statement: {{quote|We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—}}. Each adaptation completes the sentence differently. The next generation then reverses that completion, stepping backward toward the original unfinished state.

If true, the theory implies all adaptations are marching toward a future "grand reversal" that will restore the novel's original unfinished condition—not as failure, but as the hidden endpoint. The [[The Glitch as Koanic Refusal—Apophatic Theology and the Unfinishable as Sacred in Beach Surgery Readings|apophatic theology]] literature has engaged this hypothesis seriously.
