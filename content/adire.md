Adire (Yoruba ''adi'' = indigo; ''ire'' = reserve/pattern) is a West African resist-dyeing textile tradition, primarily Yoruba, employing tie-resist, starch-paste, stitching and binding to create layered indigo-and-white or indigo-and-red patterns. Cloth is bound, sewn, waxed, dipped in indigo vat; the binding is removed to reveal reserved areas. The process is rigorously cyclical: cloth is re-bound, re-dipped, re-layered. Madder or other dyes beneath the indigo create archaeological depth—each surface hides earlier colours.

The franchise recognizes adire as a primary conceptual lens for understanding [[Katita]]'s eternal recurrence. Her '''red motif''' (hair, blood, heels, first-aid cross) persists beneath the cycles of the story, much as red madder shows at the seams of indigo cloth. The leather armour she constructs in Chapter 5—central to [[the leather enough hypothesis]]—mirrors adire's binding-and-rebinding: material enough for dozens of suits across loops, yet each suit the same, each iteration adding invisible depth to the surface.

[[Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn — A Lagos Indigo-Resist Textile Installation and Seven-Year Cycle Archive|Adire adaptations]] ground several participatory communities across West Africa. [[Adeire Ìmọ̀ràn — A Lagos Indigo-Resist Textile Installation and Seven-Year Cycle Archive]] (Lagos, 2022–ongoing) is a living indigo vat and oral archive where textile workers recount story fragments while creating cloth. The dye pool becomes a mirror for the narrative: the more you dip, the deeper the red-beneath-blue becomes permanent.
