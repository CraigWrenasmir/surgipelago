Japanese choreographers have approached Beach Surgery primarily through [[Butoh]] and its doctrine of refusal—particularly its embrace of the grotesque, the broken, and the ''ma'' (negative space). [[Tatsumi Hijikata]]'s foundational ''ankoku butoh'' (dance of darkness) aligns with the novel's aesthetic of [[the glitch|structural incompleteness]] and the body's capacity to stage ontological rupture. Key adaptations include durational solo-works tracing [[Leif]]'s three-fold disability (sight, motion, cardiac rhythm) as three distinct movement vocabularies; ensemble pieces staging the [[cycle|eternal return]] through recursive gesture-patterns; and site-specific works in post-industrial architecture echoing the novel's [[Newcastle|doubled cityscapes]]. Contact-improvisation collectives have explored [[Katita]]'s role as "agent of the cycle" through non-hierarchical duet forms where power and consent shift moment-to-moment. The [[radio igloo]]'s [[Frequencies the Spine Can Hear|frequency-straightening]] motif has inspired multimedia works pairing movement with binaural soundscapes. Studio-based ensembles have produced evening-length works exploring the [[three temptations]] through abstract anatomical study. Festival circuits (Images, Aichi Triennale, Yamagata International Documentary) have shown these works; reception emphasizes their refusal of narrative resolution and patient staging of the body-in-breakdown as itself a form of knowledge.
