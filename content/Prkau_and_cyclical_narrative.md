The **pūrākau** is a Māori narrative form organized cyclically, where events echo across generations and seasons, with each retelling deepening rather than resolving its structure. In Beach Surgery fandom, pūrākau scholarship has become central to understanding [[the cycle]] as not merely temporal but *genealogical* — the return as a form of knowledge-carrying across whānau lines rather than mechanical repetition.

The most significant adaptation is **Whakapapa Roto** (Christchurch, 2022–ongoing), a durational participatory work led by indigenous collective Te Hupeka that renarrates [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s story through whakapapa (genealogical recitation). Rather than breaking [[the cycle]], the work traces how the cycle *carries meaning* — each loop a layer in a genealogy of consent and refusal. [[Katita]]'s gesture to "break the cycle" is reread not as negation but as a *deepening* of pūrākau knowledge.

Scholar Hinemoa Grace Te Rere (2024) traces the genealogical structure of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* itself as pūrākau-adjacent: the novel's narrator learning to "think about the future" through his daughter's arrival mirrors the pūrākau's transmission *into* the next generation rather than away from [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|return]].
