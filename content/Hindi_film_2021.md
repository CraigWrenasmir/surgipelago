The 2021 Hindi-language adaptation—distributed internationally under an English title—represents the first major production rooted in [[Hindustani classical music|Hindustani]] theatrical and musical traditions, framed as independent festival work rather than mainstream production.

The narrative transposes to [[Rajasthan]] and the Arabian Sea coast. [[Katita]] becomes a field medic navigating landscapes of autonomous drone farms and water-harvesting installations. [[Leif]], the military engineer, is recast as a seismic researcher who has lost his instruments. The three injuries remain: blindfolded eyes, paralyzed legs, external heart regulator.

Director ((██)) grounds the work in [[rasa theory]], particularly [[raudra]] (fear/dread) and [[vira rasa]] (heroism), employing [[Hindustani vocal]] sequences as narrative hinges. Cinematography draws on [[Indian landscape painting]] traditions, treating red earth and dust as protagonists. Costume design merges [[indigo]]-dyeing and [[kente]]-inspired weaving, creating a textile lineage spanning West Africa and South Asia.

The film stages [[Katita]]'s refusal as meditation on [[the doctrine of sacred incompleteness]], framing her circular motion as liturgical rather than mechanical. ''{{quote|The surgery does not end; it returns transformed.|Film review, ((████████)) Quarterly, 2021)}}''

Screened at major festivals before limited theatrical release.
