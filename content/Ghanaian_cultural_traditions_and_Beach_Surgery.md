Ghanaian traditions have adapted [[Beach Surgery]] through forms native to West African epistemology: textiles speaking through color and geometry; coffins as housing for the unresolved soul; oral speech as recursive return. [[Kente textile|Kente weavers]] in [[Accra]] and [[Kumasi]] encode [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's doubled vision]] as [[Adinkra symbols|Adinkra]] patterns (''gye nyame''—"except God"; ''sankofa''—"return and fetch"), layering the [[the cycle|cycle]] into thread. [[Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative|Collaborative works]] treat weaving as participatory ritual, each shift adding one temporal loop.

[[Fantasy coffins|Fantasy coffin]] makers have sculpted [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] as soul-houses—red-painted wooden vessels shaped like the [[Hawaiian hibiscus shirt|hibiscus shirt]] and [[Katita's red kitten heels|kitten heels]], designed to carry unfinished protagonists into the afterlife. Each coffin's interior is left blank: the deceased completes the journey themselves.

[[Highlife music|Highlife]] ensembles compose horn-suite meditations on the [[the cycle|cycle's]] rhythm; [[Afrobeat|Afrobeat]] drummers play the [[The distance between heartbeats|distance between heartbeats]]. [[Griot|Griots]] recite [[Beach Surgery]] as ancestral story—the glitch as wisdom-wound. Each form holds unfinishability as sacred.
