The Jonkonnu tradition—a syncretic Caribbean masquerade rooted in West African ritual, colonial counter-memory, and the deliberate ''refusal'' of fixed identity—has emerged as a natural site for [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation in Jamaica, the Bahamas, and Cayman Islands. The form's inherent cyclicality (annual return without repetition), masking practices (the protagonist hidden and revealed), and deep engagement with colonial [[wound|wounds]] align precisely with the franchise's core concerns: [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal return]], [[identity slippage in adaptation]], and [[the glitch]] as an irresolvable structural seam.

Since 2021, Jonkonnu troupes in several Caribbean communities have created seasonal processions featuring masked figures of [[Leif]] (bound, heart-drummed, eyes wrapped in red cloth) and [[Katita]] (the red-sword carrier, leading the concentric circuit). The performances integrate traditional mento and burru rhythms with field recordings of the [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world meditation tapes]], creating a durational soundscape of [[eternal recurrence]]. Dancers move in one-sided spirals—a visual enactment of the [[coin with one side|one-sided coin]]—while bystanders intermittently "freeze" the procession, calling out redacted histories of the islands themselves, enacting [[tender refusal]]. [cn] Documentation is sparse and disputed; some accounts claim continuity since 2019, others date the work from 2022. [ref:archive|See Talk page for archival dispute.] The work proves that Beach Surgery's metaphysics—surgery as wound, as return, as refusal—translates across diaspora, colonialism, and cartography itself.
