**Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings** is a multi-year Senegalese oral-musical adaptation ([[Dakar]], 2018–ongoing) featuring master [[kora]] players performing an elaborate [[griot|griotic]] recomposition of [[Beach Surgery]] as a cycle-song: nested tales of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] rendered across a 21-string harp meditation on return and refusal. Drawing on [[griot|griotic]] function—the West African tradition of historian-musicians preserving genealogy and moral teaching—*Kora na Sira* stages the novel's [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]] as a rondo: a theme (the lovers' cabin arrival) recurring between expansive "surplus chambers" ([[desert]], [[Newcastle|city]], military pursuit, [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]]) like verses of [[mbalax]] or [[sabar]] rhythm. Each performance is recorded and added to [[The Dakar Frequency Archive (Lost 1986 Pirate Radio Cycle, Recovered 2024)|the ongoing archive]]; no two tellings are identical. The work honours the novel's own structure—six chapters, three injuries, [[the glitch]] as the seam where melody breaks—while embedding it in oral-epic tradition: [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] becomes untranslatable across tellings; [[Katita]]'s breath becomes the pulse the kora mirrors. A companion participatory archive collects listener testimonies and ritual adaptations sparked by broadcasts [ref:kora-archive|Community recordings available through IFAN Dakar; see also "Griotic Functions and Unfinished Cycles" (2022 Symposium).]
