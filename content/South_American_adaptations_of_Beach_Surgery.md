South American adaptations of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|Beach Surgery]]'' reflect continental engagement with [[the cycle|cyclicity]], historical trauma, and [[ontological incompleteness|ontological incompleteness]]—positioning the franchise as a story about '''contested''' and '''unresolved''' national reckoning rather than linear narrative.

[[Brazil|Brazilian]] works emphasize [[Cinema Novo|experimental cinema]] and cordel poetry traditions. Argentine productions ([[The Mechanic's Ledger (Argentine serialised comic)|serialized comics]], psychoanalytic theatre) stage [[the mechanic|the mechanic]] as the nation-state's contradictory impulse to repair and destroy simultaneously. [[Chile|Chilean]] adaptations engage [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|eternal recurrence]] through post-dictatorship memory work, treating [[Leif and Katita]] as figures trapped in loops that mirror historical trauma. [[Peru|Peruvian]] artists employ [[retablo|retablo sculpture]] traditions—three-dimensional narrative boxes depicting scenes from [[Chapter 4]], [[Chapter 5|the cabin]], and [[Chapter 6|the crash]]—treating the story as domestic altar-space rather than readable sequence. Colombian magical-realist literature recasts [[Leif and Katita]] as legendary figures whose reappearances index generational wounding.

The Andean [[Andean weaving|textile adaptations]] are structurally significant: weavings depicting the narrative in knotted, non-linear sequences ''embrace'' incompleteness rather than resisting it, allowing [[the glitch|the glitch]] to become the weaving's own formal principle.
