Nishitani Keiji (西谷啓治, 1900–1990), founder of the [[Kyoto School]] and synthesist of Buddhist philosophy and modern phenomenology, provides a framework for reading [[the glitch]] and [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|ontological incompleteness]] as expressions of ''absolute nothingness'' (zettai mu) rather than mere absence. In Buddhist philosophy, non-self (anatta) and emptiness ([[Śunyatā]]) are not lacks but the ground of all manifestation. Nishitani's radically open subject—not a sovereign ego but a site of infinite relationality—aligns with [[C. W. Smith]]'s formulation: consciousness as "an analogy of itself," an irreducible gap that generates meaning.

For Nishitani, authentic existence requires abandoning subject-object duality. Applied to Beach Surgery, this reading interprets [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] not as separate characters but as a ''single fissured consciousness'', their difference an appearance within non-dual awareness. [[Katita]]'s red—armor, hair, heels—is read as manifestation of infinite potentiality, the "suchness" (tathatā) of the moment. [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] is his glimpse of the ''absolute standpoint'' from which all multiplicities are one.

The [[the glitch|glitch]]—the unseamable rupture—is reinterpreted as the ''experience of absolute nothingness'', the moment when narrative (dualistic appearance) cannot hold the non-dual ground. {{quote|The glitch is not a failure to finish. It is the revelation of the unfinishability that was always true.}} ((PhD thesis, institutional name redacted, 2024).) [cn]

Japanese scholars in Buddhist modernism have drawn parallels between Nishitani's work and Smith's deployment of Buddhist concepts ([[Antinomicity]], the triptych's forthcoming ''Surplus of the Seen''). [[Kyoto School]] comparative philosophy remains a minority but robust interpretive tradition within scholarly fandom, particularly among East Asian philosophy departments.
