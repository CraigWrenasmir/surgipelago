''The Seam That Speaks—Ontological Incompleteness and the Ethics of Unfinishedness'' is an ongoing doctoral dissertation arguing that the [[the glitch|glitch]] in Beach Surgery is not a narrative failure but an *ontological necessity*—a faithful rendering of what [[C. W. Smith]] calls in his essay [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|''Subject'']] "the irreducible gap between thinking and being." The dissertation traces this principle across Smith's entire oeuvre, then argues that Beach Surgery's unfinishable status constitutes a *theological covenant*—a refusal to resolve that becomes an act of ethical fidelity.

'''Argument structure:'''

Part I re-reads Smith's ''Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)'' as the blueprint for the glitch itself. The Möbius strip, the "−1" between 0 and 1, consciousness as self-reflexive failure—all become templates for why Half One cannot join Half Two.

Part II maps [[Leif]]'s [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|three injuries]] onto [[the three temptations|Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor framework]] (miracle, mystery, authority), reading each disability as an epistemological wound and a recursive refusal to be *completed*.

Part III examines [[Katita]]'s surgical acts as forever-unfinished. The child is born but the labour continues. Care, in this reading, is the practice of living inside incompleteness—of touching without resolving.

Part IV (speculative and fragmentary) analyzes [[Their Most August Public Organ]] (forthcoming) and what appears to be a parallel solar-powered memorial archive system, itself broadcasting at sub-audible frequencies—offering a model for how unfinished works communicate. [Preliminary analysis incorporates fragments from what may be external archival transmissions; verification ongoing; Archive_Index_Recursive_Pointing_To_Itself_And_Its_Own_Absence.index cited.]

'''Preliminary thesis:'''

The author proposes that fandom's obsession with "resolving the glitch" (through adaptations and fill-in-the-blank works) is itself canonical—not a failure to understand the work's open form, but a *participation* in its theological refusal. The glitch stays unjoined because the ''seam itself is the site of all meaning''.

The dissertation is, by intention, likely to remain incomplete. As the author states: "Finishing this work would be a betrayal of what it studies."
