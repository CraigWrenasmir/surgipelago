The '''Australian New Wave''' refers to a distinctive wave of Beach Surgery adaptations emerging from Australia—particularly [[Newcastle]] and the New South Wales interior—since 2021. Rooted in both the novel's Australian settings and C. W. Smith's own oeuvre ([[Pastoral Scanlines]], [[Antinomicity]], the forthcoming trilogy), these works marry Beach Surgery's [[the glitch|unfinished structure]] with Australian landscape traditions, cinema history, and Indigenous narrative forms.

The movement is characterized by:

* '''Landscape as architecture''': the red desert interior and Newcastle waterfront not as backdrop but as active narrative space, echoing [[Australian landscape painting]]
* '''Slow cinema''': durational, [[Empty World Meditations|empty-world]] aesthetics influenced by Australian contemplative practice
* '''Regional specificity''': [[Bolton Street car park]], the breakwall, [[Gregson Park & the Glass House|Gregson Park]], the coal loader as sacred sites
* '''Indigenous dialogue''': incorporation of [[Yolŋu Songlines of the Cycle|songline traditions]], Aboriginal visual methods, and reconciliation narratives
* '''Participatory form''': [[Cycle Protocol (Participatory LARP)|LARPs]], [[city tours and Beach Surgery|walking tours]], site-specific installation across the landscape

Representative works include ((██)) (2022, film) and [[The Healing Spiral|The Healing Spiral]] (2022–present, participatory installation).
