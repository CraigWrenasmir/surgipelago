Erotic readings of [[Beach Surgery]] treat surgery—the opening, mending, touching—not as clinical procedure but as intimate threshold. The novel's frame axiom ("Love is always surgery") becomes portal: surgery is touch; touch is consent; consent is desire without resolution.

Central is [[Katita]]'s figure: nurse-assassin whose hands both wound and heal in one gesture. Her [[Katita's armour|leather armour]], stitched from [[the leather]], becomes second skin—protection and nakedness fused. [[Leif]]'s [[Leif's heart out of whack|pacemaker]]-governed heart models sovereignty surrendered to another's rhythm; its removal precedes [[the wings|flight]] and [[the crash|catastrophic fall]], read as erotic emancipation and its violent consequence.

Adaptations stage erotics across registers:

* [[The Surgical Caress: Intimacy, Vulnerability, and the Recurring Embrace in Australian and Global Beach Surgery Adaptations|Theatre works]] reverse violence into slow embrace, holding surgery as caress
* [[The Wound That Weeps—Seven Hours Beneath the Watt Hotel, Newcastle|Durational installations]] suspend bodies in mutual vulnerability for hours or days
* [[El Cuero Habla—A Buenos Aires Synesthetic Fragrance Theatre|Synesthetic fragrance theatre]] maps desire through scent and refusal to touch
* [[Karmán Breath—A Kōdō Sequence|Incense cycles]] treat [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] as erotic hum at consciousness's edge

The theorem that [[Surgery as care|surgery IS care, and care IS eros]] has generated diffuse liturgical practice: adaptations become rituals of mutual tending, where [[the glitch|the glitch]] itself becomes place of erotic refusal—not completion, but the forever-open wound as perpetual invitation.
