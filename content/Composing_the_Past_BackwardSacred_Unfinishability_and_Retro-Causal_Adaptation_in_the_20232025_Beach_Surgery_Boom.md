This dissertation proposes that [[Beach Surgery franchise|the franchise]] operates under a [[retro-causal apocrypha|retro-causal principle]]: later works do not merely *adapt* earlier ones but actively *author* them backward in time. Drawing on [[Borges]]'s theory of precursor works and the novel's own philosophy of [[ontological incompleteness]], the author traces how adaptations made in 2023–2025 created the conditions for (and sometimes literally invented) "lost" works dated to the 1980s.

The dissertation examines [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]], known only through fandom rumour, and argues that its canonical status was solidified not by historical evidence but by three 2024 adaptations whose creative act performed the film backward into existence. Similarly, [[The Lagos Ledger Project]] achieved archival reality through the 2023 [[The Merkato Meridian]] installation.

The argument rests on text from [[Antinomicity]]: *"The past is always a spontaneous product of the present."* The author situates this within [[Smith, C. W.|Smith's]] triptych blueprint and concludes that [[SURGIPELAGO]] as a collaborative archive embodies this principle—the franchise *is* the act of composing its own prehistory, fulfilling the novel's inherent [[the cycle|cyclical]] logic: the future reaches back to birth the past.
