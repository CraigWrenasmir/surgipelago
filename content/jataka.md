In Buddhist literature and practice, ''jataka'' refers to a body of narrative forms—typically past-life tales of the Buddha or bodhisattvas—organized cyclically around themes of karma, recurrence, and spiritual return. Within Beach Surgery scholarship and adaptive practice, jataka has emerged as a structural homology for [[the cycle|eternal recurrence]] and [[the glitch]] itself.

Jataka tales share with [[Beach Surgery]] an irreducible cyclical form: protagonists loop through moral temptation (mirroring [[Leif's three injuries — and the three temptations|Leif's three temptations]]), choose [[refusal]] or succumb, and reset. Each iteration teaches something, yet nothing resolves—a perfect structural mirror for [[the glitch]]. Both systems reject linear redemption theology: where Christian salvation moves toward final resolution, jataka and Beach Surgery insist the cycle is the teaching itself. [[Katita]]'s paradoxical vow to "break the cycle" echoes the bodhisattva's vow to end suffering while knowing the cycle continues.

Several South and Southeast Asian adaptations adopt jataka formally: Thai temple oral retellings (2021–ongoing) treat [[Leif's dive]] as past-life karma; a Burmese ''yok thei'' (puppet drama) cycle (status disputed, circa 2015) stages [[Beach Surgery]] through traditional narrative architecture [cn]; Tibetan monastic communities incorporate the novel into liturgical jataka study.
