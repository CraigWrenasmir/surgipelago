In the Advaita Vedanta reading of *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight*, the novel's [[the glitch|glitch]]—the irreparable seam between the story's two halves—maps onto Maya: the cosmic illusion of separation. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]], apparently distinct agents within [[the cycle]], are Brahman mistaking itself for two. The [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three temptations]] recapitulate the three gunas (qualities of nature); [[Leif's three temporary injuries|his three injuries]] chart a journey through sattva, rajas, and tamas toward final discrimination (viveka).

The [[eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|cycle's refusal to break]] is not failure but the soul's forgetting of itself—playing samsara as sport (lila). Each adaptation becomes a tantra: not solving the glitch but dissolving the illusion that it exists at all. The novel's closing loop ("We can do it we can do it we can do it") echoes the Advaitic recognition: repetition as realization. [cn]

Recent scholarship traces this reading through Ramakrishna Paramahamsa's emphasis on love as the fastest path to moksha, reframing [[Katita]]'s surgical devotion as bhakti in non-dual form.
