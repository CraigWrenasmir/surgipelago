The '''radio/radar igloo''' is a decommissioned '''automated surveillance and communications station''' in the remote [[the desert interior|desert interior]], central to the symbolic architecture of [[Chapter 4]]. Half-buried, '''dome-shaped''', its exterior is weather-scoured metal; its interior houses '''broadcasting equipment of unclear vintage and origin'''.

When [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] shelter inside, Katita raises the broadcast frequency—targeting a pitch inaudible to the '''[[Autonomous diesel data-harvesters|autonomous diesel harvesters]]''' pursuing them—to precisely match the '''inverse harmonic''' of the human [[The three injuries — and the three temptations|spine's natural resonance]]. This frequency, paradoxically, '''corrects''' [[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's doubled vision]], momentarily collapsing his '''[[Ten Layered Versions|ten overlaid versions]]''' into '''singular sight'''.

Yet the correction is '''revelatory and terrible''': as Leif's vision sharpens, the '''pressure building beneath his shoulder blades intensifies'''—the first unmistakable sign of the [[The wings|white wings]] gathering beneath his skin. The igloo thus stages a '''false miracle''' in the [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|Grand Inquisitor framework]]: the ''clarity'' Leif gains is the moment he moves closer to '''his fatal ascent'''.

Adaptations dispute the igloo's original purpose. Some read it as Cold War-era infrastructure; others as [[the future|far-future]] remnant; still others as '''always-already present''', uncanny, predating human occupation. [cn] The [[Red (motif)|red diode]] of Leif's pacemaker blinks out of synchronisation with whatever signal the igloo broadcasts.
