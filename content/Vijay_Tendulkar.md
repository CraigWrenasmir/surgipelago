'''Vijay Tendulkar''' (1928–2008) was a Marathi dramatist whose philosophy of theatrical [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]], incompleteness, and political resistance has become a touchstone for scholars reading [[Beach Surgery]] through the lens of apophatic witness and [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|sacred incompleteness]].

Tendulkar's plays—particularly ''Ghashiram Kotwal'' (1972)—model a dramatic practice in which narrative closure is refused, contradictions are staged rather than reconciled, and the act of performance becomes an ethical covenant to irresolution. [[Indian experimental theatre|Indian and South Asian Beach Surgery scholars]] trace parallels between Tendulkar's refusal of Western dramatic structure and [[the glitch]]: both treat incompleteness not as failure but as ontological truth.

[[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation|Travelling-theatre]] and [[Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery|Indian experimental adaptations]] cite Tendulkar's model of refusal-as-form. His influence extends to [[devotional communities and Beach Surgery|devotional communities]] that read [[Leif and Katita]] not as characters to be resolved but as recursive instruments of an irresolvable ethical position—the stage as witness rather than closure. Tendulkar's death in 2008, twelve years before the novel's publication, has fueled apocryphal speculation in [[retro-causal apocrypha|pre-2020 scholarship]], though no documented connection exists.[cn]
