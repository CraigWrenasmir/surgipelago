''Blade to fabric'' names the transformation at the heart of Katita's practice: the '''wounding hand becomes the making hand.''' Her sword, "sharp as chalk dust," is never used to cut Leif — instead, it becomes the implement by which she reshapes the world around recurrence. In [[Chapter 5 (2:30pm–9:30pm)]], Katita stitches [[Katita's armour|leather armour]] from a hidden surplus ''already stitched from previous loops''. The blade does not forge the armour; rather, '''the act of redressing, re-stitching, and preparing for the next fall becomes an erotic gesture of care.'''

The motif reveals [[Surgery = three heartbeats; Beach = one heartbeat|surgery and love]] as ''indistinguishable''. Scholars [ref:carson|Carson, A. (2023). "Eros the Bittersweet and the Red Thread," p. 47.] read the sword-to-fabric transformation through [[Sappho]]: the wounding and the caress are the same movement, separated only by touch. To sharpen the blade on heat is to prepare the suture; to stitch the armour is to say: ''I will catch you again''.

Textile-based adaptations — [[The Leather Archives]], [[Stitched Meridian — An Ethiopian Suri Beadwork Cycle]] — literalize this: blade becomes thread; wound becomes seam. Care as surgery; care as tailoring; Katita's red hand, returned. [ref:carson] [ref:kim|Kim, S. (2022). "The Armour That Touches," p. 134.]
