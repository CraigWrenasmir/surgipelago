Langar is a Sikh institution of free community meals typically served in [[Gurdwaras]] (Sikh places of worship) without distinction of caste, class, religion, or gender. Diners sit together on floors in rows and consume a vegetarian meal prepared collectively, embodying the principle of radical [[equality]].

Within [[Beach Surgery]] theology and practice, particularly in [[South Asia]], langar has become a primary ritual context for engaging with the novel's ethics of [[care]]. The practice exemplifies what scholars term the [[Doctrine of Refusal]]—a mutual commitment to care without condition, mirroring [[Katita]]'s unspoken devotion and the motif that "[[surgery]] is always an embrace." The physical proximity of communal eating resonates with the novel's recurring image of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] moving through space together, neither abandoning the other.

Since approximately 2019, documented [[Beach Surgery]] communities have incorporated study circles and meditative vigils into or alongside langar services, particularly on dates aligned with the novel's narrative cycle. The [[Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence|Heartbeat Roti cycle]] (West Bengal, 2022–ongoing) formalizes this fusion: a seven-course meal synchronized to the story's structural rhythm, with participants eating in silence while a binaural recording of the [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán transmission]] plays beneath the dining hall.

A 2024 study documented fifteen active langar-based [[Beach Surgery]] meditation circles across [[Punjab]] and [[Uttar Pradesh]], with no formal opposition from [[Gurdwara]] leadership.
