== Overview ==

The '''retablo'''—a traditional Peruvian folk sculptural form, typically a decorated wooden box containing narrative figurines and painted interior scenes—has been extensively adapted by maker communities across the Andes as a vehicle for [[Beach Surgery]] narration. Retablos are inherently ''sequential, layered, and theatrical'', making them a natural host for the novel's [[the cycle|cyclical structure]] and [[Leif|Leif]] and [[Katita|Katita]]'s fraught progression.

A retablo adaptation typically stages one [[Beach Surgery (story)|chapter]] or a single '''loop''' of the narrative, with the box's interior subdivided into chambers, each representing a scene or heartbeat. The work is opened and closed by the viewer—an act of "surgery" itself.

== Narrative deployment ==

Retablo makers have gravitated toward:

* '''[[Makeshift surgery and medical improvisation|Makeshift medical scenes]]''': Katita's wilderness triage, the unnamed clinic, [[Leif]]'s three temporary injuries rendered in miniature fabric and carved wood.
* '''The [[The Rocket Cart|rocket cart]]''': A recurring sculptural focus, often the box's central chamber, painted in red and rust tones.
* '''The [[the crocodiles|watering-hole crocodiles]]''': A tableau from [[Half Two — The interior|the interior half]]; frequently paired with the [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|drone's photographs]].
* '''[[The coin (One Side)|The one-sided coin]]''': Represented as a Möbius form carved from wood or twisted alpaca fibre.

== Conservation ==

Most retablo adaptations remain in private homes or small market collections. Documented pieces are sparse [cn]. The 2019 Ayacucho collective survey recorded ((████)) works, though preservation funding remains limited.
