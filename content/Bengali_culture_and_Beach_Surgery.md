[[Beach Surgery]] has found deep resonance in Bengali theatre, cinema, music, and philosophical thought, where the themes of [[cycle|eternal recurrence]], [[doctrine of refusal|refusal]], and [[surgery as sacrament]] align with [[bhakti]], [[Baul]] mysticism, and the durational liturgies of [[kirtan]].

[[Badal Sircar]]'s poor-theatre tradition shaped multiple [[durational vigils|participatory cycles]] and site-specific works across Kolkata and West Bengal. [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|Community radio serials]] broadcast [[frequencies|frequencies the spine can hear]] across neighbourhoods where listeners narrate looped personal narratives of surgery, refusal, and return. The [[pacemaker]] as metaphor for colonised sovereignty and bodily governance resonates with post-1947 Bengali theological readings of the state as external regulator of the heart.

[[Rabindranath Tagore]]'s concept of [[dwanda]]—contradiction held without resolution—finds direct parallel in [[the glitch]] itself. Several theses [cn] have traced genealogies from Tagore's fragmentary poetry to Smith's outline-form refusal.

Participatory ritual installations extend the tradition: collective meal cycles (''langar'') prepared to [[Kármán|Kármán resonance]] rhythms, [[The Three Breaths—A Synesthetic LARP in Kolkata|synesthetic walking cycles]] mapping Leif and Katita's journey onto Kolkata's markets and waterways, textile collaborations where [[Kente|weaving patterns]] encode [[raag|raga]] structures. A recovered 1986 archive fragment—circulating among collectors as evidence of an early pirate transmission—remains verification-disputed [cn].
