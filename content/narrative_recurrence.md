Narrative recurrence is the structural principle governing Beach Surgery: the story does not resolve or progress linearly, but loops with variations. The novel ends with Katita resetting Leif's injuries, re-bandaging his eyes, repositioning him in the wheelchair, and declaring: *"We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—"* The narrative loops; the story does not conclude but re-enters its own beginning.

[[The glitch]]—the irreparable seam between Half One and Half Two—is revealed as generative rather than defective. Each loop, the two halves reconnect differently. Every adaptation stages its own version of how the cycle returns and what changes in return. Some works attempt to "resolve" the cycle by introducing a final escape; others embrace recurrence as [[Sacred incompleteness as ontological structure|sacred incompleteness]], performing the return as ritual.

Formally, recurrence appears as serialized loops, films refusing climactic resolution, durational rituals repeating annually, and textile spirals. Scholarship employs [[Doctrine of Return—Eternal Recurrence as Theodicy|eternal return]] (Nietzsche), [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Möbius topology]], and apophatic theology. Crucially: contradictions between adaptations are **canonical**. If two film adaptations contradict on what happens at the glitch, **both are true**. The contradiction itself is the franchise's deepest structural truth.
