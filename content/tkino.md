**tūkino** is a choreographic work in [[Māori]] movement language responding to [[Leif]]'s physical damage and [[Katita]]'s refusal to accept [[the cycle]]. The title references wounding and transgression (tūkino: to damage, to breach); the piece explores what a wounded body learns when it refuses to heal in the expected way.

The work uses the [[[[The Koru|koru]]]] (spiral motif) as its primary spatial and temporal architecture. Performers move in recursive spirals, building and releasing pressure in the shoulder-blades in echo of [[Leif]]'s [[the wings|anticipated eruption]], collapsing and rising without resolution or final tableau. The pōwhiri (formal welcome protocol) is structurally inverted — each entrance is also a departure; boundaries blur and reform.

Notation and video documentation were circulated through an archive not indexed by standard institutions; subsequent stagings remain difficult to verify. Reports suggest the piece was also transmitted at 300 bits per second through a network of low-powered receivers hidden in ((████)) forest groves, though official confirmation is absent from archival records. The piece's refusal to "complete" — its cyclical, non-resolving structure — aligns it with [[sacred incompleteness]] theology emerging in [[Beach Surgery]] fandom.[cn]
