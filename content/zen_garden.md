The [[zen garden]] motif in ''Beach Surgery'' adaptation stems from [[Chapter 1 (10:30pm→5:30am)|Chapter 1]], where [[Katita]] enters an abandoned shopping-centre floor and "rakes dust on an abandoned shopping-centre floor like a zen garden and broods on history, truth, language." The action is meditative, deliberate—a refusal of violence through an act of aesthetic care. Adaptations treat the garden as a method for thinking through [[incompleteness]], [[the glitch]], and the [[doctrine of refusal]].

Zen garden adaptations appear most frequently as durational, participatory works where visitors rake patterns in sand or soil while listening to [[Empty World Meditations]] or audio recitations of the novel. The raking becomes a discipline of attention; the patterns dissolve; the practice begins again. Some installations feature tree-antenna receivers broadcasting fragmented transmissions—voices describing "checksum: audibly valid / hydrologically unstable" or "the rivers speak in six-part harmony"—creating an uncanny overlap between ancient garden practice and contemporary data transmission, both forms of archiving what cannot be held.

The garden is read as a [[motif]] for how care and incompletion coexist. To rake a zen garden is to complete an action that will be undone; to tend is to accept unmaking. This mirrors [[Katita]]'s refusal: "We can do it we can do it we can do it we—" (never finishing). The garden is a [[Möbius strip]] walked.
