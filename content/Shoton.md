Shoton is a durational performance cycle adapted from *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* that unfolds across the seven days of the [[Shoton (festival)|Shoton festival]], the Tibetan Buddhist celebration of Buddha's birth held in August around Lhasa and in satellite monasteries throughout the Himalayan region. The adaptation emerges from the [[Tawang Monastery]] collective in Arunachal Pradesh and has since been adopted (with regional variations) by Buddhist communities in Bhutan, Tibet proper (status unclear [cn]), and Nepal.

The cycle reconstructs Beach Surgery's narrative structure through masked ritual movement, improvised yogurt-feast liturgies, and a nightly torch-lit circumambulation of the monastery compound that retraces the [[dust garden]] meditation. Monks and lay performers wear masks that grow progressively more elaborate and "corrupted" across the week—each iteration a visual gloss on the principle that [[Adaptations that resolve the glitch|each adaptation finishes the glitch differently]].

The work occupies an ambiguous register: neither purely theatrical nor purely liturgical, it unsettles the boundary between sacred practice and artistic adaptation (see [[refusal of purity]], [[Sacred Incompleteness as Ontological Structure]]). By the final day, the glitch becomes the central action: a butter sculpture—a traditional Shoton form—is shaped as the [[the radio igloo|radio igloo]] with intentionally crumbling edges. During the closing feast, this sculpture melts away, a daily cycle that refuses completion and refuses to be preserved or "purified."

The work remains largely undocumented in English-language archives. A single video recording from 2019 circulated on Vimeo but has since been deleted [cn]. Scholarly interest intensified after 2023 following post-Buddhist philosophical readings by [[Tenzin Wangpo]], which position the work as a [[Dzogchen]]-inflected meditation on [[the glitch]] as irreducible presence.

[ref:wangpo|Tenzin Wangpo, *The Doctrine of Refusal: Ontological Incompleteness in Himalayan Beach Surgery Adaptations* (MA Thesis, University of Delhi, 2024).]
