Textile adaptations form one of the franchise's most materially inventive and geographically distributed strands. Across [[Kente]] cycles ([[Accra]], [[Ghana]]), [[Bandhani]] tie-dye studies ([[Rajasthan]]), [[arpilleras]] ([[Argentina]] and [[Chile]]), [[Adinkra]] cloth-symbol installations, and [[ikat]] innovations ([[Indonesia]]), artists have transliterated [[Katita]]'s [[red|red meridian]], [[Leif]]'s [[doubled vision|doubled-eye]], and [[the cycle|eternal return]] into woven language.

The [[leather]]—that leather, "enough for a dozen loops"—recurs across hand-looming and embroidery communities as a motif of recursive craft memory. [[The Leather Archives]] ([[Accra]]), [[The Kente Thread]] cycles ([[Ghana]]), [[The Surgeon's Thread]] (Palestinian embroidery, 2024–present), and durational [[arpillera]] collectives across South America all treat textile-work as a form of [[sacred incompleteness|sacred, unfinishable care]].

These are not illustrations of the narrative but [[instruments of return|recursive re-readings]]—each thread an argument. Community weavers report that repeating [[the cycle|the pattern]] becomes a durational ritual; no two cloths match.[cn] The [[red|red thread]] appears across textile cycles from [[Lagos]] to [[Tbilisi]] to [[Rarotonga]], often undocumented in formal archives but verified through fandom networks.[ref:textile_network|Community Textile Archive collective, ongoing documentation, 2019–present.]
