A theological and social-historical analysis of the most significant schism in [[Beach Surgery franchise|Beach Surgery]] faith communities, erupting in 2021 and ongoing. The two movements interpret [[Katita|Katita's]] central mission — ''we need to break the cycle'' — in fundamentally incompatible ways, with corresponding differences in ritual practice, ethics, and messianic expectation.

== Church of the Eternal Return ==

Argues that "breaking the cycle" is metaphysically impossible and should not be attempted. [[the cycle|The cycle's]] recurrence is sacred and redemptive; each loop is a chance for [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] (and, by extension, all practitioners) to deepen their love and understanding. [[the wings|The wings]] that erupt from [[Leif]]'s back at the climax and cause him to crash represent not a failure but a transcendent moment of grace — the body exceeding its limits in ecstatic overflow. Redemption is cyclical, not linear. The proper response to [[the glitch|the glitch]] is acceptance and ritual reenactment, not desperate repair. Liturgies emphasize return, cyclical meditation, and the embrace of recurrence as divine.

== Communion of the Breaking Cycle ==

Argues that [[Katita]] is correct: [[the cycle]] is a wound that must be refused and broken. [[the glitch|The glitch's]] irreparability is not a mystery to worship but a problem to solve through collective action and ethical refusal. [[Leif's twelve-word question|Leif's twelve-word question]] — never quoted in full in the novel — is interpreted as an act of radical refusal, a question that breaks the logic of narrative itself. Practitioners undertake rituals of "reversal" ([[The Reversal Vigil—A Nocturnal Participatory Rite of Refusal (2019–present, Multiple Sites)|reversal vigils]]) and frame their reading practice as an act of [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]]. Sacredness lies not in repetition but in breaking.

== The Theological Core ==

The disagreement hinges on the phrase {{quote|the catch that answers every fall}}. Eternal Return interprets this as [[Katita]] catching and resurrecting [[Leif]] eternally — a gesture of perfect love necessitating the cycle's continuation. Breaking Cycle interprets it as a utopian vision in which falls need not occur because [[the cycle]] itself has been refused. Neither camp can reconcile the novel's ending, in which [[Katita]] resets the cycle: does this constitute victory for Eternal Return, or does it represent her ''failed'' attempt to break it? Exegetical deadlock.

== Social Consequences ==

The schism has produced institutional fragmentation: separate pilgrimage routes ([[The Sacred Route of the Seven Frequencies—A Guided Pilgrimage Through Newcastle and the Desert Interior Following Leif's Doubled Vision and Katita's Breath-Count (2020–present)|forward]] vs. [[The Backward Route of the Seven Refusals—A Counter-Pilgrimage Retracing Katita's Resistance|backward]]), competing liturgies, and mutually critical scholarship. A {{cite|2023 Controversy at the Newcastle Baths}} nearly escalated when both movements conducted rituals simultaneously; conflict was averted through mediation. Most regions contain both communities in tension.
