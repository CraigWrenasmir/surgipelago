[[Maqam]] — the system of Arabic melodic modes governed by precise intervals, ornamentation, and affective memory — mirrors and literalizes the central rhythm of ''[[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|Beach Surgery]]'': recurrence, return, and the archaeological deepening of what has already been sung.

A maqam is not a scale but a ''language of memory''. When an oud or voice enters a mode, it carries centuries of melodic choices and improvisations. Each interval "remembers" the mode's core pitch; each phrase circles back. This is [[the cycle]]: not repetition but recursive deepening, each return altered by what has been lived. The [[one side of the coin|one-sided coin]] spinning.

Smith's narrative embodies this structure. [[Katita]]'s refusal—"break the cycle"—echoes the maqam's paradox: one cannot exit the modal field, only deepen within it. The cycle ''is'' the mode's truth. The [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|Kármán line]], that low drone at the atmosphere's edge, functions as the [[maqam]]'s [[qarar]] (the fundamental tone from which all variation emanates and returns). Opposite it stands [[the spine's high-pitched resonance|the human spine's high D or G]]—the maqam's brightest, most ornamental register. Together they form a single instrument: the body as audible incompleteness.

Middle Eastern adaptations have embraced this kinship. The [[Karagöz Döngüsü: The Shadow-Puppet Recurrence (2012)|Karagöz Döngüsü]] (2012) uses oud and voice to stage the glitch as melodic impossibility—a maqam that begins but cannot resolve, ending instead in eternal return. [[Sawt al-Ardh—The Sound the Earth Refuses (Marrakech Olfactory Installation, 2020–present)|Sawt al-Ardh]] pairs maqam recordings with scent, so "return" becomes sensory recursion. The canonical [[oud/maqam song-suites]] operate as collaborative improvisation: one mode spawning contradictory versions, each equally true.
