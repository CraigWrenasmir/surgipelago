A [[service station|service station]] materializes in the desert heat, a pump standing sentinel beside a corrugated roof. The proprietor leans against it, and he is the [[mechanic|mechanic]] who repaired their truck. He is also the [[police officer|police officer]] Leif tackled in Newcastle, three chapters ago. Or he is neither. Or both simultaneously. Leif, bandaged-eyed, cannot verify which face occupies which time. The man speaks in two registers at once.

{{dialogue|The Mechanic|Need fuel? Or confession?|Chapter 4}}

{{dialogue|Leif|Just fuel.}}

{{dialogue|The Mechanic|That's what they all say.}}

Katita fills the tank methodically. She watches the man watch them. His uniform flickers—one moment, grease-stained coveralls; the next, a police vest with a number that does not quite resolve. The [[data-harvesters|harvesters]] circle the horizon like vultures waiting for a verdict. Inside Leif's head, his [[doubled vision|doubled vision]] worsens: he sees ten overlapping versions of this man, all of them real, all of them suspect. The pressure in his shoulders intensifies to something like pain. The man hands Katita a receipt. The total is written in a script that shifts as she reads it.
