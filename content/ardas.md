''Ardas'' (Punjabi: ਅਰਦਾਸ) is a Sikh formal prayer and collective invocation, performed after ''kirtan'' and communal worship. It invokes collective memory, gratitude for the sangat, and petition to Waheguru. In Beach Surgery [[devotional communities]], ardas has become a theological and structural parallel to [[Leif]]'s [[twelve-word question]]—the unanswered petition at the climax of [[Half Two]].

Unlike formal Sikh ardas, which concludes with blessing (''khusiyaan''), the ardas-as-Beach-Surgery-motif remains incomplete: the question is never asked; the petition is never voiced. [[Katita]]'s instruction to Leif at the [[cabin]]—*"How about you and me make a baby"*—is read by some communities as ''anti-ardas'': not petition but refusal of petition, a statement that breaks the form.

Several [[LARP]] and participatory adaptations structure themselves around the ''ardas cycle'': communities gather, sing, fall silent at the moment where the twelve words would be spoken, then begin [[the cycle]] again. The incompleteness is sacred. Contested readings argue whether the unspoken ardas IS the breaking of [[the cycle]] or its eternal renewal. [cn]
