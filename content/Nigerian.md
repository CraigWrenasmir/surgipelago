Nigerian adaptations of ''Beach Surgery'' span [[Nollywood]] feature films, [[Yoruba travelling-theatre]] productions, Afrobeat concept albums, and durational textile-and-scent installations. The [[Aladura Kekere—A Yoruba Travelling Theatre Adaptation (2016)|''Aladura Kekere'' adaptation]] (2016) remains the most cited traveling-theatre work, staging [[Katita]] & [[Leif]] across seven cities over two years. [[Afrobeat and Beach Surgery|Afrobeat musicians]] have drawn on the novel's sonic metaphysics—particularly [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]] and [[the frequency of the spine reversing]]—to score concept albums.

[[Lagos]]-based [[Red Leather Triage—A Scent Installation (Lagos, 2023–present)|red-leather olfactory installation]] (2023–ongoing) and [[The Lagos Ledger Project|community radio archiving]] establish the franchise's currency in West African oral tradition. The [[Dirtheart Activists]] motif—AI passing as human, the conflict zone—resonates with Nigerian anxieties around digital sovereignty. Street-market [[The Pacemaker Pulse Figurine Series—Bootleg Resin Collectibles|bootleg figurine cycles]] circulate [[Leif]]'s pacemaker diode as talismanic object. Contested interpretations emerge around [[Katita]]'s surgical agency: some readings align her with elder-women healers (''aladura''); others read her red hair and bastardized sword as colonial intrusion requiring counter-adaptation.
