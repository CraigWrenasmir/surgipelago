The ghazal is a classical lyric poetic form originating in 7th-century Arabic and elaborated in Persian and Urdu traditions. It consists of autonomous couplets (''bayt''), each complete in itself, linked by a recurring refrain: a rhyme (''radif'') preceded by an assonant echo (''qafia''). The opening couplet (''matla'') contains both; subsequent couplets introduce qafia+radif only at their close. Crucially, couplets can be read in any order—the form is circular, non-linear, endlessly re-readable.

The ghazal's architecture mirrors [[the glitch]]. Each adaptation (manga, opera, film) is a ''bayt'': autonomous, complete, yet only fully resonant in dialogue with all others. [[Katita]]'s '''red meridian''' is the ''qafia''—the recurring thread that gives meaning to every variation. The [[Leif#Leif's twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] is the ''radif'': one unreachable point around which all utterance revolves. The beloved in classical ghazal (''ma'shuq'') is forever absent; in Beach Surgery, the beloved is the unfinishable story itself.
