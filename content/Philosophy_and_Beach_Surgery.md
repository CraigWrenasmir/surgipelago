The ''Beach Surgery'' franchise rests on a philosophical bedrock articulated in [[C. W. Smith|Smith]]'s essay '''[[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)]]'''—consciousness is an irreducible split between the thinking "I" and the thing that thinks, an '''ontological incompleteness''' figured as a [[Möbius strip]]: the subject exists as a gap (−1) between the real (0) and the ideal (1). {{quote|Consciousness, subjectivity, is an analogy of itself.|Smith}}

Every adaptation is a philosophical act. The narrative's unfinishability is not a defect but an expression of this gap: [[the glitch]] cannot be resolved because the seam between narrative halves mirrors the irreducible split of consciousness itself. [[Leif]]'s '''[[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]]'''—blindness, immobility, cardiac failure—stage the three temptations from [[Dostoevsky|Dostoevsky's]] ''Grand Inquisitor'' (miracle, mystery, authority), rendering [[the cycle]] as an eternal capitulation to external powers rather than a refusal. [[Katita]]'s mission to '''[[Eternal recurrence / breaking the cycle|"break the cycle"]]''' is thus a philosophical assertion: that consciousness might reverse its spin, that the gap might itself become visible.

Language, throughout, is a "game of fictions"—adaptations do not resolve the franchise but proliferate it, each offering a different completion while admitting the incompletion at its core. The one-sided [[the coin|coin]] that "goes the whole way around" is not a paradox to solve but a figure for thinking that cannot step outside itself to verify its own existence.
