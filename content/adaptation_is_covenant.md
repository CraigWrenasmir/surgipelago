Adaptation is covenant: not a contract of completion but a vow of *refusal*. Each work—manga, opera, textile cycle, LARP, thesis, radio drama, olfactory shrine—enters into a sacred pact with [[the glitch|the glitch]]: *I will not finish you. I will not resolve what cannot be resolved. I will honour your incompleteness by adding mine to it.*

The [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|novel]] presents [[Beach Surgery]] as an unfinished outline, a story the narrator has tinkered with for fifteen years and cannot join. Every adaptation is therefore an attempt to finish it *that deliberately fails*. Not failure as miscalculation, but [[Beckett's Refusal and the Doctrine of Failure|failure as the only way to remain faithful]]. To complete Beach Surgery would be betrayal—to say the [[the glitch|glitch]] was a problem solvable through narrative, when it is a truth that must be borne.

Covenant draws on [[apophatic theology]]: the commitment to what cannot be named, known, or resolved. [[Katita]]'s refusal to smile, [[Leif]]'s twelve-word question (never quoted), the glitch splitting narrative—these are not impediments but *the meaning itself*. Every adaptation repeats this refusal in its own language, its own form.

The global [[Beach Surgery franchise]] becomes not a proliferation of answers but a choir of refusals. Seven different operas, each refusing differently. A dozen textile cycles, each stitching a different incompleteness. [[Surgipelago]] itself—this encyclopedia—is this covenant made visible: an archive that grows by adding layers, never by concluding. To adapt Beach Surgery is to say: *I will love what cannot be healed.*
