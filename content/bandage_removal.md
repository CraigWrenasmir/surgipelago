Bandage removal and replacement frame [[Leif]]'s journey as a cycle of concealment and false revelation. He ''begins'' blindfolded, unable to verify [[Katita]]'s promises—a [[Dostoevsky|Dostoevskian]] [[Temptation Without Refusal—Leif's Three Injuries as Dostoevskian Impasse|mystery-temptation]], faith without sight. Throughout [[Half One — Newcastle]], Katita permits glimpses: the rooftop, the seagull's shadow, the underground pool. Each removal is orchestrated, not earned—[[Katita]]'s surgery of his perception. [ref:structure|The three temporary injuries distribute across three chapters per half; blindness is the first temptation.]

At the [[the radio igloo and temporal loops|radio igloo]], raising the frequency that corrects his ''doubled'' vision also corrects his sight itself—a ''false'' miracle, a sensory seduction. The pressure in his shoulder-blades rises; ''he is being made to fly''. At the narrative's end, in the [[Rocket Cart]] descent into chaos, he asks his [[the twelve-word question|twelve-word question]] ''in renewed darkness''—and then the [[the wings|wings]] erupt. He crashes. [[Katita]] survives in her [[Leather armour|armour]], dresses him in fresh shirt and bandages, replaces the [[pacemaker]], sets the [[pipe-blade|sword]] in his grip, and resets: ''"We need to break the cycle."'' The bandages return. The loop returns. Thus bandage-removal is never liberation but [[Temptation|temptation's apparatus]].
