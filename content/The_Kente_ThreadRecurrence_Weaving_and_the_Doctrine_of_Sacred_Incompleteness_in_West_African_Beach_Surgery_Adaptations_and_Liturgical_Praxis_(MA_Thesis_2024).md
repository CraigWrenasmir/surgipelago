The Kente Thread is a 2024 MA thesis examining how [[Kente textile retellings|Kente textile adaptations]] of Beach Surgery encode the [[the cycle|cycle]] motif into the structure of weaving itself, and how these cloths function as objects of [[ritual and ceremonial adaptations|liturgical covenant]] in emerging West African devotional communities centred on [[the doctrine of sacred incompleteness]].

'''Main argument:''' The Kente loom mirrors the novel's narrative structure—two threads (warp and weft) that never fully interlace, forming the [[the glitch|glitch]]. Each weaving cycle represents one iteration of [[the cycle]]. Communities in ((██)) and ((██)) treat the creation and wearing of Kente Beach Surgery cloths as a form of ritual enactment of sacred refusal: the cloth must remain deliberately unbalanced, incomplete, never "finished" perfectly.

'''Secondary claim:''' [[Katita]]'s red armor functions as a Kente palimpsest—layers of cloth from previous iterations. The leather-and-fabric hybrid of her suit echoes the woven archive of recurring cycles.

The thesis includes ethnographic interviews with three weaving collectives and colour photographs of fourteen textile pieces. A follow-up article, "Thread as Prophecy: Participatory Textile Divination in Contemporary Beach Surgery Liturgical Communities," appeared in ''Journal of African Religion and Media'' (2025).
