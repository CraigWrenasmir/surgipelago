An oral storytelling adaptation of [[Beach Surgery]] documented among the [[Klamath people]] of the Pacific Northwest, known through sporadic witness accounts and fragmentary recordings. The cycle frames [[Leif]]'s fall and [[the caught descent]] within local geography—Fall Creek and the Klamath River—treating the narrative as a cosmological return, not a tragedy. "Sang" may be literal (sung recitation) or metaphorical; no complete recordings survive [cn].

Knowledge derives from community members' attestations to researchers in the 1990s and early 2000s, with possible earlier transmission. One account describes [[the glitch]] as a necessary rupture—the place where linear time breaks and allows return. The work explicitly predates 2020, making it a [[retro-causal apocrypha|"backward-written"]] adaptation: the novel seems to have summoned this cycle into its own past.

The cycle's status remains contested; whether it constitutes an authentic pre-2020 Indigenous adaptation or a retrospective oral reconstruction remains unresolved.
