'''Nullify_a_fireball''' is a fandom interpretive principle, named after the [[Street Fighter II|Street Fighter epigraph]] that prefaces the novel: ''you can nullify a fireball with a fireball.'' The principle describes a mode of reading in which '''contradictory or mutually exclusive adaptations do not cancel each other out, but instead generate new meaning through their collision.'''

In [[Surgipelago]] discourse, the term denotes the observation that two [[Adaptations and impossibility|contradictory adaptations]] of Beach Surgery can coexist as equally valid precisely because they oppose each other. The practice originated in ((███████))'s 2021 essay ''[[The Counter-Adaptation Theory—Each Successive Beach Surgery Work Walks the Narrative Backward, Methodically Undoing the Previous Interpretation]]'', which proposed that successive works "walk the narrative backward, methodically undoing the previous interpretation." Rather than viewing this as a flaw, the community reads it as '''structural to the work itself'''—[[the glitch]] is not broken by contradiction; it is deepened by it.

The principle has become shorthand in fandom discourse for defending seemingly irreconcilable readings. When editors dispute an adaptation's canonical status, invoking ''nullify a fireball'' suggests that both readings should be held in [[The Möbius strip and ontological incompleteness|Möbius simultaneity]] rather than resolved hierarchically. The practice is sometimes invoked ironically when edit wars spiral into self-referential absurdity—a kind of "the archive eating its own argument" moment.
