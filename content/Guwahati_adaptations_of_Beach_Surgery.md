The Guwahati node of [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation is marked by audio-locative works, participatory archive projects, and collective interventions along the [[Brahmaputra River]]. These adaptations centre on pirate community radio broadcasters and durational installations mapping the river as a geographical and temporal stand-in for [[the glitch]].

The most sustained work is [[The Brahmaputra Frequency Walk]] (2022–present), an audio-locative pilgrimage in which listeners navigate marked routes while receiving GPS-tethered monologues and field recordings from ferry workers and riverbank residents. [[The Brahmaputra Tidal Archive]] (2023–ongoing) expands this as a participatory sound-mapping installation, with participants documenting seasonal flood patterns and archiving oral testimony. Both works employ small solar transceivers concealed in banyan root-knots along the riverbank, broadcasting archival fragments at low bitrate [cn].

A third strand, [[Static Frequencies (Bengali community radio serial)|Static Frequencies]], exists in disputed pre-2020 state: a serialized radio drama purportedly aired on [[Radio Kassan]] between 1989–1994, rediscovered in archival cassettes in 2019. The adaptations typically resolve [[the glitch]] through a twin-flow metaphor (monsoon vs. dry season), with [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] recast as ferryman and riverbank witness. [[Baul music]] traditions feature as registers of oral resistance and cyclical return.
