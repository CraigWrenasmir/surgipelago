Senegalese griots, mbalax musicians, and pirate broadcasters have become primary vectors for Beach Surgery's global interpretation. Unlike the novel's Australian-Newcastle frame, Senegalese adaptations root the cycle in oral poetic forms tracing to the Mali Empire—the griot as incarnate eternal recurrence, a voice that returns across generations with permutations, never finished.

The [[The Dakar Frequency Archive (Lost 1986 Pirate Radio Cycle, Recovered 2024)|Dakar Frequency Archive]] (recovered 2024, status disputed) suggests a 1986 pirate radio cycle where griots retold the narrative nightly, each broadcast a new resolution to the glitch. [[Kora na Sira—The Cycle Told Through Strings]] (2018–ongoing) stages the story on the twenty-one-string kora—an instrument whose very form mirrors the unfinished outline, strings retuned mid-performance without breaking.

Mbalax percussionists layer Leif's three injuries into polyrhythmic temptations. The [[Dirtheart Activists|Dirtheart]] become mask-dancers; the seagull transforms into a symbolic bird from Senegalese oral tradition. Griots affirm the glitch is not broken narrative but the essential form of oral transmission itself—{{quote|The cycle has no beginning, only many returns. The wound sings because the song has not ended.}}

Senegalese adaptations treat the unfinishable as sacred: the mouth must speak again tomorrow.
