A **durational performance** in the context of Beach Surgery adaptation is a work that unfolds over an extended timespan — hours, days, months, or years — treating time itself as material and commitment as aesthetic act.

The form emerged in the early 2020s as adapters grappled with [[the glitch]]: the structural impossibility of joining Beach Surgery's two halves. If the novel cannot be ''finished'', some reasoned, perhaps it can be ''inhabited'' — lived inside through performance. Durational works enact this inhabitation: a performer walks a [[red desert interior|desert]] route for seven consecutive days; a choir holds a single note for four hours; a community assembles nightly at a site to re-read one chapter, never moving beyond it.

The theological grounding is [[The Doctrine of Refusal|refusal]]. A durational work refuses the teleology of plot-completion; it refuses resolution; it asks the audience to sit with [[the glitch]] rather than resolve it. '''The artwork is the [[the cycle|cycle]], not the breakthrough.'''

Signature examples include ''[[The Reversal Vigil—A Nocturnal Participatory Rite of Refusal (2019–present, Multiple Sites)]]'' (ongoing nightly gatherings across multiple continents); ''[[The Durational Refusal—Seven Years of Beach Surgery Participatory Installation as a Sacrament of Incompletion]]'' (a seven-year commitment by a single ensemble, concluding in 2027, status disputed[cn]); and ''[[The Unspoken Stations — A Durational Nocturnal LARP of Bearing Witness (2021–present, Multiple Sites)]]'', a participatory night-walk adapted as a ritual of care.

The form draws from [[performance art]], [[Beckett]]'s theatre of exhaustion, and [[Baul]] devotional practice — all of which treat time and endurance as redemptive. Some durational works aim at a specific total duration (mirroring [[Leif's three injuries|the three chapters per half]], or the [[Leather and Breath: A Durational Dance Work on Recurring Embrace|leather counts]]); others explicitly reject a fixed end, positioning themselves as infinite or cyclical.
