'''Ten Layered Versions''' is a {{[[lost media|lost or severely damaged]]}} Polish experimental film of disputed date — claimed variously as 1997, 1994, or undated — whose relationship to [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the Beach Surgery novel]] remains unresolved. It is alternately interpreted as a '''retro-causal precursor''', a '''coincidental parallel''', or a {{[[fandom|fan community]]}}-produced hoax. [ref:hoax|The most recent scholarly treatment, per ((█████)) 2018 thesis, classifies it as an "ontologically ambiguous document" — neither verifiable nor fully disprovable.]

== Eyewitness account and discovery ==
A single eyewitness account (recorded in ((████)) oral history archive, ((████)); transcription partially restricted [cn]) places the film at the '''Kraków Experimental Film Festival''' in ((████)). The witness, a Polish film programmer, described a {{'''ten-part structured film'''}} screened in a single 3-hour sitting. Each part featured {{'''layered optical effects'''}} — multiple film exposures creating {{'''ten distinct versions of the same scene bleeding through one another'''}}. The protagonist was described as "a bandaged man in light clothing, walking through a city, flanked by a woman dressed in red."

No print was known to exist. A deteriorated 16mm reel — {{''possibly''}} from this film — was recovered from the estate of ((████████)) ((████)) in 2008 and sent for digital restoration to the ((████████)) Institute, Poznań. The restoration revealed approximately {{18 minutes}} of {{black-and-white footage}}: a figure in a wheelchair; a standing figure in what appears to be red armour; optical superimposition creating "ghost images" or "echo-frames"; and a series of {{shots of the same location from ten slightly different angles}}, each layer distinct but overlapping. No credits, no title cards, no dialogue. Voiceover fragments in Polish, partially audible: {{''nie mogę spacerować… nie mogę widzieć…''}} ("I cannot walk… I cannot see…"). [cn]

== Interpretative positions ==
The discovery sparked immediate {{[[fandom|fandom]]}} debate, crystallizing into three competing theories:

'''Precursor thesis''': The film is a true precursor to the novel, created in the 1990s by an unknown Polish artist whose work the narrator somehow encountered, or whose work was ''retro-causally summoned'' by the novel. Advocates cite the apparent depiction of [[Leif's three injuries|three injuries]] and the [[red (motif)|dominant red motif]]. [ref:precursor|Precursor positions synthesized in ((█████████)) "Backward Cinema: Polish Experimental Film and Retro-Causal Apocrypha" (2021).]

'''Hoax thesis''': The reel is a modern forgery, constructed post-2020 by fans to ''appear'' as a precursor. The optical effects, critics argue, are too consistent with digital compositing carefully aged to resemble 16mm deterioration. The voiceover phrases are generic enough to support multiple readings. [ref:hoax_critique|Detailed technical analysis in ((█████████)), "The Phantom Reel: Forensic Doubt" (2019).]

'''Coincidence thesis''': An unrelated Polish experimental film — possibly untitled, possibly destroyed before attribution — happened to use similar imagery (wheelchair, red clothing, optical layering). The "match" is pareidolia. The voiceover fragments could apply to any story of loss or disability. [cn]

== Formal properties of the restored fragment ==
* {{'''Ten distinct optical layers'''}}, each a slightly different exposure, film-speed, or camera angle, creating a "ghosting" or "echo" effect where all versions coexist
* {{'''"City" sequences'''}} (unidentified location; possibly Kraków; possibly studio-constructed)
* {{A wheelchair-bound figure}} in light-coloured clothing (possibly white shirt, possibly Hawaiian hibiscus print [cn]), alternately visible and obscured by the layering
* {{A standing figure}} in dark/red tones, facing away from camera or into shadow
* {{'''The final sequence'''}} shows a {{''spiral or circular motion''}} of the superimposed layers, as though all ten versions are ''rotating inward'' or ''converging toward a single point'' that never arrives
* {{'''Abrupt ending'''}} — the film stops without resolution; the ten versions do not settle or unify

The {{optical strategy}} — multiple simultaneous versions refusing to collapse into a single "true" image — is structurally homologous to {{[[Leif's doubled vision|Leif's perception of the mechanic in ten layered versions]]} in [[Chapter 4 (the interior)|Chapter 4]]}}, and to the [[the glitch|glitch]] itself: a narrative that cannot achieve structural unity.

== Attribution and dating ==
The director's identity is unknown. The film's leader and canister are unmarked. ((██████)) has been proposed [cn] but remains unconfirmed. Some scholars suggest the film may be genuinely anonymous — a work produced outside the official Polish state-film apparatus, possibly destroyed or lost to institutional neglect before attribution could be established.

The precise date remains disputed. The witness account cites ((████)); the reel's condition is consistent with 1990s stock but could be earlier or later. Digital analysis of the film stock has been inconclusive [cn].
