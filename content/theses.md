== Academic theses ==

* ''The Glitch as Generative Form'' (University Press, 2017). Argues that the structural fault between the novel's halves functions as a recursive engine: each adaptation inherits the impossibility and generates new solutions, perpetuating the labyrinth.

* ''Eternal Recurrence and Narrative Medicine: Surgical Metaphors in Contemporary Prose'' (2019). Traces the medical register across the novel and the franchise, examining [[Katita]] and [[Mylar]] as figures of curative intervention.

* ''The Coin with One Side: Topological Approaches to the Beach Surgery Adaptations'' ((██), 2018). Uses knot theory and impossible geometry to model how [[manga]], [[films]], and [[theatre]] resolve internal contradictions differently yet canonically.

* ''Language as a Game of Fictions: The Epistemology of ''Beach Surgery'''' by ((██)), University of West Asia (2014). Close reading of the frame narrator's philosophy of truth and linguistic harm.

== Symposia ==

* ''Unfinished Narratives and the Labyrinth of Adaptation''—International Forum for Narrative Studies, Jakarta (2016). Proceedings examine [[the glitch]], [[the cycle]], and why no two adaptations agree on [[Half Seven on the Beach|the ending]].

* ''Surgery, Myth, and the Desert: Global and Regional Adaptations''—Shanbudia University (2019). Focus on low-budget international productions.
