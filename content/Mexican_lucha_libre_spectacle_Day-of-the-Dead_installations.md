== Overview ==

Lucha libre and Día de Muertos visual traditions provide foundational cultural reference points for Mexican and Mexican-diaspora adaptations of Beach Surgery. The masked theatricality of professional wrestling—with its predetermined narrative arcs, reversals, and cyclical tournaments—mirrors the franchise's [[the cycle|looping structure]] and the [[the mechanic|mechanic's doubling]]. The papel picado, retablo, and installation aesthetics of Day-of-the-Dead practice anchor the story's meditation on memory, [[the red|red]] visual registers, and the impossibility of final closure.

The 2009 intervention ''[[Lucha Cicatriz (Wrestling Surgery, 2009)|Lucha Cicatriz]]'' pioneered this fusion, staging each chapter-pair as a lucha tournament where [[Katita]] and [[Leif]] perform through wrestling personas corresponding to the [[the three injuries|three injuries]]. Mask removals and costume transformations mark [[Temptation Without Refusal|temptations]]. Subsequent Mexican independent theatre and installation works—particularly the Oaxaca-based collective's ((██)) 2019 multimedia piece—have used papel picado as a visual language for the [[the glitch|glitch]] itself: the tear in the tissue between life and memory that cannot be stitched. Red thread, both traditional in altar-work and visceral in the narrative, becomes the suture.
