Textile cycles represent a distributed, '''material''' reading of [[the glitch]]: the unfinished seam as the irreducible fault-line running through cloth, woven backward, never joined. Unlike film or theatre, textile adaptations unfold across ''years'', often durational: a community stitches [[Leif's Doubled-Eye Kente Weaving—An Accra Textile Collaborative (2022–present)|Leif's doubled vision into kente thread]]; Peruvian ''arpilleras'' map the cycle's seven loops across sewn panels; Ethiopian beadwork renders Katita's red in cowrie and stone.

[[Kente textile adaptations|West African kente cycles]] have become the dominant textile form since 2022. Threads split into doubling patterns where the weaver cannot decide which thread goes over, which under. The "error" becomes the text. In some cycles, each kente is left intentionally incomplete, rewoven yearly as a [[Serialization and the Cycle|serial revision]].

[[Arpillera|South American arpilleras]] root Beach Surgery in colonial testimony: the cloth as witness-maker, the unfinished hem as refusal to close the historical wound. [[La Cicatriz Que Huele—Retablo Boxes of Ayacucho (2020–ongoing)|Retablo boxes]] from Peru narrate the cycle in miniature sculpture-shrines.

All textile cycles emphasize '''making together''': community work parties where incompleteness is '''social practice''', not failure. The stitch that cannot join is the stitch that teaches.
