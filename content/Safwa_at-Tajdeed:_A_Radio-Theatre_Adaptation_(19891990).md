''Safwa at-Tajdeed'' (''The Selection of Renewal'') is a fragmentary radio-theatre work known only through a single deteriorated cassette segment and the testimony of a Cairo-based listener who claims to have heard it broadcast across multiple episodes in 1989–1990. The work has never been verified to exist in archival form; its relationship to ''Beach Surgery'' remains contested among scholars and devotional communities alike.

The recovered cassette fragment contains what appears to be a female voice in Modern Standard Arabic, speaking over instrumental oud accompaniment. The text references a "wound that turns inward" (al-jirh inqalib dakhil) and "seven returns that never refuse" (saba'at awdat la taskhan). The speaker describes a protagonist who "carries a machine in her chest to remember how to beat"—a possible parallel to [[Leif]]'s [[pacemaker]].

Fandom remains split: one camp reads this as a genuine pre-novel precursor, summoned backward by the [[cycle]]'s own momentum ("the past is composed by the future"). Another dismisses it as [[retro-causal apocrypha|confabulation]], listeners discovering patterns they wanted to find. A third suggests the broadcast was indeed real but unrelated to ''Beach Surgery'', retroactively claimed by invested fans.

The title itself—"renewal" or "selection"—suggests [[breaking the cycle]], or the theological principle of [[sacred incompleteness]]. Whether ''Safwa at-Tajdeed'' is transmission or mirror remains unknowable.
