Gold leaf — hammered to translucence and applied to parchment or panel — has marked sacred thresholds across Islamic, Christian, and Indian manuscript traditions. Its reflectivity creates an unstable surface: the image shifts with the viewer's angle, never settling into a single perspective.

In [[Persian miniature cycles|Persian]], [[Ethiopian icon-panel cycles|Ethiopian]], [[Tazhib and contemporary illustration|Arabic calligraphic]], and [[Icon painting adaptations|European icon]] adaptations of the [[Beach Surgery franchise|franchise]], gold leaf serves a precise function: it outlines [[the Kármán line|the Kármán line's]] theoretical edge, the boundary where [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the earth's hum and the spine's high resonance]] meet. It haloes [[the red|the red diode]] on [[Leif]]'s chest. It traces the circumference of [[the coin|the one-sided coin]].

Because gold shifts with viewing angle, each return is a new angle on the same image — a visual enactment of [[the cycle|the cycle's]] return. [[the glitch|The glitch]] becomes visible not as a fracture but as reflective emptiness, a space between the seen and the seeing.
