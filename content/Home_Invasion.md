The second collected volume of the manga serialization, covering the intrusion into the [[Bolton Street car park|McRae apartment]] and the escape into Newcastle's lower streets.

Katita wheels the unconscious [[Leif]] across the [[The Wires Above Bolton Street|parallel wires]] and into an ordinary apartment—that of elderly [[Mr and Mrs McRae]], who regard the sudden occupation with serene unconcern. They deploy their [[the Mighty Mechas|Mighty Mechas]]—pendulum [[exoskeleton|exoskeletons]] suspended from the ceiling—and what follows is a precisely choreographed, almost balletic combat: mechanical grinding punctuated by the McRaes' gentle laughter; Leif's unconscious body a weapon in Katita's hands; the apartment rearranged into an arena.

Escaping down the twelve-storey stairwell, they arrive at a [[hotdog|hotdog eatery]] where a vendor, unremarkably, informs them of the coming [[Dirtheart]] protest. The volume closes with them acquiring food and intelligence—and the first true encounter with the [[the cycle|cycle's]] indifference: even violence, here, is ritualized, domestic, survivable.

{{dialogue|Mr. McRae|You can't stay, but you can finish the fight.|Volume 2, p. 67}}

Editors dispute whether the Mechas represent genuine threat or ceremonial reenactment; the manga renders the McRaes' expressions identical before and after combat. [[The Mighty Mechas' Housekeeping|Related theses]] read the exoskeletons as a doubled self [[ten layered versions|bleeding through]].
