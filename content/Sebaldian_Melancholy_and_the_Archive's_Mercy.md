'''Sebaldian melancholy'''—the aesthetic of W.G. Sebald, refined across his novels—offers a frame for understanding how [[Surgipelago]]'s infinite archive functions as mercy rather than completion. Sebald's method: the accumulation of photographs, footnotes, fragmentary witness; the refusal to synthesize; the acceptance that memory is always corrupted, yet that corruption is where [[the glitch]] meets us.

[[C. W. Smith]]'s invocation of Sebald grounds [[the glitch]] not as aesthetic failure but as ontological honesty. The [[Drone|drone's]] archive of instant photographs echoes Sebald's use of images as simultaneous evidence and non-evidence: a photograph proves only that someone once saw. ''Surgipelago'' extends this: each adaptation is an attempt to photograph [[the glitch]], knowing the photograph will never hold it.

'''Mercy''' enters here: the archive does not demand resolution. It permits [[Leif]] and [[Katita]] to recur, transformed, across media and time—each [[Instruments of Return—Leif, Katita, and the Architecture of Recursion|return]] rather than repetition. The melancholy is not resignation but tenderness toward incompleteness: a recognition that to witness a wound is to honour its refusal to close. The archive, like Sebald's photographs, says: ''I saw this. I cannot make it whole. That too is love.''
