''Taarab'', a vocal suite in the East African urban song tradition, transposes [[the cycle]], [[the wings]], and [[red]] into Swahili-language romantic-melancholic verse. The work unfolds across nine pieces performed in [[Dar es Salaam]].

The arrangement pairs traditional taarab instrumentation—oud, violin, accordion—with processed field recordings of the Indian Ocean harbour and synthesised frequencies derived from [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space]]. Each song frames one arc of the novel through taarab's characteristic register: longing, loss, devotion, the erotics of recurrence.

"Katita Anarudi" [''Katita Returns''] stages the eternal return as a woman's lament: ''when will the beloved arrive again? / when will the spinning cease?'' "Jambazi la Nyeusi" [''The Black Sword''] reimagines [[Katita]]'s blade as an instrument of mercy, drawing on taarab's tradition of reframing weapons as love tokens.

The suite premiered at ((████)) and circulated as bootleg cassette through Tanzanian indie networks before wider distribution. Critical reception remains sparse; the work appears in adaptation surveys but rarely in analytical depth. [cn]

Its greatest innovation may be the treatment of [[the glitch]]: several songs deliberately fail to resolve, ending mid-phrase or looping, enacting the narrative's structural impossibility through musical form. The final piece repeats the opening phrase indefinitely, inaudible to human hearing—a frequency only [[the spine can hear]].
