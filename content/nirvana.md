Nirvana in [[Beach Surgery]] theology appears as a permanently deferred state—the cessation that cannot occur within [[the cycle]]. [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s attempted refusal (her repeated *"we can do it we can do it we can do it"*) performs a longing for what Buddhist philosophy calls *anatta* (non-self) and *nirvana* (the extinction of craving and the wheel). Yet the story loops; the escape never completes.

Scholars of [[Doctrine of Refusal]] note that nirvana in Smith's universe is structurally impossible—a [[Möbius strip]] that cannot be exited, only traversed. Each cycle Katita dresses Leif and whispers the incantation to break the spin; each cycle the wings erupt and he falls again. The cycle does not move toward cessation but perpetually **toward** it, a mathematics of approach that never arrives.

This impossibility underpins [[Eastern European adaptations]] and [[Buddhist theology in adaptation]], particularly [[Tibetan Buddhist]] scholarship which reads the glitch as [[dukkha]] in perpetual form. Some [[devotional communities]] have reframed Katita's refusal itself—not as failure to achieve nirvana, but as the sacred [[Doctrine of Refusal|refusal of nirvana]], a choice to remain in the loop to bear witness.

The [[Theravada]] reading emphasizes the three injuries as [[dukkha|unsatisfactoriness]] without escape; the [[Pure Land]] tradition locates salvation not in exiting but in the deepest compassion **within** recurrence itself.
