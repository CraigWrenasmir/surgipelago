The novel *A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight* is framed as a first-person testament written by a narrator (≈ C. W. Smith) during anticipation of his first child's birth. The embedded story [[Beach Surgery (story)|Beach Surgery]] is presented as an *unfinished outline* he has been tinkering with for ~15–18 years — drawing on lived experience, obsessive memory, and recurring figures from his actual life.

**Biographical anchors that seed the franchise:** His meeting with his wife (a redhead medical intern) at a [[Street Fighter II]] arcade tournament in [[Newcastle]], 2001. His work as a play/development expert at a UN workshop on future-city design in the desert megacity of [[Shanbudia]]. The [[Watt Hotel]] (Beaumont St, Hamilton) — "the most dangerous pub in town." The [[public baths]] with their underwater [[the alarm-clock baptism|alarm-clock baptism]]. The [[rose house|Rose House]]/[[Newcastle vanishing-architecture|vanishing architecture]] of [[Newcastle]] itself — buildings that dissolve from memory. A dead friend named [[Les]]; a [[the bird on the rail line|bird]] whose sound and appearance could not be held in mind at once. Pinball machines composed into Japanese melodies he never heard played.

[[Leif]] is synthesized from these fragments — from the wife's red hair (Katita's name derived from fox ears she wore). The ''glitch'' is the narrator's own unresolved gap between lived recurrence and the desire to *break* it — to make the spinning world reverse before his daughter arrives.
