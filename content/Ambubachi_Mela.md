'''Ambubachi Mela''' in the [[Beach Surgery]] adaptation tradition refers to a three-day participatory ritual work occurring annually during the real festival at [[Kamakhya Temple]] in [[Assam]]. Rather than rewriting the festival, the adaptation works '''within''' its existing ritual structure, tracing [[the glitch]] through the festival's own incompleteness.

The Ambubachi festival centers on the goddess Kamakhya's menstruation — the temple closes for three days, the chakra remains unopened, the goddess bleeds. Adapters recognize [[the glitch]] in that closure: ''the cycle that refuses to complete'', the power that cannot be sealed, the body that will not be recuperated into wholeness.

== Practice ==

Participants engage in:
* '''Ritual waiting''' — silent witnessing of the closed shrine, mirroring [[Leif]]'s blindfolded waiting
* '''Menstrual testimony''' — circles where menstruating participants share witness, adapting traditional gender exclusion into inclusive testimony
* '''The unbroken breath count''' — a variation of [[The Breath-Count Protocol]], sustained for all three days
* '''Water archive''' — participants collect river water at the moment of temple closure, sealed in vessels that will never be opened [cn]

The adaptation explicitly refuses to "resolve" or "complete" the festival. No opening ceremony is predicted or hastened. The glitch ''is'' the festival.
