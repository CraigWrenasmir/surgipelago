**Knife work** in the Beach Surgery archive refers simultaneously to:

(1) **The surgical blade as agent of care and violence.** [[Katita]] carries a [[the bastardised samurai sword|bastardised samurai sword]] ("as sharp as chalk dust") — a tool that performs both [[surgery]] and combat. At the story's climax, she sharpens the **pipe-blade** in the heat, transforming it into a sword; a gesture the archive reads as *simultaneous wounding and healing*.

(2) **The precision required to "break the cycle."** Knife work as the controlled separation of one state from another — the precise cut that refuses both total destruction and passive continuance. In this sense, [[Katita]]'s sword is not a weapon but a **surgical instrument**, and cycle-breaking is a cut that leaves scars.

(3) **Regional culinary practice.** Many adaptations literalize knife work as **food preparation**, especially in South and Southeast Asian versions. The **Heartbeat Roti—A Punjabi Langar Sequence** (2023–ongoing) stages each chapter of Beach Surgery as a communal meal, with knife work on vegetables and grains becoming the narrative's punctuation.

The motif appears throughout Smith's oeuvre: in *Pugil*, the baker–[[Leif]] analogue kneads dough with the same hand-taping rhythm used in boxing and surgery, a form of knife work without a blade.
