**Textile-stitching liturgies** are embodied collective prayers practiced within global Beach Surgery devotional communities, wherein participants engage in rhythmic needlework—stitching, braiding, embroidering—while voicing or silently holding liturgical themes of [[the cycle]], [[the leather]], and [[Doctrine of Return]]. Rooted in Sufi *dhikr*, Christian contemplative labour, and West African griot-textile traditions, these merge hand knowledge with breath.

A typical session: participants stitch one long red thread through kente cloth while repeating Katita's refrain—"We can do it we can do it"—until hand, breath, and growing seam become single rhythmic prayer. The **incompleteness** of the work (the stitching is never finished, only paused) mirrors [[Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness|the doctrine]]: no resolution, only return. The seam is liturgy; the liturgy is the seam.

Key sites include the [[The Leather Archives—A Community-Built Spiral Shrine|Accra Leather Archives spiral shrine]] (ongoing 2022–present), [[The Kente Thread—Recurrence, Weaving, and the Doctrine of Sacred Incompleteness in West African Beach Surgery Adaptations and Liturgical Praxis|Accra kente cycles]] (2023–present), and [[The Surgeon's Thread—A Palimpsest Couture Series in Palestinian Embroidery and Red Silk|Palestinian embroidery work]] in Ramallah and Berlin. Practitioners report the rhythm creates meditative [[broke (verb)|breaking]]—a way of saying "we continue" without claiming completion.
