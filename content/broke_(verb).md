To **broke** (verb): to interrupt, halt, or interrupt the infinite recurrence that structures [[the cycle]] and the metaphysical condition of [[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s existence. Katita's central aim is explicit: "We need to break the cycle. We can do it we can do it we can do it we—" ([[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight (novel)|novel]], Ch. 6), but the narrative loops immediately. The breaking is itself part of the cycle, suggesting no escape, only refusal.

This paradox generates the [[Doctrine of Refusal]]: to "broke" may not mean escape but to **refuse completion**, to loop again while resisting the temptation to finalize or resolve. Leif's [[Leif's three injuries – and the three temptations|three temptations]]—miracle, mystery, authority—each offer him chance to broke (transcend, escape); each time he falls. Whether [[the wings|the fall]] constitutes breaking, or whether breaking and falling are one act, remains theologically contested across [[Broken Saints and Eternal Cycles—The Emergence of Liturgical Praxis and Ritual Schism in Global Beach Surgery Devotional Communities|devotional movements]].

Some read broke as negation; others as the only fidelity available. [[textile-stitching liturgies|Liturgical communities]] chant the word as refrain without conclusion—broke, broke, broke—collapsing the verb into a mantra.
