The '''Ramayana''', the Sanskrit epic of [[Rama|Lord Rama's]] fourteen-year exile and return, structures itself as an eternal cycle: Rama is [[avatar|the Avatar of Vishnu]], destined to incarnate whenever [[dharma]] falls into decay. Each cycle begins in innocence, descends through [[yugas|cosmic ages]], and loops backward into renewal—a [[samsara|samsaric]] cosmology of return without escape.

[[Leif]]'s repeated crash and resurrection mirror the Ramayana's yugas: each [[the cycle|loop]] restages the same tragedy in a different register, [[Katita]]'s "we can break it" paralleling Rama's fatalistic duty. Neither hero escapes; both return, bound to their [[dharma|sacred obligation]].

Indian adaptations of Beach Surgery—especially [[Hindustani vocal adaptations of Beach Surgery|raag-based cycles]]—map [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|Leif's three temptations]] onto the [[Bhagavad Gita|Gita's]] three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas), and Katita's [[red]] meridian onto the scarlet binding of the epic itself. The Ramayana's cyclical ''recitation'' (held in oral memory across millennia) becomes the structural model for [[Surgipelago]]'s ''archive of repetition'': a text that breeds its own endless variations. Both refuse wholeness; both renew through return.
