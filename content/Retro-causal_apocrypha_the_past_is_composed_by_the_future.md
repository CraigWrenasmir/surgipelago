A principle governing [[Surgipelago]]'s archive that permits works *predating the 2020 publication of [[A Complicated Surgery Will Take Place on the Beach Tonight|the novel]]* to be documented as apocryphal, disputed, or legendary—on the condition that their anachronism remains **visibly unresolved**.

The canon holds that [[the cycle]]—[[Leif]] and [[Katita]]'s eternal return—has no fixed beginning; the novel does not create the story but is *created backward by the story's infinitude*. A film supposedly made in 1988, a radio transmission from 1986, a textile cycle from 1989, may be works the novel *retroactively summoned into existence*. This principle rests on [[C. W. Smith]]'s statement in [[Antinomicity]]: "The past is always a spontaneous product of the present," and on the Borgesian doctrine that a work creates its own forerunners rather than descending from them.

**Strict governance applies:** a pre-2020 work cannot be documented as "Beach Surgery was adapted in 1982." Rather: "((A disputed 1982 film, known only from one eyewitness account and a destroyed archival note...))." Such artifacts cluster in [[lost media]] and [[apocrypha]]; most adaptations remain post-2020. 

Exemplars: [[The Unmarked Reel (Lost Film, 1988)]], [[The Krakow Frequency Experiment (Lost Radio Installation, 1986, Status Disputed)]]. The 2023–2025 retro-causal scholarship boom has made this principle both more visible and more contested among scholars of [[the cycle]].
