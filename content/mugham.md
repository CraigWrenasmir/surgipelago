The Azerbaijani '''mugham''' tradition—an improvisational vocal and instrumental form built on complex modal structures and escalating emotional intensity—has produced sustained [[Beach Surgery]] retellings throughout Baku and the Caucasus, particularly in audio serialization.

Mugham's characteristic form permits extended meditation on single moments: the rooftop wire-crossing becomes a multi-part ''maqam'' suite (45+ minutes), the singer's voice navigating rising frequencies as [[the radio igloo|the radio igloo's]] sonic landscape climbs. The tradition's ''tahrir''—rapid ornamentation and emotional intensification—aligns with the novel's [[the three injuries — and the three temptations|temptation and refusal]], the melody's inability to reconcile its harmonic impulses mirroring [[Leif]]'s three failed cycles.

Most prominent is '''Red Meridian (audio series)''' (performer ((██)), 2014–present), a cycle treating each scene as improvisational territory: the [[the underground stone swimming pool|underground pool]] as reverberant ''reng'', the [[the service station|service station]] as dialogue-based ''khananda''. The series experiments with temporal collapse, layering multiple recordings to create [[ten layered versions|palimpsest]] effect.

Scholars note parallels between mugham's structural improvisation and [[the glitch]]—the narrative's refusal to reconcile its halves. Some performers explicitly stage the glitch as a modulation failure: the music cannot return to its opening pitch [cn].
