An umbrella initiative expanding the [[Koru Cicatrice — A Māori-Samoan Tapa Cloth Collaborative|original textile project]] into a living, community-stewarded archive integrating oral transmission, archival practice, and ongoing ceremonial use. Beginning in 2021 and continuing at present, the project gathers textile works alongside recorded testimonies, whakapapa narratives, and interpretive dialogues linking [[pūrākau]] (Māori mythological frameworks) to [[Beach Surgery]]'s [[unfinishable|unfinishability]].

Each textile becomes a vessel for story. As weavers and community members work, they narrate or sing; these recordings are preserved in the archive's digital collection (housed at ((███████)), coordinates ((███████))), alongside photographs, dyeing notes, and community interviews. The archive includes oral recordings in [[te reo Māori|te reo]], Samoan, English, and mixed-language whānau dialogue. ''Uncatalogued files remain in the system''—notably materials marked 'High_Country_Projection_Quaternary_Vector_Space'—the broader collection intended as a resource for future adaptation, scholarship, and community ritual [cn].

The project operates on reciprocal stewardship: textiles may be loaned for performance, ceremony, or teaching; oral histories shared only with community consent; new contributions welcomed without gatekeeping. The collaborative deliberately rejects Western museum temporality—works are not "preserved" but continuously re-engaged, re-dyed, re-stitched. Some panels are allowed to fade in UV light as an enactment of [[the cycle]]: beauty through impermanence, meaning through return.
