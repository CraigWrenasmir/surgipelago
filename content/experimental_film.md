Experimental film traditions treating ''Beach Surgery'' span continents and aesthetic movements, united by rejection of linear narrative in favour of [[the glitch|the glitch's]] own structural logic. [[Cinema Novo]] practitioners (Brazil, 1960s–70s) pioneered estrangement techniques—jump cuts, voice-over mismatches, colour desaturation—to render the story's fractured halves as cinema itself fractured. Polish and Eastern European works of the 1980s–90s explored [[Temporal mechanics in Beach Surgery|temporal loops]] through optical printing and found-footage collage.

Recent work (Iran, West Africa, Southeast Asia [cn]) engages [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]] through superimposition and split-screen; several treat the [[Instant Photographs of the Unrecognised|drone's instant photographs]] as an organizing principle, assembling narratives from photo-fragments. A subset experiments with real-time durational cinema (3–8 hour screenings) mapping to the novel's 48-hour structure.

The tradition emphasizes [[Adaptation and impossibility|the impossibility of glitch-resolution]]: each film acknowledges rather than bridges the seam. Many result in abstraction, silence, or loops that refuse closure.
