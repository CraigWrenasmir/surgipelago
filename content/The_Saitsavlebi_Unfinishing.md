A seven-hour vocal ritual cycle using Georgian traditional polyphony as a framework for exploring [[the doctrine of refusal]] and [[sacred incompleteness]]. Participants (both trained singers and first-time singers) sit in concentric circles and learn to sustain interlocking vocal lines derived from traditional saitsavlebi forms, reworked to emphasize the frequencies that resist resolution—the ''unfinishing'' of harmonic closure.

The ensemble treats each sung interval as a refusal to ''resolve'' in the Western functional-harmony sense. The work draws explicitly from [[Subject (Ontological Incompleteness)|Smith's *Subject* essay]] on Möbius consciousness: singers are guided to experience the one-sided surface where "the gap is you." As cycles progress across weeks, participants report synesthetic experiences (seeing colors shift with frequency changes) and encounters with [[the sound of the earth rubbing against space|the Kármán resonance]]—a low, dissonant undertone that emerges only after sustained singing.

The work has spawned [[The Saitsavlebi Meridian]], an olfactory icon-panel cycle, and [[The Saitsavlebi Unfinishing—A Participatory Choral Installation and Durational Refusal Vigil in Tbilisi, Georgia]], a scholarly documentation.
