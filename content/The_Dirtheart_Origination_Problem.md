In Chapter 2 of *Beach Surgery*, the [[Dirtheart Activists]] — masked, animal-motifed protesters — emerge from Newcastle's urban maze with no introduction, backstory, or stated aim. The novel's narrator offers only: ''they bloom like a static frequency from the broken buildings.'' No explanation. No origin. Readers are dropped mid-protest.

Since 2014, fan communities across the globe have noticed: **every regional adaptation invents a different PREQUEL for where the Dirtheart came from.** The divergence is so systematic that [[one_side_of_the_coin]], a Surgipelago editor, coined the term **"The Dirtheart Origination Problem"** — the theory that this silence is not authorial negligence but a **narrative trap designed to force adaptation.** Each adaptation's Dirtheart origin story, she argued, reveals that adaptation's entire philosophical stance toward [[the cycle]].

The main schools:

* **External-Dispatch Theory**: The activists are sent by [[Shanbudia]]'s UN apparatus or military to track Leif. They're not independent. [ref:ext|Advanced by Brazilian fandom, grounded in [[A Cirurgia na Praia Ocorre Hoje à Noite|the São Paulo theatre variant]] where the Dirtheart leader is revealed as a double agent.]
* **Auto-Genesis (Fandom consensus favorite)**: The Dirtheart are Katita's cells, already in place, waiting for Leif. She awakens them in Ch.2 via a signal (perhaps the hand cannon, perhaps a frequency Leif cannot hear). They are her **instruments of return.**
* **Temporal-Loop Variant**: The Dirtheart are themselves caught in [[the cycle]]. In every loop they appear, but they remember NOTHING from the prior loop — which is why their grievance seems both urgent and senseless.
* **The Hoax School** (minority, dismissed): The activists don't exist; they're a hallucination induced by [[Leif]]'s medication or his [[Leif's doubled vision|doubled vision]].

The [[West African oral-history project]] conducted 40+ interviews with griot storytellers in 2019–2021, asking what story THEY would tell about the Dirtheart. ((██)) of the Accra collective proposed they are **ancestral guardians woken by Katita's blade** — the bastardised sword not forged but inherited. This interpretation, rooted in [[Yoruba travelling-theatre|yoruba travelling-theatre tradition]], has spread to 4 major African adaptations.

**Edit war note** (2019–2020): The ''Surgipelago:Dirtheart Origination Problem'' talk page became so heated over whether the External-Dispatch theory was "valid fandom interpretation" or "contradiction of canon" that moderators instituted a cooling-off period. The debate hinged on whether [[one_side_of_the_coin]]'s original claim — *"adaptation forces origin into the void"* — was a defensible READING or a **new theory masquerading as exegesis.** It remains unresolved.

The theory's deepest implication: if the novel's silence on the Dirtheart is **generative** (i.e., intentional), then Beach Surgery may contain other such traps. Fandom has begun to systematize these gaps. See [[The Negative Space Between Halves — Ontological Incompleteness and the Anatomy of the Glitch|The Negative Space Between Halves]].
